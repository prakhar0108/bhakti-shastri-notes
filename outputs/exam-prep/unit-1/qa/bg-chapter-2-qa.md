<ExamQuestion n="12" refs="BG 1.27 – 2.7" question="List Arjuna's arguments for not fighting.">

Arjuna's five reasons — remembered as **C-D-E-F-I**:

1. **Compassion** — he is moved by (material) pity for his kinsmen before him.
2. **Destruction of family tradition** — killing the elders will destroy the dynasty's tradition.
3. **Enjoyment** — with these people killed, how could he enjoy the kingdom? There would be no one to enjoy with.
4. **Fear of sin** — killing so many will bring heavy sinful reaction.
5. **Indecision** — after all his arguments he ends up confused (BG 2.6).

The first four appear in Chapter 1; the fifth arises in Chapter 2. The Lord answers each of them subsequently.

<ExamQuestion n="13" refs="BG 2.2" question="List six symptoms of Bhagavān in English or Sanskrit.">

As explained by Parāśara Muni, the Supreme Personality who possesses **all** of the following, in full, is called Bhagavān:

1. **Riches** (wealth)
2. **Strength**
3. **Fame**
4. **Beauty**
5. **Knowledge**
6. **Renunciation**

Many persons have some of these, but no one possesses them **entirely** — only Kṛṣṇa, because He is the Supreme Personality of Godhead. In class: these six opulences reside in Him **in full and eternally**.

<ExamQuestion n="15" refs="BG 2.5" question="According to scriptural codes, when is a teacher fit to be abandoned?">

- According to scriptural codes, **a teacher who is engaged in abominable action and has lost his sense of discrimination** (between what should and should not be done) **is fit to be abandoned**.
- Example from the purport discussion: Bhīṣma and Droṇa took Duryodhana's side because of financial assistance, and in such circumstances lost their respectability as teachers.
- Example given in class: **Bali Mahārāja gave up Śukrācārya**.

<ExamQuestion n="17" refs="BG 2.17" question="What is the size of soul and what is the symptom of its existence?">

- **Size:** **one ten-thousandth part of the upper portion of the hair point.** When the tip of a hair is divided into one hundred parts, and each part again into one hundred parts, each such part is the measurement of the spirit soul (Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 5.9).
- **Symptom:** **consciousness.** The soul's influence spreads all over the body as consciousness; that is the proof of the presence of the soul. The body minus consciousness is a dead body.
- Analogy from class: the sun is in one place, yet its light is everywhere — the soul is localised, its influence (consciousness) pervades the body.

<ExamQuestion n="18" refs="BG 2.20" question="List the six kinds of transformations the body is subject to.">

1. **_Jāyate_** — it takes birth (from the womb of the mother's body)
2. **_Asti_** — it remains (exists) for some time
3. **_Vardhate_** — it grows
4. **_Vipariṇamate_** — it produces some effects / by-products (offspring)
5. **_Apakṣīyate_** — it gradually dwindles
6. **_Vinaśyati_** — at last it vanishes (is destroyed)

The soul does not go through any of these changes.

<ExamQuestion n="19" refs="BG 2.20" question="Give the English meaning of: _aṇu-ātmā_ and _vibhu-ātmā_.">

There are two kinds of souls:

- **_Aṇu-ātmā_** — the **minute particle soul**, the atomic individual soul (_jīvātmā_).
- **_Vibhu-ātmā_** — the **Supersoul** (_Paramātmā_).

Both are situated on the same tree of the body, within the same heart (Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.2.20).

<ExamQuestion n="20" refs="BG 2.31" question="Why is it that the killing of animals in sacrifice not considered an act of violence?">

- Killing on the battlefield on religious principles and killing animals in the sacrificial fire are not considered violence **because everyone is benefited by the religious principles involved**.
- **The animal sacrificed gets a human life immediately**, without undergoing the gradual evolutionary process from one form to another.
- Likewise the _kṣatriyas_ killed on the battlefield attain the heavenly planets, as do the _brāhmaṇas_ who attain them by offering sacrifice.

<ExamQuestion n="22" refs="BG 2.31" question="What is meant by _sva-dharma_ and what are the two types of _sva-dharma_?">

**_Sva-dharma_** means one's **own specific (prescribed) duty**.

Two kinds of _sva-dharma_:

1. **Material (bodily) _sva-dharma_** — as long as one is **not liberated**, one has to perform the duties of one's particular body, according to religious principles, to achieve liberation. On the bodily plane this is called **_varṇāśrama-dharma_** — man's stepping-stone for spiritual understanding.
2. **Spiritual _sva-dharma_** — when one is **liberated**, one's specific duty becomes **spiritual** and is not in the bodily concept.

In class: bodily duty (_sva-dharma_) should remain **secondary**, and the eternal occupation of the soul — taking shelter of the Lord (_sanātana-dharma_) — **primary**.

<ExamQuestion n="26" refs="BG 2.45" question="The _Vedas_ deal mostly with what?">

The _Vedas_ deal mainly with the subject of the **three modes of material nature** (_trai-guṇya-viṣayā vedāḥ_) — that is, with **religiosity, economic development and sense gratification**.

Kṛṣṇa therefore tells Arjuna to become transcendental to the three modes.

<ExamQuestion n="29" refs="BG 2.54" question="A well-dressed fool cannot be identified unless what?">

**Unless he speaks.**

- A fool is undiscovered as long as he does not speak; a well-dressed fool cannot be identified unless he speaks, but **as soon as he speaks, he reveals himself at once**.
- Speech is the most important quality of any man. The immediate symptom of a Kṛṣṇa conscious man is that **he speaks only of Kṛṣṇa** and matters relating to Him.

<ExamQuestion n="31" refs="BG 2.61" question="Who is the example of a _mat-paraḥ_ given in 2.61?">

**Mahārāja Ambarīṣa.**

- He fixed his **mind** on the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, his **words** in describing the Lord's abode, his **hands** in cleansing the temple, his **ears** in hearing the Lord's pastimes, his **eyes** in seeing the Lord's form, his **body** in touching the devotees, his **nostrils** in smelling flowers offered to the Lord, his **tongue** in tasting _tulasī_ offered to Him, his **legs** in going to holy places, his **head** in offering obeisances, and his **desires** in fulfilling the Lord's desires (Bhāg. 9.4.18–20).
- These qualifications made him fit to be a _mat-para_ devotee. Though not as powerful a _yogī_ as Durvāsā Muni, he controlled his senses and emerged victorious.
