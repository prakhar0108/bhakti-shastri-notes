<ExamQuestion n="1" refs="BG 1.1" question="What is the significance of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's saying _māmakāḥ_?">

- _Māmakāḥ_ means "my party (sons)". Dhṛtarāṣṭra says _māmakāḥ pāṇḍavāś ca_ — "**my** sons and the sons of Pāṇḍu".
- Both parties belong to the **same family**, yet he **deliberately claimed only his own sons as Kurus** and **separated the sons of Pāṇḍu from the family heritage**.
- This discloses his mind and his specific position towards his nephews, the Pāṇḍavas.
- The teacher's comment: once this "my sons vs. Pāṇḍu's sons" mentality appears, **war follows**. The opening verse exposes **attachment**, and the attached king's own words announce the outcome he fears.

<ExamQuestion n="2" refs="BG 1.1" question="Why was Dhṛtarāṣṭra fearful?">

- The battle was arranged at **Kurukṣetra, a _dharma-kṣetra_** — a sacred place of pilgrimage, described in the Vedas as a place of worship even for the denizens of heaven.
- He became **very fearful about the influence of the holy place on the outcome of the battle**, knowing it would influence Arjuna and the sons of Pāṇḍu favourably, because by nature they were all virtuous.
- He was **doubtful about his sons' ultimate victory** and **did not want a compromise** between the cousins; he wanted to be sure of his sons' fate.
- The teacher adds: his anxiety ran both ways — if either Yudhiṣṭhira's or Duryodhana's mind changed, that would trouble him, since a compromise would settle nothing and he wanted an unchallenged kingdom.

<ExamQuestion n="3" refs="BG 1.1" question="How was Sañjaya able to see the Battlefield of Kurukṣetra?">

- Sañjaya was a **student of Vyāsadeva**.
- **By the mercy of Vyāsa**, Sañjaya was able to envision the Battlefield of Kurukṣetra **even while he was in the room of Dhṛtarāṣṭra**.
- The teacher compares this vision to a camera that could go anywhere and zoom in at will.

<ExamQuestion n="4" refs="BG 1.3 Lecture" question="What is the significance of Duryodhana's saying _dhīmatā, tava śiṣyeṇa_?">

- _Drupada-putreṇa tava śiṣyeṇa dhīmatā_ — "by the son of Drupada, **your very intelligent disciple**".
- Background: Drupada, insulted and defeated through Droṇa, performed a sacrifice from which Dhṛṣṭadyumna was born **for Droṇa's destruction**; Droṇa knew this and still trained him, because he was qualified.
- Duryodhana's sentence therefore means: **your own disciple has taken your full training and used it to arrange this army against you**.
- The unspoken message is an **accusation of foolishness, delivered to provoke Droṇa's anger**.
- Purpose: Duryodhana pointed out this weakness so that **Droṇa would stay alert in battle and not compromise** — a single sentence used as a weapon.

<ExamQuestion n="5" refs="BG 1.4" question="List the vows Bhīma made after the gambling match.">

1. To **kill all the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra**.
2. To **break Duryodhana's thigh** — because Duryodhana gestured at his thigh (towards Draupadī).
3. To **tear open Duḥśāsana's chest** / **tear out the arm** that dragged Draupadī into the assembly by her hair.

Because of these unfulfilled vows Bhīma was lodged in Duryodhana's mind, which is why Duryodhana names Bhīma first in BG 1.4.

<ExamQuestion n="6" refs="BG 1.11" question="Why was Duryodhana confident of full support of Bhīṣmadeva and Droṇa?">

- Because **they did not even speak a word when Draupadī, in her helpless condition, appealed to them for justice** while she was being forced to appear naked in the assembly.
- Although he knew both generals had some affection for the Pāṇḍavas, he **hoped they would now completely give it up, as they had done during the gambling**.

<ExamQuestion n="7" refs="BG 1.14 – 1.20" question="List four signs of victory for the Pāṇḍavas.">

1. **Lord Kṛṣṇa is on their side**, seated on Arjuna's chariot — _jayas tu pāṇḍu-putrāṇāṁ yeṣāṁ pakṣe janārdanaḥ_. (1.14)
2. **The goddess of fortune is present** — the Lord is called _Mādhava_, and she never lives without her husband. (1.14)
3. **The chariot was donated by Agni**, the fire-god, and could conquer all sides in the three worlds. (1.14)
4. **Hanumān on Arjuna's flag** (_kapi-dhvaja_) — he helped Lord Rāma, and Rāma was victorious. (1.20)

Other signs given in class: the transcendental conchshells of Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna, the divine horses, _dharma-kṣetra_ itself, Bhīṣma's conchshell, and the shattering of the Kauravas' hearts.

<ExamQuestion n="8" refs="BG 1.20 Lecture" question="What is the significance of Hanumān's being on Arjuna's flag?">

- **A sign of victory:** Hanumān cooperated with Lord Rāma against Rāvaṇa, and Rāma was victorious. Now **the same Lord (Kṛṣṇa is Rāma Himself) and the same servant Hanumān** are both present on Arjuna's chariot, so victory is assured.
- Wherever Lord Rāma is, His eternal servitor Hanumān and His eternal consort Sītā, the goddess of fortune, are present — so **Arjuna had no cause to fear any enemy**.
- **Lesson of the willing instrument (lecture):** Hanumān, who single-handedly destroyed powerful demons in the Rāmāyaṇa, here sits on the flag giving encouragement rather than fighting. He knew his service: here it was to **support**, and the "man of the match" was Arjuna. Devotees want to do whatever pleases Kṛṣṇa, great or small.

**Source:** BG 1.20 purport ([vedabase.io](https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/1/20/)) · Class notes: [Day 8 · BG 1.16 – 1.20](/docs/day-08-bg-1-16-1-20) `34:00 – 36:00`, `40:00 – 44:00`

<ExamQuestion n="9" refs="BG 1.24" question="What is the meaning of the word _guḍākeśa_?">

- **One who has conquered sleep.** _Guḍākā_ means sleep, and one who conquers sleep is called _guḍākeśa_. Sleep also means **ignorance**, so Arjuna **conquered both sleep and ignorance** because of his friendship with Kṛṣṇa — by thinking of Kṛṣṇa constantly.
- Other readings explained in class:
  - Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura — one who gives **sweetness** (_guḍa_) to Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva (_ākeśa_), who see the Lord become his charioteer.
  - Literal — one with **curly hair**.

**Source:** BG 1.24 purport ([vedabase.io](https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/1/24/)) · Class notes: [Day 9 · BG 1.21 – 1.27](/docs/day-09-bg-1-21-1-27) `28:00 – 32:00`

<ExamQuestion n="10" refs="BG 1.36" question="List the six kinds of aggressors.">

According to Vedic injunctions:

1. A **poison giver**
2. One who **sets fire to the house**
3. One who **attacks with deadly weapons**
4. One who **plunders riches**
5. One who **occupies another's land**
6. One who **kidnaps a wife**

Such aggressors are at once to be killed, and no sin is incurred by killing them. (The teacher notes the Kauravas had committed every one of these.)

<ExamQuestion n="11" refs="BG 1.39 – 1.42" question="List the progressive steps leading toward the destruction of a dynasty.">

1. **The dynasty is destroyed** — the senior members of every family are killed in war.
2. **The family tradition is lost** — the elders who understand and protect it are gone.
3. **Irreligion becomes prominent** in the family.
4. **The women of the family become polluted** (left unprotected and degraded).
5. **Unwanted progeny** (_varṇa-saṅkara_) appear.
6. **Offerings to the ancestors stop**, so the ancestors also fall.
7. **Community duties and projects collapse** (_utsādyante jāti-dharmāḥ_).

**Source:** Class notes: [Day 11 · BG 1.31 – 1.46](/docs/day-11-bg-1-31-1-46) `28:00 – 30:00`, `58:00 – 60:00`
