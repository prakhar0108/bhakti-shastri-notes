<ExamQuestion n="34" refs="BG 3.1" question="What is Kṛṣṇa consciousness sometimes misunderstood as?">

- Kṛṣṇa consciousness is sometimes misunderstood to be **inertia** — **retirement from active life**.
- One with such a misunderstanding often withdraws to a **secluded place** to chant the holy name; but without training in the philosophy of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, one may acquire only **cheap adoration from the innocent public**.
- Arjuna too thought of _buddhi-yoga_ as retirement and austerity in a secluded place — an excuse to avoid fighting. In answer Kṛṣṇa explained _karma-yoga_, work in Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- In class: giving up engagements without accepting positive service leaves an empty space that laziness fills; the corrective is active engagement.

<ExamQuestion n="35" refs="BG 3.2 – 3.15" question="Give the English meaning of the following.">

**a. _tad ekaṁ vada_ (3.2)** — **Therefore, please tell me only one** (decisively, which will be most beneficial for me).
_tat_ — therefore; _ekam_ — only one; _vada_ — please tell.

**b. _mithyācāraḥ_ (3.6)** — **A pretender** (hypocrite): one who restrains the senses of action but whose mind dwells on sense objects. In class: taking the facilities of a renounced status without accepting its responsibilities.

**c. _karma-yogam asaktaḥ sa viśiṣyate_ (3.7)** — **He who (engages in) _karma-yoga_ (devotion) without attachment is by far the better (superior).**
_karma-yogam_ — devotion; _asaktaḥ_ — without attachment; _saḥ_ — he; _viśiṣyate_ — is by far the better.

**d. _tad-arthaṁ karma kaunteya mukta-saṅgaḥ_ (3.9)** — **O son of Kuntī, perform work for His (Viṣṇu's) sake, liberated from (material) association.** Work done as sacrifice for Viṣṇu frees one from bondage.
_tat_ — of Him; _artham_ — for the sake; _karma_ — work; _kaunteya_ — O son of Kuntī; _mukta-saṅgaḥ_ — liberated from association.

**e. _yo bhuṅkte stena eva saḥ_ (3.12)** — **He who enjoys (the gifts of the demigods without offering them in return) is certainly a thief.**
_yaḥ_ — he who; _bhuṅkte_ — enjoys; _stenaḥ_ — thief; _eva_ — certainly; _saḥ_ — he.

**f. _annād bhavanti bhūtāni_ (3.14)** — **From grains the material bodies grow** — all living bodies subsist on food grains.
_annāt_ — from grains; _bhavanti_ — grow; _bhūtāni_ — the material bodies.

**g. _vikarma_ (3.15)** — **Unauthorized or sinful work** — anything performed without the direction of the _Vedas_. In class: self-designed spirituality, without the guidance of _guru_, _sādhu_ and _śāstra_, risks becoming _vikarma_.

<ExamQuestion n="36" refs="BG 3.17" question="Why is a fully Kṛṣṇa conscious person not obliged to follow the Vedic injunctions?">

- Being Kṛṣṇa conscious, **all impiety within him is instantly cleansed** — an effect of many, many thousands of _yajña_ performances.
- By such clearing of consciousness he becomes **fully confident of his eternal position in relationship with the Supreme**.
- His **duty thus becomes self-illuminated by the grace of the Lord**, and therefore he no longer has any obligation to the Vedic injunctions.
- He is fully satisfied in the self (_ātma-tṛptaḥ_) and no longer takes pleasure in material arrangements. In class: he needs neither sense objects nor subtler rewards such as worship, profit or prestige.

<ExamQuestion n="37" refs="BG 3.21" question="Give the English meaning of _ācārya_.">

**The ideal teacher** — one who **teaches by his own behaviour**.

- Lord Caitanya said a teacher should behave properly before he begins teaching; one who teaches in that way is called _ācārya_.
- A leader cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he himself smokes.
- The teacher must follow the principles of _śāstra_ and cannot manufacture rules against the revealed scriptures.

<ExamQuestion n="38" refs="BG 3.23" question="Why did Kṛṣṇa perform prescribed duties?">

- The rules and regulations are for the conditioned souls, not for Kṛṣṇa; but **because He descended to establish the principles of religion, He followed the prescribed rules**.
- Otherwise **common men would follow in His footsteps**, because He is the **greatest authority** — "if I ever failed to engage in carefully performing prescribed duties, all men would follow My path."
- He performed all the religious duties **at home and out of home, as required of a householder** (in class: Nārada Muni saw Him attentively doing household and royal duties in every palace of Dvārakā).
- In class: freedom from personal need does not cancel the responsibility to **set a proper example**.

<ExamQuestion n="39" refs="BG 3.26" question="What qualifications are required for beginning practice of Kṛṣṇa consciousness?">

- **No prior Vedic qualification is required.** A **slightly developed Kṛṣṇa conscious person** may **directly be engaged in the service of the Lord without waiting for other Vedic formulas**.
- For this fortunate person there is no need to follow the Vedic rituals, because by direct Kṛṣṇa consciousness one gets all the results one would otherwise derive from prescribed duties.
- In class: unlike the contemplative path, **bhakti does not require an already purified heart — devotional engagement itself purifies**. A person can be invited directly into devotional service **when faith in that service is established**.

<ExamQuestion n="40" refs="BG 3.30" question="Give the English meaning of the phrase _nirāśīr nirmamo_.">

**Without desire for profit, and without ownership (claims of proprietorship).**

- _nirāśīḥ_ — without desire for profit (in class: without expectation of personal gain)
- _nirmamaḥ_ — without ownership (in class: without possessiveness; what comes from duty belongs to the Lord)

<ExamQuestion n="41" refs="BG 3.39" question="Give the English meaning of the phrase _nitya-vairiṇā_.">

**By the eternal enemy** — lust, which covers the pure consciousness of the wise living entity.

In class: ordinary enemies change, die, or stop pursuing you; this one is a **constant threat that never lets go**.

<ExamQuestion n="42" refs="BG 3.40" question="List the three sitting places of lust.">

1. **The senses** (_indriyāṇi_)
2. **The mind** (_manaḥ_)
3. **The intelligence** (_buddhiḥ_)

Through them lust covers the real knowledge of the living entity and bewilders him. In class: impure senses supply the taste, the mind inflates the projected pleasure, and the intelligence plans how to fulfil the desire.
