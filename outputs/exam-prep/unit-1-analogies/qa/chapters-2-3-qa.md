<ExamQuestion n="1" refs="BG 2.1" question="Compassion for the dress of a drowning man is senseless; similarly a man fallen in the ocean of nescience cannot be saved simply by rescuing his outer dress.">

**Context.** Chapter 2 opens with Arjuna overwhelmed by compassion, his eyes full of tears, and Kṛṣṇa (Madhusūdana) about to speak.

**Explanation (purport).**
- Material compassion, lamentation and tears are signs of **ignorance of the real self**; compassion for the eternal soul is self-realization.
- No one knows where compassion should be applied. The **dress** is the gross material body; the **drowning man** is the soul fallen in the ocean of nescience. Saving the dress does not save the man.
- One who laments for the outward dress is called a _śūdra_ — one who laments unnecessarily.

**Application to Arjuna.** Arjuna, a _kṣatriya_, was lamenting for the bodies of his kinsmen — conduct not expected of him. Kṛṣṇa, as Madhusūdana, would kill the "demon" of his misunderstanding.

**General application (class).** Swimming out at risk to one's life and returning with only the man's coat accomplishes nothing — he has still drowned. Śrīla Prabhupāda pressed this against social service and philanthropy: widely appreciated, but not the ultimate solution, because ultimately the **soul** has to be rescued. Health is worth maintaining when its purpose is service to the Lord.

<ExamQuestion n="2" refs="BG 2.2" question="Absolute Truth is realized in three phases of understanding — Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān. This can be explained by the example of the sunshine, the sun's surface and the sun planet.">

**Context.** The first words of Kṛṣṇa in the Gītā (_śrī-bhagavān uvāca_). The purport explains who is speaking — Bhagavān.

**Explanation (purport).**
- **Sunshine** — the universal, impersonal effulgence → **Brahman** realization (preliminary student).
- **Sun's surface (disc)** → **Paramātmā**, the localized aspect within the heart (further advanced).
- **Sun planet itself** (entering its heart) → **Bhagavān**, the personal feature (the highest).
- The three cannot be separated, yet the students of the three phases are not in the same category; the _bhaktas_ who realize Bhagavān are the topmost transcendentalists.

**Application to Arjuna.** In the presence of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Arjuna's lamentation for his kinsmen is unbecoming — hence Kṛṣṇa's surprise, _kutaḥ_ ("wherefrom?").

**General application (class).** The sun analogy shows **increasing detail**: knowing the Person, His nature and His potencies is the most complete understanding of the Absolute Truth.

<ExamQuestion n="3" refs="BG 2.17" question="Just like the influence of the active principle of medicine is spread throughout the body, similarly the influence of soul is spread throughout the body as consciousness, and that is the proof of the existence of soul.">

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa expands "of the eternal there is no cessation" (2.16): that which pervades the entire body is indestructible.

**Explanation (purport).**
- The soul is atomic — one ten-thousandth of the tip of a hair — yet **its influence spreads all over the body**, as the active principle of a medicine spreads throughout the body.
- This current of the soul is felt all over the body as **consciousness**, and that is the **proof of the presence of the soul**. The body minus consciousness is a dead body, and consciousness cannot be revived by any material means.

**Application to Arjuna.** The verse is spoken to establish for Arjuna that the soul pervading the body is imperishable — **no one can destroy it**.

**General application (class).** The purport explains that the soul pervades the body **through consciousness**, by which pleasure and pain are felt throughout the body. The teacher's parallel image: the sun occupies one place, yet its light is everywhere.

<ExamQuestion n="4" refs="BG 2.20" question="Sometimes we don't find the sun in the sky owing to clouds, but the light of the sun is always there, which is indicative of the presence of sun. Similarly, even though one may not be able to see the soul in the region of heart, one can understand the presence of the soul by consciousness.">

**Context.** BG 2.20 states that the soul is never born and never dies, and is **not slain when the body is slain**.

**Explanation (purport).**
- The soul is full of knowledge — always full of consciousness — so **consciousness is the symptom of the soul**.
- Even if one cannot find the soul within the heart, its presence is understood **simply by the presence of consciousness**, just as daylight tells us the sun is in the sky though clouds hide it.
- Since there is some consciousness in all bodies, man or animal, we understand the presence of the soul.

**Application to Arjuna.** Arjuna lamented the coming death of his elders; this verse teaches that the soul in their bodies is not killed.

**General application (class).** We may not see the soul directly, just as the sun may be hidden behind clouds — but daylight tells us the sun is there; **consciousness in the body tells us the soul is there**.

<ExamQuestion n="5" refs="BG 2.22" question="As a person puts on new garments, giving up the old ones, the soul similarly accepts new material bodies, giving up the old and useless ones.">

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa returns to the change of body (first given in 2.13), now in an image Arjuna cannot miss.

**Explanation (purport).**
- Change of body by the atomic soul is an accepted fact — childhood, youth, old age — and from old age the change is transferred to another body.
- This transference is made possible by the grace of the **Supersoul**, who fulfils the desire of the atomic soul as one friend fulfils another's (the two birds in one tree).

**Application to Arjuna.** Arjuna is advised **not to lament for the bodily change of his old grandfather and teacher**. One who lays down his life in a righteous battle is at once cleansed of bodily reactions and promoted — so there was no cause for lamentation. Arjuna, the "eating bird", has now turned his face to his friend Kṛṣṇa and can be free from lamentation.

**General application (class).** Bhīṣma and the others have grown old; the garment is worn out and a new one is coming — where is the cause for anxiety? Life does not begin at birth or end at death; the body is external, placed upon the soul.

<ExamQuestion n="6" refs="BG 2.21" question="Although the justice of peace awards capital punishment to a person condemned for murder, the justice of the peace cannot be blamed because he awards punishments according to the codes of justice. Similarly, when Kṛṣṇa orders fighting it is for supreme justice, and Arjuna will not incur sinful reaction.">

**Context.** BG 2.21: one who knows the soul to be indestructible — how can he kill anyone or cause anyone to kill? Arjuna feared becoming a killer.

**Explanation (purport).**
- Everything, **violence included, has its proper utility**, and how to apply it rests with the person in knowledge.
- The justice of the peace orders violence **according to the codes of justice**, so he is not blamed. Manu-saṁhitā supports that a murderer be condemned to death so that **in his next life he will not have to suffer** for his great sin — so the king's punishment is actually beneficial.

**Application to Arjuna.** When Kṛṣṇa orders fighting, the violence is **for supreme justice**; Arjuna should follow the instruction, knowing that such fighting for Kṛṣṇa is not violence at all, since the soul cannot be killed.

**General application (class).** A judge orders an execution and an executioner carries it out, yet neither incurs the blame of a murderer. Kṛṣṇa's message: "this is a war of principle; I am impelling you to _dharma_, not to violence." If such a man is not stopped, ten others will be killed, and others are warned by the example.

<ExamQuestion n="7" refs="BG 2.20" question="One can understand the presence of the soul simply by the presence of consciousness. Sometimes we do not find the sun in the sky owing to clouds or for some other reason, but the light of the sun is always there, and we are convinced that it is therefore day-time.">

**Context.** Same verse and purport as analogy 4 — BG 2.20, the soul is unborn, eternal and not slain when the body is slain.

**Explanation (purport).**
- As soon as there is a little light early in the morning, we understand the sun is in the sky; **similarly, since there is some consciousness in every body, we understand the soul is present**.
- The individual soul's consciousness, however, is **different from the Supreme's**: the Supreme is all-knowing — past, present and future — while the individual soul's consciousness is prone to be **forgetful**, and so needs enlightenment from Kṛṣṇa.

**Application to Arjuna.** Arjuna is the atomic soul, forgetful of his real nature, and therefore requires to be enlightened by Kṛṣṇa or by His bona fide representative.

**General application (class).** The symptom by which we detect the soul is **consciousness** — daylight proves the sun; consciousness proves the soul.

<ExamQuestion n="8" refs="BG 2.21" question="A surgical operation is meant to cure the patient and not meant to kill the patient. Similarly, fighting on the order of Kṛṣṇa is for the benefit of all, and thus there is no possibility of sinful reaction.">

**Context.** Same verse as analogy 6 — Arjuna's fear that by fighting he would kill or cause killing.

**Explanation (purport).** For the administration of justice, so-called violence is permitted. **A surgical operation is not meant to kill the patient, but to cure him.** Therefore the fighting executed at Kṛṣṇa's instruction **with full knowledge** carries no possibility of sinful reaction.

**Application to Arjuna.** Arjuna's fighting, done on Kṛṣṇa's order in full knowledge of the self, is like the surgeon's cut — meant for benefit, not harm.

**General application (class).** The Lord's instruction to Arjuna, given in full knowledge of the self, is of the surgeon's character. "One who knows" means one actually situated on the platform of the soul — not one who merely knows the theory; without that distinction the verse can be badly misapplied.

<ExamQuestion n="9" refs="BG 2.41" question="As by watering the root of a tree one can automatically distribute water to the leaves and branches, so by acting in Kṛṣṇa consciousness one can render the highest service to everyone, namely self, family, society, country, humanity, etc.">

**Context.** BG 2.41 — those on the path of _buddhi-yoga_ are **resolute in purpose and their aim is one**; the intelligence of the irresolute is many-branched.

**Explanation (purport).**
- The resolute purpose of a Kṛṣṇa conscious person is **based on knowledge**: Vāsudeva (Kṛṣṇa) is **the root of all manifested causes**.
- Watering the root waters the leaves and branches; so acting in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the highest service to everyone. **If Kṛṣṇa is satisfied, everyone is satisfied.**
- Such service is best practised under a bona fide spiritual master.

**Application (purport/class).** One engaged in Kṛṣṇa consciousness need not act with obligations to family tradition, humanity or nationality. He works and cares for his family, but **they are never his purpose** — as long as devotion is secondary and family primary, there is no growth.

**General application (class).** The devotee is not a sentimentalist: he knows Vāsudeva is the root — please Him and everything else is accomplished. The teacher's challenge: put devotion at top priority for two months and the taste itself will hold you.

<ExamQuestion n="10" refs="BG 2.58" question="The tortoise can at any moment wind up his senses and exhibit them again at any time for a particular purpose. Similarly the senses of a Kṛṣṇa-conscious person are used only for some particular purpose in the service of the Lord and are withdrawn otherwise.">

**Context.** Arjuna's third question about the _sthita-prajña_ — _kim āsīta_, "how does he sit?", i.e. how does he restrain the senses. BG 2.58 begins the answer.

**Explanation (purport).**
- The test of a _yogī_ or devotee is that he controls the senses **according to his plan**; most people are servants of the senses.
- The tortoise withdraws its limbs and exhibits them again **for particular purposes**; the devotee's senses are used **only in the Lord's service** and withdrawn otherwise.

**Application to Arjuna.** **Arjuna is being taught to use his senses for the service of the Lord, instead of for his own satisfaction.**

**General application (class).**
- The tortoise's shell is extraordinarily strong while its limbs are delicate; **our senses are our weak point**, and _māyā_ attacks exactly there — so each person must know his own weak sense.
- Every contact of a sense with a sense object leaves an impression ("out of sight, out of mind" — the billboard effect). Arjuna "looked a little" at the army, and the whole Gītā had to be spoken.
- Restraint gives safety and **conserves will-power** for saying "yes" to devotion. But withdrawal alone is not the whole answer — **engagement** is the other half; and one should never "test" oneself by exposure.

<ExamQuestion n="11" refs="BG 2.58" question="The senses are compared to venomous serpents and the devotee to a snake charmer. A devotee should be strong like a snake charmer in controlling his serpent-like senses. He should not allow his senses to act independently.">

**Context.** Same verse as analogy 10 — how the steady person restrains his senses.

**Explanation (purport).**
- **The senses are like venomous serpents** — they want to act very loosely and without restriction.
- The _yogī_ or devotee must be **very strong, like a snake charmer**, and never allow them to act independently.
- This requires following the scriptural **do's and do-not's**; without restricting oneself from sense enjoyment one cannot be firmly fixed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

**Application to Arjuna.** As with the tortoise, Arjuna is taught to keep his senses under control for the Lord's service, not his own satisfaction.

**General application (class).** The senses are like serpents — they want to act loosely and are not easily caught. One in Kṛṣṇa consciousness knows the senses belong to the master of the senses (Hṛṣīkeśa); they are to be **engaged in His service and kept from everything else**.

<ExamQuestion n="12" refs="BG 2.59" question="The process of restriction from sense enjoyment by rules and regulations is something like restricting a diseased person from certain types of eatables. The patient, however, neither likes such restrictions nor loses his taste for eatables.">

**Context.** BG 2.59: though the embodied soul may be restricted from sense enjoyment, **the taste remains**; it ceases only by **experiencing a higher taste** (_paraṁ dṛṣṭvā_). Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's objection: a sick fool also abstains — what then is special about the _sthita-prajña_?

**Explanation (purport).**
- Unless one is transcendentally situated, it is not possible to cease from sense enjoyment.
- Restriction by rules (e.g. _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ — _yama, niyama_, etc.) is like a patient's diet: **the object is given up, the taste is not**. Such restrictions are for less intelligent neophytes, good **until one actually has a taste for Kṛṣṇa consciousness**.
- **One who has tasted the beauty of the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa no longer has a taste for dead, material things.**

**Application.** The answer to Arjuna's question about the _sthita-prajña_: his taste itself has ceased, because he has experienced something higher.

**General application (class).**
- A person who gave up meat can still identify from the smell what is being cooked — the object gone, the taste not.
- **We can give up sense objects exactly to the degree we have experienced Kṛṣṇa.** At the beginning the senses must be engaged somewhat forcibly — more chanting, reading, service — and one must actually **give time** to devotion.
- Three stages of the senses: impure → semi-pure → pure.

<ExamQuestion n="13" refs="BG 2.67" question="As a strong wind sweeps away a boat on the water, even one of the roaming senses on which the mind focuses can carry away a man's intelligence.">

**Context.** Part of Kṛṣṇa's answer to Arjuna's questions about the _sthita-prajña_ — why a **single unguarded sense** is enough to cause a fall.

**Explanation (purport).** Unless **all** the senses are engaged in the Lord's service, **even one** engaged in sense gratification can deviate the devotee from transcendental advancement. As in the life of Mahārāja Ambarīṣa, all the senses must be engaged in Kṛṣṇa consciousness — the correct technique for controlling the mind.

**General application (class).**
- _Caratām_ — the senses roam like grazing cows; whichever one the mind follows carries it away.
- **We are all in the ocean of material existence, and the wind is desire**; there are so many winds that one must keep protected to cross. When a storm is forecast, one does not take the boat out to watch it.
- Casual attitudes — "a few rounds missed, what of it", "a little news", "chanting while driving" — let one sense carry away the whole boat.
- Remedies: **remove the option** (don't test yourself) and **create a spiritual culture** in your own surroundings.

<ExamQuestion n="14" refs="BG 2.70" question="As the ocean is always being filled by rivers but remains steady and not agitated, a person fixed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness remains undisturbed even amidst the incessant flow of desires.">

**Context.** Near the end of Kṛṣṇa's description of the _sthita-prajña_ — the state of one who is **complete**. Only such a person achieves peace, not one who strives to satisfy desires.

**Explanation (purport).**
- The ocean, especially in the rainy season, keeps receiving water, yet it **remains steady**, not agitated, and does not cross its brink.
- As long as one has a body, its demands continue; but the devotee is **not disturbed, because of his fullness** — the Lord fulfils all his necessities.
- Fruitive workers, salvationists and _yogīs_ after mystic powers are all unhappy from unfulfilled desires; the devotee, satisfied in the Lord's service, has no desires to fulfil and so enjoys **full peace**.

**Application to Arjuna (class).** **Duryodhana** represents one empty within — he got the kingdom and was still unhappy; the **Pāṇḍavas** were ready to settle for five villages. Rāma and Bharata each urged the other to rule, because both were full.

**General application (class).** The ocean is not still because no rivers enter it; **it is still because it is already full**. Like a man who has eaten fully passing food stalls unaffected. Filling oneself from within is what matters — changing external circumstances will not move the vehicle forward.

<ExamQuestion n="15" refs="BG 3.14" question="When there is an epidemic disease, an antiseptic vaccine protects a person from the attack of such an epidemic. Similarly, food offered to Lord Viṣṇu and then taken by us makes us sufficiently resistant to material affection.">

**Context.** BG 3.14 — the cycle of sacrifice: living bodies subsist on grains, grains come from rain, rain from _yajña_, and _yajña_ from prescribed duties.

**Explanation (purport).**
- When Kṛṣṇa, the _yajña-puruṣa_, is worshipped, the demigods are automatically worshipped; so devotees **offer food to Kṛṣṇa and then eat**, which nourishes the body spiritually.
- By this, past sinful reactions are vanquished and **the body becomes immunized** to the contamination of material nature — as a **vaccine** protects one in an epidemic.
- The material world is full of contamination; one immunized by **_prasādam_** is saved, while one who does not do so increases the volume of sinful action.
- _Yajña_, specifically the _saṅkīrtana-yajña_ for this age, must be performed.

**Application to Arjuna.** In this section Kṛṣṇa urges Arjuna to perform his prescribed duty as _yajña_ rather than give up work.

**General application.** Eat only food offered to Kṛṣṇa — it counteracts past material infections, which are impediments to self-realization.

<ExamQuestion n="16" refs="BG 3.30" question="The cashier may count millions of dollars for his employer, but he does not claim a cent for himself. Similarly, one has to realize that nothing in the world belongs to any individual person, but that everything belongs to the Supreme Lord.">

**Context.** BG 3.30 — "surrendering all your works unto Me, with full knowledge of Me, **without desires for profit** (_nirāśīḥ_), **with no claims to proprietorship** (_nirmamaḥ_), and free from lethargy, fight."

**Explanation (purport).**
- _Nirāśīḥ_: one acts on the order of the master but does not expect fruitive results — like the **cashier** who handles millions and claims not a cent.
- Nothing belongs to any individual; **everything belongs to the Supreme Lord** — the real purport of _mayi_, "unto Me". Acting this way, one claims no proprietorship: _nirmama_, "nothing is mine".

**Application to Arjuna.** Arjuna was ordered to fight **as if the Lord were his military commander** — he had only to execute the order, without claiming proprietorship, and to throw off reluctance about so-called kinsmen (_vigata-jvara_).

**General application (class).** _Nirāśīḥ_ — without expectation of personal gain; _nirmamaḥ_ — without possessiveness: **what comes from duty belongs to the Lord, not to an independent enjoyer**. Offer work to Kṛṣṇa without possessiveness or lethargy.

<ExamQuestion n="17" refs="BG 3.34" question="One has to follow those rules and regulations, unattached to them, because practice of sense gratification under regulations may also lead one to go astray — as much as there is always the chance of an accident, even on the royal roads.">

**Context.** BG 3.34 — there are principles to regulate attachment and aversion to sense objects; one should not come under their control, for they are **stumbling blocks** on the path of self-realization.

**Explanation (purport).**
- Those not yet in Kṛṣṇa consciousness should follow scriptural regulations (e.g. sex life only within marriage); unrestricted enjoyment causes material encagement.
- **But one should not rely on such allowances.** Even regulated sense gratification can lead one astray, just as **no one can guarantee there will be no accident even on the safest, best-maintained road**.
- Therefore attachment even to regulated enjoyment must be avoided; **attachment to Kṛṣṇa consciousness** detaches one from all sensory activities.

**Application to Arjuna.** In this section Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to act according to his own nature under authorized direction, without coming under the control of attachment and aversion.

**General application (class).**
- Regulation is the **beginning, not the goal**. Deciding on one sweet or one film a week is a start, not something to depend on.
- A smoker reduced from a packet to one cigarette a day has progressed, but treating one a day as the destination defeats the purpose.
- The goal is attachment to Kṛṣṇa; giving up sense gratification is the means, not the end.

<ExamQuestion n="18" refs="BG 3.37" question="The sense of love of God becomes transformed into lust, as milk in contact with sour tamarind is transformed into yogurt.">

**Context.** Arjuna asks (3.36) by what one is impelled to sinful acts, even unwillingly, as if by force. Kṛṣṇa answers (3.37): **it is lust**, born of the mode of passion, later transformed into wrath — the all-devouring sinful enemy.

**Explanation (purport).**
- In contact with material creation, the living entity's **eternal love for Kṛṣṇa is transformed into lust**, in association with passion — **as milk in contact with sour tamarind becomes yogurt**.
- Unsatisfied lust turns to wrath, wrath to illusion, and illusion continues material existence.
- If lust is transformed back into love for the Supreme — **desiring everything for Kṛṣṇa** — both lust and wrath are spiritualized (Hanumān's wrath in burning Laṅkā).

**Application to Arjuna.** In the Gītā the Lord **induces Arjuna to engage his wrath upon his enemies for the satisfaction of the Lord**; lust and wrath employed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness become friends instead of enemies.

**General application (class).**
- Lust is a **perverted reflection of love of God** — "180° opposite: equal in magnitude, opposite in direction".
- If one is trained in Kṛṣṇa consciousness from the beginning, love cannot deteriorate into lust.
- Once milk becomes yogurt, the yogurt will not turn back into milk — returning is **very difficult, but not hopeless**: even a late beginner can become a lover of God by following the regulative principles. Do not become hopeless; begin now.

<ExamQuestion n="19" refs="BG 3.39" question="It is said in the Manu-smṛti that lust cannot be satisfied by any amount of sense enjoyment, just as fire is never extinguished by a constant supply of fuel.">

**Context.** BG 3.39 — the wise living entity's pure consciousness is covered by his **eternal enemy, lust**, which is never satisfied and **burns like fire** (_duṣpūreṇa analena_).

**Explanation (purport).**
- Lust cannot be satisfied by **any amount** of sense enjoyment, just as **adding fuel never extinguishes a fire**.
- The material world is called _maithunya-āgāra_, the shackles of sex life; civilization based on sense gratification increases the duration of material existence.
- The so-called happiness of sense gratification is **the ultimate enemy** of the sense enjoyer.

**Application to Arjuna.** Kṛṣṇa is naming for Arjuna the enemy he must conquer (developed in 3.41 – 3.43).

**General application (class).**
- **Do not attempt to satisfy lust; attempt to surrender.** "Do not try to satisfy lust — try to satisfy Kṛṣṇa." Change the **object** of desire, not its quantity.
- As age advances the capacity to enjoy decreases but the desire does not. Indulgence gives temporary relief and then **fuels the next craving** (the ever-rising salary).
- Unlike ordinary fire, which one runs away from, this fire **draws one closer** to its object.
- Sign of progress: proposals of lust come **less often and with less force**.
