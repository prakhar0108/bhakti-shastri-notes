## Opening Verse: Why Scriptures Exist at All [04:00 – 08:00]

The teacher opens with a verse he identifies as SB 10.2.31 and uses it to frame the whole course. [Reference as spoken; see Verification Flags.]

**The image he draws out:**

- The ocean of material existence is extremely difficult to cross.
- Acharyas cross it by taking shelter of the Lord's lotus feet, which are the boat by which it can be crossed.
- Having crossed, out of the compassion natural to their hearts, they leave the boat behind on this side.
- **The question he anticipates:** how can the boat be left behind here, if it was the means of crossing? His answer is that the boat stands for the scriptures. The acharyas study the scriptures, take the Lord's shelter, and cross — and they leave those scriptures behind so that others may cross too.

This, he says, is why Srila Prabhupada began this movement and gave these books.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The books exist because those who crossed left them deliberately. Studying them is not an academic exercise but the use of something left behind out of compassion. [04:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

## Why Srila Prabhupada Created the Course [08:00 – 14:00]

The teacher gives several reasons, drawn from what Srila Prabhupada began saying within a year or two of founding the movement, when there were still very few devotees.

#### To create seriousness [08:00 – 10:00]

- He contrasts the prevailing habit with what a course requires, asking whether one has ever seen someone bring a notebook to a spiritual programme. The established convention is to listen to a recitation.
- Srila Prabhupada wanted to break that pattern so that people would become serious about devotion.

#### The name itself states the aim [08:00 – 10:00]

- The course is called Bhakti Shastri: it is a study of scripture, but its purpose is to refine devotion.
- **Becoming a scholar of scripture is not the objective.** The teacher notes bluntly that many scholars outside the movement never became devotees, and that there are cases where excessive scriptural learning became an obstacle to devotion.
- At the same time, knowledge is needed to mature devotion — which is why the course was designed.

#### Because mixed-up knowledge is harder to correct than none [12:00]

- Srila Prabhupada observed that in India everyone already has some philosophy of their own.
- The teacher's analogy: teaching mridanga to someone who knows nothing is straightforward; teaching someone who has learned a wrong beat is far harder, because your instruction and their response keep diverging.

#### Because a course is treated differently from an optional subject [12:00 – 14:00]

Classes, attendance, and examinations exist for the same reason that optional, unexamined subjects in school are neither read nor understood nor noted.

#### Reading is not studying [14:00]

The teacher marks this as a distinction to hold onto: coming to devotional life and beginning to read Srila Prabhupada's books is very good, but studying them attentively is a different activity altogether.

---

## The Two Extremes to Avoid [10:00 – 12:00]

- **First extreme:** "Why is any of this necessary? The Lord sees the heart's mood. Reading and writing is what we left behind."
- **Second extreme:** wanting only to know, without practising — needing every detail while doing nothing.

For the second, the teacher tells the story of the ninety-nine club. A king sees a poor farmer eating with his family outside his hut, entirely content, and asks his minister why he himself, with a vast kingdom, has less satisfaction. The minister leaves ninety-nine gold coins at the farmer's door. The farmer counts them, counts again, and becomes agitated that they are not a hundred. Thereafter he spends himself trying to make the hundred, becomes irritable, has no time for his wife or children — and tells each of them that he is working for their sake.

**The balance the teacher asks for:** we want to know about the Lord in order to serve Him, so there must be time for service. Knowing is not the goal; surrender is.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Both refusing knowledge and accumulating it without practice defeat the course. The measure is whether study is producing service. [10:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

## How to Study [14:00 – 24:00]

The teacher gives a method and then repeats it in summary at [46:00 – 48:00].

#### Do not do too many things [14:00]

His three-word formula for anything: **regularly, properly, sufficiently.** He supports it with the observation that we become what we digest, not what we eat.

#### Keep reading and hearing in sync [16:00 – 18:00]

- Classes are twice a week; the books are already in the students' hands, and Srila Prabhupada's lectures exist on all four.
- As a section is covered in class, listen to Srila Prabhupada's lectures on that same section and make notes.
- **Why synchronization matters:** when life is not in sync, effectiveness is lost. His illustration is someone whose native language, films, schooling, and daily speech are each in a different language, leaving no definition anywhere.
- He notes that Srila Prabhupada's Gita lectures also exist in a printed transcription volume, available in some places though not as a soft copy. [Details flagged; see Verification Flags.]

#### Write a reflection on every section [20:00 – 22:00]

- For each section of the Gita covered, write your own reflection, in written form.
- When you later teach that section, read your own reflection alongside the verses — and it improves each time you do.
- He says he has been attempting this himself for a long time and writes them.

#### Find one person and teach them [18:00 – 20:00; 22:00 – 24:00]

- Find one sincere, available person and honestly try to teach them what is being covered.
- **Not downloading.** The teacher distinguishes this from the habit of simply delivering a lecture at someone, which he says is not unhealthy exactly but yields no nourishment. The sequence he wants is: hear, make notes, hear Srila Prabhupada on it, write your reflection, then teach — after which your own reflection becomes further refined.
- He states this is a particular aim of how he is taking the course: that students become teachers.

#### Pray, and apply [20:00 – 22:00]

- These books are _śabda-brahma_ and non-different from the Lord's own form; he mentions the Padma Purana's description of the Gita's eighteen chapters in relation to the Lord's body. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- So pray to the Lord that we may be able to apply what we read.
- His caution: reading and hearing a great deal has nothing wrong with it and is good from the standpoint of engagement — at least one is hearing something good. But from the standpoint of application and of spiritual health, this method is what builds strength.

#### Go slowly [18:00 – 20:00]

The mind will say it wants to finish. Watch instead whether absorption is actually happening.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The method is small and repeatable: keep pace, hear Srila Prabhupada on the same section, write your own reflection, teach one person, and pray to apply it. Its point is absorption rather than completion. [14:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

## How Each Book Will Be Approached [26:00 – 34:00; 48:00 – 52:00]

#### The available approaches [26:00 – 28:00]

The teacher notes several legitimate ways the course is taught elsewhere, none of them faulty:

- Word-by-word, discussing the meaning of each Sanskrit word.
- Detailed engagement with the principal commentaries — he names Baladeva Vidyabhushana's _Gītā-bhūṣaṇa_ and Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura's commentary as the ones referred to most within this tradition, with Ramanujacharya and Sridhara Swami also having written.
- Leaving aside word-for-word meanings and going deeply into Srila Prabhupada's purports.

#### His own approach, book by book [28:00 – 34:00; 48:00 – 52:00]

| Book                          | Approach                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| :---------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bhagavad-gita**             | First the flow — the division of each chapter into sections. Then verse by verse. The commentaries will be touched, and particular focus will go to Srila Prabhupada's purports, so that appreciation for what he wrote develops.                                                                                                            |
| **The Nectar of Instruction** | Eleven verses, read mainly in the direction of practical application: what to do, what not to do, and what to be cautious about.                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **The Nectar of Devotion**    | A concepts book. Srila Prabhupada called it a summary study, so the original verses are not present, though the sequence is preserved. The first nineteen chapters are studied: the definition of pure devotional service, the distinctions between _sādhana_, _bhāva_, and _prema_ devotion, and the principal limbs of devotional service. |
| **Śrī Īśopaniṣad**            | One of the principal Upanishads, with Srila Prabhupada's explanation. Study centres on the first verse establishing that everything belongs to the Lord, and proceeds to who the Lord is, how to pray to Him, and how to take His shelter.                                                                                                   |

#### On Srila Prabhupada's summary study [32:00]

The teacher relates that when someone asked Srila Prabhupada what a summary study is, the reply was that it is when you write what you want to write. [Account flagged; see Verification Flags.]

#### Why the Gita is given the most weight [28:00 – 30:00]

- Almost everything is contained in it; the Lord gave nearly all the principles there.
- The setting is extraordinary: spoken on a battlefield.
- Arjuna was confused and the Lord gave clarity about what dharma is — and every conditioned soul in this world is in that same perplexed condition, not knowing what his duty is. The teacher points to the purport of BG 2.7.
- So the Gita's message is universal, which is why so many have commented on it.

#### The planned sequence [50:00 – 52:00]

1. Bhagavad-gita chapters 1–6.
2. _The Nectar of Instruction_ — because the first six chapters are somewhat technical and a short book gives a sense of completion.
3. Bhagavad-gita chapters 7–12, which are more devotional.
4. Bhagavad-gita chapters 13–18.
5. _The Nectar of Devotion_.

His reason for interleaving: the Gita takes so long that students can otherwise feel that not a single book has been completed.

## Practical Arrangements [34:00 – 36:00; 54:00 – 60:00]

- **Schedule:** Two classes a week, ordinarily Monday and Tuesday, generally 7:30 to 8:30, with occasional adjustment for travel or emergencies. Zoom opens about five minutes early.
- **Duration:** At two classes a week, the course should complete in about two years.
- **Questions** may be asked at the end of class, written in chat, or sent personally.
- **Recordings** are posted to the group for those who miss a class.
- **Notes:** Make your own. The teacher is emphatic that others' notes do not help, and that copying them does little.
- **Materials:** Keep a notebook and a hard copy of the text. He will not share his screen for every verse, only where he writes something out to explain it.
- **Handbook:** Posted to the group as a PDF, containing the course division and examination-related material. He recommends taking a printout.
- **Examinations** include a test on the verses, which he describes as easy and not a cause for worry.

#### Minimum qualification, announced at the end [58:00 – 60:00]

The teacher adds this as a point he had nearly forgotten: participants should be chanting at least eight to ten rounds of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra — the ideal being sixteen — should know the movement's basic principles, and should be following the four regulative principles. Anyone who does not fit this is asked to speak with the course coordinator.

## On Memorizing Verses [52:00 – 54:00]

The teacher gives the method he will repeat throughout the course:

- Recite; do not try to memorize. If you try to memorize, it will not be memorized.
- Understand the meaning, then recite with that meaning in view.
- His framing: this is how we serve the verses, and repeated recitation produces recollection by default.
- **His illustration:** cabin announcements before a flight, repeated so often that anyone remembers them without effort.
- **His warning:** putting too much pressure on remembering means you will neither remember nor relish.

## What the Course Should Change [24:00 – 26:00; 48:00 – 50:00]

The teacher names two outcomes:

- **Your own understanding of devotion.** He singles out _The Nectar of Devotion_ and _The Nectar of Instruction_ as especially important for a practitioner, because they give the science of devotion — where I am in devotion, where I want to go, what stage I am at, what devotion is in each of the three modes.
- **Your guidance of others.** Everyone present is guiding someone in spiritual life, and we guide according to our own understanding. Where that understanding is missing, guidance becomes a challenge.

He also observes that after reading many books, people are often unable to name one book they have studied well enough to teach — and that the point of this course is to be able to do that with these four.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Two years of consistent work on four books is meant to produce something specific: a clear scriptural basis for one's own practice, and the ability to guide others from it. [24:00 – 26:00; 48:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

### 1. Could the meeting's automatic notes be shared for those who are travelling? [54:00 – 56:00]

- **Question:** The class time coincides with a commute; could automatically generated notes be shared to the group to help with reflection and note-making?
- **Answer:** The teacher grants it as a reasonable accommodation for someone travelling, but repeats his position that another's notes will not really help.
- His suggested use: review such notes afterwards and from them make your own.

### 2. What should be done with the PDFs shared to the group? [56:00]

- **Question:** Should they be opened and read, or printed?
- **Answer:** It is the Bhakti Shastri handbook. Keeping a printout is better, for reference at any time.

### 3. How can a working person find someone to teach? [56:00 – 58:00]

- **Question:** Could one record what has been studied and send it to a friend instead, since finding a student in person is not practical for someone working?
- **Answer:** The teacher calls it a very good idea.
- His remark on the difficulty: finding a student is the hardest thing today — there is no shortage of teachers. He asks where one would find a Parikshit Maharaja.
- The method he endorses: study it, make notes, write your reflection, record it, and send it to someone with whom you have a good relationship, since availability rarely coincides.

### 4. Attending only on days off [58:00]

- **Question:** A participant explains she is working and leaves home before the class begins, so she will attend when possible and otherwise work through the recordings and make notes.
- **Answer:** The teacher accepts this and confirms the recording will be posted to the group.
