## Paragraphs 33–40: The Fourfold Sanatana [06:00 – 20:00]

**The teacher's framing:** Srila Prabhupada is playing with a single familiar word to explain the whole concept. Four things are eternal:

| Term                       | What is eternal                                         |
| :------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Sanatana Lord**          | The Supreme Personality of Godhead                      |
| **Sanatana dhama**         | The abode beyond this material sky                      |
| **Sanatana living entity** | The soul                                                |
| **Sanatana dharma**        | The relationship between the living entity and the Lord |

And the whole purpose of the Gita, as paragraph 33 states it, is to revive that eternal occupation.

#### Why "religion" is an inadequate translation [10:00]

- The dictionary sense of religion is a faith, and a faith can change.
- _Sanātana_ means what cannot change. The teacher gives Srila Prabhupada's standing examples: liquidity cannot be separated from water; heat and light cannot be separated from fire.
- So the definition he asks the class to work with is: that which can never be separated from us.

#### The search paragraph 37 sets up [10:00 – 12:00]

The body is continuously changing, and every material relationship is continuously changing. The instruction is to look for what is _not_ changing — and that is the eternal occupation.

#### Paragraph 38 — what that occupation is [12:00 – 14:00]

The teacher calls this paragraph important enough to read in full.

- It draws on Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's instructions to Sanatana Gosvami — he notes the parallel bodies of instruction given to Rupa Gosvami over ten days and to Sanatana Gosvami over a month, both of which survey the vast number of species, the small proportion that are human, and the smaller proportion still that are civilized, before arriving at the answer.
- The answer is that the constitutional position of the living entity is to be the Lord's eternal servant. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **The argument from observation.** Srila Prabhupada writes that if one goes on searching, there is no exception anywhere in the society of living beings to the activity of service. The teacher's illustrations: a politician asks for votes on the promise of service; husband and wife serve each other; and where family members do not live together, people keep animals in order to have something to serve. The propensity to serve is the living entity's constant companion. **The only question is whom.**

#### Paragraph 40 — the conclusion [14:00 – 18:00]

- Our relationship with the Lord is one of service. He is the enjoyer and we are the servants; we are created for His pleasure, and by participating in that eternal enjoyment with Him we become happy. **We cannot become happy otherwise.**
- The teacher notes that a person will scan for some other route, assuming there must be one — and quotes the Lord's statement that from the highest planet down, everything is a place of repeated birth and death.
- **His formulation of the actual difficulty:** we want to become happy without serving Krishna wholeheartedly. He lists the usual conditions we wait for — the family settling, an understanding with one's spouse, the children becoming devotees, a better superior at work, a house of one's own.
- **The second formulation, which he treats as the core problem:** when it comes to Krishna consciousness we are not exclusive and we do not offer our heart. We are exclusively available for family and society and act wholeheartedly there; devotion gets less, and is not done from the heart.

#### Srila Prabhupada's redefinition of dharma [18:00 – 20:00]

The teacher treats this as revolutionary:

- The prevailing understanding of religion was a label — one tradition or another — and the prevailing marker of a religious person was ritual observance: lighting incense, an occasional temple visit, wearing tilaka. That could coexist with anything at all in private life.
- Srila Prabhupada took the definition of dharma beyond ritual: dharma means how we engage in Krishna's service, and that is the purpose of life.
- His observation on what religion had otherwise become: a business, where the single expectation is that the Lord will arrange one's material life.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Everyone is already serving something — that is not in question and admits no exception. What is in question is the object, and the whole argument turns on the claim that happiness is unavailable on any other basis. [06:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

## Paragraphs 41–42: Why Not Worship Demigods? [20:00 – 24:00]

- If service must be rendered anyway, why not to the demigods? The Gita does not sanction it.
- The teacher notes that the Lord did not even permit it in His own family, recalling the Govardhana pastime where Nanda Maharaja was preparing worship of Indra.
- **Who does worship them:** those whose desires are so strong that their knowledge is covered.
- **The objection he anticipates:** we also have desires; ours are not zero either.
- **His answer** is about restlessness rather than desire as such. Such a person cannot wait. His illustration: the Lord has not forbidden eating — He has said to cook it, offer it, and then eat it. Someone who cannot wait even that long, who needs it instantly, will turn elsewhere. Even for the fulfilment of desires, devotional service is what is recommended. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- Paragraph 42 ends by noting that the residents of Vrindavana worship the Lord alone, because their ultimate goal is to return to His abode.

## Paragraphs 43–48: The Sanatana Dhama [24:00 – 32:00]

- Paragraph 43 establishes that the Lord's abode needs no sun, moon, or electricity.
- Paragraph 44 contrasts the effort spent on reaching other destinations with the abode called Goloka, described in scripture, from which the Lord descends — and which even then the foolish fail to recognize, as some did not during His presence.
- **Two problems with every other destination,** given in paragraph 45:
  - **One must come back.** The teacher's image is a fairground ride: everyone wants to get on, and after one round the excitement is over and they want to get off — but the operator completes the full run first.
  - **The four principles remain.** No planet in the material universe is free from birth, old age, disease, and death.
- Paragraph 46 acknowledges there is a mechanism for interplanetary travel — those who worship the demigods go to them, those who worship the ancestors go to them — but paragraph 47 concludes that the Gita does not advise going to any planet in the material world.
- **The teacher's reframing, which he treats as the section's best point:** if you want to go higher, why stop partway? He rejects the idea that spiritual life kills ambition, and states the opposite — that spiritual life is for super-ambitious people. His example is Dhruva Maharaja, and his comparison is the enormous effort people make for a visa to one country while not even considering what lies beyond. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- Paragraph 48 supplies a short description of the material world through the image of the inverted tree, so that the eternal abode can be understood by comparison.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Gita does not dampen ambition; it redirects it. The objection to other destinations is not that they are too high but that they are not high enough, and that the four fundamental problems follow you there. [24:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

## Paragraphs 49–55: Qualification and the Science of Remembrance [32:00 – 42:00]

#### Freedom from designation [34:00 – 38:00]

- Paragraph 49, which the teacher calls a beautiful paragraph, gives the qualification: being beyond material designations. So long as a person is attached to material designation, a body must be accepted.
- **His illustrations of designation set from within:**
  - Maharaja Prataparudra, outwardly a king, who was ready to give up the kingdom and the body itself, and who — asked by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu who he was — answered from the heart that he was the servant of the servants of His servants.
  - Hanuman, who when brought before Ravana identified himself by whose servant he was.
  - Hanuman again in the Ramayana, where he is given no official post although others hold them.
  - Lakshmana's introduction of himself on first meeting, where he introduces Rama by lineage and himself only as an insignificant servant.
- **The teacher's point:** the designation must be set internally. Externally anyone can say it. Once it is genuinely internal, the Lord will consider that His servant does not belong in this world.

1. Count how many times in the Introduction Srila Prabhupada stresses that Krishna is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and how many times that we are His servants.
2. Count how many verses of the Gita are quoted within the Introduction itself.

#### The science of remembrance [38:00 – 42:00]

- Paragraph 51 concludes that one who remembers the Lord at the end attains Him.
- Paragraph 52 makes the point that the next body is in fact already decided while this body still exists, according to the activities performed in it. The teacher cites the Bhagavatam's image of a creature that lifts its back foot only after the front one has found its place — the next position is secured before the present one is released. ## Paragraphs 56–62: How to Remember the Lord While in This World [42:00 – 48:00]

#### The problem stated [42:00]

Paragraph 56 asks it directly: we are accustomed to thinking of material energy — how is thought transferred to spiritual energy?

#### The solution: transfer what you read [42:00 – 44:00]

- The paragraph's answer is scripture. The teacher quotes the principle that the conditioned soul cannot revive Krishna consciousness by his own effort, which is why the Lord mercifully gave the Vedic literature. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **Srila Prabhupada's instruction as the teacher reads it:** just as materialists engage their minds in newspapers, magazines, and so much other literature, we must transfer our reading to the literature given by Vyasadeva.
- **His own application:** the habit of regular reading is slow to form, while we read a great deal of material content — and because of that our absorption never changes. He relates the counsel that two hours are given for chanting, and a remark he attributes to Gopal Krishna Gosvami Maharaja that maya cannot touch one who reads these books for two hours. [Attribution flagged; see Verification Flags.]

#### But not by abandoning responsibility [44:00 – 46:00]

- Paragraph 57 addresses the obvious next thought — whether one should leave everything and go to the forest. The teacher's summary: the Lord never instructs anything impractical. This is to be done while living in the world and discharging one's duties.
- Paragraph 58 asks how that is possible, and answers with the comparison Chaitanya Mahaprabhu gave Rupa Gosvami: a woman attached to someone continues all her household responsibilities while remembrance of that person persists. Attachment produces remembrance. So attachment to the Lord has to be developed, after which other activities continue while the main thing is known to be remembrance of Him.
- Paragraphs 59 to 62 develop the means — the process of hearing and chanting, and practice.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Remembrance is not manufactured by effort at the moment it is needed. It is a by-product of attachment, and attachment is built by changing what one habitually reads and hears. [42:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

## Paragraphs 63–65: The Conclusion [46:00 – 52:00]

- **Paragraph 63:** all of this having been explained, one must still learn it from someone with realization — approach a spiritual master.
- **Paragraph 64:** quoting verses the teacher calls hope-giving, the point is that great scholarship and exceptional intelligence are not required. Anyone who accepts the principle of _bhakti-yoga_ and accepts the Supreme Lord as the shelter and ultimate goal of life can reach the spiritual sky.
- **Paragraph 65:** one who properly follows the Gita's instructions can be freed from all miseries and anxieties of life, and the next life will be spiritual.
- **The teacher's observation on consistency:** paragraph 11 stated that the Gita's purpose is to deliver humanity from ignorance, and the concluding paragraph says the same thing. A good author's message stays consistent from start to finish.
- He notes the further paragraph after the Gita-mahatmya verses, where Srila Prabhupada draws out the point that one scripture, one deity, one mantra, and one occupation are sufficient.

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

### 1. If the Gita has five subjects and the Upanishads three, why is it called an Upanishad? [58:00 – 60:00]

- **Question:** The essence of the Vedas is in the Vedanta-sutra, and the Upanishads discuss the soul, the world, and the Lord. But the Gita has five subjects, two of which are not in that list — so what makes it an Upanishad?
- **Answer:** The teacher first sketches the three divisions of Vedic literature:
  - **Karma-kanda**, much the largest portion, describing how to enjoy here — remaining here, but within the Lord's system.
  - **Jnana-kanda**, mainly in the Upanishads: there is no enjoyment here; what appears to be enjoyment is only advertisement, and any attempt at it produces a shock.
  - **Upasana-kanda**: not merely fleeing, but a positive alternative — devotion.
- **On the subjects:** the five map onto the three. The living entity is common to both lists; _īśvara_ is _jagadīśa_; and time, action, and nature all pertain to the world. So the subject matter is almost the same, which is why the Gita is called Gitopanishad.

### 2. Do the verses have to be memorized in order? [60:00 – 62:00]

- **Question:** Must one memorize from the first chapter onward, or can one memorize whichever verses are coming naturally — and can these be recorded and sent?
- **Answer:** The teacher defers the examination specifics to the course coordinator.
- **His own request:** whatever you do, study should stay in the creative zone rather than the anxiety zone. Do not study with negative anxiety; a healthy anxiety is fine. Reading and understanding scripture is very relishable, and with some thoughtfulness the test will be passed — that being the purpose of having one.

### 3. How does one become free of designation when the mind resists service? [62:00 – 66:00]

- **Question:** We know intellectually that we cannot do anything ourselves, but in practice the mind often does not want to perform a service, and devotees too can feel entitled to decline. In a servant's mood one would never say no. How does one get into that mentality?
- **Answer:** The final barrier is ego. False ego is _I am the enjoyer_ and _I am anything other than Krishna's servant_; real ego is _I am Krishna's servant_ — and that genuinely takes time to arrive.
- **His image:** Krishna is an extremely powerful magnet and the living entity is like iron filings, so the attraction ought to work — but there is a rust of lust on the iron, the rust of the propensity to enjoy.
- **The three things to attend to:**
  - **Do not waste time.**
  - **Be engaged in continuous, meaningful service** — one positive, tangible service connected to the Lord, engaging mind, intelligence, body, and resources.
  - **Pray** — with a longing to reach the next stage. The arrangements beyond that are the Lord's.
- **His analogy for how capacity grows:** physical capacity in a gym — two kilos, then four, then five, then ten and twenty. What is impossible on the first day becomes natural later. As the heart is purified, higher service becomes natural and only then can it actually be performed.

### 4. How does one keep the Lord's mission always in view? [66:00 – 72:00]

- **Question:** Hanuman performed many activities in Lanka but never for a moment forgot the mission Rama had given him. What should be in our lives so that the mission is never out of view?
- **Answer:** That is precisely the purpose of _sādhana_ — that the goal stays at the forefront. Why chant, why read, why hear lectures? So that we remain engaged in the Lord's service, and in His mission, in the proper mood.
- **The diagnosis:** the reason we forget is that we make something other than the Lord's mission the primary mission of our lives. Scripture says to discharge duties everywhere, but the primary mission should be His — because the heart can only be given to one place. When the heart goes to our individual mission, His becomes secondary.
- **The practical remedy is incremental:** increase time for reading and hearing; increase practical services; increase the engagement of one's resources. As these increase, identification with the mission increases.
- **His concrete questions:** where does a working devotee spend the weekend? When a promotion and a raise come, where is the increased income being planned for?
- **His examples of identification:** Maharaja Prataparudra in the Caitanya-caritamrta, who put everything at the disposal of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and the Bengali devotees, arranging accommodation and the rest — engaging all his resources and energy although he had not yet even met Him, and developing an extremely strong identification. He also mentions a devotee who gave half his earnings in service, and devotees in responsible professional positions who engaged their means the same way.
