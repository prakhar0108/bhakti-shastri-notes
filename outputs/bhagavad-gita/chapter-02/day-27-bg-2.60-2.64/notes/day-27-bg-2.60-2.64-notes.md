## Class Snapshot & One-Line Argument Map

- **Class Snapshot:** The heart of the practical section. BG 2.60 states how rare the steady person is — the senses carry away even the mind of the discriminating; 2.61 gives the one solution, _mat-paraḥ_, and is the first verse in which the Lord touches the concept of devotion; 2.62 – 2.63 trace the eight-step descent from contemplation to ruin; and 2.64 answers Arjuna's fourth question — how the senses are _engaged_.
- **One-Line Argument Map:** Even a man of discrimination who is endeavouring is forcibly carried away by the senses -> therefore restraining them is not enough by itself; restrain them **and** fix the mind on Me -> because contemplation produces attachment, attachment lust, lust anger, anger delusion, delusion loss of memory, loss of memory loss of intelligence, and then one falls -> whereas one free of attachment and aversion, whose senses are controlled and who acts within the regulations, moves among the sense objects and obtains the Lord's mercy.

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.60 [02:00 – 18:00]

<!-- verse:2.60 -->

**Context.** How rare the _sthita-prajña_ is.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yatataḥ_ [04:00]: one who is making the endeavour.
- _Vipaścitaḥ_ [04:00 – 06:00]: a man of discrimination — one who knows the distinction between body and soul and is working towards realising it.
- _Pramāthīni_ [06:00]: the senses described as maddening.
- _Haranti prasabham manaḥ_ [06:00]: they carry off the mind by force.

#### The point of the verse [06:00]

This is **not** about a foolish person with no scriptural knowledge — his absorption in enjoyment needs no explanation. It is about **one who has the knowledge and is actively endeavouring for realisation.** Even his mind is dragged away.

#### The two directions of attack [06:00 – 10:00]

- **Sometimes the senses drag the mind.** Something wrong is seen on the road, or a wrong sound reaches the ears on a train, and the mind follows it there. **Ajāmila first saw, and then began to contemplate.**
- **Sometimes the mind drags the senses.** In chanting, the tongue speaks, the ears hear and the hand touches the beads — the senses are engaged; and the mind goes elsewhere, and then even the chanting stops.
- **But ultimately what the mind accepts is what the person does.** Real engagement is engagement of the mind; until the mind is engaged, engagement has not occurred, and when it is, the effect is deep.
- **Everyone's experience:** we know what is right and cannot take it up; we know what is wrong and cannot give it up.

#### The purport's two examples [10:00 – 14:00]

1. **Viśvāmitra** — after thousands of years of austerity, the sound of Menakā's ankle bells disturbed him and everything was lost; and on a second occasion he lost it to anger. The historical evidence that controlling the senses is not easy.
2. **Yāmunācārya** — quoted at the opposite pole: since his mind became engaged at the Lord's lotus feet, where he continually relishes ever-new pleasure, **the very thought of worldly union makes his face turn away in disgust**, as if something unwanted had entered the mouth and must be spat out. This is a very high stage.

#### The Lord's programme makes enjoyment distasteful [14:00]

The purport's principle: Kṛṣṇa consciousness is such a transcendental engagement that material enjoyment automatically becomes distasteful. **The analogy:** a hungry man handed a full plate of rich preparations throws away the dry flatbread at once. Material happiness is that dry flatbread.

#### Clear no, clear yes, and "wait" [14:00 – 16:00]

- **The pure devotee's answer to any temptation is a clear no.** There is no option.
- **The materialist's answer is a clear yes** — a red-carpet welcome.
- **At the practitioner's stage we are in between**, and the teacher's honest observation is that a practitioner usually lacks the power to say no and so says _wait_: not in front of devotees; perhaps when alone; perhaps a small indulgence when nobody is watching.

#### Confidence without over-confidence [16:00 – 18:00]

- **The journey is difficult but not impossible.** The wrong conclusion — "I will never be able to give these things up; I have been chanting for so long and nothing has left me; this is only theory" — is itself the error. Where progress is not happening, one has to look at where the mistake in the practice lies. **The principle of spiritual life is that material attraction should be going down.**
- **And the verse is equally a warning against over-confidence:** one who becomes over-confident puts himself into wrong surroundings, thinking he can practise anywhere. **That is where the difficulty starts.**

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The verse is addressed precisely to the person who knows and is trying — which is why knowledge and effort alone are not the safeguard. [06:00, 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.61 [18:00 – 30:00]

<!-- verse:2.61 -->

**Context.** The most important verse of this section — and the first place in the Gītā where the Lord touches devotion directly.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tāni sarvāṇi saṁyamya_ [18:00]: keeping all those senses under control.
- _Mat-paraḥ_ [18:00 – 20:00]: **"fix the mind on Me."** The teacher calls this the key word of the verse.

#### Why restraint alone fails [18:00 – 22:00]

- Controlling the senses is difficult; restraining the mind is also difficult. **Therefore: restrain the senses and fix the mind on Me.** Once the mind is fixed there, it becomes easy.
- **This is why sense control is so hard in the paths of knowledge, of selfless action and of mystic yoga** — the positive object of attraction is not clear in them. **In devotion it is relatively easy,** because the focus is on the positive.
- **The observable difference** [20:00]: a person may be following observances of all kinds and still be unable to give up tea, coffee, meat or tobacco. In devotional practice the gross things generally fall away within a few months, and as the chanting improves, films and the rest also begin to go — **without very much effort being spent on giving them up.**

#### The purport's example: Mahārāja Ambarīṣa [22:00]

Śrīla Prabhupāda quotes the full description from the Ninth Canto. The first line is the mind fixed on the Lord's lotus feet, and then each sense follows:

- **words** in describing His qualities; **hands** in cleansing His temple; **ears** in hearing about Him; **eyes** in seeing His form; **body** in touching the bodies of His devotees; **nostrils** in smelling the _tulasī_ offered to Him; **legs** in going to His holy places; **head** in offering obeisances to Him.
- **The point:** along with the senses, the mind must be engaged — and the mind comes first in the list.

#### The three stages: control, concentration, conquest [24:00 – 28:00]

- **Stage one — control.** Coming from material life, no sense is regulated and the mind is full of refuse. A basic level of regulation is necessary — which is why the regulative principles are given.
- **The error:** focusing on control alone and forgetting the rest of the project. **One who focuses only on control will eventually fail to control.**
  - **The illustration:** the fasting days observed before coming to devotional life, where the whole content was "nothing is to be eaten until a certain hour" — waiting for the clock, with no positive engagement in it. In Kṛṣṇa consciousness the same thing becomes comparatively easy.
- **Stage two — concentration:** fixing the mind on the Lord. The mind is restless; we stand before the Deity and chant and the focus does not come. **Until the mind engages in something, we look for nourishment elsewhere.** As the mind gradually concentrates, nourishment begins to be felt in the practice itself.
- **Stage three — conquest**, which follows from absorption.

#### Why the old habits take over [28:00 – 30:00]

- **The unlocked house:** a man leaves his house with the windows open and goes away. He returns to find it filled with dirt — and if it was left unlocked, perhaps occupied by people who will not easily leave. **The tenant who will not vacate.**
- Our wrong habits have had long occupancy. **So whenever free time appears, something not connected with Kṛṣṇa consciousness is generally taken up for quick pleasure.**
- **The remedy is the positive alternative,** which is what devotional life uniquely supplies: where there was dancing elsewhere, there is now congregational chanting; where there were film songs, there are now devotional songs; where there was eating out, there is now feasting on the Lord's food. **The desires themselves are small; their pull is strong only because there is nothing positive in the life.**
- **And the direction of taste follows purification:** at present there is no taste in hearing and reading precisely because we are impure, and the impure takes its taste in impure things. **Which is exactly why more energy is put into cultivating positive absorption.**

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Control, concentration, conquest — and the middle step is the one usually skipped, which is why the first one eventually collapses. [24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.62 – 2.63 [30:00 – 50:00]

<!-- verse:2.62 -->

<!-- verse:2.63 -->

**Context.** The famous eight-step descent, taken as a single connected unit.

#### The eight steps [32:00 – 40:00]

| #   | Sanskrit                      | Stage                    | What it sounds like inside                         |
| --- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | _dhyāyataḥ viṣayān_           | **Contemplation**        | the mind turns the object over                     |
| 2   | _saṅgaḥ upajāyate_            | **Attraction**           | "this is rather nice"                              |
| 3   | _saṅgāt sañjāyate kāmaḥ_      | **Obsession**            | "I want it"                                        |
| 4   | _kāmāt krodhaḥ abhijāyate_    | **Irritation / anger**   | "who can stop me?"                                 |
| 5   | _krodhāt bhavati sammohaḥ_    | **Delusion**             | no longer knows what should and should not be done |
| 6   | _sammohāt smṛti-vibhramaḥ_    | **Forgetfulness**        | cannot turn to scripture; forgets what he heard    |
| 7   | _smṛti-bhraṁśāt buddhi-nāśaḥ_ | **Loss of intelligence** | intelligence is tied to memory                     |
| 8   | _buddhi-nāśāt praṇaśyati_     | **Fall**                 | back from spiritual to material consciousness      |

- **The child wanting a toy** is given as the everyday version: first it is appealing, then "I want it", then weeping, then — when the parents explain — anger, and things being broken.
- **The same sequence for an attraction between persons:** a first connection, then regular interaction, then a strong desire, then anger at whoever comes in between — "the subject of films."
- **On anger and delusion** [34:00 – 36:00]: in anger a person does not know what should be done or not done, so he does the wrong thing. If he were merely confused he could turn to the scriptures — but **delusion takes the memory**, so he does not turn to them at all. **Record what a person says in anger and he will not believe it himself.**

#### Where to intervene [36:00 – 42:00]

- **It is best to stop it at the first stage.** In real life these steps do not take the time it takes to explain them; often it is very fast.
- **Ajāmila's problem was not that he saw** — it was that he began to contemplate.
- **The galloping horse:** a rider can still manage a horse that is starting off slowly; as the speed builds, control becomes progressively harder.
- **The snowball:** a small piece begins to roll, grows as it descends, and finally knocks a man down. Negative thought works the same way.
- **The same mechanism works in the positive direction too** — the sequence is reversible; the problem is only that the later the stage, the harder the control.
- **What we take lightly becomes a habit,** and the habit is then hard to give up. **So attention must be paid to what we are hearing, reading and seeing.**

#### Filling the gaps [42:00 – 44:00]

- **Material impressions register extraordinarily fast.** The teacher recounts his own experience: after three or four hours of reading the Bhāgavatam one day, a devotee showed him a ten-minute video on a current social topic — **and that night the dream was about the video, with nothing from the four hours of reading.**
- Hence the instruction to fill the gaps in the day with Kṛṣṇa conscious engagement rather than with whatever is easiest, since "easy" means material.

#### Remembrance, and the garden of the mind [44:00 – 48:00]

- **Remembrance is one of the principal processes**, alongside hearing and chanting — and everyone is remembering something. Prahlāda Mahārāja is the teacher of remembrance; Kaṁsa also had the Lord constantly in mind, but negatively; Hanumān and Bharata had their minds on His service. **The remembrance must be positive.**
- **The garden analogy:** planting roses for the Deity, one weeds constantly — otherwise the water goes to the wrong plant, the labour is wasted and the garden is spoiled. **The mind's inner garden works the same way, and its seeds are our thoughts.**
- **And it needs only one nourishment:** an outer plant needs sunlight, water and fertiliser; the inner one needs only **contemplation.** A small thought, once emotion and desire become involved in it and contemplation begins, affects a whole life. The teacher notes how thoroughly this mechanism is used when young people are indoctrinated towards violence.
- **Therefore:** read the lives of great souls, so as to know what runs in their minds; and value reading, hearing, quality association and quality conversation, because these produce right contemplation.
- **Wrong contemplation comes from wrong content** [48:00]: news, scandal, controversy, wrong role models, characterless figures presented as heroes, material celebrity, the desire for name and fame, and the manner of speaking of those who deceive.

#### The purports on these two verses [48:00 – 50:00]

- **2.62 — "the senses require real engagement."** If real engagement is not given, they will go to material things.
- **The comparison that sometimes causes confusion:** Lord Śiva, described as grave, was agitated in the presence of Pārvatī, while Haridāsa Ṭhākura, chanting the holy name, was not disturbed when tested. **The teacher's clarification:** Śrīla Prabhupāda is commenting on the _process_, not on the personalities. **However great a person is, if his process is not devotional service, the chances of falling are greater** — while Haridāsa Ṭhākura was fixed in the holy name and had the higher taste.
- **And a second point:** material position and power, however exalted, **are not sufficient to deal with temptation.** Haridāsa Ṭhākura was born in a Muslim family and had no such position at all; what is required is the Lord's mercy, which comes through devotional service.
- **2.63 — _phalgu-vairāgya_ and _yukta-vairāgya_:** merely giving things up is _phalgu_ — the false, dry renunciation, and it is not easy; **engaging things in connection with Kṛṣṇa is _yukta-vairāgya_.**

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The sequence is not stopped by will-power at step five; it is stopped at step one, by what is allowed to be contemplated. [36:00, 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.64 [54:00 – 62:00]

<!-- verse:2.64 -->

**Context.** The answer to Arjuna's fourth question — _vrajeta kim_, how does he walk, i.e. **how does he engage the senses** — running from 2.64 to 2.71.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Caran_ / _vrajeta_ [54:00]: to move about — how he moves among the sense objects.
- _Ātma-vaśyaiḥ_ [56:00]: with the senses under his own control.
- _Vidheyātmā_ [56:00]: acting according to the regulations given in the scriptures.
- _Rāga-dveṣa-vimuktaiḥ_ [56:00]: free from attachment and aversion.
- _Prasādam adhigacchati_ [56:00]: he attains the Lord's mercy — and in that mercy, purity, the higher taste and a higher happiness.

#### What attachment and aversion actually look like [56:00 – 58:00]

- **In the conditioned state the mind has attachment for the wrong things and aversion for the right ones.**
- **Attachment to what is forbidden:** a film that has been restricted is the one people most want to see — _because_ it is restricted.
- **Aversion to what is prescribed:** rising early is difficult; chanting is resisted — particularly in the cold season.
- **Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's gloss** is cited to this effect: what the scripture forbids is what we want to do.
- **The definitions the teacher offers:** _rāga_ is excessive feeling for what is unhealthy; _dveṣa_ is aversion towards what is genuinely necessary. **The child who only wants to play** and must nevertheless be given a balance is the everyday parallel.

#### Why boundaries are not restrictions [58:00]

**A roof without a parapet** is dangerous — a child could fall. The boundary protects and facilitates. Engagement of the senses is therefore not unlimited engagement; it is engagement **within limits.**

#### What the engagement looks like [58:00 – 62:00]

Within the limits the scriptures set: **the eyes see the Lord's form; the ears hear His topics.** This is where the section turns — until now the focus has been entirely on control; **engaging the senses is equally important.**

- **Why we get stuck and he does not** [60:00]: the problem is not in the world; **the problem is inside** — attachment to the wrong and aversion to the right. Because his mind and senses are controlled and he is free of both, there is no difficulty when he engages them.
- **And the external activities can look identical** [60:00 – 62:00]: an advanced devotee and an ordinary person both sleep and both eat; even honouring the Lord's food, we may be doing it for enjoyment. **The consciousness is different.**

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications [62:00 – 66:00]

**Q — Once contemplation has already started, stopping the rest of the sequence feels very difficult. What can be done at that point?** [62:00 – 66:00]

- **Exactly why the emphasis falls on the first stage.** The greater part of the energy should go into guarding contemplation itself.
- **How to notice it:** if very negative thoughts about a devotee begin to appear, that is the point at which one should recognise that something is not going well.
- **Thinking, feeling, willing** — once it has passed from thinking into feeling, and further into willing, the difficulty is much greater. **The cut has to be made while it is still a thought.**
- **Two things to do:** engage in something positive and pray to the Lord; and **keep the thoughts filled with positive content** — plan the whole day so that it is filled with positive engagement as far as possible. An organised schedule leaves fewer openings.
- **The everyday example:** while chanting, the thought comes — "I slept late, the sleep was not complete, this is why the chanting is not going well, I will have to sit in the morning." **That is a thought process.** If one is not careful about it, then the moment an opportunity comes, when nobody is watching, he will go and sleep — **because he was not careful at the stage of contemplation.**
