BG 2.65 states what follows on receiving the Lord's mercy; 2.66 states the same thing in reverse for one who is disconnected; 2.67 gives the image of the boat swept by the wind; 2.68 concludes the point about restraint; 2.69 contrasts the night of one with the day of the other; 2.70 gives the ocean into which rivers enter without disturbing it; 2.71 defines real desirelessness; and 2.72 glorifies the spiritual situation from which one is not bewildered even at the hour of death.

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.65 [04:00 – 18:00]

<!-- verse:2.65 -->

**Context.** What follows on receiving that mercy.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prasāde_ [04:00]: on receiving the causeless mercy of the Lord — and _prasāda_ also carries the senses of purity and peace.
- _Sarva-duḥkhānāṁ hāniḥ_ [04:00]: the destruction of all miseries.
- _Prasanna-cetasaḥ_ [04:00]: the consciousness becomes delighted.
- _Āśu_ [06:00]: at once.
- _Buddhiḥ paryavatiṣṭhate_ [06:00]: the intelligence becomes sufficiently established — rock solid, unshakeable, so that the world's advertising can no longer move it.

#### Three principles the scriptures keep repeating [06:00 – 12:00]

The teacher sets these out as the framework for the verse:

1. **The happiness we are looking for is not outside but inside.** Whatever is obtained externally — comfort, position, respect — is not where it is.
2. **The quantity of the inner happiness is vastly greater.** If the outer is five grams, the inner is five tons.
3. **Purification precedes happiness.** It is found in purification, not in gratification.

- **The source** [08:00 – 10:00]: Ṛṣabhadeva's final instructions to his sons in the Fifth Canto — do not work so hard for sense gratification in this human form, since even animals obtain it without going to school, taking a job, running a business or building a house; **perform austerity, by which the existence is purified.**
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda's principle** [10:00]: enjoyment in a diseased condition aggravates the disease. **A diabetic can eat the sweet — and the condition worsens.**

#### What "all miseries are destroyed" means [12:00 – 14:00]

- **Not** that the devotee never falls ill, never has difficulties at home, never has a disagreement at work. **It means the consciousness rises, and what is below no longer affects him.**
- **The material parallel:** a man whose salary has just been substantially raised is cut off in traffic on the way home and is not disturbed. The same man, whose promotion has just been withheld and whose bonus has been cancelled, is disturbed by the slightest thing. **That is a material higher taste; the spiritual one works the same way.**
- **And the deeper reason** [14:00]: there may be many causes of distress, but **the principal cause is impure desire** — the enjoying propensity. When there is no desire to enjoy, the distress goes. As long as the living being strives to enjoy here, he is bound and he suffers.

#### Identity determines what disturbs us [14:00 – 16:00]

- As the mirror of the heart is cleansed, one sees clearly: **I am Kṛṣṇa's servant, Kṛṣṇa is my master.**
- **Whatever identity a person holds is what matters to him.** If "I am an employee of this company" is the operative consciousness, then what the employer says, and whether the company profits, matters greatly. If it is membership of some organisation, then what happens there matters.
- For a devotee, the settled identity means **what matters is what pleases Kṛṣṇa** — and the many voices of the world lose their power to disturb.

#### The peace formula [16:00 – 18:00]

- The purport's first line: without Kṛṣṇa consciousness there is no possibility of peace; and the Fifth Chapter's peace formula is cited — **the Lord is the enjoyer, the proprietor and the friend of all.**
- **Why these three disturb us** [16:00]: when I think **I am the controller**, nothing comes under control and there is frustration; when I think **I am the enjoyer**, everything is seen with an enjoying vision, **and distress hides behind enjoyment like someone hidden behind a curtain** — it is not visible, one enjoys, and it follows afterwards; when I think **I am the proprietor**, then this body, this wealth, all of it must be used for me.
- **The difference between the selfless worker and the devotee** [18:00]: the selfless worker discharges his prescribed duties without expectation of the fruit, but his attachment to the work and to family remains. **The devotee, who may also be a householder, discharges the same duties in a different consciousness:** the family are Kṛṣṇa's servants, not his; he has been posted to serve them by some past arrangement — with Kṛṣṇa at the centre.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The miseries do not vanish from the world; the consciousness rises above the level at which they register — which is why impure desire, and not circumstance, is named as the principal cause of distress. [12:00, 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.66 [18:00 – 22:00]

<!-- verse:2.66 -->

**Context.** The same statement in reverse — the condition of one who is not connected.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ayuktasya_ [20:00]: of one not connected; whose mind is not controlled and whose intelligence is not fixed on the self or on the Lord.
- _Bhāvanā_ [20:00]: the fixed mind; meditation. **Without a controlled mind and fixed intelligence, meditation is not possible.**
- _Śānti_ [22:00]: peace — defined by the teacher as **the cessation of agitation from sense objects; no thirst for material objects.**
- _Sukham_ [22:00]: happiness, meaning the actual bliss of the soul.

**The chain:** no connection → no transcendental intelligence → no fixed mind → no meditation → no peace → and without peace, no question of happiness.

### Bhagavad-gita 2.67 [22:00 – 30:00]

<!-- verse:2.67 -->

**Context.** Why a single unguarded sense is enough.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Caratām_ [24:00]: roaming — **as cows go out to graze,** so the senses browse for pleasure.
- _Yan manaḥ anuvidhīyate_ [24:00]: whichever of them the mind follows and settles on.
- _Vāyur nāvam ivāmbhasi_ [24:00]: as the wind carries off a boat on the water.

#### The two directions again [24:00 – 26:00]

- Sometimes the senses go out and the mind follows — something is seen and the mind goes after it.
- Sometimes the senses are engaged — one is chanting — **and the mind goes elsewhere.**

#### The purport [26:00 – 28:00]

**Unless all the senses are engaged in the service of the Lord, even one of them engaged in sense gratification can deviate one from the path of transcendental advancement.**

- **The teacher's application:** "the chanting is happening anyway", "at least the Deity was seen — that is already a great mercy", "the morning programme was missed, what of it", "a few rounds were missed, what of it."
- **A practitioner is by definition someone practising towards a goal;** a casual person cannot be placed in that category.
- **The illustration:** a devotee who reported that his chanting was "countless" — done while walking in the morning with friends, so that the walk, the conversation and the chanting all happened together. The teacher cites a senior devotee's remark about "driving _japa_", "walking _japa_", "sleeping _japa_": **if you chant while driving, both will meet with an accident** — the chanting and you.
- **And the "a little is fine" attitude:** a little news, a little of whatever is trending. **Any one sense can carry the whole boat away.**

#### Why the boat image [28:00 – 30:00]

We are all in the ocean of material existence, and **the wind is desire.** Since there are so many winds of desire, one has to keep oneself protected in order to cross. The teacher's aside: when a storm is forecast, one does not take the boat out to watch the storm.

#### What to guard against, and how [30:00 – 34:00]

**Two things a devotee must avoid:**

1. **Temptation towards sense objects.**
2. **Offensive thoughts towards anyone** — where the same eight-step cycle applies: if it is not stopped at the contemplation stage, if no brake is applied and no prayer offered, it becomes dangerous.

**Two practical measures:**

1. **Remove the option.** Do not test yourself. The device, the laptop, whatever it may be — there should be controls, so that there is simply no option of misuse.
2. **Create a spiritual culture in your own vicinity.** The mainstream culture cannot be changed and the billboards cannot be replaced; **but wherever you live, make it a spiritual world** — good, high-quality pictures of the Lord's pastimes, well placed; the desk at the office arranged accordingly; and **inspiring quotations placed around you.** The teacher's observation: nobody instructs us to read ten billboards between here and the station, and we read them all. **The reading habit will operate either way.**

#### Internal results and external results [32:00 – 34:00]

When something wrong is done and nobody catches it, a person thinks there is no great difficulty. **But every act has two results:** the external one, which manifests in its own time, and **the internal one, which is immediate — the impressions deepen at once and do not go away.** The tendency to titillate the senses "just a little" has a very large result.

### Bhagavad-gita 2.68 [34:00 – 38:00]

<!-- verse:2.68 -->

**Context.** The conclusion of the section on restraint.

- _Mahā-bāho_ [34:00]: mighty-armed — **as you conquer enemies, now conquer your senses.** The teacher's line: **the real hero is one who can control the senses.**
- _Sarvaśaḥ_ [34:00]: **all** the senses, altogether — because, as the previous verse said, even one going astray creates the difficulty.
- **"The best time to battle against temptation is before it begins"** [36:00] — that is, while one is in a good situation and a good environment.
- **A cultural observation** [36:00]: in earlier times people were more protected simply because the wrong thing was often not even known of, and seeing and hearing it was largely avoided.

#### The purport [36:00 – 38:00]

- **The only way to give up the urges of the senses is to engage them in the Lord's service** — the teacher calls this the most practical statement of all, since keeping the senses away from their objects is not otherwise possible.
- **And the closing line:** this art of engaging the senses in the Lord's service should be learned under the training of a bona fide spiritual master — **and one who does so is called a _sādhaka_, a suitable candidate for liberation.**

### Bhagavad-gita 2.69 [38:00 – 46:00]

<!-- verse:2.69 -->

**Context.** The contrast between two orientations. The distinction here is between the practitioner still working towards the steady stage and one who has arrived at it.

#### Four readings of "day" and "night" [40:00 – 46:00]

1. **Literally.** The teacher recounts his own hostel years: a group of student devotees took adjoining rooms and their seniors said plainly, _"You are the day people; we are the night people."_ — and it was exactly so. They rose at half past three for the morning programme and went to bathe just as the others were going to sleep; and the music in the other rooms began at the hour they themselves went to bed.
2. **Two kinds of intelligence.** One directed towards the soul, the other towards matter. **What occupies most people's intelligence is night to the introspective sage,** and what occupies his is night to them. **The purport's first line** states this: one is intelligent in material activities for sense gratification, the other is awake to the cultivation of self-realisation.
3. **Activity and inactivity.** Night is the period of inactivity, day the period of activity. **The spiritualist rises in the auspicious pre-dawn hour and works through the day; the materialist becomes active at night.** The teacher notes this is stated as a general principle.
4. **Interest.** Night means where there is no interest, day where there is. Tell a thoroughly materialistic man to come to the temple and it is a waste of time to him; tell a devotee to come to a party and it is a waste of time to him.

#### Why this matters practically [46:00]

- **Like-minded association is essential,** and it makes practice easier.
- **This is precisely the challenge for those in employment** — the devotee is honest, but everyone around has an entirely different interest, and we are social beings and are affected by the society around us. **So the question of how to navigate it has to be addressed.**

### Bhagavad-gita 2.70 [46:00 – 54:00]

<!-- verse:2.70 -->

**Context.** The ocean — the state of one who is complete.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Āpūryamāṇam acala-pratiṣṭham_ [48:00]: always being filled, steadily situated.
- **The image:** in the rainy season the rivers enter the ocean and in the hot season they diminish — **and the ocean never crosses its shore and is never agitated.** It remains within its own limit.
- _Na kāma-kāmī_ [48:00]: and not the man who strives to fulfil desires.

#### What it describes [48:00 – 50:00]

- Whatever sense objects exist in this world, **he sees them and is not affected**, because he is satisfied in the self. **His internal software has changed** — it has become pure — and nothing can affect it.
- **The analogy:** a man who has eaten fully passes stall after stall on his way — snacks, sweets, coffee, a speciality dish — **and being already complete, he is unaffected.**
- **Applied to a practitioner** [52:00]: as long as there is a body, the senses have some requirement; the point is that **he is not disturbed.** Desires may enter the mind as rivers enter the ocean, and he remains steady in his duties. **The purport: he has lost all inclination for sense gratification.**

#### Śrīla Prabhupāda's example [50:00 – 52:00]

The teacher recounts the occasion on which disciples in the West, with a very different cultural background, arranged a programme for Śrīla Prabhupāda at a venue where the members did not wear clothes at all — that being their idea of enjoyment.

- **He went, led the congregational chanting, and then spoke.** His address began from where they were: that with many layers of cloth on the body a person feels heavy, and as the layers come off he feels lighter and freer — **which they agreed with entirely.** He then said that in the same way **this body is itself an external covering on the soul, and one may consider how much freedom there is when the soul has no such covering at all** — to which they again agreed — and then told them that for this they should chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra.
- **The teacher's caution:** he preached in such places, and this is not to be replicated by anyone, because one would simply be affected.

#### Empty within, and full within [52:00 – 54:00]

- **Duryodhana represents the personality that is empty within:** he obtained the kingdom and everything with it and was still unhappy; the Pāṇḍavas went to the forest and he was still not satisfied, still disturbing them there.
- **The Pāṇḍavas were ready to settle for five villages,** though the whole kingdom was rightfully theirs.
- **In the Rāmāyaṇa,** Lord Rāma and Bharata dispute with each other in the opposite direction — _you take it, you become king_ — **because both are full.** Bharata wants nothing except the service of the Lord.
- **Sugrīva is the example of the ordinary soul:** he was empty. Deprived of the kingdom he was disturbed by its absence; given it back, he became disturbed again, and the Lord had to send Lakṣmaṇa.
- **The conclusion:** filling oneself from within is what matters. **Changing external circumstances will not move the vehicle forward.**

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The ocean is not still because no rivers enter it; it is still because it is already full. [48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.71 [54:00 – 64:00]

<!-- verse:2.71 -->

**Context.** The definition of real desirelessness.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vihāya kāmān sarvān_ [54:00]: having given up all desires for gratification.
- _Carati niḥspṛhaḥ_ [54:00 – 56:00]: he lives and moves in this world **without hankering** — eating as much as is needed, using the eyes as much as is needed, sleeping as much as is needed.
- _Nirmamaḥ_ [56:00]: without the sense of "mine", without possessiveness.
- _Nirahaṅkāraḥ_ [56:00]: without false ego — he does not think that he is this body.

#### The purport's definition [56:00 – 60:00]

- **Desirelessness does not mean having no desires at all**, which is impossible. **It means not desiring anything for sense gratification — desiring instead to become Kṛṣṇa conscious.**
- **What it is not** [58:00]: it is not frustration. The teacher lists the various kinds of false renunciation — the man who quarrels with his wife, announces he is leaving the house for good, and is back by evening explaining that he returned only for the children's sake.
- **How it is actually done:** when a person understands his real position — **that he is eternally the servant of the Lord, and that the body and everything given with it is for the Lord's service and not for himself** — he arrives at the stage where **everything must be used for the satisfaction of Kṛṣṇa.**
- **The purport's example: Arjuna himself** — he had no desire to fight, and fought with all his strength for Kṛṣṇa's satisfaction. **Real desirelessness is desire for the satisfaction of Kṛṣṇa.**

#### Desirelessness is not passivity [60:00]

- A common misreading in devotional life: _"I want nothing now, I need do nothing."_ **The teacher notes that the Lord is striking hard at precisely this, because it is so common — and He does not appreciate it.**
- **"I harm nobody, I cheat nobody" is the platform of goodness,** and a person in goodness still wants his own comfort and is, in that sense, self-interested. The picture given: retired, sitting in the garden with the newspaper and tea, cheating nobody, speaking well with everybody, giving a little in charity, paying the electricity bill on time, staying within the comfort zone and never exerting himself.
- **Devotion means dedication:** doing what Kṛṣṇa wants, and being prepared to take on anxiety for His sake.

#### Hanumān as the illustration [60:00 – 64:00]

- **When the ocean had to be crossed,** each of the monkeys declared how far he could leap — one a certain distance, another further, and the greatest of them still far short of the hundred required. **Their calculations were all based on their own capacity.**
- **Hanumān was not dependent on his own capacity.** The teacher gives the commentators' explanation: he thought of himself as **an arrow in the hands of Lord Rāma** — and how far an arrow travels depends on the one who draws the bow, not on the arrow.
- **And in Laṅkā** he searched the entire city without difficulty, passing through every place, **because there was no desire for enjoyment in him** — _vihāya kāmān … nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ._

### Bhagavad-gita 2.72 [64:00 – 74:00]

<!-- verse:2.72 -->

**Context.** The concluding verse of the chapter.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Eṣā brāhmī sthitiḥ_ [64:00]: this is the spiritual situation.
- _Na enāṁ prāpya vimuhyati_ [64:00]: having attained it, one is not bewildered.
- _Anta-kāle api_ [64:00 – 66:00]: **even at the hour of death** — which is specified because in ordinary conditions it is comparatively easy to focus, whereas at death all the senses slacken and the mind is scattered.
- _Brahma-nirvāṇam ṛcchati_ [66:00]: he attains the supreme liberation, the spiritual situation.

#### The purport's opening line, and the confusion it causes [66:00 – 68:00]

**One can attain Kṛṣṇa consciousness or divine life at once, within a second — or one may not attain such a state of life even after millions of births.**

- **How can the two be so far apart?** Because **in one second a person may decide to surrender completely** — that there will be no going back. **For one who surrenders, it is immediate.**
- **And the millions of lifetimes are the time it takes to arrive at that consciousness** — to be able actually to surrender fully.
- **The illustration** [68:00]: the account of the wealthy merchant of Vṛndāvana whose temple still stands there. Someone remarked in passing that it had become late, that the sun was going towards the west. **That single line entered his consciousness** — it has already become very late to become serious about spiritual life; I have grown old in material life; I could die at any time. **He gave up his entire material life at once**, and, having been a wealthy man whom some had resented, became so humble that he would go begging for alms at the houses of those very people.
- **Mahārāja Khaṭvāṅga** is the purport's example: he asked how much time remained and was told a single _muhūrta_. The demigods, wishing to reward his help, offered him whatever he wished — a kingdom, wealth, anything. **He asked first how much time was left**, and on being told, dismissed the offers and turned his attention immediately to Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

#### The purport's conclusion [70:00 – 74:00]

- **"Let us finish off the business in this life."** The resolve should be that one need not enter any mother's womb again, that one is not coming back to the material world, and that **one is not trying to settle down here.**
- **How long it took to get here** [70:00 – 72:00]: the soul passes through some eighty million species to obtain a human body; then many human births pass before one even comes to know that devotional service is to be practised. **So a person who has both the knowledge and the association of devotees, and is still slack — "let us pass the time, let us settle this and that first" — is the most unfortunate of all,** since that is precisely what has been done for all those births.
- **And the final point:** there is no difference between the spiritual kingdom and devotional service to the Lord, **since both are on the absolute platform.** Hearing, chanting and serving the Lord is what is going on there as well. **So it should be done here with joy** — and if reading, chanting and service feel like torture here, then there will be no entry there, because there is no interest in it.
- **The conclusion drawn in class: we should take spiritual life seriously.**

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The second and the millions of lifetimes are not alternatives — the lifetimes are what it takes to be able to use the second. [66:00]
</Callout>
