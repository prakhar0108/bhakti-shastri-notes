## Class Snapshot & One-Line Argument Map

- **Class Snapshot:** Arjuna completes his case. BG 2.5 argues that begging is better than killing worshipable teachers, and that a kingdom won that way would be tainted with blood. BG 2.6 is his fifth reason — indecision: he does not know whether victory or defeat is better, and would not wish to live even after winning. The commentaries read this same verse as showing Arjuna's _qualification_ to receive knowledge, and BG 2.7 records the turn: he stops advising and formally accepts Kṛṣṇa as his spiritual master. The verse is introduced here and taken in full in the next class.
- **One-Line Argument Map:** Arjuna argues that even a kingdom gained by killing worshipable elders would be tainted with blood -> then admits he cannot tell whether conquering or being conquered is better, since he would not want to live either way -> this exhausts his own analysis and leaves him bewildered about duty -> so he gives up the role of advisor, declares himself Kṛṣṇa's disciple, and asks to be instructed decisively.

## Continuity & Recap from Preceding Class [02:00 – 10:00]

- **The chapter in outline** [02:00 – 04:00]: Chapter Two has 72 verses; in Sanskrit it is known as the chapter distinguishing the self from the non-self, and Śrīla Prabhupāda titles it _Contents of the Gītā Summarized_. Its four sections were revised — _G_ for guru (2.1 – 2.10), _I_ for identity (2.11 – 2.30), the two _dharmas_ with the description of _karma-kāṇḍa_ and _niṣkāma karma-yoga_ (2.31 – 2.53), and _A_ for _ātmārāma_, the _sthita-prajña_ (2.54 – 2.72).
- **Why identity comes first** [02:00]: whatever we identify with is what we then labour to serve, so knowing what we are must precede knowing what to do.
- **BG 2.1 – 2.4 recapped** [06:00 – 10:00]: Arjuna's collapse and Sañjaya's report; Kṛṣṇa's question about where such impurity came from at such an hour; the three losses; the rebuke of impotence and petty weakness of heart; and Arjuna's reply that Bhīṣma and Droṇa are worshipable — one gave him his life and the other taught him archery, so they deserve a shower of flowers, not a shower of arrows.
- **A note from the commentary** [10:00]: Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa observes that Arjuna's use of two similar names in BG 2.4 — Madhusūdana and ari-sūdana — is technically a fault of repetition by the rules of grammar; but a person overwhelmed by emotion no longer attends to such things.

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.5 [10:00 – 18:00]

<!-- verse:2.5 -->

**Context.** Answering the implicit argument that without fighting there will be no kingdom and therefore no means of livelihood, Arjuna says he would rather beg than kill his teachers, and that enjoyment purchased by their deaths would be stained with their blood.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Gurūn ahatvā_ [10:00]: not killing the superiors.
- _Mahānubhāvān_ [14:00]: great souls — not ordinary teachers.
- _Bhaikṣyam_ [10:00 – 12:00]: livelihood by begging.
- _Hatvārtha-kāmān_ [14:00]: even granting that they desire worldly gain.
- _Rudhira-pradigdhān_ [16:00]: tainted, smeared with blood.
- _Śarīra-yātrā_ [10:00]: the maintenance of the bodily journey — the practical necessity that even Chapter Three acknowledges.

#### Why begging is a strong concession [10:00 – 12:00]

- Scripturally, living by begging is the prerogative of the _brahmacārī_, the _brāhmaṇa_ and the _sannyāsī_; a _kṣatriya_, a _vaiśya_ or a _śūdra_ may not beg.
- Arjuna therefore knows that what he is proposing is blameworthy for a man of his order and that people will criticise him — and he accepts that, because in his view it at least avoids the slaughter of his elders.

#### May a guru ever be given up? [12:00 – 16:00]

- The objection arises immediately: these very teachers have taken Duryodhana's side.
- Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's commentary is cited for the principle: a guru who becomes proud and who loses the discrimination of what should and should not be done may be given up. This is the scripture's own provision, not an exception invented against scripture.
- **Arjuna's counter:** they are _mahānubhāvān_ — great souls, and therefore not to be abandoned.
- **Bhīṣma's stated reason:** Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa quotes a verse from the Mahābhārata, spoken by Bhīṣma to Yudhiṣṭhira, that a man is a servant of wealth while wealth is no one's servant — Bhīṣma and Droṇa were maintained by Duryodhana, and this is the external reason they fought on his side. The teacher notes that this is the outer reason only; the real reason appears in the Ninth Chapter of the First Canto of the Bhāgavatam.
- **Prabhupāda's purport** [16:00]: according to scriptural codes, a teacher engaged in abominable action and bereft of discrimination is fit to be abandoned; Bhīṣma and Droṇa were obliged to take Duryodhana's side because of financial assistance, although on such grounds alone they should not have accepted that position, and in these circumstances they had lost their respectability as teachers. Arjuna, however, continues to consider them superiors, and holds that enjoying material gains after killing them would be to enjoy spoils stained with blood.

#### Their greatness [14:00]

- Bhīṣma had conquered death, in the sense that he could choose the time of his own passing, and had conquered lust through his lifelong vow of celibacy — such persons are not ordinary.

#### How a disciple should behave with a teacher [16:00 – 18:00]

- One should never fight with one's teachers, and should not even argue with them in jest or sarcasm — this applies to the instructing as well as the initiating spiritual master.
- One may state one's position, but once the teacher has spoken, proper conduct is to accept it and not push one's own point, even where one is logically right.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's final argument is moral rather than tactical: a kingdom obtained by killing worshipable elders is enjoyment smeared with their blood, and he would rather beg — even though begging is itself blameworthy for a _kṣatriya_. [10:00, 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.6 [18:00 – 34:00]

<!-- verse:2.6 -->

**Context.** Arjuna's fifth and final reason: indecision. He cannot determine which outcome is better, and concludes that even winning would be a form of defeat.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Na caitad vidmaḥ_ [18:00]: we do not know this.
- _Katarat garīyaḥ_ [18:00]: which of the two is better, weightier.
- _Jayema / jayeyuḥ_ [18:00]: whether we conquer them, or they conquer us.
- _Na jijīviṣāmaḥ_ [20:00]: we would not wish to live.

#### The deadlock [18:00 – 22:00]

The teacher reconstructs the exchange implicit in the verse:

- Arjuna: I do not even know which is better, that we should win or that they should win.
- If the Lord answers, "do not worry, you are on the side of _dharma_ and I am with you — you will win": Arjuna replies that after killing the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra he would not wish to live at all.
- So even victory becomes defeat — "I will win, and having won, I will have lost."
- Should he fight, there is distress; should he refuse, there is distress. And he cannot take up begging either, since that is not permitted to a _kṣatriya_.

#### Analysis that ends in paralysis [20:00 – 30:00]

- After long and intelligent analysis Arjuna is himself confused — a familiar pattern: a person thinks a great deal, applies the mind thoroughly, and arrives at no conclusion.
- **The doctor's dilemma** [28:00]: two patients are critically ill; neglect either and he dies, yet even with treatment there is no guarantee, and whichever one he attends to first, criticism will follow. The choice paralyses.
- Arjuna's case is heavier still: not only his own future but generations to come and the whole world depend on what he decides.
- **Where devotees face this** [30:00]: choosing an _āśrama_, or deciding from whom to take initiation when many spiritual masters are glorious — confusion of this kind consumes a person's energy until it is resolved.

#### Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa: this verse shows Arjuna is _qualified_ [22:00 – 28:00]

The commentary reads the same speech as evidence that Arjuna is a fit candidate for transcendental knowledge, listing the qualifications a recipient must have:

- **Control of mind and senses:** shown by his earlier question in Chapter One — what use is a kingdom, and what use is enjoyment, to me?
- **Detachment (_uparati_):** shown by "I do not want the kingdom."
- **Tolerance (_titikṣā_):** shown by his willingness to live by begging, which requires enduring a great deal.
- **Faith (_śraddhā_):** appears from the very next verse.

Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport follows the same line [26:00 – 28:00]: Arjuna's statements prove that he was not only a great devotee but highly enlightened and in full control of his mind and senses; his willingness to live by begging, though born in a royal family, is another sign of detachment; these qualities, together with his faith in the words of his spiritual master, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, show that he was truly virtuous and altogether fit for liberation — for unless the senses are controlled one cannot rise to the platform of knowledge, and without knowledge and devotion there is no liberation.

The teacher's caution [26:00]: much of Prabhupāda's purport here is a condensed summary of the _ācāryas'_ commentaries, so reading those commentaries makes clear why these particular points appear.

#### The turning point: the devotee stops advising the Lord [30:00 – 34:00]

- Until this verse Arjuna has been playing the role of an **advisor** to Kṛṣṇa, presenting five reasons why the war should not be fought — and the Lord has not begun His discourse.
- We do the same: we light a lamp before the Lord and hand Him our instructions — arrange this, fix that, settle this match, provide this job.
- The pure devotee's prayer runs the other way: do with me whatever You wish. The teacher cites the Vaiṣṇava mood of offering everything to the Lord's will, and its parallel in the Biblical prayer that God's will be done, not one's own.
- **Why the advisor is left alone** [32:00]: as long as we play advisor, no one interferes in our life — not the Lord, not the spiritual master, not the Vaiṣṇavas — because we are already convinced of what we intend to do; and everyone else is left in the role of spectator.
- **What advising costs us** [34:00]: giving advice to the spiritual master and to Kṛṣṇa only frustrates and confuses us. Frustration, because we cannot alter what time ordains — nobody can manipulate life into the shape he wants, since we are within the three modes of nature. Confusion, because the mind itself keeps changing the advice it gives us.
- The message of the Gītā begins at the point where advising stops and cooperation begins.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The same speech is read two ways: as Arjuna's collapse into indecision, and as proof that he now possesses the sense control, detachment and tolerance that qualify a person to receive transcendental knowledge. [22:00, 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.7 — introduction [34:00 – 40:00]

<!-- verse:2.7 -->

**Context.** Arjuna abandons argument and formally takes shelter. The class introduces the verse here; the detailed treatment is taken up in the next class.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kārpaṇya-doṣa_ [36:00]: the fault of miserliness, which has overcome his natural character.
- _Dharma-sammūḍha-cetāḥ_ [36:00]: bewildered in heart about what his duty is.
- _Niścitam brūhi_ [36:00]: tell me decisively — not as one more opinion among his own.
- _Śiṣyas te 'ham_ [36:00 – 38:00]: I am Your disciple — no longer merely Your friend.
- _Śādhi māṁ tvāṁ prapannam_ [36:00]: instruct me, who am surrendered unto You.

#### Questions are allowed; arguing is not [38:00]

Arjuna continues to ask questions throughout the rest of the Gītā — inquiry is permitted and expected. What ends here is argument against the instruction.

#### When and how a guru is approached [38:00 – 40:00]

From Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's commentary and the Upaniṣadic sources it draws on:

- **When:** one should approach a spiritual master at the point of resolving that one does not want material enjoyment but wants to attain the Lord.
- **How:** the classical instruction is to approach with fuel in hand — that is, one never goes to the guru empty-handed, but always with something for his service.
- **Whose:** the teacher must himself be qualified — fixed in the Absolute and conversant with scripture, knowing not merely its words but its essence.
- **_Ācāryavān puruṣo veda_** [38:00]: as _dhanavān_ means one who possesses wealth and _rūpavān_ one who possesses beauty, _ācāryavān_ means one who possesses an _ācārya_ — who has the mercy of his teachers and has pleased them. Such a person knows the essence of scripture.

#### The disciple's attitude decides what is revealed [40:00]

- In the Second Chapter of the First Canto of the Bhāgavatam, Sūta Gosvāmī's knowledge of the Vedas is stated as his external qualification; the principal qualification given next is that he is the gentle, affectionate disciple of his teachers, and therefore whatever is confidential was revealed to him.
- An arrogant person may master languages and become a Sanskrit scholar, and still the essence of scripture will not be in his heart. Where there is a serving mood, the _ācāryas_ reveal that essence.
- **The doctor analogy** [50:00]: the desire to treat a patient is not enough — the physician must also know the surgery. This is why the qualification of the teacher, and not only the attitude of the student, is specified.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
"I am Your disciple" is the hinge of the chapter: Arjuna changes his role from friend and advisor to student, and only then does the Gītā's instruction begin. [36:00, 38:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications [42:00 – 58:00]

**Q1 — Scripture says to follow the spiritual master without raising logical objections, yet here Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to oppose his teachers. How do both hold together?** [42:00 – 46:00]

- The same scripture that enjoins following the guru also provides that a guru who becomes proud, and who has lost the discrimination between what should and should not be done, may be given up — as Bali Mahārāja gave up Śukrācārya.
- That is the principle the Lord is applying here: Droṇa and Bhīṣma remained silent when Draupadī was insulted in the assembly, knowing it to be wrong.
- As long as a spiritual master is himself on the right path, he is not to be abandoned.
- **Follow-up — what if we cannot judge, and are misled by our own limitation?** As long as we are honest, this need not be feared: an honest person is never ultimately cheated, and even if a teacher were a cheat, the Lord will make arrangements — as He did for Bali Mahārāja. Kṛṣṇa has an essential role here, and not everything can be settled by logic. Continued study of scripture gives a broad ability to see whether principles are being grossly violated; the details of another's life and mind we cannot know. Where nothing has been grossly violated, one follows, performs one's own duty, and remains protected — and with the turning of time things become clear.

**Q2 — If we are not to play the advisor, how much effort should we make for our own plans — as Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī repeatedly tried to leave home and was repeatedly caught?** [46:00 – 50:00]

- **Manipulation is the real danger.** Dealing with the spiritual master and Vaiṣṇavas by manipulation is what Caitanya Mahāprabhu calls _kuṭīnāṭī_; such a person will not be able to chant, and a _brahmacārī_ living that way will not remain in the āśrama for long.
- **Present honestly:** "this is what I wish to do; this is my plan and my desire."
- **If it is not sanctioned** but the desire remains strong, present it once more, asking to at least try — normally a senior will then give his blessing.
- **If it is still refused** in an extreme case, according to _dharma_ the plan should be given up.
- **If instead one manipulates** — arranging every presentation so that sanction becomes inevitable — the result may be a materially successful devotee, but not a spiritually successful one. Transgressing one's own domain becomes a habit, and that habit is a long-term loss.

**Q3 — Where is it stated that Droṇa and Bhīṣma acted for financial assistance?** [50:00 – 52:00]

In the purport, following Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa, who cites the Mahābhārata verse spoken by Bhīṣma to Yudhiṣṭhira that a man is a servant of wealth while wealth serves no one. The teacher repeats that this is the external reason; Bhīṣma was a devotee and knew the Lord's desire, and the real reason is found in the Ninth Chapter of the First Canto of the Bhāgavatam.

**Q4 — If a service connected with the Lord is sanctioned and then fails, how should that be taken?** [52:00 – 54:00]

In spiritual life nothing is a failure. There may be a material failure, and some devotees may criticise the loss — that has to be tolerated — but an attempt made for the Lord's service is never a spiritual failure.

**Q5 — Do the Lord's eternal associates feel distress as we do — for instance Bhīṣma watching Draupadī insulted?** [54:00 – 58:00]

- **Every situation is dealt with according to one's current level of consciousness.** The teacher lays out an ascending scale of response to adversity:
  1. "Who wants this trouble — let it go."
  2. "This is the reaction of my own past activities; I must have made a mistake."
  3. Folding one's hands and thanking the Lord that the reaction is returning now, recognising that no one else is at fault.
  4. Seeing the difficulty as the Lord's mercy — the mood of Queen Kuntī's prayer welcoming calamities, because they end the repetition of birth.
- Before coming to devotional life we were disturbed by very small things; as practice deepens, the same things disturb less, and the capacity to tolerate rises by degrees. This is why spiritual life works on the consciousness.
- **The eternal associates** come to play their role. Externally the nature of the body applies to them too — Mādhavendra Purī was bedridden at the end, and all his activities had to be performed there — but their remembrance of Kṛṣṇa is never lost, and they do whatever the Lord's service requires, gladly.
- **Prayers reveal the contents of a devotee's heart:** in Bhīṣma's prayers in the Ninth Chapter of the First Canto he does not describe his own pain at all, but only the Lord's beauty; Queen Kuntī's prayers are of the same character.

**Q6 — Is today Snāna-yātrā?** [58:00]

Yes. It is the last _darśana_ of Lord Jagannātha before He departs for the Ratha-yātrā, and the crowd is nearly as large as at the Ratha-yātrā itself. A _tithi_ generally spreads across two dates of the English calendar, which is why such doubts arise about the day.

## Last-Page Revision Sheet

### High-Yield Facts & Terminological Anchors

- **BG 2.5:** _gurūn ahatvā ... mahānubhāvān_ — better to beg than to kill great souls who are teachers; gains won that way are _rudhira-pradigdhān_, tainted with blood.
- **Begging is scripturally permitted** to the _brahmacārī_, _brāhmaṇa_ and _sannyāsī_, not to a _kṣatriya_ — so Arjuna's proposal is itself blameworthy for him.
- **A guru may be given up** if he becomes proud and loses the discrimination of what should and should not be done — Bali Mahārāja and Śukrācārya is the example; otherwise a teacher on the right path is never abandoned.
- **Bhīṣma and Droṇa's external reason** for siding with Duryodhana was financial dependence — "a man is a servant of wealth"; the real reason appears in SB Canto 1, Chapter 9.
- **BG 2.6 is Arjuna's fifth reason — indecision**, completing the four given in Chapter One.
- **Qualifications of a candidate for knowledge**, drawn from the commentary on 2.6: control of mind and senses, detachment (_uparati_), tolerance (_titikṣā_) and faith (_śraddhā_) — all shown in Arjuna's own words.
- **BG 2.7 vocabulary:** _kārpaṇya-doṣa_ (fault of miserliness), _dharma-sammūḍha-cetāḥ_ (bewildered about duty), _niścitam brūhi_ (tell me decisively), _śiṣyas te 'ham_ (I am Your disciple), _prapannam_ (surrendered).
- **_Ācāryavān puruṣo veda_** — as _dhanavān_ means one who has wealth, _ācāryavān_ means one who has an _ācārya_; such a person knows the essence of scripture.
- **A devotee stops serving God as an advisor.** Advising the Lord and the spiritual master produces only frustration (we cannot change what time ordains) and confusion (the mind's advice keeps changing).
- **Questions are permitted; arguments are not** — Arjuna keeps asking questions through the rest of the Gītā.

### Core Analogies & Metaphors

- **A shower of flowers, not a shower of arrows** — what Arjuna feels is owed to Bhīṣma and Droṇa.
- **Spoils stained with blood** — the character of a kingdom won by killing one's elders.
- **Winning and still losing** — the shape of Arjuna's indecision in BG 2.6.
- **The doctor with two critical patients** — a choice in which every option invites loss and criticism.
- **Lighting a lamp and handing over instructions** — the devotee in the role of advisor.
- **The physician who wants to operate but does not know surgery** — why the guru's own qualification is specified.
- **_Dhanavān_, _rūpavān_, _ācāryavān_** — possession of wealth, of beauty, and of a teacher's mercy.
