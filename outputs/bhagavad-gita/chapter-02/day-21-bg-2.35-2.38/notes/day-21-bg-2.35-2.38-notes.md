## Class Snapshot & One-Line Argument Map

- **Class Snapshot:** The section on prescribed duty closes and a new one opens. BG 2.35 – 2.36 complete the account of what withdrawal would cost Arjuna socially; 2.37 concludes the argument by converting his apparent lose-lose into a win-win — death brings heaven, victory brings the earth; and 2.38 shifts the level again to selfless action, where equanimity in happiness and distress, gain and loss, victory and defeat means no sin at all. BG 2.39 announces the transition from analytical study to _buddhi-yoga_.
- **One-Line Argument Map:** The great generals will attribute Arjuna's withdrawal to fear, and his enemies will vilify his very competence -> so there is nothing more painful available to him than the course he is considering -> whereas fighting is a win-win: killed, he attains the higher planets; victorious, he enjoys the earth -> and if he fights with equanimity in happiness and distress, gain and loss, victory and defeat, no sinful reaction attaches at all.

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.35 [06:00 – 10:00]

<!-- verse:2.35 -->

**Context.** The infamy of the previous verse is made concrete: what the great generals in particular will conclude.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bhayād raṇād uparatam_ [06:00]: withdrawn from the battlefield out of fear.
- _Maṁsyante tvāṁ mahā-rathāḥ_ [06:00]: the great chariot-warriors will _think_ this of you.
- _Bahu-mataḥ_ [08:00]: one held in high regard in their estimation.
- _Yāsyasi lāghavam_ [08:00]: you will become light — insignificant — in their eyes.

#### Arjuna's counter, and the answer [08:00]

Arjuna's objection is natural: "I am not withdrawing out of fear; I am withdrawing out of compassion, and because I do not wish to incur sin." The Lord's answer is that his own reasoning is not what others will record. Duryodhana, Karṇa and the rest will not weigh the five reasons of Chapter One — those are studied by students of the Gītā, not by his opponents. They will simply say he fled in fear, and the man they had considered the foremost archer will be reckoned worthless.

#### A note on the level of the argument [08:00 – 10:00]

One might object that reputation is a material consideration. It is — and so, in a sense, is elevation to heaven. The Lord is deliberately addressing Arjuna at the material level here, within the section on regulated duty.

### Bhagavad-gita 2.36 [10:00 – 14:00]

<!-- verse:2.36 -->

**Context.** The vilification described in its own terms.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Avācya-vādān_ [10:00]: words that should not even be spoken — things one would not imagine being said.
- _Ahitāḥ_ [10:00]: those who wish you ill; the enemies.
- _Nindantas tava sāmarthyam_ [12:00]: vilifying your very capability.
- _Tato duḥkha-taraṁ nu kim_ [12:00]: what could be more painful than that?

#### Why success produces detractors [10:00 – 12:00]

- It is a general principle of this world: when a person rises or his fame spreads, those who envy him cannot tolerate it, and the moment a loophole appears they publicise it far beyond its actual substance.
- The more prominent the person, the more will be said — and in Arjuna's case it will be said in terms that could not be repeated.

#### Character and competence [12:00]

A person has two things that may be questioned: **character** and **competence**, and a question over either is painful. Here it is competence — _sāmarthya_ — that will be attacked. The Lord's conclusion: "you think that by refusing to fight you will escape distress; in fact you will have to bear a great deal of it, and for a long time."

#### The structure of 2.33 – 2.36 [12:00 – 14:00]

Across four verses the gravity is raised step by step: loss of prescribed duty → loss of reputation → sinful reaction → and then vilification of a kind that will not stop, in words that cannot be repeated.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's own explanation of his motives will not survive him: what will be remembered is that he left the field, and his competence — not his reasoning — is what his enemies will attack. [08:00, 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.37 [14:00 – 28:00]

<!-- verse:2.37 -->

**Context.** The conclusion of the entire section: get up and fight, with determination.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Hato vā prāpsyasi svargam_ [16:00]: being killed, you attain the heavenly planets.
- _Jitvā vā bhokṣyase mahīm_ [16:00]: conquering, you enjoy the earth.
- _Uttiṣṭha ... kṛta-niścayaḥ_ [16:00]: get up, with your decision made — spoken because Arjuna had sat down at the end of Chapter One.

#### Lose-lose turned into win-win [14:00 – 18:00]

- In BG 2.6 Arjuna had seen only losses: he did not know whether he should fight, nor who would win, and even victory would leave him unwilling to live. **What he saw before him was a lose-lose situation.**
- The Lord's answer reverses the perception completely: there are only two outcomes in a war, and both are gains — defeat, which for a _kṣatriya_ means death in battle, brings the higher planets; victory brings the kingdom of the earth.
- The teacher's summary: **Arjuna's perception is turned 180 degrees.** That is what taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa does to a situation.

#### The gold-miner [18:00 – 20:00]

A senior devotee's illustration, drawn from Prahlāda Mahārāja's instructions, is recounted:

- A gold miner recognises land that contains particles of gold, buys it, and digs. A great deal of earth comes out, and along with it the gold — which he extracts. The earth has no value; the gold has enormous value.
- Similarly the body by itself has no value: the body even of the most celebrated person cannot be sold for a rupee once life has left it, and cremation itself has to be paid for.
- But within this same body of blood, bone and flesh the Supersoul is also present. **The practitioner should be like the gold miner** — using this material body to attain the Lord. Without that, the body is a lose-lose proposition; with devotional practice, it becomes a win-win.

#### Who actually faces a lose-lose [20:00 – 24:00]

- **Materialists think spiritual life is the lose-lose option:** no enjoyment now, no parties, no adventure — and as for the spiritual side, who has seen God? "Neither māyā nor Rāma."
- The devotee's answer is that it is a win-win, because real satisfaction begins as soon as the practice begins, and the connection with Kṛṣṇa is there from the start.
- **A disciple's analysis** [20:00 – 22:00]: one of Śrīla Prabhupāda's disciples, before joining, deliberately worked out what he would lose and gain materially and spiritually — and concluded it was a win-win. Materially too, devotees live well: prasāda, festivals, outings, satisfying relationships; and spiritually there is far more to gain.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda's famous exchange** [22:00]: asked what he would do if at the end of life he discovered that Kṛṣṇa did not exist, he replied that even so they had lived a good life — chanting, festivals, prasāda — and then turned the question around: _but what if at the end of your life you find that Kṛṣṇa does exist and is the ultimate reality?_
- **The materialist ends where he did not intend** [22:00 – 24:00]: he strives for a win-win but, leaving Kṛṣṇa out, arrives at a lose-lose. Duryodhana is the example — refusing even a needle-point of land out of insecurity, arranging the gambling match, seeking benedictions to use against the Pāṇḍavas, and losing everything. The Pāṇḍavas did essentially one thing: **they always chose Kṛṣṇa** — as Arjuna did when offered the Lord Himself or His army.

#### Three ways of dealing with the Lord [24:00 – 26:00]

- **Demons try to cheat Kṛṣṇa** — and end up cheated themselves.
- **Demigods and mixed devotees negotiate with Kṛṣṇa** — all is well while the enjoyment lasts, and when a problem arises they approach Him to have it solved.
- **Devotees simply surrender**, because their understanding is that Kṛṣṇa knows best — Bali Mahārāja, Vibhīṣaṇa, and Sudāmā, who went to Kṛṣṇa with no calculation of what his handful of rice ought to fetch. Even Gajendra, who begins by praying for rescue, ends by simply surrendering.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The same battlefield that looked like a lose-lose to Arjuna is a win-win once Kṛṣṇa is factored in — and that reversal, rather than any new fact, is what the verse supplies. [16:00, 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.38 [28:00 – 40:00]

<!-- verse:2.38 -->

**Context.** A different kind of verse. The previous seven spoke at the level of regulated fruitive duty; this one speaks from selfless action.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sukha-duḥkhe same kṛtvā_ [28:00]: making happiness and distress equal.
- _Lābhālābhau jayājayau_ [28:00]: gain and loss, victory and defeat.
- _Yuddhāya yujyasva_ [28:00]: then engage in the fight.
- _Na evaṁ pāpam avāpsyasi_ [28:00]: in this way you will not incur sin.

#### The objection this verse answers [28:00 – 30:00]

Arjuna's remaining difficulty: granted that refusing would be sinful, fighting still involves killing a _brāhmaṇa_ who is also his teacher, and Bhīṣma besides — surely _that_ must carry sin. The answer is that it depends on the consciousness in which the act is done. Acting with personal desire brings reaction; acting without it does not.

- **The soldier at the border** [30:00]: a soldier who kills at the frontier incurs no sin; the same man killing at home does. The act is read by its level and its authority.

#### The purport's statement [30:00 – 32:00]

There is no consideration of happiness or distress, profit or loss, victory or defeat in activities performed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness; everything is to be performed for Kṛṣṇa's sake, in transcendental consciousness, and there is no reaction to such activity. Reaction accrues to one who acts for his own sense gratification.

#### The six debts [32:00 – 36:00]

The purport quotes the well-known verse on the debts that every living entity carries, which the teacher enumerates:

- **To the demigods**, from whom rain, sunlight and food come.
- **To the sages**, who gave the scriptures.
- **To other living entities**, who maintain the ecological balance.
- **To relatives and friends**, from whom much affection and cooperation is received.
- **To people and society at large** — schools, hospitals, public arrangements.
- **To the forefathers**, from whom a great deal is received.

Discharging even one of these fully is extremely difficult. But one who has taken complete shelter of Mukunda is no longer a debtor to anyone — the Lord then takes charge of the account.

**The Lord's logic to Arjuna, in the teacher's phrasing** [34:00]: _reaction attaches when you fight for yourself; fight for Me, and there is none._

#### The lotus leaf, and the millionaire [36:00 – 38:00]

- The Fifth Chapter's image is cited: as a lotus leaf in water is untouched by the water, one who works in this consciousness is untouched by reaction.
- **Why equanimity is a matter of consciousness** [36:00 – 38:00]: a very wealthy man who finds a fifty-rupee note on the road does not celebrate, and if the same note falls from his pocket he does not grieve — both are inconsequential to him. Put a man in his place who earns ten or twenty rupees a trip, and the same note is a festival when found and half a day's earnings when lost. The event has not changed; the position of the person has.

#### Why Śrīla Prabhupāda introduces devotion so early [38:00 – 40:00]

The teacher notes that Śrīla Prabhupāda brings Kṛṣṇa consciousness into the purports from the very beginning — what the Lord will eventually establish in _bhakti-yoga_ is stated throughout — because he did not want people to miss the essence. Not everyone will read to the end; some read only the introduction, some a few verses. Commentators have sometimes objected to this, but it is exactly why so many became devotees through his Gītā: they may not have followed every technicality, but they caught the essence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The same act carries reaction or does not, depending on the consciousness behind it — fight for yourself and it binds; fight without personal motive, for the Lord, and it does not. [30:00, 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.39 — the transition [40:00 – 42:00]

<!-- verse:2.39 -->

**Context.** The Lord announces the change of subject himself.

- _Eṣā te abhihitā sāṅkhye_ — what has been described so far was given through analytical study.
- _Buddhir yoge tv imāṁ śṛṇu_ — now hear the same in terms of working without fruitive results.
- _Karma-bandhaṁ prahāsyasi_ — acting in this understanding, you will be released from the bondage of works.
- **_Buddhi-yoga_** is glossed by Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura and by Śrīla Prabhupāda as **_bhakti-yoga_** — the Lord will now give the understanding by which Arjuna can work in devotion, and so incur no bondage.
- The remaining sections will therefore treat the practical question: how to implement this intelligence, how to live and work in this world, and how to avoid reaction while acting — before turning to the characteristics of the _sthita-prajña_.

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications [44:00 – 58:00]

**Q1 — Bhīṣma and others had taken vows. Did they fight in a selfless mood, or from those vows?** [44:00 – 46:00]

- First, note what is happening in this section: the Lord is arguing from one level after another — from analytical knowledge, then from regulated duty, and now from selfless action — and every one of them concludes that Arjuna should fight.
- As for Bhīṣma, such devotees are **beyond** selfless action as a method. In _niṣkāma-karma-yoga_ the practitioner is still aiming at knowledge and, through it, at liberation; a devotee of that standing is an instrument in the Lord's hands and acts to please Him.
- **The scale the teacher gives:** regulated fruitive work is like one rupee, selfless action like a hundred, the paths of knowledge and mysticism higher still, and devotional service like a crore — it contains everything.
- **Externally similar, internally different:** alum and sugar candy look identical from outside; their qualities are entirely different. A vow taken by such a personality is not the vow of a film hero.

**Q2 — If one takes full shelter of the Lord, are the six debts simply cancelled?** [46:00 – 50:00]

- Partly as you say. One who takes shelter naturally develops gratitude — the Bhāgavatam states that all good qualities manifest in a devotee, and gratitude is the foundation of those qualities. Service and thankfulness become natural to him rather than obligatory.
- At the same time, these injunctions exist in scripture precisely so that people do not fall off the path of _dharma_: the conditioned soul's tendency is self-interest, which leads to sin, whereas service delivers him. Serving those to whom one is indebted also brings their blessings, which in turn help one's practice — so the two are complementary.
- **But the foundation is _sanātana-dharma_.** Serving family members who are themselves atheistic will certainly bring pious credit, but it will not accomplish everything; serving the Lord accomplishes everything. Knowing that, the devotee discharges his duties to all with gratitude, without compromising devotional service.

**Q3 — Arjuna had the Lord personally beside him. What do we have?** [50:00 – 54:00]

- The threefold shelter remains: **scripture**, which is eternal and most reliable; **the sādhu**, who is relatively accessible; and **the spiritual master**.
- **The principle to remember:** for anyone sincerely engaged in devotional service, _nothing whatever is lacking except sincerity._ Śrīla Prabhupāda's books are available; his disciples' and other teachers' lectures are available everywhere; the holy name is non-different from Kṛṣṇa, and the Bhāgavatam likewise.
- **Śrī Īśopaniṣad's invocation** is cited: the material world too is complete — in the sense that there is a complete arrangement here for going back to Godhead. Kṛṣṇa is accessible to us as the holy name and as the Bhāgavatam; the question is how much shelter we take.
- **And it will not be easy** — it was not easy for Arjuna, standing beside the Lord and still bewildered, nor for the Pāṇḍavas. One should not expect everything in devotional life to run smoothly; but with sincere effort the Lord's reciprocation is seen regularly.

**Q4 — Arjuna's bewilderment about his relatives was removed directly by the Lord. How is ours removed?** [54:00 – 56:00]

That is precisely why the Gītā exists — the conversation between the Lord and Arjuna is still available. The Padma Purāṇa's glorification of the Gītā states that the Gītā is non-different from the Lord's own body, so He remains accessible. To the degree that shelter is taken, and gradually, the same clearing takes place; Arjuna also needed time.

**Q5 — If one attains self-knowledge through selfless action, does one not end in the impersonal effulgence?** [56:00 – 58:00]

Each path has its own means and its own end — the path of knowledge aims at liberation and attains that effulgence. The full comparison of the means and ends of the different paths was deferred to the next session.

**Q6 — Does selfless action mean offering the result of one's work to the Lord?** [58:00]

Yes. Practically: one keeps what is required for maintenance — rent, prasāda, electricity and so on — and the remainder is offered in the Lord's service.

### High-Yield Facts & Terminological Anchors

- **BG 2.35:** _bhayād raṇād uparatam_ — the generals will attribute the withdrawal to fear; the man who was _bahu-mataḥ_ becomes _lāghava_.
- **BG 2.36:** _avācya-vādān_ — words that should not be spoken; and it is _sāmarthya_, competence, that will be attacked. **A person has two things open to question: character and competence.**
- **2.33 – 2.36 escalate in four steps:** loss of duty → loss of fame → sin → lasting vilification.
- **BG 2.37 is the conclusion:** killed → the higher planets; victorious → the earth; therefore rise with determination.
- **Lose-lose turned into win-win** — the perception is reversed 180 degrees when Kṛṣṇa is included.
- **The gold miner** — the body has no value in itself, but the Supersoul is within it; use it to attain the Lord.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda's reply:** "we had a good life anyway — but what if, at the end of your life, you find He does exist?"
- **Three modes of dealing with the Lord:** demons cheat (and are cheated), demigods negotiate, devotees surrender.
- **BG 2.38 changes the level** to selfless action: reaction attaches to work done for one's own sense gratification, not to work done without personal motive.
- **The six debts** — demigods, sages, other living entities, relatives, society, forefathers — from which one who has taken shelter of Mukunda is released.
- **The lotus leaf in water; the millionaire and the fifty-rupee note** — equanimity is a function of one's position, not of the event.
- **BG 2.39:** from _sāṅkhya_ to _buddhi-yoga_, which the commentators gloss as _bhakti-yoga_ — acting in this understanding, one is released from the bondage of works.
- **"Nothing is lacking for a sincere devotee except sincerity."**

### Core Analogies & Metaphors

- **The gold miner and the worthless earth** — the body's value, and how to extract it.
- **"Neither māyā nor Rāma"** — the materialist's picture of spiritual life.
- **Duryodhana's needle-point of land** — striving for win-win and arriving at lose-lose.
- **Arjuna choosing Kṛṣṇa over Kṛṣṇa's army** — the one thing the Pāṇḍavas consistently did.
- **The soldier at the border and the same man at home** — why the level of an act decides its reaction.
- **The lotus leaf in water** — working without being touched by reaction.
- **The millionaire and the rickshaw-puller with the same note** — consciousness, not the event, decides the impact.
- **Alum and sugar candy** — identical in appearance, entirely different in quality.
