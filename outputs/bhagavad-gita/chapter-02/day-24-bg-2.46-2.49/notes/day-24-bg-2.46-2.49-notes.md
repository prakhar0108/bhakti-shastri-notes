## Class Snapshot & One-Line Argument Map

- **Class Snapshot:** The famous section on selfless action. BG 2.46 gives the analogy of the well and the great reservoir — know the essence of the Vedas and all their purposes are served; 2.47, the most quoted and most misunderstood verse in the Gītā, fixes the right to action and removes the claim on results; 2.48 defines _yoga_ as equanimity in success and failure; and 2.49 dismisses fruitive action as abominable and calls those who work for results misers.
- **One-Line Argument Map:** Whoever knows the essence has already obtained everything the Vedas offer, as a reservoir already serves every purpose of many separate wells -> so work, because the right to work is yours, but the result is not, nor are you its cause, and inaction is no escape either -> perform that work established in _yoga_, equipoised in success and failure, for equanimity _is_ yoga -> and therefore keep fruitive activity at a distance and take shelter of that consciousness, since those who work for results are misers.

The previous verse told Arjuna to transcend the three modes that the fruitive section of the Vedas deals with, to be free of dualities, established in pure existence, without anxiety for gain and protection. Today's verses continue that same instruction and show what work then looks like.

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.46 [04:00 – 22:00]

<!-- verse:2.46 -->

**Context.** The well and the reservoir — and the two great commentaries on this verse.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yāvān … tāvān_ [04:00]: a correlative pair — "as much as … so much."
- _Uda-pāna_ [04:00]: a well.
- _Sampluta-udaka_ [04:00]: a large body of water — a lake or a river, entirely filled.
- _Brāhmaṇasya vijānataḥ_ [06:00]: of one who knows the purpose behind the Vedas.

#### The analogy itself [06:00 – 08:00]

- In villages there were separate wells for separate purposes — one for drinking, one for washing clothes, one for bathing. Whatever purpose each of those separate wells serves, a single river serves all of them at once: a man bathes there, washes his clothes there, draws water there, washes his grain there.
- In the same way, the Vedas contain an enormous amount; but one who has grasped their essence has grasped the whole.

#### Two commentaries on this verse [08:00 – 14:00]

**1. Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's reading, which Śrīla Prabhupāda follows** [08:00 – 10:00]

- The essence is: **do devotional service and everything is accomplished.**
- The failure being corrected: a person thinks devotion will cover the spiritual side, but health needs a separate practice, intelligence a separate prayer, and business success yet another observance on the appropriate day. The fruitive section is exactly like those many separate wells — one rite for a son, another for a spouse, another for intelligence, another for wealth, each with its own stated result and its own stated duration.
- The Bhāgavatam verse is cited that whether one is free of desire, full of desire, or desiring liberation, one should worship the Supreme Person with intense devotion.
- **The conviction a devotee needs:** if we are sincere in the practice of spiritual life, everything else falls into place.
- **What this does not mean** [10:00]: that one need not study, prepare, or pay the electricity bill. Material duties continue; what changes is that devotion is known to be the main thing, done with one-pointed focus. From that a consciousness is formed, and from that consciousness everything else is done — and done better than it otherwise would be.
- **The real problem is not the principle but sincerity** [10:00].

**2. Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's reading** [12:00 – 14:00]

- From a well one draws exactly as much water as one needs. Coming to a great river, one still draws exactly as much as one needs — one does not carry away more simply because more is there.
- Similarly, when studying the Vedas, **take only what serves the purpose** — devotional service — and leave the rest. Otherwise one gets stuck in the vast fruitive material, with its endless promises: sleep on the floor and this follows, give up salt and that follows, eat once a day and this follows.

#### Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport [14:00 – 22:00]

- **BG 15.15 is quoted** — by all the Vedas, He is to be known. That is the purpose; and the principle of watering the root of a tree so that trunk, branches and twigs are all nourished applies here.
- **On difficulty in the beginning** [14:00 – 16:00]: early difficulties are usually created by practising wrongly; later difficulties are simply part of the purification. The medical analogy: a physician warns that after the medicine the fever will first rise for two or three days and then stabilise — a patient who knows this is _pleased_ when the fever rises, because the medicine is working. Devotion therefore requires patience, since circumstances in life keep changing.
- **The essence, stated four times in one purport** [16:00 – 22:00]: the purport cites the verse in praise of one on whose tongue the holy name is present — such a person has already performed every austerity, every sacrifice, bathed at every holy place and studied all the Vedas. The teacher's comment: this is the verse to return to whenever any doubt about the holy name arises. Against this, the mystic powers advertised in popular videos are external and do not touch the unwanted things in the heart.
- The purport continues that it is not possible for a common man in this age to follow all the Vedic rules and rituals — not even to read them, let alone understand or apply them — and that **the best purpose of Vedic culture is served by chanting the holy name of the Lord, as recommended by Lord Caitanya to deliver all fallen souls.**
- The closing lines: the best purpose of Vedānta philosophy is served by offencelessly chanting the holy name; Vedānta is the last word of Vedic wisdom, its author and knower is Lord Kṛṣṇa, and the highest Vedāntist is the great soul who takes pleasure in chanting the holy name — **that is the purpose of all Vedic mysticism.**
- **The teacher's application** [20:00]: one may not remember many verses or understand much, but this much must be understood — the holy name is to be taken with great faith, because in this age there is no other essence. A man may know the Upaniṣads, give classes and deliver lectures, and if he is not chanting, nothing is happening to him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The many wells are not wrong — they are simply unnecessary once one has the river. Knowing the essence is not a shortcut around the Vedas; it is what the Vedas were for. [06:00, 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.47 [22:00 – 42:00]

<!-- verse:2.47 -->

**Context.** The most quoted verse of the Gītā, and, as the teacher says, the most misunderstood — each of its four lines carrying a distinct instruction.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karmaṇy eva adhikāraḥ te_ [24:00]: your right is in the work itself, and only there.
- _Mā phaleṣu kadācana_ [24:00]: never in the fruits.
- _Mā karma-phala-hetuḥ bhūḥ_ [24:00]: do not consider yourself the cause of the result.
- _Mā te saṅgaḥ astu akarmaṇi_ [24:00]: and do not become attached to inaction.

#### The checkmate [24:00 – 26:00]

The verse closes every exit: you have a right to the work; you have no right to the result; you may not claim to be the cause; and you may not walk away. In this world most people assume total ownership of results — and the teacher's observation is that people claim credit even for what they did not do, since in any organisation, when something goes well, many hands go up.

#### Why we have no right over the result [26:00 – 28:00]

- The result depends on many factors — the five factors of action given later in the Eighteenth Chapter.
- **The examination analogy:** you can write the best paper you are capable of; but the examiner's state of mind on the day he marks it will be reflected in what he awards. Control is simply not total.
- **This is not a licence for mindlessness.** One must work, apply intelligence, and work to the best of one's capacity. What is denied is control, not effort.

#### Work itself as the reward [26:00 – 28:00]

- **Perfect work is itself a reward.** The student who studies well obtains marks as an external result, but obtains _learning_ as an internal result — and the internal result is the one that never fails.
- On the material platform both success and failure are problematic; on the spiritual platform there is only success, because the material result was never fully in our hands.

#### Quality of consciousness [28:00 – 30:00]

- In devotional service the quality of consciousness has the decisive role. During the book distribution season, one devotee with less skill distributes fewer books but tries regularly and honestly; another distributes more by cutting corners and his name appears higher on the list. **Whom is Kṛṣṇa more pleased with, and who actually advances?** The Lord looks at the quality of consciousness.
- **The arithmetic of worry** [30:00]: a student who spends his study time worrying about the result — opening the book and drifting into dreams of admission — loses thirty or forty per cent of his energy to the worry, prepares with the remaining sixty, and naturally the result suffers. So the verse is not saying results will not come; **it is saying that working this way produces a better result,** because a hundred per cent of the energy goes where the right actually lies.

#### Examples from the Rāmāyaṇa [30:00 – 32:00]

- **Jaṭāyu fought Rāvaṇa and lost** — and yet he succeeded. He gave his best in the right consciousness and pleased the Lord.
- **Hanumān went to Laṅkā and succeeded.**
- **The squirrel did something very small** — and pleased Him too.
- The point: give your best according to your own capacity, and understand that the right lies in the consciousness, not in the result.

#### The peace that comes from giving up attachment to results [32:00 – 34:00]

- The devotee who plans a large festival or a distribution target, gives his best, and falls far short remains peaceful within — even when others follow up with advice about how the planning should have been more practical, or fail to appreciate it at all.
- **That is the litmus test:** the disturbance felt after performing service is a sign of internal instability rooted in attachment to an external cause.
- **Success disturbs too**, where the consciousness is wrong — because success invites comparison. The businessman delighted with a certain profit shares it with a friend who casually reports a figure ten times larger, and the joy converts into grief; he mourns what he had been celebrating.

#### The fourth line: do not become attached to inaction [34:00 – 36:00]

Do not become lazy in the name of devotion. "The Lord will see to it", "it will happen when the Lord wishes", "service happens only through whomever He chooses" — these are the slogans of the idle. The same person descends fully into the field for material life and reaches for these phrases the moment spiritual life is mentioned. Advancement requires that the senses, the body and one's resources actually be engaged.

#### The purport: categories of work [36:00 – 38:00]

- **Karma** — prescribed work; **vikarma** — work contrary to the scriptures; **akarma** — work that produces no reaction.
- **Nitya-karma** — regular duties: what maintains the body, and what the scriptures prescribe for one's social order. **No reaction attaches to these.**
- **Naimittika-karma** — occasional or emergency observances.
- **Kāmya-karma** — work performed from a specific desire: for wealth, for property, for a son. The very word carries desire in it.
- The purport's statement: fruitive work is a cause of bondage and is therefore inauspicious. One should not work in this world for material results.

#### Line by line, as a practical framework [38:00 – 42:00]

- **Line 1 — the area of influence.** Put maximum effort exactly here. Am _I_ doing my duty properly? — rather than what others, or the government, are failing to do.
- **And perfection is not the requirement:** one may not be able to sing sweetly, but one can sing prayerfully; one may not be able to cook many varieties or very tastily, but one can cook with devotion; one may not be able to dance artistically, but one can dance enthusiastically. **What pleases the Lord is what lies within our domain.**
- **Line 2 — the area of concern.** Most people spend their energy where they have no control at all: the weather, the employer, superiors, children, the spouse. That is where frustration is manufactured.
- **Line 3 — humility.** "I did it, I did it" — when things go wrong the same person does not say it. It happened through you by the Lord's mercy. This line keeps a person humble in success and helps him go deeper.
- **Line 4 — avoid the opposite extreme.** Not "then I shall do nothing at all." Do your work.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The four lines are one instruction in four directions: maximum effort where there is control, no energy where there is none, no credit where there is a claim, and no retreat where there is a duty. [38:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.48 [42:00 – 48:00]

<!-- verse:2.48 -->

**Context.** The Lord's own definition of _yoga_.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yoga-sthaḥ kuru karmāṇi_ [42:00]: perform your work while established in _yoga_ — here devotional service, and technically also selfless action.
- _Saṅgaṁ tyaktvā_ [42:00]: having given up the desire for the fruit.
- _Siddhy-asiddhyoḥ samo bhūtvā_ [42:00]: becoming equipoised in success and failure.
- _Samatvaṁ yoga ucyate_ [44:00]: **equanimity is called yoga** — one of the several definitions the Lord gives in the Gītā.

#### What the equanimity consists of [44:00]

I have my duty to do — in happiness or distress, honour or dishonour, gain or loss, cold or heat. One deeply attached to results cannot possibly be equipoised.

#### The purport's definitions [44:00]

- _Yoga_ means to concentrate the mind upon the Supreme by controlling the ever-disturbing senses.
- _Yoga_ also means union — the living being connecting with the Lord.
- **And the Supreme is Kṛṣṇa Himself,** who is telling Arjuna to fight and to leave aside victory and defeat: **following Kṛṣṇa's dictation is real yoga**, and that is possible only in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Hence duty is to be performed in the mood of the servant of the servant.

#### If not for success, then for what? [46:00 – 48:00]

- The obvious objection: if not for success, what is one working for? The teacher's answer goes deeper — **nobody actually works for success.** Success is wanted because one believes that being successful will bring love; what is really being sought is an exchange of love.
- Three reasons working for external success fails:
  1. **Sometimes the success simply does not come.**
  2. **Sometimes it comes and the expected response does not.** The account is given of a devotee who had achieved a record-breaking examination result and was appreciated by many — and then a friend came, said nothing about it at all, and that single silence was what affected him, outweighing ten people's praise.
  3. **And when the response does come, the love is for the success, not for the person.** A man becomes wealthy and gains many friends; the business declines and the calls stop being answered.
- Working for external success therefore leaves a person permanently insecure.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Equanimity is not indifference to outcomes but freedom from needing them — because what the heart was actually seeking through success was never available from success. [44:00, 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.49 [48:00 – 58:00]

<!-- verse:2.49 -->

**Context.** The verse that links the Second Chapter to the Third.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Avaraṁ karma_ [50:00]: abominable work — here, fruitive action performed out of attachment to its result, and the ritual activity prescribed for such ends.
- _Dūreṇa_ [50:00]: keep it far off, at a distance.
- _Buddhi-yoga_ [50:00]: work joined to right intelligence — selfless action; and, the teacher notes, the shelter being spoken of is this consciousness, not any personality of that name.
- _Buddhau śaraṇam anviccha_ [50:00]: take full shelter in that consciousness.
- _Kṛpaṇāḥ phala-hetavaḥ_ [50:00]: those who work for results are misers.

#### Why the ordinary way of working is condemned [50:00 – 52:00]

The whole world works precisely for results — who works without one? The teacher cites a senior devotee's written observation: **what we consider normal in this material world is, in spiritual terms, actually insane.** Much passes for acceptable; by the standard of the scriptures it is condemned. This is not the way a life is to be spent.

#### Two meanings of "miser" [52:00 – 56:00]

**1. Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's reading** [52:00 – 54:00]

- A miser is one who has great wealth and gives nothing away. His commentary, read out in class: misers, greedy for some inexperienced morsel of happiness from their hoarded wealth, cannot give up that wealth and are cheated of the great happiness of giving.
- **Why that is a loss and not a gain:** our capacity to enjoy is tiny. How many pieces of bread can one person eat — three, four? But if one has the means to feed four hundred, a hundred people eat, and their blessings come with it. There is no comparison between the two happinesses. The miser had the opportunity to acquire that much merit, and died with the wealth instead — losing the wealth anyway and losing the merit as well.

**2. The reading for devotional life** [54:00 – 56:00]

- **A miser is one who employs the human body only for material enjoyment.** Why a miser? Because the human form is an immense asset: engaged in devotion, this material body can obtain the spiritual world and the love of God. To spend it on acquiring small things is to waste an enormous fortune.
- The purport's statement is quoted: one who understands that he is the eternal servant of the Lord gives up all engagements except working in Kṛṣṇa consciousness; **except for work in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all activities are abominable, because they continually bind the worker to the cycle of birth and death.** Everything should be done in Kṛṣṇa consciousness for the satisfaction of Kṛṣṇa. And the closing line of the purport, which the teacher points out as the practical application: **like misers, unfortunate people do not employ their human energy in the service of the Lord.**

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications [58:00 – 66:00]

**Q1 — When difficulties arise in devotional life, how do we tell whether it is the process working (the fever rising from the medicine) or our own mistake?** [58:00 – 60:00]

- **The test is faith.** If we are sincere, there will be difficulty, but the conviction will not fall away. In devotion, faith is the currency.
- Even a sincere devotee may have struggles — as the Pāṇḍavas did — but **the Lord protects his faith,** and he does not give up his devotional activities.
- **Two kinds of suffering:** what comes because of past activity or our own fault feels one way; what the Lord gives a devotee in order to purify him feels another. In the second, faith remains intact — he does not question or blame the Lord, and he becomes more serious.
- **The illustration:** trouble comes into two lives. One man courier-returns his beads. The other increases his chanting. The difference is that in the second case it is the Lord's arrangement — He saw that something was loose and tightened it, and the man immediately grips the beads properly, calling out like a child calls his mother, where earlier the practice was half-asleep.

**Q2 — The purport speaks of prescribed duties according to the acquired mode of nature; today everyone's mode differs, yet the same thing is taught to all. How is this to be understood?** [60:00 – 64:00]

- The classical correspondence: the priestly class generally in goodness; the administrative class predominantly in passion with goodness; the mercantile class predominantly in passion with some ignorance; the labouring class generally in ignorance. Different duties were prescribed accordingly — **prescribed duty according to the mode of nature.**
- In this age the social divisions are disordered, so we cannot apply it in that way; **we apply it by stage of life.** A student's prescribed duty is study; an employed person's is the work of his job; a married person's is the responsibility of his family. The Gītā states it briefly; the details belong to a separate subject.
- **And the word "prescribed" carries a caution** [62:00]: one cannot justify a sixteen-hour job under that heading. Something prescribed requires a prescription — someone must certify how much. Hence even our duties should be discharged under some guidance, or there is every possibility of drifting under their cover.

**Q3 — Why is there a difference in the effort we make for ourselves and the effort we make for the Lord?** [64:00 – 66:00]

- **Because there is no sense of belonging towards the Lord,** while there is a great deal towards the body. Hungry at midnight, a person will order food at once; returning from travel, he will not cook at midnight for the Deity at home.
- **The living being puts in effort wherever there is that sense of belonging.** For the conditioned soul, attraction to the Lord is essentially zero; the whole practice of chanting and hearing is the process for awakening that attraction, since attachment is already fully present towards the world — something happens to a child or a grandchild and the man becomes entirely active.
- **Therefore duties are to be discharged as duty,** which is why the scriptures repeatedly warn against laziness in spiritual duties: left to the mind, a person will do everything for himself with full energy and the minimum for the Lord. **That is precisely why there are regulations** — the system of worship specifies when offerings are to be made and when the ceremonies are to be performed.

## Last-Page Revision Sheet

### High-Yield Facts & Terminological Anchors

- **BG 2.46 — well and reservoir:** whatever many separate wells accomplish, one great body of water accomplishes entirely.
- **Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura / Śrīla Prabhupāda's reading:** do devotional service and everything is accomplished. **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's reading:** from the Vedas take only as much as serves the purpose.
- **BG 15.15** — by all the Vedas, He is to be known; and the root of the tree waters the whole tree.
- **The purport of 2.46 states the essence three or four times:** the best purpose of Vedic culture, and of Vedānta philosophy, is served by offencelessly chanting the holy name; **the highest Vedāntist is the one who takes pleasure in chanting.**
- **BG 2.47 — the four lines:** (1) the right is in the work — _area of influence_; (2) never in the fruits — _area of concern_; (3) not the cause — _humility_; (4) not attached to inaction — _no laziness_.
- **Work itself is the reward:** marks are the external result, learning the internal one. Externals disturb; the internal stabilises.
- **Kṛṣṇa sees the quality of consciousness,** not the figure on the list.
- **Jaṭāyu failed but succeeded; Hanumān succeeded; the squirrel did something tiny** — all three pleased the Lord.
- **Worry over results consumes the energy meant for the work** — which is why detachment produces better results, not worse.
- **Karma / vikarma / akarma;** and **nitya- / naimittika- / kāmya-karma**, of which only prescribed regular duty carries no reaction.
- **BG 2.48 — _samatvaṁ yoga ucyate_, equanimity is called yoga.** Yoga also means concentrating the mind on the Supreme, and union; and **following Kṛṣṇa's dictation is real yoga.**
- **Nobody works for success; they work for the love they believe success will bring** — which is why success is an unreliable goal on three counts.
- **BG 2.49 — _avaraṁ karma_** is fruitive work; take shelter of _buddhi-yoga_; **those who work for results are misers.**
- **Two senses of "miser":** one who hoards wealth and is cheated of the happiness of giving; and one who spends the human form on material enjoyment alone.
- **The purport's line:** except for work in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all activities are abominable, because they bind the worker to birth and death.

### Core Analogies & Metaphors

- **Separate village wells and one river** — many rites versus the essence.
- **Drawing only what one needs, even at a river** — how to read the Vedas.
- **The fever that rises before it falls** — difficulty as part of the treatment.
- **The examiner in a bad mood** — why the result was never fully ours.
- **The two book distributors** — quantity versus quality of consciousness.
- **Jaṭāyu, Hanumān and the squirrel** — capacity differs, consciousness decides.
- **The businessman whose profit turned into grief on comparison** — why success also disturbs.
- **Singing prayerfully, cooking devotionally, dancing enthusiastically** — the area of influence.
- **The friend who said nothing about the record result** — the insufficiency of external success.
- **Four pieces of bread eaten versus four hundred given** — the miser's arithmetic.
- **The two devotees in trouble: one returns his beads, the other increases his chanting** — distinguishing reaction from the Lord's arrangement.
- **Ordering food at midnight for oneself but not cooking for the Deity** — where the sense of belonging lies.
