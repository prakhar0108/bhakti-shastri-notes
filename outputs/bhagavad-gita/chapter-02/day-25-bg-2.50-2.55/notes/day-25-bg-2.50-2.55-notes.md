## Class Snapshot & One-Line Argument Map

- **Class Snapshot:** The section on selfless action closes and the final section of the chapter opens. BG 2.50 gives the Gītā's most famous — and most misapplied — definition of _yoga_ as the art of all work; 2.51 states the result; 2.52 – 2.53 describe the perfected stage, where the fruitive promises no longer move a person; and then Arjuna asks his four questions in 2.54, opening the description of the _sthita-prajña_ which runs to the end of the chapter, with 2.55 giving the first answer.
- **One-Line Argument Map:** Work done in this consciousness frees a person from both good and bad reactions, and _that_ is the skill in work -> the intelligent, giving up the fruit, are freed from the bondage of birth and death and reach the place beyond all miseries -> when the intelligence has crossed the dense forest of delusion, all that has been heard and all that is to be heard becomes a matter of indifference -> and when it stands unmoved by the fruitive promises of revelation, fixed in trance, that is _yoga_ attained -> at which point Arjuna asks: what are the symptoms of such a person, how does he speak, how does he sit, how does he walk?

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.50 [02:00 – 10:00]

<!-- verse:2.50 -->

**Context.** A further definition of _yoga_ — and a widely misread one.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Buddhi-yuktaḥ_ [04:00]: one engaged in devotional service, or in selfless action; in Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport, devotional service.
- _Jahāti_ [04:00]: casts off, becomes free of.
- _Sukṛta_ [04:00]: the reaction formed by pious activity.
- _Duṣkṛta_ [04:00]: the reaction formed by sinful activity.
- _Yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam_ [06:00]: yoga is skill in works.

#### Why even pious reaction is a bondage [04:00 – 10:00]

- A pious reaction is materially positive — but one has to come back to experience it. Seen from the whole, that is not positive at all, since the cycle of birth and death remains intact.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda's image:** handcuffs of gold and handcuffs of iron are both handcuffs. Whether merit or sin, one has to return for the reaction; both bind.
- The purport's opening: since time immemorial every living entity has accumulated the various reactions of its work; surrendering to the Lord and acting as His servant is the way out of the entanglement.

#### What "skill in work" actually means [06:00 – 08:00]

- **The common misreading:** that the verse praises expertise — how well a person plays a sport, how technically skilled he is. It has been adopted as an institutional motto in that sense. **That is not the meaning here.**
- **The skill is this:** to act in such a way that one does not become bound. "Catch the fish, but don't get wet."
- Whatever a person is doing — a student studying, an employee working, a businessman in business, a householder managing his home — the skill is to arrange it so that it does not produce bondage, and so that Kṛṣṇa consciousness increases and remembrance of the Lord continues. **Otherwise everything in this world binds.**

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Skill in work is not doing work well; it is doing work in such a way that the work does not do anything to you. [06:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.51 [10:00 – 18:00]

<!-- verse:2.51 -->

**Context.** The result of working that way.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma-jaṁ phalaṁ tyaktvā_ [10:00 – 12:00]: giving up attachment to the fruit born of work.
- _Manīṣiṇaḥ_ [12:00]: the intelligent — great sages or devotees.
- _Janma-bandha-vinirmuktāḥ_ [12:00]: specifically freed from the bondage of birth and death.
- _Anāmayam padam_ [12:00]: the place without disease — _āmaya_ meaning disease; the place where there is no anxiety at all.

#### The reversal of the ordinary expectation [12:00 – 14:00]

- A person assumes that the more he is attached to the result and the more he broods on it, the more he will gain. In fact he loses in the long term — the external result also suffers, and the internal result never appears at all.
- Following the principle the Lord gives, the external result comes out better _and_ the internal result is beyond comparison: **the place free of all miseries.**
- Whatever is acquired here through attachment will be left behind anyway, and much of it cannot even be enjoyed, since happiness and distress follow past activity regardless.

#### The verse quoted in the purport [14:00 – 16:00]

The purport quotes the well-known verse on taking full shelter of the Lord's lotus feet — that this material world is a place of danger at every step, and that for one who has taken that shelter the ocean of material existence becomes as small as the water held in the hoof-print of a calf, and he attains the supreme abode where there is no distress. **The Lord's lotus feet are the boat; the world is the ocean.**

#### The purport's sharpest line [16:00 – 18:00]

- **Out of ignorance, less intelligent people try to adjust the situation by fruitive activity,** thinking the resultant actions will make them happy — not knowing that no kind of material body anywhere within the universe can give life without miseries.
- The teacher's application: the endless adjustments we imagine — a slightly better job, the temporary post made permanent, the visa, the house finally built, the posting near home — "then everything will be set." **Happiness cannot be obtained by adjustment, here or anywhere in the material universe.**
- **But** one who understands his real position as the eternal servant of the Lord and engages in His loving service becomes qualified to enter the spiritual world.

### Bhagavad-gita 2.52 [18:00 – 30:00]

<!-- verse:2.52 -->

**Context.** The stage at which the promises stop working on a person.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Moha-kalilam_ [18:00]: the dense forest of illusion.
- _Vyatitariṣyati_ [18:00]: when the intelligence crosses beyond it.
- _Nirvedam_ [18:00]: indifference, detachment.
- _Śrotavyasya, śrutasya_ [18:00 – 20:00]: towards all that is yet to be heard and all that has already been heard.

#### What exactly is heard and to be heard [20:00 – 22:00]

- Literally, the fruitive section — the promises that doing this yields health, that doing that increases the prosperity of the house, that giving in charity here benefits ten generations or returns manifold in the next life.
- **In plainer terms:** the entire body of proposals about how one will become happy in this world. Every advertisement of happiness — a car, a house, health, a marriage, a job, a settlement in a good locality, and then the next thing after that. The teacher's phrase: **the false promises of happiness in the material world** — much of which we have already heard, and much more of which we shall go on hearing.
- **At that stage one sees it is all a drama.**

#### Why the fruitive route looks so attractive before that [22:00 – 24:00]

The teacher recalls his own earlier practice of several devotional hymns for material ends — a hymn for four months and a promised transformation; oil offered on seven consecutive appointed days under a tree for a business problem. **It appears very exciting** — half an hour of recitation against years of hard study; a small expense against something complex. That is precisely its appeal.

#### What changes after one becomes a devotee [22:00 – 26:00]

- The purport: when a person understands Kṛṣṇa and his relationship with Him, **he naturally becomes completely indifferent to ritualistic activities.**
- Devotees do not take shelter of those things on a gross level, because there is clarity: Kṛṣṇa is the master, we are His servants, the demigods are also His servants and are to be respected — **but shelter is taken of Kṛṣṇa alone.** Duties and dealings are to be discharged well, with the purpose always in view.
- **Why:** fruitive religion is essentially a business — a transaction, a reciprocation, like withdrawing money from a machine. Devotional service is not a transaction.
- **The example of Mādhavendra Purī** [24:00 – 26:00], quoted in the purport: he addresses the daily rites of worship and bathing, offers them respects, and asks them to let him go — being on a very high stage of devotion. **The teacher's caution: that stage is not ours.** We cannot give up the regulations connected with devotion — rising early, bathing, attending the morning worship. Śrīla Prabhupāda's reply to a devotee who explained his lateness to the morning programme by a dream of Kṛṣṇa is recounted to this effect: **that was not Kṛṣṇa; come to the morning programme on time.**

#### Where the exception does apply [26:00 – 30:00]

- One in a very advanced state, in whom love for Kṛṣṇa has awakened, is not bound by the rules — and yet great personalities still follow them in order to set an example.
- **Akrūra** approaching Vṛndāvana from Mathurā, so overwhelmed on seeing the Lord's footprints that he rolled on the ground — the intensity of remembrance was that acute.
- **The elderly woman at Jagannātha Purī** during Lord Caitanya's presence, who in her eagerness for darśana put one foot on Garuḍa and the other on Lord Caitanya's shoulder, and whom Lord Caitanya defended when she was rebuked, wishing He had that much eagerness Himself.
- Hence one absorbed in remembrance who forgets to offer obeisances on entering the temple is not to be judged.
- **And the same principle at the institutional level** [30:00]: at the opening of a temple where the traditional sacrificial rites were being performed, Śrīla Prabhupāda told the devotees to keep the congregational chanting going — **the main sacrifice is the chanting** — while still having the other ceremony performed for the sake of social convention.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Indifference to the fruitive promises is a symptom of having crossed the forest, not a technique for crossing it — which is why the regulations of devotional practice remain binding on us. [18:00, 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.53 [30:00 – 40:00]

<!-- verse:2.53 -->

**Context.** The definition of the perfected stage.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śruti-vipratipannā_ [30:00]: not influenced by the fruitive results held out in revealed scripture.
- _Niścalā, acalā buddhiḥ_ [32:00]: intelligence become unmoved and unflinching.
- _Samādhau_ [32:00]: fixed in trance.
- _Tadā yogam avāpsyasi_ [32:00]: then you will have attained — here, attained perfection.

#### Practitioner and perfected [32:00]

- **A _sādhaka_ is one still practising** towards a realisation — still working at "I am not this body, I am the soul."
- **A _siddha_ has arrived there** and is situated in it; he is no longer making the effort. That is the stage described here, and nothing disturbs him, because the whole apparatus of fruitive promise is addressed to the body and its comfort.
- The purport's statement: to be in trance means to have fully realised Kṛṣṇa consciousness — to have realised Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān. The highest perfection is to understand that one is eternally the servant of the Lord, with one business in life: to serve Him.

#### Why Śrīla Prabhupāda writes every purport from the devotional platform [38:00]

- The section from 2.47 to 2.53 is technically about selfless action, and the teacher is explicit that Śrīla Prabhupāda knows this perfectly well. **He writes from the devotional platform for two reasons.**
- **First, because he does not dwell on what is impractical.** The analogy: a demonstrator describes an exercise that will cure every ailment, and then the posture itself turns out to have a hundred and fifty steps, each one so complex that only the demonstrator could perform it. There is no point in theory that cannot be implemented.
- **Second, because everything said here applies to devotional service anyway** — as a hundred-rupee note contains a one-rupee note. Every principle of selfless action applies within devotion, and devotion contains more besides.

### Bhagavad-gita 2.54 [40:00 – 52:00]

<!-- verse:2.54 -->

**Context.** Arjuna's four questions, which structure the rest of the chapter.

#### The four questions and where each is answered [42:00 – 48:00]

The teacher asks the class to write these down:

| #   | Question                              | Meaning                                                                             | Answered in |
| --- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| 1   | _kā bhāṣā_ — what is his language?    | _kiṁ lakṣaṇam_ — what are his symptoms?                                             | 2.55        |
| 2   | _kiṁ prabhāṣeta_ — how does he speak? | what runs in his mind, and comes out in his words, when happiness and distress come | 2.56 – 2.57 |
| 3   | _kim āsīta_ — how does he sit?        | how he restrains the senses from contact with objects unfavourable to devotion      | 2.58 – 2.63 |
| 4   | _vrajeta kim_ — how does he walk?     | how he engages the senses in what is favourable to devotion                         | 2.64 – 2.71 |

with 2.72 as the conclusion of the chapter.

- **On "language"** [44:00]: the question is not which language he speaks — English, Sanskrit, or any other. The words are to be taken in their figurative sense: what is his _definition_, what are his characteristics.
- **On "how does he speak"** [44:00]: when happiness and distress come in life, **what is the commentary running in his own mind?** Does he say "because of this person my life has become like this", "this should not have happened to me", "what is the Lord doing"? And what is in the mind comes out in speech, since speech displays consciousness.
- **On "how does he sit"** [46:00]: when the senses contact sense objects that are not favourable to devotion, how does he restrain them?
- **On "how does he walk"** [46:00]: living in this world, the senses must be engaged; some things are in fact desirable. The teacher's aside on the well-known maxim about seeing, hearing and speaking no evil — one cannot literally walk about with the eyes and ears closed. So the question is how he engages the senses in what is favourable.

#### The purport [50:00 – 52:00]

- Everyone has symptoms by which he is recognised — a wealthy man is identifiable by his clothes, his vehicle, his way of living; a sick man by his bearing. So too a person in Kṛṣṇa consciousness has his symptoms.
- **The purport singles out speech as the most important:** a foolish man cannot be detected until he speaks; well dressed, he passes. And one in Kṛṣṇa consciousness speaks only of Kṛṣṇa.
- **The teacher's three qualifications on "speaking"** [52:00]:
  1. **Not only in a lecture.** A spiritual lecture can be prepared. The question is what a person says all day long, as situations come and go — is he expressing his gratitude to Kṛṣṇa?
  2. **The speech points to the thoughts.** What comes out was already inside.
  3. **And it is not merely speech** — what he speaks is what he lives, and it will be visible in his life.

### Bhagavad-gita 2.55 [52:00 – 54:00]

<!-- verse:2.55 -->

**Context.** The Lord begins to answer the first question — the symptoms of the _sthita-prajña_.

- _Prajahāti yadā kāmān sarvān mano-gatān_: when he gives up all varieties of desire for sense gratification arising from mental concoction.
- _Ātmany evātmanā tuṣṭaḥ_: and when the purified mind finds satisfaction in the self alone.
- The detailed explanation was carried over to the next class.

#### An important distinction the purports supply [40:00 – 42:00]

The teacher anticipates the section ahead. The verses from 2.54 to 2.72 describe one perfected in selfless action; they apply to a devotee as well, **but for a devotee there is something more.**

- The _sthita-prajña_, when distress comes, understands: this is coming from my own past activity. **That is already a high consciousness.**
- **The devotee's level is beyond it:** he keeps Kṛṣṇa at the centre and understands that there is some plan of Kṛṣṇa in it — that Kṛṣṇa has mercifully reduced what was due and given him only this much.
- **That difference cannot be obtained from the verse alone.** It is what Śrīla Prabhupāda has placed in his purports, which is why the purports are so relevant to us.

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications [54:00 – 60:00]

**Q1 — If selfless action is the elementary form of devotional service, in what sense is it "not practical"?** [54:00 – 56:00]

- It can be seen as an elementary form, and Śrīla Prabhupāda does mention it in many purports.
- **Why it is nonetheless impractical on its own:** in selfless action the practice is to work without desire for the fruit, and the goal is the impersonal realisation. There is no clarity about the Lord — only the clarity that one does not wish to be entangled. **Giving up the fruit at that level, or at the level of knowledge, is genuinely difficult.**
- **The teacher's analogy, repeated from earlier classes:** a child holding something tightly will not let go when told to let go — attachment does not release on instruction. But ask him to pick up a pair of hand cymbals, and as he reaches for them one finger releases, then the next, and the object falls away by itself. **That is devotional service.** Selfless action is included in it, and yet the giving up costs so little. That is why it is practical.

**Q2 — Many householder devotees, even full-time ones, cannot give up the traditional ritual observances — for example remaining at home for the prescribed days after a death in the family, even when temple authorities advise that devotional service is what matters. How should this be viewed?** [56:00 – 60:00]

- **This can also be asked of the whole discussion, which is at a high level** — and the verses ahead are higher still.
- The root issue: **if a person's purpose is not to take shelter of the Lord's lotus feet, growth generally does not happen,** because he is not trying. He is satisfied. **That is the great challenge — remaining materially satisfied,** in which case philosophy remains theory and no change comes into the life.
- **Real change comes to one who is dissatisfied with his present position.** The teacher's comparison from career life: a man in a secure position with no prospect of advancement arrives late and begins preparing to leave hours early, because he knows there is no growth either way; a man who wants promotion works at the office and again at home.
- The same applies in devotion: "I am already chanting my rounds and following the principles — what more is there?" is the same settled satisfaction in another form.

## Last-Page Revision Sheet

### High-Yield Facts & Terminological Anchors

- **BG 2.50 — _yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam_.** The skill is acting without becoming bound, **not** expertise in the activity itself. "Catch the fish, but don't get wet."
- **Golden handcuffs and iron handcuffs are both handcuffs** — _sukṛta_ and _duṣkṛta_ both require a return.
- **Since time immemorial the living entity has accumulated reactions;** the way out is to act as the Lord's servant.
- **BG 2.51 — _anāmayam padam_,** the place without disease, without anxiety. The purport's verse: the world is dangerous at every step; with shelter at the Lord's lotus feet the ocean shrinks to the water in a calf's hoof-print.
- **The purport's decisive line:** no kind of material body anywhere in the universe can give life without miseries — so no adjustment can work.
- **BG 2.52 — _moha-kalilam_, the dense forest of delusion;** crossing it produces indifference to everything heard and to be heard — i.e. to the false promises of happiness.
- **Fruitive religion is a transaction; devotion is not.**
- **Mādhavendra Purī's verse asking the daily rites to release him** is a very high stage; we cannot apply it. Śrīla Prabhupāda: "That was not Kṛṣṇa — come to the morning programme on time."
- **Akrūra and the footprints; the elderly woman at Purī** — exceptions arising from intensity of absorption, not from negligence.
- **At a temple opening Śrīla Prabhupāda said the main sacrifice is the chanting,** while still permitting the traditional rite for social convention.
- **BG 2.53 — _sādhaka_ versus _siddha_:** one still practising towards the realisation, one already situated in it.
- **Why the purports are written from the devotional platform:** Śrīla Prabhupāda does not elaborate what cannot be practised, and everything here applies within devotion anyway — a hundred-rupee note contains a one-rupee note.
- **BG 2.54 — the four questions and their answers:** _kā bhāṣā_ → 2.55; _kiṁ prabhāṣeta_ → 2.56–57; _kim āsīta_ (restraining the senses) → 2.58–63; _vrajeta kim_ (engaging the senses) → 2.64–71; 2.72 concludes.
- **Speech is the most telling symptom** — a fool is not detected until he speaks. And speech is to be judged all day long, not during a prepared lecture.
- **The devotee's level exceeds the _sthita-prajña_'s:** not merely "this is my own past activity" but "this is Kṛṣṇa's merciful arrangement."
- **BG 2.55** — all desires of mental concoction given up; satisfaction found in the self alone.

### Core Analogies & Metaphors

- **Catching the fish without getting wet** — the actual skill in work.
- **Golden and iron handcuffs** — pious and sinful reaction.
- **The calf's hoof-print** — the ocean of material existence for one who has taken shelter.
- **The endless "one more adjustment"** — why material arrangement cannot produce happiness.
- **The recitation for four months and the oil offered on seven appointed days** — why the fruitive route looks attractive.
- **The transaction at a cash machine** — fruitive religion as business.
- **The exercise with a hundred and fifty steps** — why Śrīla Prabhupāda does not elaborate the impractical.
- **The hundred-rupee note containing the one-rupee note** — devotion containing selfless action.
- **The wealthy man and the sick man recognised by their bearing** — symptoms of consciousness.
- **The child's fingers releasing as he reaches for the cymbals** — how attachment actually falls away.
- **The secure post versus the man seeking promotion** — dissatisfaction as the condition for growth.
