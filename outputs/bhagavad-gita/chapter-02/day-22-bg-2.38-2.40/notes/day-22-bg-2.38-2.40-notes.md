## Class Snapshot & One-Line Argument Map

- **Class Snapshot:** The most practically applicable section of the chapter opens. BG 2.38 restates equanimity as the condition under which no sin attaches; 2.39 announces the shift from analytical study to _buddhi-yoga_, and the class settles the question of how the _ācāryas_ read that term; and 2.40 gives the great promise of the path — no effort is ever lost, no reverse result is ever produced, and even a little of this practice protects from the greatest fear. The hierarchy of paths — _karma-kāṇḍa_, _niṣkāma-karma-yoga_, _jñāna_, _dhyāna_ and _bhakti_ — is laid out along the way.
- **One-Line Argument Map:** Work performed in equanimity, without regard for happiness or distress, gain or loss, victory or defeat, incurs no sin -> so the Lord now teaches the intelligence by which such work is done, which the commentators identify as selfless action and which Śrīla Prabhupāda identifies throughout as devotional service -> and on this path alone no endeavour is ever wasted and no contrary result is ever produced -> therefore even a very small beginning is permanent and carries forward, which is why nothing spiritual should ever be compromised.

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.38 [08:00 – 16:00]

<!-- verse:2.38 -->

**Context.** The verse that begins the new section, recapitulated from the previous class.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sukha-duḥkhe same kṛtvā_ [08:00]: making happiness and distress equal.
- _Lābhālābhau jayājayau_ [08:00]: gain and loss, victory and defeat.
- _Na evaṁ pāpam avāpsyasi_ [08:00]: acting in this mood, you will not incur sin — answering Arjuna's fourth reason directly.

#### The instructor does not change, even when the instruction does [08:00 – 10:00]

An observation the teacher draws out: the Lord has changed His level of argument three times, and **Arjuna has not changed his teacher.**

- A disciple receives instructions from an instructor; the instructions may change, but the attachment is to the one giving them.
- **The historical illustration** [10:00]: in the early days of Śrīla Prabhupāda's society, when funds were short, even the temple residents — including _brahmacārīs_ — were told to take up jobs and earn. Some time later he told them to give up the jobs and take to _harināma-saṅkīrtana_, and they did — because they were attached to Śrīla Prabhupāda, not to a particular instruction. His senior disciples' lives show the same: life membership, cow protection, cooking for the Deities, preaching — whatever was asked.

#### Why detachment from the result actually helps the work [10:00 – 12:00]

- When attachment to the result exceeds attention to the duty, anxiety follows — and that anxiety consumes the very energy the work requires.
- **The student who is desperate to stand first** spends most of his energy on the anxiety rather than the study. Study properly and the result follows if it is to follow.
- **And enjoyment itself is limited** [12:00]: given ten sweets, a person cannot enjoy beyond a certain point — and if he forces it, the result is illness. Offer them to Kṛṣṇa, however, and He both accepts and digests them. This is why the result is to be offered.

#### Where compromise is affordable, and where it is not [12:00 – 16:00]

- **A striking principle:** a small compromise in material life is not a great issue, since what comes is largely fixed by past activities anyway. **Compromise in spiritual life is a very great problem** — and people generally do the exact opposite.
- **The postponement test** [12:00 – 14:00]: we tell ourselves that if only a particular material problem were solved — if that sum of money came — we would give ourselves fully to devotional life. The teacher's suggestion is to write that resolution down and frame it: when the money actually arrives, the same person will still not do it, because a new anxiety will have taken its place — how to maintain it, how to keep it from shrinking. Ten years ago there were reasons; there are reasons today.
- **Yāmunācārya** [14:00]: he had no material desire, and debated only to uphold the honour of his spiritual master — the condition of the debate being half the kingdom. When half the kingdom actually came to him, he became absorbed in it for a time, until he was recovered. _Māyā is very powerful; there is no stability in material position._
- **The devotional attitude in the mood of the verse** [16:00]: to live in whatever condition the Lord keeps one, continuing devotional practice through gain and loss alike — that, the teacher says, saves a person from a great many problems.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Compromise where it costs little — in material arrangements, which are largely fixed anyway — and never where it costs everything: in spiritual practice. Most people do the reverse. [12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.39 [16:00 – 32:00]

<!-- verse:2.39 -->

**Context.** The Lord announces the change of method, and the class works through what the commentators mean by _buddhi-yoga_.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sāṅkhye_ [16:00, 24:00]: analytical study — the description of the nature of the soul given in 2.11 – 2.30. Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport, following Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa, defines _sāṅkhya_ as that philosophy which describes the nature of the soul.
- _Buddhiḥ_ [16:00]: intelligence — also to be understood as the consciousness in which one operates.
- _Yoge_ [16:00 – 18:00]: here, working without fruitive result.
- _Karma-bandhaṁ prahāsyasi_ [16:00]: you will be released from the bondage of works.

#### How the commentators divide this section [16:00 – 18:00, 28:00 – 30:00]

The teacher sets out the different readings and then resolves them:

- **Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura:** 2.39 – 2.46 treats _bhakti-yoga_; 2.47 – 2.53, _niṣkāma-karma-yoga_.
- **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa (and Rāmānujācārya):** the whole section is _niṣkāma-karma-yoga_.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda:** _buddhi-yoga_ here means devotional service, and the purports throughout are written from that standpoint.
- **There is no contradiction**, because the principles stated apply fully to both. Since we are practitioners of devotional service, the class follows Śrīla Prabhupāda's reading — and where someone objects that the section is technically about selfless action, the answer is simply that the principle applies to devotion as well.
- **Where the term occurs** [30:00]: _buddhi-yoga_ appears in BG 2.39, in BG 2.49, and in BG 10.10 — the second in the context of selfless action, the third within the section on devotion; and Śrīla Prabhupāda renders it as devotional service in each.

#### The ladder of paths [18:00 – 24:00]

The teacher lays out the whole hierarchy, which the rest of the section presupposes:

- **_Vikarma_** — action against scriptural direction; material desire keeps the living entity returning.
- **_Karma-kāṇḍa_ / _sakāma-karma-yoga_** — the lowest of the sanctioned levels, for one who still has material desires: prescribed sacrifices for health, wealth, progeny, or elevation; the Vedas as a desire-tree. It is not itself a great achievement, it rarely works today for want of purity of place, article and priest, and it produces **no spiritual growth**.
- **_Niṣkāma-karma-yoga_** — where the transcendental path begins. The practitioner performs the duties of his _varṇa_ and _āśrama_ but begins offering the fruit; he cultivates the propensity to give. He keeps what is needed for maintenance and directs the rest to the Lord's service, and by this the enjoying spirit is purified. His heart need not yet be fully pure — he may begin at ten or twenty per cent and increase.
- **_Jñāna-yoga_** — requires a heart already purified, with the desire for enjoyment ended; one who still wants enjoyment has no entry. Its aim is Brahman, and the practitioner steps outside _varṇāśrama_ duties.
- **_Dhyāna-yoga_** — actual meditation begins only when the heart is purified; its aim is the Supersoul.
- **_Bhakti-yoga_** — the topmost. Its method is the nine processes of devotional service, and its object is love of Kṛṣṇa. The means is simple and the attainment the highest, which is why it is called supreme.
- **Why devotion already contains selflessness** [30:00 – 32:00]: Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition of pure devotion begins with freedom from all other desires and from being covered by knowledge and fruitive action, and requires favourable service for the Lord's pleasure — so the selfless element is built in.

#### Two kinds of _sāṅkhya_ [24:00 – 28:00]

- The purport distinguishes the **atheistic _sāṅkhya_** — which enumerates the elements (the knowledge-acquiring and working senses, the sense objects, the gross elements, mind, intelligence and false ego) but leaves out the Lord — from the **real _sāṅkhya_** described by Lord Kapila in the Third Canto of the Bhāgavatam.
- **The teacher's parallel:** modern science does something similar — it may describe the beginning of the universe in detail, but omits who caused it. As Śrīla Prabhupāda put it, there was a great explosion, but who exploded it?
- The purport also notes that Arjuna had already accepted Kṛṣṇa, so devotion was in effect already his position; the Lord nevertheless argues from every level, because the Gītā is universal and must give an understanding to a person at any stage. Devotion is treated in detail from the Seventh Chapter onwards.
- A line the teacher highlights from the purport: **only to those engaged in the Lord's transcendental loving service does the Lord award pure knowledge of devotion in love.**

### Bhagavad-gita 2.40 [32:00 – 52:00]

<!-- verse:2.40 -->

**Context.** The famous promise of the path, and the reason it is quoted so often.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Iha_ [32:00]: in this — on this path.
- _Abhikrama-nāśaḥ nāsti_ [32:00 – 34:00]: no endeavour undertaken here is ever lost.
- _Pratyavāyaḥ na vidyate_ [34:00]: nor is any contrary result produced.
- _Svalpam apy asya dharmasya_ [38:00]: even a very little of this occupation.
- _Trāyate mahato bhayāt_ [38:00]: delivers from the greatest fear.

#### Why the verse implies a comparison [34:00 – 36:00]

If it is stated that on _this_ path nothing is lost, there must be a path where things _are_ lost. The comparison is with the regulated fruitive path treated in 2.31 – 2.37:

- In _karma-kāṇḍa_ the place, the articles and the priest must all be pure; if any is deficient, the result does not come.
- **The work cannot be carried forward:** a sacrifice half completed is not resumed at fifty-one per cent in the next life. The previous result is simply lost.
- **And a contrary result is possible** [34:00 – 36:00]: the Sixth Canto records a sacrifice performed to produce a son who would kill Indra, in which a mispronunciation produced instead a son who would be killed _by_ Indra. The teacher's wry application: consider what would follow if such a rule applied to the way we chant.

#### What carries forward [36:00 – 38:00]

- Someone to whom the process was preached takes to it with an intensity and gravity out of all proportion to the preaching — **because he is carrying on from where he left off.** One who had reached fifty per cent begins at fifty-one; one who had reached eighty begins at eighty-one.
- Therefore neither envy such a person nor be disturbed by the comparison.
- **The biographies bear it out** [36:00 – 38:00]: the teacher recalls a senior devotee whose realization after a single round of chanting was that all forms of prayer culminate in the chanting of the holy name and that this is the topmost form of prayer; and another, a householder in a senior professional position, who placed his entire salary in Śrīla Prabhupāda's hands — while we find ten or fifteen per cent difficult.
- **And no one should conclude that this excuses low effort** [38:00]: whatever effort is made is never lost — but a slack life leaves one resuming next time at five or six per cent.

#### Why no contrary result arises [38:00 – 40:00]

The Lord is **_bhāva-grāhī janārdana_** — He accepts the mood. One pronounces the name one way, another differently; the Lord accepts it: "somehow or other he is calling — he is My own child."

#### The purport [40:00 – 44:00]

- Activity in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, performed for Kṛṣṇa's benefit without expectation of sense gratification, is the **highest transcendental quality of work**; even a small beginning of it meets no impediment and can never be lost.
- **Any work begun on the material plane has to be completed, or it is a failure.** A student cannot ask for seventy per cent of a medical degree after four of five years; an engineering student must clear all thirty subjects, not twenty-eight.
- **But one per cent performed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness bears a permanent result**, so that the next beginning is from two per cent — whereas material activity without one hundred per cent success yields no profit at all.
- **The contrast the teacher presses** [42:00 – 46:00]: everything we build materially neither accompanies us nor satisfies the heart here, and after death the remainder becomes a quarrel among the survivors. What is done spiritually begins to satisfy immediately and always goes with us.
- **Prahlāda Mahārāja's verse** is cited: people do not even know their own self-interest — that it lies in the service of Viṣṇu — and instead accept as valuable what has no value. Hence the teacher's phrase: _devotees should be selfish in the real sense._
- The Bhāgavatam verse on one who abandons his prescribed duty to serve the Lord is also referred to: even if he falls, what loss is there — while what does one who perfectly discharges his duty without devotion actually gain?

#### Devotion as the best investment [46:00 – 50:00]

- **"Minimum investment, maximum output"** is the definition of a good business, and by that standard devotional service is the best business.
- **The management-school illustration** [46:00 – 48:00]: a devotee speaking at a business school described an organisation with more than four hundred full-time workers across two hundred and fifty centres, none of whom draws a salary, all of whom work around the clock — and the students were startled. The point: Śrīla Prabhupāda built exactly such an organisation, because those workers know it is the best investment.
- **Everything material carries an expiry date** — it is spent, it loses value, it is taken by others, or we ourselves leave. If an investment existed in which nothing was ever lost, how eager a person would be to place his wealth there.
- **And what has to be invested here is the treasure of time** [48:00 – 50:00], which is itself being depleted with every passing day — with each sunrise and sunset. The question is where we are spending it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
On this path the ledger never resets: one per cent completed is permanent, and the next life begins from two — which is exactly what does not happen to anything built on the material platform. [42:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications [52:00 – 78:00]

**Q1 — We are beginners and chant with offences. Does "nothing is lost" apply to us as well?** [52:00 – 54:00]

- Devotional service is not destroyed. Small offences — even towards devotees — **cover** devotion for a time; they do not annihilate it.
- The exception is an offence against a great devotee, which is a genuine danger; one should keep a healthy fear of that and be especially careful with seniors, and generally respectful with everyone.
- **And intention is what makes an offence an offence:** a wrong motive, a desire to give pain. Accidentally touching someone with one's foot, or an ordinary remark, is not counted in that category. Complete loss of devotion is a very rare case.

**Q2 — The purport to 2.36 says Arjuna described compassion in terms befitting a non-Āryan. What does that mean?** [54:00 – 58:00]

_Ārya_ means one who is cultured — who lives according to Vedic culture, who knows the value of life, who knows what is temporary and what is eternal, and the difference between the body and the soul. The Lord had already used _anārya-juṣṭam_ in 2.2. The point is that what Arjuna called compassion is weakness of heart, and does not befit one who knows the conclusions of scripture.

**Q3 — What if a senior devotee appears to depart from the tradition? Is keeping one's distance an offence?** [58:00 – 62:00]

- **Devotion is a treasure and must be preserved** — and what one does to preserve it is not an offence.
- If something appears contrary to the tradition, first seek clarification from the person; if that is not possible, one may keep some distance. Others may conclude that an offence is being committed, and so may the senior person — but it does not fall in that category.
- **This is not an argument for blind following.** One should consult scripture: this life is meant for surrender to Kṛṣṇa, and we offer obeisances to everyone who helps in that; where an obstruction or a defect appears, it must either be clarified or changed. **Bali Mahārāja gave up even Śukrācārya** — what greater precedent is needed?
- At the same time, one does not criticise such a person, remains grateful for whatever good was learned from him, and stays ready to serve him if the opportunity arises.

**Q4 — When is Arjuna's argument about the destruction of family tradition answered?** [62:00 – 64:00]

It is touched on at the level of principle here and in the coming verses, and treated in the Third Chapter — the teacher points to 3.24 for the discussion of unwanted progeny. The essential answer given: follow the Lord's instruction and engage in devotion, and the rest is taken care of — _pick up the thousand-rupee note and the ten-rupee concerns handle themselves._

**Q5 — Is the difference that a _niṣkāma-karma-yogī_ is attached to the work but not to the result, while a devotee is attached to neither?** [64:00 – 68:00]

Yes.

- **The cook who makes ten thousand gulab jamuns** for a festival, takes no payment and disappears before anyone can praise him, is detached from the fruit. Ask him the next year to cook dal and rice instead and he refuses — he will come only to make gulab jamuns. He is detached from the fruit, not from the work.
- **A devotee** does whatever is asked — gulab jamuns or dal and rice — wanting neither recognition nor payment. Śrīla Prabhupāda's standard is of this kind: one who speaks from the seat of the teacher also cleans the toilets afterwards and goes out to collect funds, without objecting that he is a _brāhmaṇa_. That is _bhakti-yoga_.
- **And _sakāma-karma-yoga_ / _karma-kāṇḍa_** is essentially the attempt to enjoy in a Vedic manner; there is no spiritual growth in it at all. The selfless practitioner begins by offering part of the fruit and is perfected when he offers all of it.

**Q6 — Please clarify _buddhi-yoga_ once more.** [68:00 – 74:00]

- The term is applied both to selfless action and to devotional service. In selfless action one performs the duties of _varṇāśrama_ and offers the fruit; in devotional service the nine processes are primary and the social duties, though performed, are secondary.
- **Devotional service is the direct process** — hearing, chanting and remembering are directly spiritual activities, performed directly for the Lord's pleasure — which is what makes it special.
- The principle of 2.40 applies to every transcendental path, which is why there is no conflict in Śrīla Prabhupāda's rendering.

**Q7 — What if one works not for oneself but for relatives and family, and not directly for the Lord?** [74:00 – 78:00]

- **Doing things directly for the Lord has to be learned**, gradually, because that is the essence.
- **The teacher's test:** who were your parents, your uncles and aunts, your brothers and sisters thirty-five years ago? And thirty-five years after you leave, who will they be? Even if one's own parents took birth again in another family or another species, nothing could be done for them.
- "I" and "mine" is what binds one here. **Duty should be discharged** — there is no fault in that — but the surrender must be directed to the Lord.
- **The examination analogy** [76:00]: materially one must begin again at the beginning every time, whatever degrees were held before; in spiritual life, one who reached a certain standard resumes from there.
- So: recognise that this family came according to past activities, discharge the duty with that understanding, and place the shelter in the One who was there thirty-five years ago and will be there fifty years hence. What is offered to Him is what goes with us; the rest is a stage that will change.

## Last-Page Revision Sheet

### High-Yield Facts & Terminological Anchors

- **The instructions change; the instructor does not.** Attachment is to the person giving direction, not to a particular instruction.
- **Compromise materially if you must; never spiritually** — people generally do the reverse.
- **The postponement test:** frame the resolution and see whether it is kept when the condition is met. Yāmunācārya's half-kingdom is the cautionary example.
- **_Sāṅkhya_** = the philosophy describing the nature of the soul; **atheistic _sāṅkhya_** enumerates the elements but omits the Lord. The real _sāṅkhya_ is Lord Kapila's in the Third Canto.
- **_Buddhi-yoga_** occurs at BG 2.39, 2.49 and 10.10; Viśvanātha Cakravartī reads 2.39 – 2.46 as devotional service, Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa reads the section as selfless action, and Śrīla Prabhupāda renders it as devotional service throughout — the principles apply to both.
- **The ladder:** _vikarma_ → _karma-kāṇḍa_ (no spiritual growth) → _niṣkāma-karma-yoga_ (the transcendental path begins; the fruit is offered) → _jñāna-yoga_ (requires a purified heart; aims at Brahman) → _dhyāna-yoga_ (aims at Paramātmā) → _bhakti-yoga_ (nine processes; aims at love of Kṛṣṇa).
- **Devotion already contains selflessness** — Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition begins with freedom from other desires.
- **BG 2.40:** _abhikrama-nāśaḥ nāsti_ — no endeavour is lost; _pratyavāyaḥ na vidyate_ — no contrary result; _svalpam api_ — even a little delivers from the greatest fear.
- **Why the comparison holds:** in _karma-kāṇḍa_ purity of place, article and priest is required, results are not carried forward, and a mistake can produce the opposite result.
- **One per cent in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is permanent**, so the next beginning is from two per cent — whereas material work without one hundred per cent yields nothing.
- **_Bhāva-grāhī janārdana_** — the Lord accepts the mood.
- **"Minimum investment, maximum output"** — and what is invested here is the treasure of time, which depletes daily.
- **Offences cover devotion rather than destroying it** — except against a great devotee; and intention is what makes an offence an offence.

### Core Analogies & Metaphors

- **The jobs taken up and then given up** — attachment to the instructor, not the instruction.
- **The student desperate to stand first** — anxiety consuming the energy meant for the work.
- **Ten sweets one cannot digest, offered to Kṛṣṇa instead** — why the fruit is offered.
- **The framed resolution about the money that will change everything** — the postponement that never ends.
- **The sacrifice that produced the opposite son** — a contrary result in fruitive ritual.
- **Fifty per cent resumed at fifty-one** — how devotional credit carries forward.
- **Seventy per cent of a medical degree** — why material work must be completed.
- **The organisation with four hundred unpaid full-time workers** — the best business.
- **The cook who will only make gulab jamuns** — detachment from the fruit but not from the work.
- **"Who were your relatives thirty-five years ago?"** — the measure of what is worth surrendering to.
