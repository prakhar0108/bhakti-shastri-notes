## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 4.15 [04:00 – 10:00]

<!-- verse:4.15 -->

**Context.** Having said in BG 4.14 that neither He nor one who knows this principle is bound by work, Krishna concludes: therefore, Arjuna, you too should work — the way the seekers of liberation of the past worked. [04:00 – 06:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Evaṁ jñātvā_: Knowing this — understanding the principle just given and acting according to it. [04:00 – 06:00]
- _Pūrvair api mumukṣubhiḥ_: As those in the past who attained liberation — who were not caught in the bondage of work — did. [04:00 – 06:00]
- _Kuru karma_: Do your work — not with a desire for results, but in a mood free of desire. [06:00]
- _Pūrvaiḥ pūrva-taraṁ kṛtam_: As it was done by the predecessors; the teacher links this to the kings like Janaka mentioned in the Third Chapter. [06:00]

#### Two candidates, one instruction [06:00 – 08:00]

- Throughout the Third Chapter Krishna keeps coming back, in the end, to the same point: act without desire for results. The teacher identifies two kinds of candidates for that instruction:
  1. **One whose heart is not pure** — he should act without desire for results, because by doing so he will be purified.
  2. **One whose heart is pure and who is qualified for knowledge** — he too should act without desire for results, for _loka-saṅgraha_: to set an example for others, as the Third Chapter explains.

#### The conduct of great souls is the standard, not the trend [06:00 – 10:00]

- The conduct of great personalities is the _pramāṇa_ — the evidence — for how one should live and work. Śāstra gives the principles, and great souls practised them in their lives.
- What is the common trend, what most people are doing, what is in fashion — none of these is a standard for action.
- **The ditch example.** If a hundred people are going to jump into a ditch together, what should the hundred-and-first do? We would say he should go back — yet he goes, because a hundred are going. The majority carries great power; whoever has the majority forms the government.
- **Culture.** That is the power of culture: if a hundred people are doing something wrong, the rest are drawn toward it too, even unwillingly. Modern culture is very strong for this reason, which is why the teacher urges that, wherever we are, we try to establish a little devotional culture.
- His principle: a hundred people doing a wrong thing does not make it right — competition does not make wrong as right.
- Even today, those who act according to dharma are few; a devotee should still follow that conduct, knowing it is the way of life śāstra describes. [08:00 – 10:00]

#### The purport: work without retiring [10:00]

- Srila Prabhupada's purport describes the same two classes — those whose hearts are impure and those already purified — and the teacher observes that in the purport Prabhupada does not discuss the path of knowledge at all, but comes directly to Krishna consciousness.
- Those who are impure act without desire for results and are purified; those already clean should also continue acting.
- One whose heart is not pure taking to the path of knowledge — like Arjuna here wanting to retire from the fight and from work — is not a good course; the teacher notes this has been explained before.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.15 gives the instruction in its simplest form: act as the liberated predecessors acted. It applies to both the impure, who are purified by working without desire for results, and the pure, who keep working to set an example. The standard is the conduct of great souls guided by śāstra — not the trend, the fashion or the majority, since a hundred people doing a wrong thing does not make it right. [04:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.16 [10:00 – 16:00]

<!-- verse:4.16 -->

**Context.** The teacher voices the question in Arjuna's mind: "Is it really so complex? Just act as the predecessors did — I've understood." Krishna's answer is that it is not so easy: although the principle is to follow the conduct of great souls, in practice people become bewildered — even very great people get confused. [10:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kiṁ karma kim akarma_: What is action, what is inaction. [12:00]
- _Kavayaḥ_: Not the modern poet of a _kavi-sammelan_, but those who have knowledge — great scholars. [12:00]
- _Atra mohitāḥ_: Even they are bewildered in this matter. [12:00]
- _Karma_: Here, _niṣkāma karma_ — work done without desire for results — and how to do it. [12:00]
- _Akarma_: Action that does not produce a reaction. [12:00]
- _Pravakṣyāmi_: "I will tell you" — the Lord Himself is going to explain the principle of work. [14:00]
- _Yaj jñātvā mokṣyase 'śubhāt_: Knowing this, you will be saved from what is inauspicious. [14:00]

#### Why even the learned are bewildered [12:00 – 14:00]

- The teacher stresses how important this principle is: without a book like the Gita, without these śāstras, people would have no hope of understanding it.
- Everyone is already acting. The activities of the body and senses are counted as work, but what one does with the mind, the intelligence and speech is also work.
- People are bewildered because from the outside it is not exactly clear what work should be done.
- **Arjuna's concern** is precisely this: "If I fight and kill people, I will get a reaction." Krishna's answer is: no — I am telling you, do it, and you will not get one.
- **The soldier.** A soldier who kills an enemy at the border gets no reaction; if he comes home and kills someone, he does. The act is the same; the reaction is different. That is why in the matter of work even great people get bewildered. [12:00 – 14:00]

#### The purport: follow the _paramparā_, not independent judgement [14:00 – 16:00]

- In Krishna consciousness one should act as the _mahājanas_ acted, not independently — in disciplic succession, following the conduct of great souls.
- The problem with acting independently: otherwise even the most intelligent man will be bewildered.
- **Two kinds of choice.** Before coming to bhakti, a person has to choose between right and wrong, which is relatively easy at the level of intelligence. After coming to bhakti, he has to choose one of **two right options**. That is why guidance is so essential after coming to bhakti; without guidance a person makes many mistakes.
- **Religion cannot be learned by imperfect experimental knowledge.** The teacher reads this line from the purport and adds that the essence of dharma is very deep; if someone thinks he will understand it completely on the basis of his own intelligence, it is not possible. Just as the Lord is beyond the experience of the senses, not everything that seems logical is necessarily according to dharma. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.16 answers the objection "isn't this simple?" with "no." The same outward act — a soldier killing at the border or at home — can carry or not carry a reaction, so even the learned are bewildered, and the Lord Himself undertakes to explain it. Since after coming to bhakti one is choosing between two right options rather than right and wrong, guidance in the disciplic succession is essential; dharma cannot be worked out by imperfect experimental knowledge. [10:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.17 [16:00 – 28:00]

<!-- verse:4.17 -->

**Context.** Because of this complexity, the Lord says that action must be understood — and this time a third term is added. [16:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Boddhavyam_: Should be understood — try to know it, try to learn it. The word appears three times in the verse, and the teacher reads it as the Lord expecting a **commitment** from the person to understand how to work in life. [16:00 – 18:00]
- _Karmaṇaḥ_: Of action — here, how to do _niṣkāma karma_. [16:00]
- _Akarmaṇaḥ_: Of inaction — action done in such a way that no reaction is created. [16:00]
- _Vikarmaṇaḥ_: The previous verse had _karma_ and _akarma_; here "a third brother" is added. Its ordinary meaning is sinful work, or work against śāstra; here the teacher reads it as **every kind of work other than _niṣkāma karma_**. [16:00]
- _Gahanā karmaṇo gatiḥ_: The course of action is complex. [18:00]

#### Same act, different consciousness [18:00]

- Remaining unattached while acting is a science, and it is important to understand it.
- An act can be the same while the consciousness is different, and so the result is different.
- **Yudhiṣṭhira and Duryodhana** both ruled a kingdom — the same work — but Duryodhana did it in one mood and Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira in a different consciousness.
- The right way: act free of the desire for results and free of attachment, simply as duty, for the Lord's pleasure.

#### Why the law of karma is complex [18:00 – 24:00]

The teacher calls all three things complex — what work to do, how to act so that no reaction comes, and how acting produces a reaction — and explains why life often cannot be exactly understood:

1. **Many acts combine into one result.** A thief starts his "career" in childhood: a pencil, a sharpener, then a geometry box, then a helmet, then — "practice makes a man perfect" — picking pockets and stealing money. When he is caught, it all comes together at once. The teacher calls this a gross example. [18:00 – 20:00]
2. **Different acts bear fruit at different times.** A vegetable comes in a month or two; a mango tree in about six months, he says; some trees take a year, some four, five or six years, and some thirty or forty years after the seed is sown. The fruits of karma likewise come at different times. [20:00]
3. **One act can bear fruit as a series of events.** The teacher tells a past-life-regression account: a small girl fell from her bicycle and hurt her knee; later, playing hockey, someone struck her knee; once, walking, she fell in an accident — repeatedly, three or four times, the injury was to the knee. When her past life was studied, she had been a general in an army in another country who had broken the knees of those captured with a hammer. He says plainly that he is telling the story to explain the principle and does not know its details. [20:00 – 22:00]

- **Shortcuts are long cuts.** This is why acting according to dharma is so important, even with some difficulty: what seems like a shortcut now is actually a long cut. [22:00 – 24:00]
- This is also why sudden jolts in life are not understood — someone suddenly gets cancer or a brain tumour, someone suddenly has an accident, a small child suddenly falls very ill. At least in whatever situation one is in now, one should act according to dharma and not take shortcuts.
- **The train ticket.** On a train, an elderly lady could not find her ticket on her mobile when the TT asked for it, and was fined. Later the people sitting around her looked on the phone and found it — she had a ticket, and was fined anyway. The teacher calls the example simple, but the principle is that there is no shortcut in life: in this world we have to make our endeavour (_puruṣārtha_) according to dharma. [24:00] [Details flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- He adds that we remember nothing of the past — memory is volatile — which is another reason the course of action is so deep. [26:00]

#### The purport: avoid _vikarma_, aim for _akarma_ [26:00]

- One who is serious about liberation from material bondage must understand action, inaction and unauthorized action.
- _Vikarma_ should not be done at all, and one should not do work that makes one fall into bondage again and again either. One should try for _akarma_, which the teacher glosses as **work done for the Lord's pleasure**.
- The purport adds that the entire Bhagavad-gita is directed toward this conclusion — how to give up _vikarma_ and engage in _akarma_.
- How will this be understood? By seeing how the _mahājanas_ acted in the past — which is why one has to read śāstra, the Gita and the Bhāgavatam.

#### Three benchmarks people act by [26:00 – 28:00]

The teacher asks what benchmark a person actually works by in this world, and lists three:

1. **Society.** "Why are you doing this?" — "Prabhuji, everybody does it." If 99% of society is eating non-vegetarian food or drinking, the person does it too — not because he has a great taste for it, but because everyone does. As society changes, he changes with it.
2. **One's own logic.** Some do what seems right to them, sometimes even against society. The problem is whether one's mind and intelligence are pure: the senses are imperfect, the mind is fickle and the intelligence is lusty. He calls this a corrupt software — uncontrolled senses, fickle mind, lusty intelligence — and someone working with it will get many jolts.
3. **Śāstra and the conduct of great souls.** Taking the principles given in the scriptures and the example of great personalities, and working out under guidance, through discussion, how to adopt them in one's own life. One who does not act this way gets stuck in life.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.17 asks for a commitment — _boddhavyam_, three times — to understand action, inaction and _vikarma_, which the teacher reads as everything other than desireless work. The same act done in different consciousness gives different results, and the law of karma is complex: many acts combine into one result, different acts fruit at different times, and one act can return as a series of events. Hence there are no shortcuts, and the benchmark for action must be neither society nor one's own corrupted logic, but śāstra and the conduct of great souls under guidance. [16:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.18 [28:00 – 38:00]

<!-- verse:4.18 -->

**Context.** Having said that action must be understood, the Lord now begins to explain it. The teacher notes that the language is a little metaphorical — figurative (_alaṅkārika_) language. [28:00 – 30:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karmaṇy akarma yaḥ paśyet_: One who sees inaction in action — who knows "I am acting, but no reaction will be created." [30:00]
- _Akarmaṇi ca karma yaḥ_: And action in inaction — the second person, who is not acting, yet incurs the reaction of action. [30:00 – 32:00]
- _Sa buddhimān manuṣyeṣu_: Such a person, who knows this science, is intelligent among human beings. [32:00]
- _Sa yuktaḥ kṛtsna-karma-kṛt_: He is properly situated, and though doing all work, he will not be caught. [32:00]

#### Inaction in action: the active devotee [30:00]

- Seeing inaction in action is the instruction to remain unattached while acting. What binds is the desire for the fruit: by not keeping it, **no new attachment arises**, and by acting for the Lord's pleasure, **the reactions of old work are also cleared**.
- So the devotee remains very active. In many spiritual organizations, the teacher observes, someone takes _sannyāsa_ and then has little to do — no activity. A devotee does a great many things — worships the Deity, collects funds, distributes books — and none of it carries a reaction, because he is not doing it for himself.
- **The cashier.** Srila Prabhupada's example, as the teacher gives it: a cashier counts lakhs and crores of rupees — no issue. If he puts five in his own pocket, there is a problem. He is working, but no reaction is created as long as the work is for the Lord's pleasure. [30:00]

#### Action in inaction: why the forest is no escape [30:00 – 32:00]

- The two lines describe two people: one engaged in work who gets no reaction, and one who is not working and still gets a reaction.
- **Krishna is shaking Arjuna's system a little.** Arjuna was thinking: "If I act I'll get a reaction; better that I go to the forest, do no work, and get no reaction." Krishna says it is not that easy — it is complex. If you act for My pleasure, you get no reaction even while acting; otherwise you get a reaction even without acting.
- **The lazy student and the security guard.** A very lazy student, or a security guard who does no work — practically, will there be a reaction? There will.
- Merely giving up work externally does not save one from reaction, because even if the working senses stop, the knowledge-acquiring senses keep functioning: the ears are still hearing something, the eyes are still seeing something.
- Human beings cannot stop acting — activity is the nature of the _jīva_ — but one must act so that no reaction results. [32:00]

#### Already discussed in the Third Chapter [32:00 – 34:00]

- The teacher notes that this principle was discussed in detail in the Third Chapter, from BG 3.9 — work done for _yajña_, meaning for the Lord's pleasure, for what pleases Vishnu, does not bind.
- Since the section up to about BG 4.33 repeats much of that, he says he is going through it a little faster. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]

#### The purport: the impersonalist's fear and the personalist's position [34:00 – 38:00]

- **The first line.** A person acting in Krishna consciousness is naturally free from the bonds of karma. Since his activities are done for Krishna, he does not enjoy or suffer the good or bad effects of the work, and so he is intelligent in human society. [34:00]
- **The path of knowledge.** Prabhupada then contrasts the impersonalists: those on the path of knowledge think they should not act at all, or they will be caught. But that path is very difficult. If the heart is not completely pure, one cannot read much śāstra, and if one does, one will not understand much — yet reading, understanding and contemplating is all there is; there is no other activity. [34:00 – 36:00]
- **The quiet temple.** Hence many religious places have no activity: many temples are completely quiet, the priest comes at his own time, the Deity has been installed and the Lord is standing waiting — "someday someone will come" — with nobody but the pigeons. No engagement.
- **Why that is so hard.** The nature of the _jīva_ is activity; with no activity and a heart that is not pure, a person cannot contemplate and meditate on śāstra very much. He has forcibly pulled the senses away from their objects, while the desire remains in the mind, so there is great pain. The teacher notes that the Lord will say later in the Gita that the trouble on that path is greater. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **The purport's contrast:** the impersonalist ceases activity out of fear, while the personalist knows his right position.
- **The active temple.** Devotional service does not withdraw the senses; it engages them. So a devotee temple is full of activity — sometimes visitors expecting "peace, peace" find it shaking them up: a book distributor catches hold of them — "take a book" — someone sits them down and tells them the temple is being built, and once inside someone pulls them into the dancing. [36:00 – 38:00]
- Because this activity is natural to the soul, sense control becomes easier; there is great joy in it, and a great deal is left behind without pain. Devotional service is natural for this reason.
- **The purport's last line**, which the teacher calls very important: the sense of eternal servitorship to Krishna makes one immune to all reactions. The more firmly the _jīva_ is established in the mind as "I am the Lord's servant," and the more he works in that identity, the more he is saved from all reactions. [38:00]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.18 overturns Arjuna's plan to escape reaction by escaping action. One who acts for the Lord's pleasure — like a cashier handling crores without pocketing any — creates no new attachment and clears old reactions, while one who gives up work outwardly still incurs reaction, because the senses keep functioning and activity is the soul's nature. So the devotee is fully active, and the sense of eternal servitorship to Krishna is what makes him immune to reaction. [28:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.19 [38:00 – 46:00]

<!-- verse:4.19 -->

**Context.** From here to BG 4.24 the Lord elaborates the principle of _akarma_ — of _niṣkāma karma_ — describing the intelligent person mentioned in the previous verse, who works in such a way that no reaction results, whether for the Lord's pleasure or, on the path of knowledge, without desire for results. [38:00 – 40:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yasya sarve samārambhāḥ_: All of his undertakings — everyone is doing some work or other. [40:00]
- _Kāma-saṅkalpa-varjitāḥ_: Free from the determination for sense gratification — he does no work for his own enjoyment. [40:00]
- _Jñānāgni-dagdha-karmāṇam_: One whose work has been burned in the fire of knowledge. [42:00]
- _Tam āhuḥ paṇḍitaṁ budhāḥ_: The learned call such a person wise. [44:00]

#### The misconception: "I am the enjoyer" [40:00]

- In the conditioned state our consciousness is "I am the enjoyer." Because of this misconception we act for our own enjoyment, and reactions are created.
- The opposite consciousness is "I am a servant; the Lord is the enjoyer."
- **The peace formula.** The teacher cites BG 5.29 as the peace formula: peace comes to one who first accepts that the Lord is the enjoyer. Otherwise life goes by in "I want to enjoy, I want to enjoy."
- **The employee and the cashier.** In a company the profit goes to the owner; as long as the employee knows that, he can remain peaceful. A bank cashier collects a great deal of money — the wealth goes to the bank — and stays peaceful; but if he takes five thousand out of the cash and puts it in his own pocket, can he remain peaceful? He cannot. The teacher calls this a simple principle to understand.

#### "Remove the first two words" [40:00 – 42:00]

- A man was praying, "God, I want peace; O God, I want peace." He heard a voice: "Remove the first two words and you will get it." Remove "I want," and what is left is peace.
- Because of "I want" — the _saṅkalpa_ of _kāma_ — a person gets stuck.
- The more prominent one keeps the desire to please the Lord and to please the spiritual master, the deeper the satisfaction one finds.
- For the conditioned soul this is a foreign concept, because before every act he thinks: "What's in it for me?"
- **The invitation to experiment.** Someone on the level of knowledge is on a different level; but at our level, if we want to experience these principles, we should experiment a little — do something selflessly and see. Only by doing it will we find out whether it works.

#### Knowledge as fire: the servant has no tension [42:00 – 44:00]

- As anything put into fire burns, so in the fire of this knowledge — "I am the Lord's servant" — the reactions of all one's work go away when one acts in that mood.
- When I am a servant, there is no tension; the tension belongs to the master.
- **Jaya and Vijaya.** When the four Kumāras went to Vishnu and Jaya and Vijaya made a mistake by stopping them, the Kumāras began to rebuke and curse them. Lord Vishnu came running and said: the mistake of the servant goes to the master. The teacher recounts that Vishnu said He was ready to give up His arms for this. [Wording flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- So the more one sees oneself as the Lord's servant, the less anything touches one; and those who are learned know that such a person is acting rightly.

#### The purport: eternal servitorship in every role [44:00 – 46:00]

- A Krishna conscious person is basically free from all determinations for sense gratification, and he is free because he sees himself as the Lord's eternal servant.
- Whatever we do — a job, taking care of the family, anything — we can do it as the Lord's servant, leaving ourselves aside. The teacher stresses that this is not only theory but a science: the Lord has given the principle, and the Bhāgavatam gives its examples.
- **The adventure of a devotee's life.** A materialist's life has no real adventure: he is a slave of the senses and the mind, working like a donkey — _go-dāsa_, a servant of the senses. For devotees there is adventure, because śāstra gives so many principles — so much homework. Take a verse that touched you and try applying it that day; applying even 5% is a lot.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.19 begins the portrait of one whose work brings no reaction: none of his undertakings is driven by the determination for sense gratification. The root misconception is "I am the enjoyer"; the cure is the knowledge "I am the Lord's servant," which burns up reactions like fire, since a servant's mistakes go to the master. Remove the "I want" and peace remains — and the teacher urges actually experimenting with selfless action to see that it works. [38:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.20 [46:00 – 56:00]

<!-- verse:4.20 -->

**Context.** The first line restates the previous verse — he gives up attachment to the fruit of work, so he does not act for sense gratification — and the verse then adds two adjectives. [46:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tyaktvā karma-phalāsaṅgam_: Having completely given up attachment to the fruit of work. [46:00]
- _Nitya-tṛptaḥ_: Always fully satisfied. [46:00]
- _Nirāśrayaḥ_: Without any material shelter — he takes only Krishna's shelter. [46:00]
- _Karmaṇy abhipravṛtto 'pi naiva kiñcit karoti saḥ_: Though fully engaged in works, he incurs no reaction. [46:00]

#### Only one who does not want to enjoy can serve [48:00]

- One who does not want to enjoy can serve. When we want to enjoy, everything becomes "rubbish," and we cannot serve well — though service is the nature of the _jīva_.
- There is not much enjoyment in the world; we think enjoyment will bring happiness, and it brings misery.
- If one acts even a little selflessly, giving up the material result — and especially if one acts for the Lord's pleasure — the result is _nitya-tṛpti_, satisfaction, and _nirāśraya_: no need for material shelter.

#### The thought experiment: losing everything [48:00 – 52:00]

- The teacher asks us to reflect: how many things do I need in life?
- Suppose the Lord's special mercy falls on you today. You go to work and your boss — the school principal, the company director — says, "That's enough, you've done a lot of work, thank you," and you lose the job and the position. You come home and see a bulldozer bringing the house down. Position gone, possessions gone. The friend leaves too; the family that was with you leaves. Everything stored in cupboards and trunks is gone.
- But your bead bag and a copy of the Bhagavad-gita are saved. In one way, nothing of yours has gone — everything is saved: Krishna is with you, the bead bag, the Gita, the Bhāgavatam. Everything belongs to Krishna; those things were not yours anyway.
- **It will all go anyway.** Will the job go one day or not? People are confused — they have never thought about it: "I won't leave the job till I die; I'll work even in the ICU." Will the possessions go? Will anyone take a 2BHK to Yamarāja's abode? Will the people with us go? Everything will go. [50:00]
- The point: the more Krishna comes into one's life, the less need there is for these things — whether people leave or wealth goes. [52:00]

#### High prayers and small attachments [52:00]

- We read prayers of devotees at a very high level of pure devotion — the teacher refers to the Sixth Canto of the Bhāgavatam, that those devoted to Nārāyaṇa see all conditions alike — and think we could never pray like that. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- But we should understand: leave that aside — if we cannot even give up small things, cannot be free of attachment to small things, then some mistake is happening in our application of Krishna consciousness.
- Someone like Vṛtrāsura, who had seen Saṅkarṣaṇa, was ready to give up even heaven — and we do not even know what heaven is.
- The point is that it is the nature of bhakti to bring satisfaction, and as satisfaction comes one becomes _nitya-tṛpta_ — there is no need for a second or third thing, and an inner satisfaction remains.

#### The millionaire and the beggars [52:00 – 54:00]

- Invite a millionaire and say, "I want to give you a donation — ₹1," or ₹10 to a crorepati. What will he say? "I don't need it." He is satisfied.
- On a Vṛndāvana or Govardhana _parikramā_, many beggars sit in a line. Take out a bundle of ten-rupee notes and give one person ten — they will understand at once and come running.
- That is the difference — _nitya-tṛpta_, _nirāśraya_ — that we have to see in Krishna consciousness: how deeply satisfied we are with Krishna, just being with Krishna, just serving.
- If service has to be motivated by acknowledgement, appreciation or recognition, that is an indication that the heart is empty. Just serving Krishna is very satisfying.

#### Insecurity without Krishna's shelter [54:00 – 56:00]

- Until one has Krishna's shelter, the _jīva_ remains insecure. How much wealth would make him secure — 5 lakh, 15 lakh, 1.5 crore? You will never be able to fix the figure.
- The proof is simple: the one with 5 lakh is unhappy and wants more; the one with 15 or 50 lakh is unhappy; the one with 1.5 crore also wants more.
- As one gets the Lord's shelter, the need for money, education, fame and followers as shelter falls away, and so he is not attached to the fruit of work — and though doing a great deal, gets no reaction.
- **The purport.** The teacher notes the purports in this series run on almost the same line: such a person is free from the bondage of work and fully in Krishna consciousness; he is not attached even for his own maintenance, having left everything to the Lord, and does not hold on to things to preserve them. [56:00]
- **The levels are rising.** He points out that each verse adds one thing: the previous verse said he is free from the determination for enjoyment; here he is not only free of it but deeply satisfied within, and takes no material shelter — not even for his own livelihood.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.20 adds two marks: _nitya-tṛpta_, always satisfied, and _nirāśraya_, taking no material shelter. Only one who does not want to enjoy can truly serve. The thought experiment of losing job, home, family and possessions while keeping the bead bag and the Gita shows that nothing that is really ours is lost — and all of it will go anyway. Like a millionaire refusing ten rupees, a satisfied devotee needs no recognition, and without Krishna's shelter no amount of wealth ever makes the _jīva_ secure. [46:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.21 [56:00 – 62:00]

<!-- verse:4.21 -->

**Context.** The level rises gradually again: one more word is added — _nirāśīḥ_, free of material desire — along with control of mind and body and freedom from possessiveness. [56:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nirāśīḥ_: Without material desire — no desire within at all. [56:00]
- _Yata-cittātmā_: His body and inner faculties (_antaḥkaraṇa_) are under control — because there is no material desire. [58:00]
- _Tyakta-sarva-parigrahaḥ_: He has given up all possessiveness — no unnecessary collecting. [58:00]
- _Śārīraṁ kevalaṁ karma_: He works only to maintain the body. [60:00]
- _Nāpnoti kilbiṣam_: He incurs no sin. [60:00]

#### External and internal renunciation [56:00]

There are two things:

1. **Externally giving up the objects of sense gratification.**
2. **Internally giving up the desire to enjoy the objects of the senses** — which is what _nirāśīḥ_ describes: no desire within.

#### Where a devotee begins: regulation first [58:00]

- For devotees, everything starts with what the Lord says in BG 6.17 — regulation. First bodily activity should be regulated; eating should be regulated, rather than eating at any time and any amount.
- After regulation, focus on bhakti; then gradually everything begins to drop away by itself.
- One whose life never comes under regulation finds focus practically impossible — he gives something up and then grabs it fully again.
- The principle: **do the needful for material life; focus on spiritual life.**

#### Possessiveness: the mind makes non-essentials essential [58:00 – 60:00]

- _Parigraha_ means unnecessary collecting. The teacher connects it to Rūpa Gosvāmī's _Upadeśāmṛta_, where _atyāhāra_ means over-accumulating — the senses' over-intake.
- **The nature of the mind** is to interpret non-essentials as essentials: whatever is useless, the mind says is very important.
- **The great cleaning.** Temples sometimes have a "mahā-cleaning," and people do it at home too; the first principle of cleaning is elimination, but letting go is hard. The teacher recounts taking on the cleaning of one room at home and throwing out whatever was useless; later his mother saw it — "this is good, this is good" — and brought 30–40% of it back inside, and the remaining 60% was thrown out only after negotiation. [Details flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- What is not being used should be given away — use it, or gift it, or donate it. Useless collection should be avoided: it occupies space in the mind and in consciousness, and it is a kind of violence when others do not have it. Keep as much as is needed.

#### Working only to maintain the body [60:00 – 62:00]

- Such a person works only to maintain the body, knowing that the body will go one day, and incurs no sin.
- He is **not a lazy person**: he knows what work he has to do.

#### The purport: the hand and the machine part [62:00]

- **The hand.** As a hand moves and does its work in the body, cooperating for the body, the Krishna conscious devotee always dovetails with the supreme desire — he has no desire for personal satisfaction, and works according to the Lord's desire.
- **The machine part.** He works like a part of a machine: a machine part needs some oiling, and just so much he keeps for maintaining himself, while remaining engaged in the Lord's service. He does nothing for his own enjoyment and has no proprietorship even over his own body. The teacher calls this a good example.
- **No unfair means.** Prabhupada adds at the end that he uses no unfair means — in earning or in anything else — and so he is free from all sinful reaction.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.21 takes renunciation inward: not only giving up sense objects outwardly but having no desire for them within, with mind and body controlled and no possessiveness. For a devotee this starts with regulation — do the needful for material life, focus on spiritual life — and includes resisting the mind's habit of making non-essentials essential. Working only to maintain the body is not laziness; like a hand serving the body or a machine part needing just enough oil, he works for the Lord's desire and incurs no sin. [56:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.22 [62:00 – 74:00]

<!-- verse:4.22 -->

**Context.** The description continues with satisfaction in whatever comes of its own accord, freedom from dualities and envy, and evenness in success and failure. [62:00 – 64:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yadṛcchā-lābha-santuṣṭaḥ_: Satisfied with gain that comes of its own accord. [64:00]
- _Dvandvātītaḥ_: Beyond dualities — happiness and distress, cold and heat at the level of the body, honour and dishonour at the level of the mind and ego. [66:00]
- _Vimatsaraḥ_: Without envy in the heart, because he has a higher goal. [66:00]
- _Samaḥ siddhāv asiddhau ca_: Equal in success and failure. [70:00]
- _Kṛtvāpi na nibadhyate_: Though acting, he is not bound. [70:00]

#### What comes of its own accord [64:00]

- Śāstra says again and again: don't over-endeavour for material life — it is not a good way to live.
- On YouTube we watch so many videos about how someone topped, how hard he worked; coaching centres advertise scores. Śāstra says material things do not change much.
- **Duty is important** — a person should not be lazy — but he should know the limit. Be satisfied with the gain that comes of its own accord.
- **Use the same energy spiritually.** We should also know why not to over-endeavour: if one does, there is no time and no consciousness left for bhakti; if one does one's duty without over-endeavouring, the same energy can be used to grow spiritually.
- Someone may say: "Can't I overdo both — material life and spiritual life?" The teacher says it does not work that way: one overdoes one thing and does duty in the other — and life is very short, time is limited.
- No one is satisfied by material gain. So many great businessmen came and went; he mentions that Ratan Tata, who passed away a short while ago, was still expanding — he was not satisfied by business.

#### Beyond dualities, free from envy [66:00 – 70:00]

- He is beyond dualities and has no envy in his heart, because he is focusing on a higher goal — the Lord's satisfaction — so lower things provoke no envy in him.
- Envy on the material level sounds like: "He's got such a nice car; he has such a big house; mine is so small."
- **Envy never ends materially.** However much you have, earn or accumulate, there will be people who have more than you. There will always be someone above.
- We hear śāstra stories of someone attacking Indra, or a demon attacking Krishna, and think how ugly it is to envy Krishna. The teacher says we are a micro-model, a mini-scale model, of the same demon — we simply cannot think that big, and are struggling at a lower level.
- **The communist joke.** One communist asks another: "Brother, if you had two bungalows, wouldn't you give me one?" — "Definitely." "If you had two BMWs?" — "Definitely." "If you had two cows?" — "Definitely not." "Why?" — "Because I have two cows." The other two were only theory, so there was no difficulty. [66:00 – 68:00]
- **"If I had it, I would..."** Sometimes someone says, "Prabhuji, if I had it I'd have built a temple myself; I'd have done this for Ṭhākurjī." The teacher's response: say what you are doing with what you have now — don't talk about the future.
- **Competition among beggars.** Envy has no end because there will always be someone with more. People of earlier ages were more talented, so their envy was seen on a bigger scale; we compete at a petty level — a competition going on among beggars. And if one kept winning that competition, one would finally become envious of Krishna, because Krishna has all opulence — the very meaning of _Bhagavān_. This person, by contrast, wants to serve the Lord, not compete with Him. [68:00 – 70:00]

#### Equal in success and failure: the _bhaṇḍārā_ line [70:00 – 72:00]

- Sometimes success comes, sometimes failure; but he has done his duty, so he is not greatly affected.
- **The higher the goal, the less lower things affect one.** One whose goal is not spiritual will be greatly affected on the material level; everything depends on the goal. This person's goal is not to enjoy here but to serve, so happiness and distress, honour and dishonour, loss and gain, success and failure do not disturb him.
- **The free-food line.** By the roadside people often hold a _bhaṇḍārā_ — purī, sabzī, cholé, kheer being distributed. If your mind is already set that you don't want to take anything, it doesn't matter to you whether the line is short or long. But if you want to take that prasāda: a long line brings sorrow, a short line brings happiness, ten people leaving the line brings great joy, and five people cutting into the middle brings great disturbance. The teacher adds with a laugh that in the prasāda line many old relationships are suddenly reawakened. All of it is for the one who wants to take; for one who doesn't, it makes no difference.
- So when one is engaged in the Lord's service, life becomes so easy; otherwise life runs on a very complex mechanism. [72:00]

#### The purport: changing direction takes lifetimes [72:00 – 74:00]

- Dualities do not affect the Krishna conscious person much; he works for Krishna's satisfaction, whether there is success or failure — he is not doing it for himself.
- Being satisfied in working for Krishna's satisfaction is a change of **direction**, and changing the direction from the enjoying mentality to service takes many births. If someone's whole consciousness has changed direction and he is able to remain in that direction, that is a very big thing.
- Otherwise most people — devotee or not — do the same one thing: they stay busy expanding their material life, and the result is of the same kind.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.22 describes one satisfied with what comes of its own accord: he does his duty without over-endeavouring, so the same energy is free for spiritual growth. Free from dualities and envy because his goal is higher, he sees that material envy can never end — there is always someone with more, and the competition ends in envying Krishna Himself. Like one who does not want the free food, he is untroubled by the length of the line; turning one's direction from enjoyment to service takes lifetimes, and staying in it is a very big thing. [62:00 – 74:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.23 [74:00 – 78:00]

<!-- verse:4.23 -->

**Context.** In this verse the Lord concludes the description: one free from attachment and liberated, whose consciousness is situated in knowledge, works for _yajña_, and his whole work dissolves. [74:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Gata-saṅgasya muktasya_: One whose attachment has gone and who has become free — untouched by the modes and by the fruit. [74:00]
- _Jñānāvasthita-cetasaḥ_: Whose consciousness is situated on the level of knowledge, of self-realization — and so is not attached to petty things. [74:00]
- _Yajñāyācarataḥ karma_: Who works for _yajña_ — for the One whom _yajña_ pleases — and for nothing else. [76:00 – 78:00]
- _Samagraṁ pravilīyate_: The entire reaction of his work becomes zero; his work becomes transcendental. [78:00]

#### Petty attachments: the empty bottles on the train [74:00 – 76:00]

- The teacher calls them petty material attachments (_tuccha āsakti_) — the things we do not want to give up, and because of which we do not want to take hold of Krishna consciousness.
- **The train.** Before getting off, every passenger checks his luggage to make sure nothing important is left, and takes everything important. Then the garbage collector comes and very carefully picks up each empty bottle. What was unimportant to the passenger is very important to him.
- In the same way, what is important to us is not important to great souls. Just as we think, "He's picking up useless garbage," they look at us and think the same. [76:00]
- The materialist's nature is to preserve, protect and nourish — keeping everything carefully so that not a single material thing slips away.

#### The desire itself is the problem [76:00]

- The materialist thinks the problem is that his desire was not fulfilled: "The problem is I only have a WagonR — when the Swift comes there'll be no problem." Then: "The problem is I don't have an Innova." "The problem is I only have a cooler."
- He does not know that the problem is not that the desire was not fulfilled — **the desire itself is the problem.**
- **The mobile data.** Someone watching only garbage on his phone, with nothing useful, runs out of data and thinks, "The problem is my data is finished." The problem is not that the data finished; the data he was using was the problem.
- As devotees we can see this a little in others' lives — but our own lives have many such problems too.

#### Knowledge and _yajña_ [76:00 – 78:00]

- His consciousness is situated in knowledge. For the _jñānī_, that knowledge is "I am not the body, I am the soul." For the _bhakta_, it is "I am the Lord's servant; His service is my life."
- Then he works only for _yajña_ — for the One whom _yajña_ pleases — and when he works like this, all the reaction becomes zero and his work becomes transcendental, which the next verse explains.
- **Krishna to Arjuna:** "Work in this way for My pleasure; you will get no reaction. It is the best way. Do not hesitate at all — whatever you have to do, do it for My pleasure." [78:00]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.23 concludes the portrait: free of attachment and situated in knowledge — for the devotee, "I am the Lord's servant" — he works only for _yajña_, and the entire reaction of his work dissolves. What we guard as important looks to great souls like empty bottles picked up on a train, and the real problem is not that our desires remain unfulfilled but that we have them. Krishna's instruction to Arjuna is therefore to act for His pleasure without hesitation. [74:00 – 78:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.24 [78:00 – 84:00]

<!-- verse:4.24 -->

**Context.** Krishna now shows what happens to work done in this consciousness: everything involved in it becomes spiritual. [78:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Brahma_: The word appears many times in the verse and has many meanings — the Supreme Brahman is _brahma_, and the soul is also called _brahma_. Here it means **spiritual**; the teacher points out that Srila Prabhupada's word-for-word gives "spiritual in nature." [78:00]
- _Brahmārpaṇam_: What is being offered is spiritual. [78:00]
- _Brahma haviḥ_: The ghee is spiritual. [78:00]
- _Brahmāgnau_: The fire is spiritual. [78:00]
- _Brahmaṇā hutam_: The oblation offered into the fire is spiritual. [78:00]
- _Brahmaiva tena gantavyam_: The destination he will reach is spiritual. [80:00]
- _Brahma-karma-samādhinā_: And all the work becomes spiritual. [80:00]

#### Consciousness spiritualizes everything involved [80:00]

- The simple way to understand the verse: a person working with the consciousness of offering to the Lord and pleasing the Lord spiritualizes everything involved in that process.
- **The sacrifice, concretely.** He may have taken a wooden spoon to make the offering — in a _yajña_ one pours in the oblation saying _svāhā_ — and is pouring Amul ghee into a burning fire. Yet all of it becomes spiritual.

#### Compositional sense and application sense [80:00 – 82:00]

The teacher explains how, by distinguishing two senses in which things exist:

1. **Compositional sense** — what the thing is made of. The spoon is wood; the ghee is Amul.
2. **Application sense** — what, and for whom, the thing is being used.

- **Prasāda.** In compositional sense it is made of rice, dal and wheat, so it is material; but because it has been offered to the Lord, it is spiritual.
- **The guru's body.** The body of the spiritual master is, by composition, material — earth, water, air, fire, ether and the other elements are in it — but because he is fully engaged in the Lord's service, it is spiritual.
- The point the Lord is making: everything becomes spiritual if the consciousness is spiritual. [82:00]

#### The battlefield as the sacrificial arena [82:00]

- The teacher gives Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's reading: Krishna wants to tell Arjuna to see this battlefield itself as the _yajña_, like a sacrificial pit.
- Arjuna says: "You speak so much of _yajña_ — then I suppose I'll have to go to the forest and do _yajña_ there." The Lord says: no — this battlefield is the place of sacrifice, your Gāṇḍīva is what you will offer the oblations with, and the oblation is the Kauravas. This is the _yajña_, and there will be no reaction.

#### The purport [82:00]

- In the first line of his purport, Srila Prabhupada explains in simple words how activities in Krishna consciousness lead one ultimately to the spiritual goal.
- As long as one is a conditioned soul, the same thing used for one's own senses is material; when it is used in the Lord's service, it becomes spiritualized. The whole purport, the teacher notes, explains this one thing.

#### Closing the section [82:00 – 84:00]

- Through this section, up to BG 4.24, the Lord has stated the principle again and again: the course of action is very deep and must be understood, and the _jīva_ should act without desire for results — for Krishna's pleasure, as Prabhupada says in the purport. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **No work can be judged from outside.** We cannot tell, by looking at an activity externally, whether it is spiritual or material; one needs to go a little deeper. Spiritual life is understood by going deep.
- He declares the section successfully completed. [84:00]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
BG 4.24 shows the outcome of working for the Lord's pleasure: the offering, the ghee, the fire, the act and the destination all become _brahma_ — spiritual. By composition, a wooden spoon, Amul ghee, prasāda or the guru's body is material; by application — being used for the Lord — it is spiritual. So the battlefield itself becomes Arjuna's sacrificial arena and the Gāṇḍīva his ladle, and no work can be judged spiritual or material from the outside. [78:00 – 84:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

**1. The fruit always comes to mind first — how do we condition ourselves to take the Lord's desire as the goal? [84:00 – 90:00]**

- A questioner explains that whenever he starts anything, its fruit is already in his mind: in the Bhakti Shastri course he thinks of the exam — five verses to learn, a paper to pass. With career choices the aim is often food, clothing and shelter, because that is how one was nurtured. He describes trying: as a doctor expected from 9 to 1, if he arrives at 9:15 he tries to stay till 1:15 and see the patients the Lord sends in that time. How can this conditioning come?
- **Go deeper into spiritual practice.** The teacher's answer is that we have to go deeper into our spiritual practice.
- **The principle:** we should not be attached to the fruit. We can be concerned about the fruit, but it should not be the primary motivation. In Bhakti Shastri we can be concerned about the exams, but the primary motivation should be learning — and the fruit is already hidden within learning; one who learns will get it automatically.
- **The hawk and the fish.** He gives Radhanath Swami's example: sitting at Rishikesh, he watched a hawk dive into the Ganga and come up with a fish in its claws. Two lessons:

  2. Had the fish been swimming deeper, the hawk could not have caught it — similarly, go deeper into meditation and spiritual practice.
- **The road and the vehicle.** On a road, the potholes and speed-breakers cannot be changed, but one can travel by battery rickshaw or by Innova; on the same road the rickshaw rider feels the jolts more and the Innova rider less.
