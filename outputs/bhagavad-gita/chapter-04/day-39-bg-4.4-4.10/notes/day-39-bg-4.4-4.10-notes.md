### Bhagavad-gita 4.5 [00:00 – 14:00]

<!-- verse:4.5 -->

**Context.** Krishna answers: both He and Arjuna have passed through many births. He knows them all; Arjuna does not. [00:00 – 02:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bahūni me vyatītāni janmāni tava ca_: Not one but many, many births of Mine and of yours have passed — I have appeared many times, and you too have come many times. [00:00 – 02:00]
- _Tāny ahaṁ veda sarvāṇi_: I know all of them. [00:00 – 02:00]
- _Na tvaṁ vettha_: You do not know them. [00:00 – 02:00]
- _Parantapa_: In general, one who scorches or defeats his enemy; the teacher also reads it here as indicating that Arjuna is **pure** — his character is completely pure. So Arjuna has both purity and power, and still he cannot remember everything. [04:00 – 06:00]
- _Acyuta_: The Lord's name used in the purport — **the one who never forgets**, even after coming in contact with the material world. [14:00 – 16:00]

#### The Lord's associates come with Him, and forget [00:00 – 04:00]

- Whenever the Lord descends, His eternal associates (_nitya-pārṣadas_) come with Him in different forms, and in every incarnation the Lord gives instruction.
- The Lord is omniscient and the Lord of all; His memory is never deleted.
- Arjuna forgets because of _yoga-māyā_ — the pastime potency covers his knowledge so that the pastime can take place.
- From the purport: devotees like Arjuna are the Lord's eternal associates and constant companions; they come when He comes, but they forget. [12:00 – 14:00]

#### From the purport: Krishna and His expansions [02:00 – 04:00]

- Srila Prabhupada's purport quotes the _Brahma-saṁhitā_ to explain the Lord's position as always transcendental. [Quotations flagged; see Verification Flags.]
  - The first verse describes the Lord as absolute (_advaita_), infallible (_acyuta_ — He never falls), without beginning, appearing in many forms; rare to reach through the Vedas, yet accessible to devotees.
  - The second verse explains that Krishna is _svayaṁ bhagavān_, the original Personality of Godhead, while in His repeated descents He is situated in regulated portions (_kalā_).
- The teacher's gloss: in other incarnations, the particular potency required is manifested. Kapila is also the Lord, but in Him it is mainly the Lord's knowledge potency that is manifested. The complete Lord is Krishna.

#### The living entity and the Lord are never equal [04:00 – 06:00; 14:00]

- Arjuna forgets under the influence of time. Srila Prabhupada writes that however powerful a living entity may be, there remains a difference between him and the Lord.
- The teacher's image: one chariot, both seated on it — Arjuna and Krishna — and still Krishna is always God. Time has no influence on Him; He knows past, present and future.
- From the purport: a living entity, however great in material estimation, can never equal the Supreme Lord. [14:00 – 16:00]

#### No injustice ever happens to any living entity [06:00 – 10:00]

- The teacher calls this a very important principle for understanding the verse. Much of the material pain or pleasure we receive in this life is connected with past lives, but we do not know that past — and so everyone is confused.
- Without faith in dharma, a person tries to solve each problem by hook or by crook — get money, do something, get ahead. Repeatedly the feeling comes: "Something wrong is happening to me, this is injustice, my relatives don't understand me."
- **His central statement: no injustice ever happens to any living entity.** To call the present an injustice you would need to know your past — only then can you comment on the present. Because we do not know what we did in previous lives, we call it injustice. Every situation has some connection with our own _karma_.
- **Where the energy should go.** The real question is how to use the situation to come closer to the Lord. Instead, a person often puts his energy into causing pain to the one through whom the pain came.
  - The situation must be dealt with, since it is present now — but the thinking "this is injustice" is wrong.
  - Secondly, one should not now do wrong in return. Adopting a "tit for tat" formula makes everything complex: we did something wrong before, we are getting its fruit, and now we retaliate and do more wrong.
  - His illustration: a criminal is caught by the police and then starts fighting in the lock-up — his punishment only increases.

#### Krishna knows best, and Krishna is most merciful [08:00 – 12:00]

- This verse should generate great faith: _Krishna knows best and Krishna is most merciful._ The Lord knows everything, can do everything, and loves us the most. If that faith is there, life becomes very secure.
- Then there is only one job left for me: to take the Lord's shelter. I do not know the past, I do not know the future, and I am confused in the present.
- **Even full information would not help.** Suppose the living entity were told everything — this was the past, this is the future — he could not handle it. Even knowing all the details of one's family members' lives ("this is going to happen to them"), what could one do?
- Therefore bhakti is the best thing: the Lord knows everything, can do everything and loves us most — "the whole project is finished." Put full energy into bhakti.
- **The balance he prescribes:**
  - Put full energy into bhakti.
  - Along with it, live one's life as duty, without becoming too entangled in it.
  - The wrong way of living is the reverse — reducing bhakti to a minimal duty while becoming fully entangled in material life. Whoever lives that way will greatly regret it.
- Days like Ekadashi give that opportunity; ideally, every day should be lived in the same way. [10:00 – 12:00]

#### We have already enjoyed it all before [12:00 – 14:00]

- The human birth is very rare. The Lord knows everything and I do not; so in this birth one should run toward the Lord, not toward sense gratification.
- Practically all types of sense gratification and enjoyment for which we are hankering and striving, we have already enjoyed in previous lives — we have simply forgotten.
- **The principle:** this birth is not for solving all material problems; it is for doing bhakti well. A person who gets a human body and engages in solving material problems gets stuck in even more serious problems. Tolerate the small things going on in life and keep the main focus on dharma. "Focus is important. This is life."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Krishna remembers every birth; Arjuna, though pure and powerful, forgets — and so do we. Because the causes of our present situation lie in a past we cannot see, no injustice ever happens to any living entity; the energy spent on retaliation should go into using the situation to come closer to the Lord. Since Krishna knows best, can do everything and loves us most, the only job left is to take His shelter, put full energy into bhakti, and live the rest as duty. [00:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.6 [14:00 – 20:00]

<!-- verse:4.6 -->

**Context.** If the Lord comes again and again and the living entities also come again and again, what is the difference? Krishna now explains how He comes. [14:00 – 16:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ajo 'pi_: Although I am unborn. And yet we celebrate Janmashtami — the Lord says He does not take birth. [14:00 – 16:00]
- _Avyayātmā_: Imperishable — I am never destroyed. [14:00 – 16:00]
- _Bhūtānām īśvaraḥ_: The Lord of all beings, that is, of all living entities. When He appears, He does not have to _become_ God. [14:00 – 18:00]
- _Prakṛtiṁ svām adhiṣṭhāya_: Situated in My own nature — _prakṛti_ here means His **transcendental** nature, not material nature. He comes in His own form. [14:00 – 18:00]
- _Sambhavāmy ātma-māyayā_: I manifest, I appear, by My own internal energy. [16:00 – 18:00]

#### Appearing like the sun [16:00; 20:00]

- The Lord always exists, as the sun always exists; sometimes it becomes visible to us.
- When the sun manifests in the east it is not born. The Lord's coming into this world is the same — it is not like birth in the material world.

#### He does not have to become God [16:00 – 18:00]

- As a tiny baby only five or six days old, He killed Pūtanā — whose size the teacher gives as twelve miles long. The Lord is always the Lord.
- _Mahā-māyā_ is subordinate to the Lord. _Yoga-māyā_ is also the Lord's potency, non-different from Him; He comes through it to facilitate His pastimes.

#### His appearance versus the living entity's birth [16:00 – 20:00]

- **The living entity:** comes dependent. His happiness and distress follow from past _karma_, and his body undergoes six changes — it is small, it grows, and so on.
- **The Lord:** has no material body, and His body never changes. He does not come under the control of material nature; He appears in the same body and lives in this world without being touched by it, as the teacher illustrates with the lotus. [Caption flagged; see Verification Flags.]
  - He never went beyond the _kiśora_ age. At the time of the battle He was over a hundred years old, with many grandchildren at home, yet always youthful — His body is _aprākṛta_, transcendental.
  - We never see a picture of Krishna grown old. Yet He is the oldest person in all creation — past, present and future — the _ādi-purāṇa-puruṣa_. He never ages, and His intelligence never diminishes.
- **Memory as the contrast.** An ordinary person forgets what happened a few hours before; ask what he ate a day ago and he will have to think hard. Yet foolish people call themselves God — while the Lord remembers everything. [18:00 – 20:00]
- From the purport: in this verse the Lord describes the special nature of His birth.

#### _Ātma-māyā_ also means causeless mercy [20:00 – 22:00]

- Srila Prabhupada gives another meaning of _ātma-māyā_: by His causeless mercy He comes into this world.
- That raises the next question — **when** does He come? The next verse answers it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Krishna's descent is not a birth. He is unborn, imperishable and the Lord of all; He appears in His own unchanging transcendental form through His internal potency, as the sun appears on the horizon. The living entity comes dependent, under past _karma_, in a body that changes and forgets; the Lord comes by His own will and mercy, never ages, and never forgets. [14:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.7 [20:00 – 28:00]

<!-- verse:4.7 -->

**Context.** The verse of the Gita that everyone remembers — at least this one. The teacher calls it very important because it reveals the Lord's compassion. [20:00 – 22:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yadā yadā_: Whenever and wherever — Srila Prabhupada's translation. [20:00 – 22:00]
- _Dharmasya glānir bhavati_: Whenever there is a decline of dharma. [20:00 – 22:00]
- _Abhyutthānam adharmasya_: And irreligion increases. [20:00 – 22:00]
- _Tadātmānaṁ sṛjāmy aham_: Then I manifest Myself. _Sṛjāmi_ means the Lord manifests. [20:00 – 22:00; 26:00 – 28:00]

#### Two important things in this verse [20:00 – 22:00]

1. **The observance of dharma.** The Lord has given the criterion: when dharma goes down, I come. This means maintaining dharma is extremely important.
   - Especially today, most people want to do whatever is easy.
   - What matters is to think about what is right — what is according to dharma — and do that. In the long term it will benefit.
   - A shortcut leads to difficulty later. [Caption flagged; see Verification Flags.]
2. **The Lord's commitment.** He has a very great commitment to saving the living entities. Like a mother who is always ready for her child — whether he wakes at night or at any time of day — the Lord says He will come at any time, again and again.

#### Three meanings of _yadā yadā_ [22:00 – 24:00]

The teacher notes he has discussed this verse a great deal before, and that there are many explanations. He gives three:

1. **At whatever time it is needed.** All the incarnations appeared at different times: Rāma at midday, Krishna at midnight, Nṛsiṁhadeva in the evening; Balarāma, Rādhārāṇī and Vāmanadeva each at a different time.
2. **In whatever form it is needed.** He came in the form of a horse, of a boar, as Nṛsiṁha, as Vāmana, as Matsya, as Kūrma.
3. **In whatever place it is needed.** Nṛsiṁhadeva came out of a pillar, Kūrmadeva in the ocean, and Krishna appeared in a jail.

#### Unlimited incarnations, because unlimited love [24:00 – 26:00]

- The Lord's incarnations are innumerable — as many as the waves of the ocean.
- Why so many? **Because His love for us is unlimited.**
- **The hostel and the home:** a child living in a hostel — the warden comes and asks whether he has eaten, perhaps once. But at home, how many times will the mother ask? There is no limit; if he doesn't eat, she asks again and again. In the same way the Lord says: I come, an unlimited number of times.

#### Appearance, not birth [26:00 – 28:00]

- In this world a living entity has a _birthday_; the Lord has an _appearance_ (_prākaṭya_).
- **The living entity** comes bound by _karma_. According to past _karma_ he receives a body, parents, and a situation in this world. [Verse fragment flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **The Lord** comes by His own sweet will, choosing His parents.
- **The mood of the event differs completely:**
  - When a living entity is born there is extreme pain — the child cries a great deal, the mother suffers; only the others celebrate.
  - When the Lord appears, He comes with great joy; the descriptions of His appearance show Him very pleased, and His parents too are very happy. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]

#### The Lord's schedule, and why He is not bound by it [26:00 – 28:00]

- From the purport: the Lord does have a schedule — in one day of Brahmā He appears at the end of the _dvāpara_ of the twenty-eighth _catur-yuga_.
- But the Lord is not obliged to follow this rule. Hence _yadā yadā hi dharmasya_: whenever dharma declines, He comes, taking different incarnations, each incarnation with its own purpose.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
_Yadā yadā_ is the verse of the Lord's compassion: He comes at whatever time, in whatever form and in whatever place He is needed, because His love for us is unlimited — like a mother who asks again and again whether her child has eaten. Its demand on us is that dharma matters: the Lord's own criterion for descending is its decline, so we should do what is right rather than what is easy. And His coming is an appearance by His own will, full of joy, not a birth forced by _karma_. [20:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.8 [28:00 – 38:00]

<!-- verse:4.8 -->

**Context.** Having explained **how** He comes (4.6) and **when** He comes (4.7), the Lord now explains **why** He comes. [28:00 – 30:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paritrāṇāya sādhūnām_: To deliver the _sādhus_. Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura explains _paritrāṇa_ as delivering **fully**, completely. [28:00 – 30:00]
- _Vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām_: To destroy the miscreants. [28:00 – 30:00]
- _Dharma-saṁsthāpanārthāya_: To establish dharma — _saṁsthāpanā_ meaning establishing it _samyak_, fully and perfectly. [34:00 – 36:00]
- _Sambhavāmi yuge yuge_: I appear, age after age. [28:00 – 30:00; 34:00 – 36:00]

#### Who is a _sādhu_? [30:00 – 32:00]

- One whose nature is to do good to others.
- One engaged with faith in remembering the Lord's name, form, qualities and pastimes.
- One whose life's goal is the welfare of others and the pleasure of the Lord.
- One who gives no importance to the _asat_ — the changing world.
- From the purport: whoever is in Krishna consciousness is a _sādhu_. [36:00 – 38:00]

#### Delivered from what? Levels of the answer [30:00 – 34:00]

Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura raises the question: a _sādhu_ is already delivered and will leave the material world anyway — so from what does the Lord come to deliver him? The teacher gives answers at different levels. [Caption numbering flagged; see Verification Flags.]

- **Protecting their faith.** Suffering also comes into devotees' lives, but devotees are not much concerned with it, because they do not value material life very much. What the Lord protects is their **faith**. Did He solve all of the Pāṇḍavas' problems? No — He protected their faith.
- **Relieving the pain of separation.** The Lord's devotees have a deep longing to see Krishna and feel intense suffering in separation from Him; their hearts carry an intense eagerness to see Him and His pastimes. The Lord comes to reciprocate with them in love. [32:00 – 34:00]
  - If someone says, "They are already liberated — why do You need to come?", the answer is: "I am coming to free them from the pain of separation from Me."
  - If someone says, "Then send someone else," the answer is: "They are suffering in separation from **Me**, so I Myself must come."

#### Who are the _duṣkṛtīs_? [32:00 – 34:00]

- The simple meaning: He comes to destroy the wicked.
- The teacher refers to the verse in which the Lord describes four kinds of people who do not surrender to Him, the first being the _duṣkṛtīs_ — which he places in the Ninth Chapter. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- These _duṣkṛtīs_ are often very intelligent — scientists, engineers, doctors. Their brains work everywhere except in bhakti.
- **Definition:** one whose life runs against the Lord and against śāstra.
- One should not imagine a _duṣkṛtī_ is only the movie demon with two horns on his head. It includes:
  - one engaged in lies, deceit and cheating;
  - one breaking the four regulative principles;
  - one giving trouble to devotees.

#### Why must He come in person? [34:00 – 36:00]

The teacher raises the same objection for each of the purposes:

- **To destroy the miscreants?** The Lord has so many parts and could do it from where He is — the teacher's example is that corona came and so many people departed. Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura answers that these are **special** demons — he names Hiraṇyakaśipu, Rāvaṇa, Kaṁsa and others. "These are special. Only I come to kill them; no one else will be able to."
- **To establish dharma?** The word _dharma_ is important: in BG 2.7 Arjuna said he was confused about dharma, and here the Lord says He comes to establish it. Couldn't any representative — a person like Srila Prabhupada — establish dharma? The answer is in _saṁsthāpanā_: to establish it _samyak_, completely and perfectly, the Lord Himself comes.
- **_Yuge yuge_ in practice.** In Kali-yuga Caitanya Mahāprabhu came and established the _yuga-dharma_, _hari-nāma-saṅkīrtana_.

#### Establishing dharma in our own life [36:00 – 38:00]

- Everyone should follow the _yuga-dharma_. Establishing dharma is important in society, in this world, and in our own life.
- In Kali-yuga the _yuga-dharma_ is the chanting of the holy name. Whether someone likes it or not, we should take shelter of _hari-nāma_ properly.

#### From the purport: six categories of incarnation [36:00 – 38:00]

- Srila Prabhupada explains the different types of incarnations in six categories — the teacher asks students to look further down in the purport:
  1. _Puruṣa-avatāra_
  2. _Guṇa-avatāra_ — such as Brahmā and Viṣṇu
  3. _Līlā-avatāra_
  4. _Śaktyāveśa-avatāra_
  5. _Manvantara-avatāra_
  6. _Yuga-avatāra_
- Each has its own examples; finding them is set as **homework**.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord comes in person for reasons no representative could fulfil: to protect His devotees' faith and relieve them from the pain of separation from Him, which only His own presence can do; to kill special demons that no one else can; and to establish dharma completely. A _duṣkṛtī_ is not a horned villain but anyone whose life runs against the Lord and śāstra; a _sādhu_ is one who lives for others' welfare and the Lord's pleasure. In Kali-yuga, dharma is established through _hari-nāma_ — in society and in our own lives. [28:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.9 [38:00 – 44:00]

<!-- verse:4.9 -->

**Context.** Having explained how, when and why He comes, the Lord now states the result for one who understands these three verses. [38:00 – 40:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Janma karma ca me divyam_: My birth and My activities are divine. [38:00 – 40:00]
- _Divyam_: Beyond the three modes; beyond both the this-worldly and the other-worldly — not of this earth, and not of the heavenly planets either. [38:00 – 40:00]
- _Evaṁ yo vetti tattvataḥ_: One who knows this in truth. [40:00 – 42:00]
- _Tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti_: On leaving the body, he does not have to take a material body again. [40:00 – 42:00]

#### Divine birth, divine activities [38:00 – 40:00]

- The Lord appears independently, in His _sac-cid-ānanda_ form.
- Demigods in the heavenly planets have bodies superior to ours, but the Lord is above them all — as the Eleventh Chapter will say, even the demigods long for His _darśana_. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **His birth is special, and His activities are special too** — no ordinary living entity can fully replicate them:
  - As Krishna He killed Pūtanā and so many demons, lifted Govardhana, delivered so many people, and showed the whole universe in His mouth.
  - As Rāma, at a very young age He killed Tāṭakā and Subāhu.
- Both birth and activities are divine — both extraordinary.

#### _Tattvataḥ_ — knowing means surrendering [40:00 – 42:00]

- The teacher marks two important points, the first being _tattvataḥ_. To know Him in truth means **he will surrender** to the Lord.
- Knowing does not mean completing the Bhakti Shastri course or memorizing the verses. **Knowledge includes application.**
- His reference is the _catuḥ-ślokī_ of the Gita, BG 10.8:
  - First line: everything emanates from Me.
  - _Iti matvā_: one who accepts this — how will that be known?
  - _Bhajante mām_: he worships Me — and how?
  - _Bhāva-samanvitāḥ_: with feeling, with devotion. [Recitation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- So the first meaning: one who knows, accepts and adopts — knowledge is not theory.
- **The second meaning:** one who, in every situation of life, contemplates how to take the Lord's shelter — seeing the Lord's will in each circumstance and trying to take shelter of Him. [42:00 – 44:00]

#### Out of the jail, not a better cell [42:00 – 44:00]

- Not being born again is a very great benediction, because repeated birth is our main problem.
- **Why the Lord repeatedly says not to go to the demigods:** they cannot take us out of the jail; they only guarantee facilities inside it.
  - Having trouble in jail? Worship some demigod and one will install an AC.
  - Worship Varuṇa and he will install an RO water purifier and arrange drinking water.
  - Another will put in better furniture.
  - But you remain inside the jail.
- The Lord says: surrender to Me, and you will not have to come back to the jail at all.

#### Only after leaving the body? [42:00 – 44:00]

- A question arises: does this happen only after leaving the body, and not before?
- The commentator explains that the verse is stating the extreme point: such a person becomes free from the bondage of birth and death while still living in this world — and, at the extreme, he does not have to take birth again. [Name flagged; see Verification Flags.]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowing the Lord's birth and activities _in truth_ is not memorization but surrender: one who accepts that everything comes from Him worships Him with feeling, and seeks His shelter in every situation of life. Such a person is freed from repeated birth — not merely given more comfort inside the jail, as demigod worship offers, but taken out of it altogether, and even while living he is free. [38:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.10 [44:00 – 48:00]

<!-- verse:4.10 -->

**Context.** The Lord has said "whoever knows in truth" — so a question arises: has anyone actually attained this, leaving the material world and reaching His divine abode? This verse answers it. [44:00 – 46:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vīta_: Free from. [44:00 – 46:00]
- _Rāga, bhaya, krodha_: Attachment, fear and anger — the same three words that came in BG 2.56 describing the one of steady intelligence; only the last line differs. [44:00 – 46:00]

- _Mad-bhāvam āgatāḥ_: Attains My _bhāva_ — attains love for Me. [46:00 – 48:00]
- _Bahavaḥ_: Many. [46:00 – 48:00]

#### Not an experiment [46:00 – 48:00]

- How many have attained this so far? One, two, three? **Many.**
- The teacher voices Arjuna's implied doubt: "You aren't experimenting on me, are You — trying out bhakti to see if it works?" The Lord answers: no experiment is happening. It is not that someone has only now come to know Me; over a very long time, very many people have taken My shelter and attained liberation from the world. There are a great many success stories.
- **The college analogy:**
  - If you join a college and learn that yours is the first batch — "whoever passes out from you, we'll put up his photo once he gets placed" — you will not get much inspiration.
  - But if it is a famous college and you know where the seniors have gone, you can join with confidence.
- So many people have given up attachment, fear and anger, taken shelter of hearing and chanting about Him, fixed the mind on Him, attained love for Him, and after leaving this world never have to return.

#### The close of the first section [48:00 – 50:00]

- This completes the first section of the chapter, BG 4.1–4.10.
- In the purport to 4.10 Srila Prabhupada also explains the **nine stages of bhakti**; students are asked to read it.
- The teacher notes that the purports are long, and that these verses could not all be discussed in great detail in class.

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

**1. Do the deities in new temples fulfil this verse today? [48:00 – 50:00]**

- A devotee shares a point he heard in a lecture by a Mahārāja in Mumbai. Asked whether the Lord's descents have now stopped, the Mahārāja answered that the deities arriving in the new ISKCON temples being opened are no less than the Lord.
- When a deity is installed in a city, then in that very city the deliverance of the _sādhus_, the destruction of the miscreants and the establishment of dharma are all taking place — so one should not think the Lord is not coming.
- The teacher accepts it: "Nice point."

**2. How can one gradually become free from attachment, fear and anger? And is worrying about one's children attachment? [50:00 – 54:00]**

- A _mātājī_ observes that one cannot be freed from attachment, fear and anger all at once. The teacher points to the verse itself: _man-mayā mām upāśritāḥ_. As the commentator explains, as a person takes the Lord's shelter — through hearing and chanting — his mind becomes fully absorbed, and in proportion he becomes free from these things. [Name flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **Giving up is not the living entity's nature.** Attachment exists in every living entity; he cannot give up anything — not even one rupee. So what we do is try to **increase our shelter** in the Lord; as shelter increases, things begin to drop away.
- **The empty heart.** Behind attachment, fear and anger lies an unfulfilled, empty heart. While the heart is empty, a person tries to fill it with many things, thinking that will bring joy:
  - one with money;
  - one with different degrees;
  - one with material relationships.
  - The living entity is searching for _ānanda_ — that is behind everything.
- **The coin and the note.** A small child holds something tightly and won't let go — but when someone offers him a one-rupee note in exchange, he lets go easily, because something positive is acting. As one takes the Lord's shelter and tastes that joy, giving things up causes no pain. [Caption flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **What has already dropped away.** In bhakti we have already given up a great deal — non-vegetarian food, tea and coffee, watching movies — all of which we did before. It went naturally. To the degree bhakti manifests, things drop away; subtler things too drop away as absorption in bhakti grows. [52:00 – 54:00]
- **On worrying for family.** Thinking about or caring for family members — affection — is not wrong. But that attachment should not be so strong that it affects one's duties. The rest happens by itself with time: **bhakti is a natural process.**

**3. With so much sin in the world, why isn't the Lord coming? [54:00 – 56:00]**

- The teacher answers that the Lord **is** coming — His manifestations simply differ.
- He comes as the _grantha-avatāra_ (scripture), the _dhāma-avatāra_ (holy place) and the _nāma-avatāra_ (holy name) — these too are incarnations.
- And, he adds with a laugh, if things get much worse, the Kalki incarnation will also come. [Caption flagged; see Verification Flags.]

**4. Since our _anarthas_ are now within us, can the Lord appear within our hearts? [54:00 – 58:00]**

- A devotee notes that, as the teacher has said before, what once happened outside in earlier ages is now within us as _anarthas_. Can the Lord appear within through bhakti?
- **Yes.** Beyond the general incarnations, as we practise bhakti authentically, the Lord manifests in the hearts of His devotees.
  - The Lord told Durvāsā Muni that He resides in the hearts of the _sādhus_. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
  - Our entire _sādhana-bhakti_ is to prepare the heart.
- **Even in His pastimes He appears in the heart first:**
  - In Krishna's pastime, He first appeared in the mind of Vasudeva.
  - In the Nṛsiṁha pastime, Viṣṇu was always in Prahlāda Mahārāja's heart, and he felt Viṣṇu's embrace — as described in the Seventh Canto.
- These are devotees of the highest order, but for every practitioner: the more the heart is purified by doing bhakti well, the more one experiences the Lord's presence.
- **That presence is why we can practise at all.** As the Supersoul He guides and inspires — what is right, and the inspiration to do it. Otherwise, how in Kali-yuga would a person chant on beads, rise early, and give up wrong things that the whole world is doing? That is the Lord manifesting in our heart. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- The **full** manifestation comes as the heart becomes completely pure and the living entity engages only for the Lord's pleasure.

**5. Should devotees perform _śrāddha_ for ancestors during _pitṛ-pakṣa_, and what should they teach their children? [58:00 – 61:44]**

- A _mātājī_ has heard that devotees do not face the _yamadūtas_ and the _pitṛ-pakṣa_ scenario, and receive their next life according to their _karma_. Should she perform _śrāddha_ for her ancestors, and should she tell her children to do it for her?
- **Some things are folk tradition (_loka-paramparā_).** As said in the Third Chapter, they are done for _loka-saṅgraha_ — setting an example. Caitanya Mahāprabhu, though the Lord Himself, offered _piṇḍa_ for His father for _loka-saṅgraha_.
- So one should tell the children; it maintains the family tradition. But **the main faith should be on _hari-nāma_.**
- **The power of Vaiṣṇava vows.** Reading about the significance of that day's Ekadashi: if someone's ancestors are, through some sin, even in hell, and Ekadashi is observed for them, they are liberated. The power of Vaiṣṇava vows — chanting the holy name, observing Ekadashi — is far greater than that of _karma-kāṇḍa_.
- Still, for _loka-saṅgraha_ people should know these things, and we too should do them; children will do them seeing us. We do not do everything, but some things we do — because the _ācāryas_, such as Caitanya Mahāprabhu, also did them.
