## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 4.25 [06:00 – 10:00]

<!-- verse:4.25 -->

**Context.** Beyond the selfless action already described, Krishna now says that there are other people who perform other kinds of sacrifice. Two are named in this verse. [06:00 – 08:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Apare_: Some others — a word that will come again and again through this section, introducing each new group. [06:00 – 08:00]
- _Daivam eva … yajñaṁ yoginaḥ paryupāsate_: Some _yogīs_ worship the demigods; that worship is also a kind of _yajña_. [06:00 – 08:00]
- _Brahmāgnau_: In the fire of Brahman — the aim of those who want to merge into the _brahmajyoti_. [06:00 – 08:00]

#### The two sacrifices named here [06:00 – 08:00]

1. **Worship of the demigods.** The teacher identifies these as _karma-yogīs_ who worship the demigods, and in the purport they are the people who want some benefit from the demigods.
   - Prabhupada's purport calls them _bahv-īśvara-vādī_, believers in many gods. The teacher's examples: some feel that the sun-god is also God, that Shani-dev is also God.
2. **Offering into the fire of Brahman.** These are the _jñāna-yogīs_, the impersonalists, who want to merge into Brahman. They feel they must become absorbed in the _brahmajyoti_, and that too is a kind of sacrifice.

#### How the purport frames the whole section [08:00 – 10:00]

- The teacher notes that in every purport Prabhupada brings in a touch of Krishna consciousness and then explains.
- **First line:** the person engaged in Krishna consciousness is the perfect _yogī_ — the first-class _yogī_.
- **Last paragraph:** a Krishna conscious person like Arjuna does everything for Krishna's satisfaction.
- So both the opening and the closing say the same thing — the devotee is the first-class _yogī_ — and the explanation of other kinds of sacrifice sits in between.
- He flags that this section will bring some practical application along the way, even though most of the sacrifices described are ones devotees do not perform.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The section opens with _apare_ — "some others" — and the first two groups are those who worship the demigods for benefits and those who seek to merge into Brahman. Prabhupada's purport brackets them on both sides with the same statement: the Krishna conscious person, who does everything for Krishna's satisfaction, is the first-class _yogī_. [06:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.26 [10:00 – 14:00]

<!-- verse:4.26 -->

**Context.** Two more sacrifices, both concerning the senses: one group restrains them altogether, the other engages them in their objects but in the mood of sacrifice. [10:00 – 12:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śrotrādīni indriyāṇi_: The hearing process and the other senses. [10:00 – 12:00]
- _Saṁyamāgniṣu juhvati_: They offer the senses into the fire of restraint. [10:00 – 12:00]
- _Śabdādīn viṣayān anye_: Others offer sound and the other sense objects into the fire of the senses. [10:00 – 12:00]

#### Restraint versus regulated engagement [10:00 – 14:00]

- **The unadulterated _naiṣṭhika brahmacārī_.** He has made his whole life a sacrifice: he offers the ears and other senses into the fire of restraint and does not engage them much in the world.
  - He will not take a job, will not go out, will not open a business — he is hardly engaging the working senses in the world at all.
- **The householder — or "other _brahmacārīs_."** The teacher notes that Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura reads the second group as _brahmacārīs_ who are not _naiṣṭhika_, as well as householders. They do engage the senses in sense objects, but in the mood of sacrifice.
  - The householder goes out, does a job, runs a business — but why? For service, in the mood of sacrifice.
  - Whether earning money or caring for the family, he does it keeping Krishna at the centre. He is gathering the means of sense enjoyment, but as a duty with the Lord at the centre, not for sense enjoyment — so that too is a _yajña_.

#### The _āśramas_ in the purport [12:00 – 14:00]

- Prabhupada's purport refers to the four _āśramas_ — _brahmacārī_, _gṛhastha_, _vānaprastha_ and _sannyāsa_.
- **The _brahmacārī_** lives in the _gurukula_, keeps his mind and senses restrained, and hears. The teacher's point: the _brahmacārī_ realizes by hearing alone — what the world is like, what its objects are, that there is no happiness in it and how much suffering there is.
- **One who needs experience** to understand has to enter the _gṛhastha-āśrama_.
- **The _gṛhastha-āśrama_** is also a proper _āśrama_ and also a sacrifice. A person engages in the world and in family, marriage takes place — but that too has a purpose given by the Lord, and the purport explains the rule for householders as well. [Recitation flagged; see Verification Flags.]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Two opposite-looking lives can both be sacrifice. The _naiṣṭhika brahmacārī_ offers his senses into the fire of restraint and learns the nature of the world simply by hearing; the householder engages the senses in their objects — job, business, family — but as duty performed with Krishna at the centre. What makes the second a _yajña_ is not the activity but the purpose of service. [10:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.27 [14:00 – 18:00]

<!-- verse:4.27 -->

**Context.** Now the teacher turns to the _yogīs_ of the Patañjali line — those who want self-realization and pursue it through breath control and restraint of the senses and mind. [14:00 – 16:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sarvāṇīndriya-karmāṇi_: The functions of all the senses, which are offered. [14:00]
- _Prāṇa-karmāṇi_: The functions of the life-breath — here _prāṇa_ means _prāṇa-vāyu_. [16:00]
- _Ātma-saṁyama-yogāgnau_: Into the fire of the controlled mind — by which the person achieves self-restraint. [16:00 – 18:00]

#### Breath regulation as a tool for the mind [16:00 – 18:00]

- In this system the breath is regulated, and regulating the breath also brings some control of the mind. His aside: "that's why _prāṇāyāma_ is still better than the gym."
- Practices like _anulom-vilom_ regulate the breath, and the mind becomes somewhat regulated and peaceful — not completely, but to a degree.
- There are different airs in the body — _prāṇa_, _apāna_, _samāna_, _udāna_, _vyāna_ — each with a role, moving in different directions. The teacher is candid: "I don't know the full science of it." The principle is what matters: they regulate the breathing and through it try to control the mind.

#### _Pratyāhāra_ — sense gratification reduced to zero [16:00 – 18:00]

- In _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ the stages run _yama_, _niyama_, _āsana_, _prāṇāyāma_ (breath regulation), and the fifth stage, _pratyāhāra_.
- At the _pratyāhāra_ stage the person brings sense gratification down to zero. This is the principle the verse describes: having controlled the breath, he then achieves self-restraint.
- His general conclusion: whether _jñāna-yogī_ or anyone else, everyone has to bring sense gratification down to zero.

#### _Parāg-ātmā_ and _pratyag-ātmā_ [18:00]

- The teacher highlights two terms Prabhupada uses in this purport.
  - **_Parāg-ātmā_** — _rāga_ means attachment; the soul in this state is attached outward, to the world and to sense enjoyment.
  - **_Pratyag-ātmā_** — the soul that turns away from the outside and inward, that is, becomes spiritual. The teacher connects the word _pratyāhāra_ with this detached state.
- **The goal of the _yoga_ system**, as he reads the purport: to come from _parāg-ātmā_ to _pratyag-ātmā_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The _aṣṭāṅga-yogī_ offers the functions of the senses and of the life-breath into the fire of a controlled mind. Breath regulation steadies the mind to a degree; its purpose is _pratyāhāra_, where sense gratification is reduced to zero — the turn from _parāg-ātmā_, the soul attached outward, to _pratyag-ātmā_, the soul turned inward. Whatever the path, sense gratification has to come to zero. [14:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.28 [18:00 – 24:00]

<!-- verse:4.28 -->

**Context.** Several more sacrifices in one verse, all performed by people who have accepted strict vows. [18:00 – 20:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dravya-yajña_: _Dravya_ means possessions — giving one's possessions. [18:00 – 20:00]
- _Tapo-yajña_: _Tapas_ means austerity — engaging in different kinds of austerity and vows. [20:00]
- _Yoga-yajña_: The sacrifice of the _yoga_ process; the purport mentions _haṭha-yoga_ and the Patañjali system. [20:00; 24:00]
- _Svādhyāya_: Study of the Vedas, reading _śāstra_. [20:00]
- _Jñāna-yajña_: Those engaged in the path of knowledge. [20:00]
- _Yatayaḥ_: Enlightened people making great endeavour. [20:00]
- _Saṁśita-vratāḥ_: With strict vows. [20:00]

#### The list of sacrifices [20:00 – 24:00]

- **_Dravya-yajña_ — giving possessions.** Feeding the hungry, distributing blankets in winter, giving clothes free.
  - With a laugh he adds that some people distribute alcohol free too — something is being distributed, but that is not _dravya-yajña_.
  - The purport's examples of this sacrifice: _dharmaśālās_, food distribution centres, guest houses, orphanages and educational institutions.
- **_Tapo-yajña_ — austerity and vows.** The purport names vows such as _cāndrāyaṇa_ and _cāturmāsya_. [22:00 – 24:00]
  - His anecdote: the previous day he had asked which month of _cāturmāsya_ was running, and people got confused.
  - "In our case" the observance is just not drinking milk in one month, not taking something else in another — and some eat _peḍā_ in place of milk.
  - The actual vow is very tough: a person eats once a day, and only a little.
- **_Yoga-yajña_** — _haṭha-yoga_ and the Patañjali system. [24:00]
- **_Svādhyāya-yajña_** — studying the Vedas, reading _śāstra_ well.
- **_Jñāna-yajña_** — those engaged in the path of knowledge.

#### Not all on one level, and not automatically _yajña_ [20:00 – 22:00]

- All these people are basically **trying to connect with God** through whatever they are able to do, for a higher purpose — certainly better than someone engaged only in sense enjoyment.
  - One has possessions, so at least he is giving; another may have no possessions but is reading some _śāstra_, perhaps not clearly, but reading; another is on the path of knowledge.
- **But these sacrifices are not on the same level.** Krishna is only stating the different types here.
- **The essence of _yajña_ is to please the Lord — to please Viṣṇu.** So any activity needs some spiritual understanding to become a _yajña_; otherwise it is not one.
  - His example: distributing food can be social service or it can be _yajña_. It depends on the consciousness — if the thought is "I am feeding them, I am doing so much, let there be media coverage," that is social service, not _yajña_.

#### The purport's conclusion [24:00]

- At the end Prabhupada always writes the conclusion. Here: Krishna consciousness is different from all these because it is direct service to the Supreme Lord; it cannot be attained by any of the sacrifices mentioned, but only by the mercy of the Lord and His bona fide devotee. [Purport reading flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- The teacher's point: Prabhupada always clears this for us. If the technicalities are not understood, that is no problem — the concept should be understood, that we are on the right path and the best path.
- **Why the clarification matters.** Otherwise someone hears that the mind is also controlled by _prāṇāyāma_, and starts doing half an hour of _kapālabhāti_ and _anulom-vilom_ during chanting time. Going to the extreme in anything is not good; we have received the best process.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Giving possessions, austerity, _yoga_, study and the path of knowledge are all attempts to connect with God and are better than mere sense enjoyment — but they are not on one level, and none is automatically a _yajña_. The essence of _yajña_ is to please Viṣṇu, so the same food distribution is social service or sacrifice depending on the consciousness behind it. Prabhupada's conclusion keeps the devotee's bearings: Krishna consciousness is direct service, attained by the mercy of the Lord and His devotee. [18:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.29 [24:00 – 36:00]

<!-- verse:4.29 -->

**Context.** The breath control described briefly in BG 4.27 is now given in more detail, together with those who curtail eating. The teacher laughs that "this section actually needs a yoga teacher" to explain it properly. [24:00 – 26:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prāṇa_ and _apāna_: Different airs in the body; _apāna_ goes downward, and the purport also speaks of _vyāna_, which circulates. [26:00]
- _Prāṇāyāma-parāyaṇāḥ_: Those who have resolved that breath control is their aim. [26:00]
- _Niyatāhārāḥ_: Those who control the eating process, eating very little. [28:00]

#### Breath practice and its purpose [26:00 – 28:00]

- The purport mentions _kumbhaka_, _recaka_ and similar processes. The teacher compares this with _anulom-vilom_ as it is practised: exhale, hold the breath outside, inhale, hold it inside.
- People who do _prāṇāyāma_ and _haṭha-yoga_ focus heavily on this.
- **The goal is one:** that the senses come somewhat under restraint, the mind becomes focused, and the person can then pursue his goal.
  - The goal of _yoga_ is not just improving health through _anulom-vilom_ and _kapālabhāti_, as it is commonly presented today.
  - The _aṣṭāṅga-yogī_ tries to fix his meditation on the Supersoul.
  - The _jñāna-yogī_ similarly restrains the senses and controls the mind, and then does _vimarśana_ — contemplation — on the _śāstra_.

#### Sense control as a foundation [28:00]

- Sense control is basically a **foundation**. Without it a person can do nothing — and that includes _bhakti_, because everything else rests on the foundation.
- His analogy: once the foundation is built you can put up whatever you want on top — a building, a villa, a bungalow, a marriage hall. Without the foundation nothing works.

#### Curtailing the eating process [28:00]

- Others control the eating process, eating very little, and so restrain the senses: eat less and the other senses become quiet — there is no energy left (laughter).

#### Take only what serves _bhakti_ [28:00 – 30:00]

- The teacher's principle: from all these practices a devotee takes **only as much as is useful in _bhakti_**, and does not go deep into them.
  - **_Yoga_** — a little, as needed, not two or three hours.
  - **Dieting** — not making it the goal of life, with constant rules about what to take and not take.
  - **Astrology** — not consulting it for everything, such as whether to step out of the house today.
- **Why:** in Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition of _bhakti_, it must be _jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam_ — not covered by _jñāna_, _karma_ and so on. [Recitation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
  - If someone goes so far into dieting that he cannot chant, or drifts into some other zone instead of reading Prabhupada's books, or does an hour or an hour and a half of _yoga_ besides _bhakti-yoga_ — _bhakti_ gets covered. That thing becomes primary and _bhakti_ becomes secondary.
- **Their legitimate use.** _Prāṇāyāma_ and such things do have some use: they help keep the body healthy and help regulate the senses somewhat. Someone who has practised _haṭha-yoga_ can understand their effect on the mind and senses better.

#### The missing piece: positive engagement [30:00]

- **The limitation:** after this, it is not clear what engagement the senses should be given.
- Look at the many _yoga_ teachers today: the health is done, the body is healthy — and beyond that there is no positive engagement. So most end up in social service, or in something else, or open a business.
- The purport explains technically how the different airs are neutralized; the teacher leaves that detail to the reader.

#### Prabhupada's conclusion: the devotee controls the senses automatically [30:00 – 34:00]

- The teacher reads the purport: a Krishna conscious person, being always situated in the transcendental loving service of the Lord, automatically becomes the controller of the senses; his senses, being engaged in Krishna's service, have no chance of becoming otherwise engaged. [Purport reading flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **Why this works — identification.** Our whole life is driven by our identification. When the identification "I am the servant of the Lord" is very strong — not the servant of any country, family, society or caste — then I must engage in the Lord's service. Like Prahlāda. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- When one is that engaged in service, the senses become controlled automatically — there is simply no time for sense gratification.
- **The fasting-day illustration.** [32:00]
  - On an _ācārya_'s appearance day with fasting till noon, if there is no engagement, hunger comes and tension builds — when will _prasāda_ be served? [Caption flagged; see Verification Flags.]
  - On Janmāṣṭamī, fasting till midnight, one does not even remember that _prasāda_ has to be taken — because one is engaged in service and forgets.
- **His instruction:** we should remain very positively engaged; then sense control becomes relatively very easy. Without engagement, sense control is a problem.
- **Utilizing time.** We should be very careful about how we use our time. One who wastes time in _bhakti_ will always have uncontrolled senses.
  - The reason: we first remove the sense-control programme — telling people, "you don't need to do this or that, you are doing the topmost _yoga_, _bhakti-yoga_." True — but then _bhakti-yoga_ must actually be done and understood, and one must stay constantly engaged in that identification. [34:00]
  - The teacher notes that Prabhupada quotes BG 14.26 in this purport.

#### Eating: _prasāda_ and its limits [34:00 – 36:00]

- At the end of the purport Prabhupada explains that the practice of reduced eating is automatically done when one eats only Krishna _prasāda_.
  - The teacher observes that devotees' eating does become very controlled: before, they ate outside and at all hours; now they do not eat what is not _prasāda_.
- The line he marks as important: reducing the eating process is very helpful in the matter of sense control, and without sense control there is no possibility of getting out of material entanglement. [Purport reading flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **Not "in the name of _prasāda_."** A devotee should not overdo it in the name of _prasāda_ — pizza, pasta, everything going on in the name of _prasāda_; an Ekādaśī cake; 108 items even on Ekādaśī.
- **Prabhupada and the gulab jamuns.** [34:00 – 36:00]
  - In the early days Prabhupada fed the devotees a great deal — he kept a whole jar of gulab jamuns, and anyone feeling sleepy in class would go out and eat them. The Western devotees would eat ten gulab jamuns at a time.
  - He encouraged them: "take more, take more."
  - Later, when the devotees had matured — chanting on beads and become serious — Prabhupada said in a lecture, "Don't eat like pigs." The devotees were shocked: "Prabhupada, you were the one who encouraged us to eat more!" His reply, as the teacher tells it: then you were following me; now I am saying "don't eat like pigs," and now you should follow.
  - And Prabhupada said that even overeating of _mahā-prasāda_ is _rājasika_ — forget ordinary food. The teacher's reason: our consciousness is not pure.
- **The basic point:** eating should be regulated.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Breath control and reduced eating are attempts to restrain the senses, and sense control is the foundation for every path, including _bhakti_. A devotee takes from such practices only what serves _bhakti_, so that _bhakti_ is never covered by them. Their limitation is that they give the senses no positive engagement; the devotee, identified as the Lord's servant and engaged in His service, controls the senses automatically — provided he actually stays engaged, uses his time carefully, and does not overeat even in the name of _prasāda_. [24:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.30 [36:00 – 42:00]

<!-- verse:4.30 -->

**Context.** Having listed the sacrifices, Krishna gives their result for all of them alike. [36:00 – 38:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sarve 'py ete_: All these people — the _naiṣṭhika brahmacārī_ restraining the senses, the other _brahmacārī_ or householder engaging them, and those doing the sacrifices of _prāṇāyāma_, possessions, austerity, study and knowledge. [38:00]
- _Yajña-vidaḥ_: Those who know the purpose of sacrifice — not doing something without knowing why they are doing it. [36:00]
- _Yajña-kṣapita-kalmaṣāḥ_: Freed from _kalmaṣa_ — sinful reaction — through sacrifice; _kṣapita_ means "goes away." [38:00]
- _Yajña-śiṣṭāmṛta-bhujaḥ_: Those who taste the nectar of the remnants of sacrifice. [36:00 – 38:00]
- _Yānti brahma sanātanam_: They go to the eternal Brahman — they finally connect with the Lord and reach the eternal destination. [38:00; 42:00]

#### First result: purification — and what _kalmaṣa_ is [38:00 – 40:00]

- The first result of _yajña_ is **purification**: anyone who performs some sacrifice in this way becomes purified.
- **What is the _kalmaṣa_?** Everything is for Krishna; everything belongs to Krishna, and we have the opportunity to give it to Him. Yet we use everything for ourselves.
- The teacher connects this to the Seventh Chapter, where the Lord says He is the ability in man: whatever talent anyone has comes from the Lord. [Recitation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
  - As long as the soul is in the body, we have the opportunity to engage it in the Lord's service.
  - After death, who asks whether one had a PhD or never went to school? Everyone is equal — and in the next birth both start together.
- So _kalmaṣa_ is the impurity that misdirects our consciousness: instead of the Lord we look only toward the external world, and use its objects for our own enjoyment.
- **What _yajña_ reverses:** what we could have enjoyed for ourselves, we offer to the Lord — we give it to someone else. That is the beginning of selflessness. And _yajña_ means giving with the Lord at the centre, for His pleasure.

#### Second result: the nectar — even in this world [36:00 – 40:00]

- The _amṛta_ here is not some nectar that is drunk: it means the fruit of sacrifice — some benefit in this world, such as reputation. The teacher attributes this reading to Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa.
- When a person performs charity and the like, he receives mercy even materially; he benefits in this world too.
- **The principle he draws:** generally, if someone in this world is wealthy now, it means he gave in charity before. Wealth does not come simply by our hard work — it comes according to one's karma from the previous birth.

#### Third result, and the common aim [42:00]

- _Yānti brahma sanātanam_: finally they move toward the spiritual destination.
- **The purport's first paragraph** gives the common aim of all these different sacrifices: to bring the senses under control. Sense gratification is the root cause of material existence.
  - The teacher notes this continues from where the previous purport ended: the desire for enjoyment keeps the living entity bound in this world, and until he gives it up, getting out is very difficult.
  - Sacrifices help a person regulate the senses.
- **The simple version:** someone engaged in charity had, say, a hundred rupees, and would have spent all hundred on enjoyment. Now he has donated fifty — so the senses got controlled. [Figure flagged; see Verification Flags.]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
All these performers, if they know the purpose of sacrifice, receive the same three results: purification from _kalmaṣa_, some benefit even in this world, and finally the eternal destination. _Kalmaṣa_ is the habit of using for ourselves what belongs to Krishna; _yajña_ reverses it by offering what we could have enjoyed, which is the beginning of selflessness. Underneath every sacrifice is one aim — control of the senses, because sense gratification is the root of material existence. [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.31 [42:00 – 54:00]

<!-- verse:4.31 -->

**Context.** A one-line verse. The teacher notes a pattern: whenever Krishna explains a concept in the Gita, He also says what happens if you do not do it and what happens if you do. [42:00 – 44:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ayajñasya_: Of the _ayajña_ person — one who does not perform sacrifice. [42:00 – 44:00]
- _Nāyaṁ loko 'sti_: This world is not there for him — nothing is attained even in this life. [44:00]
- _Kuto 'nyaḥ_: What to speak of the next life. [44:00]

#### The claim, and the obvious objection [44:00 – 46:00]

- The Lord's statement: leave aside what happens after death — the one who performs no sacrifice cannot enjoy even now. The teacher calls this a very important principle to understand practically: no one can ever live happily even on this planet without sacrifice.
- **The objection:** "But we see so many people who enjoy — they give no donation, do no charity, certainly no austerity, and are engaged only in enjoyment."
- **His answer:** that is how it appears externally.
  - They received wealth from their previous birth, and with it they can gather objects of opulence — a big house, a car.
  - But they cannot obtain happiness. One who is not engaged in sacrifice cannot obtain happiness.
  - The foolish think the answer is to increase their wealth further.
- **The miser.** A miserly person is very unhappy — a principle every householder should know. He can neither use his wealth, nor give it, nor engage it in the Lord's service. [44:00 – 46:00]

#### Why money alone does not make anyone happy [46:00 – 48:00]

- **_Dharma_, _artha_, _kāma_, _mokṣa_ go together.** From _dharma_ comes _artha_, and wealth that comes through _dharma_ brings real prosperity.
- **Just by having money one cannot be happy.** If money alone made people happy, people in Western countries would be the happiest: the government gives money, many people do not work at all and still live — yet look where stress, anxiety and social issues are found.
- **The mistaken definition:** we assume that whoever has more objects of enjoyment will be happier. But happiness does not come out of sense gratification that way. The Lord has already said in BG 5.22 that the more a person engages in sense gratification, the more misery he receives. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **The observation:** the more a person accumulates for himself, the more unhappy he is. Someone very big who is not engaged in any religious work is under more tension; an ordinary person who does not have that much does not carry that tension.

#### "I am missing one thing — suffering" [48:00]

- The teacher recounts a youth seminar in Bombay (Juhu) by a _sannyāsī_ who wrote a book on Vedic India. When he was introduced, his education at a big Western university was described, and then that he had left it all for _sannyāsa_. [Name flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- A boy stood up and asked: "Swamiji, don't you feel you are missing something in life?"
- The _sannyāsī_ replied: "Yes, you are right. I am missing one thing — that is suffering."
- The teacher notes the Eighteenth Chapter will also say that one who lives this way does not get happiness. His practical test: look closely at anyone who is accumulating wealth and is miserly — he is 100% unhappy; look at his home overall and the understanding will come.

#### This world and the next are connected [48:00 – 52:00]

- **The usual assumption:** some people give up the happiness of this world, expecting that renunciation here will bring happiness in the other world. The _karma-kāṇḍīs_ performed sacrifices and gave much charity on this basis — less happiness here, more happiness hereafter.
- **What this verse says is different:** if such people get no happiness even in this world, how will they get it in the next? There is a connection between the two — what one does here is the foundation.
- **His analogy:** someone who says "I don't have enough money even for a 1BHK" — then there is no discussion at all of a villa.
- **Why the non-sacrificer is unhappy: he is disconnected.** [50:00 – 52:00]
  - In the Third Chapter (the _prajāpati_ verse) the Lord explained that at the very beginning of creation He created the facility of _yajña_.
  - The purpose: people in this world who want to enjoy a little should do so keeping the Lord a little at the centre, following the system of sacrifice, so that they are gradually purified and stay connected with the Supreme.
  - One who does no sacrifice stays disconnected — not properly connected to the whole system — and therefore stays dissatisfied.
  - And because he did not do sacrifice-like work in this life — that is, he did wrong work — he is unhappy in the next life also.

#### Past and future: why acting rightly needs both [52:00 – 54:00]

- The last paragraph of the purport: if a person is not inclined to perform _yajña_ according to the Vedas, how can he expect to be happy in this life, and what to speak of another. [Purport reading flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **The principle the teacher draws from the purport:** one who is not clear about the past and the future — who does not know that this life is not everything, that there was life before and will be life after — how can he act rightly in the present? He will certainly do wrong in the present.
- **Modern versus Vedic understanding.** According to the _śāstras_ we know that:
  - We did something in the past, and we are receiving its reaction — a particular body, a particular family, a particular intelligence; whatever _vāsanās_ and _saṁskāras_ are in our mind are a combined reaction, good and bad.
  - There is also a future: something waits for us after this birth too. This birth is not everything.
- **The person who knows this** can act thoughtfully: this is a short phase, and in it one must act properly and follow _dharma_. Whatever is done in this phase will bring its reaction later.
- **The CCTV analogy:** everywhere it is written, "You are under CCTV surveillance." When a person sees the camera, he behaves properly. In the same way, knowing that this life is not everything and a much larger life lies ahead, one acts according to _dharma_.
- **The purport's point:** the main cause of the happiness and distress in this birth is sinful work done out of _avidyā_. But among so many species, it is in the human birth that one can get out — by doing work in the form of sacrifice, one can get out of the cycle of this material world.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Krishna says one who performs no sacrifice cannot be happy even here, let alone hereafter. Wealth from past karma can buy a big house and a car but not happiness; the miser and the over-accumulator are the most anxious. Sacrifice keeps a person connected to the Supreme through the system the Lord created at the beginning, and this life and the next are linked — so one who knows that his present comes from his past and shapes his future acts thoughtfully, as if under the camera. [42:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.32 [54:00 – 58:00]

<!-- verse:4.32 -->

**Context.** Krishna sums up the catalogue: all these many sacrifices come from the Vedas, are born of work, and knowing them as such leads to liberation. [54:00 – 56:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Evaṁ bahu-vidhā yajñāḥ_: Thus the different kinds of sacrifice. [54:00]
- _Vitatā brahmaṇo mukhe_: Spread from the mouth of Brahman — here _brahma_ means the Vedas; these are actions and knowledge given directly in the Vedas. [56:00]
- _Karma-jān viddhi_: Know them to be born of action — _yajña_, _dāna_ and _tapas_, which will come again in the Eighteenth Chapter. [56:00]
- _Evaṁ jñātvā vimokṣyase_: Knowing this, a person can attain liberation — _vimokṣa_, a special liberation. [56:00 – 58:00]

#### Why the scriptures prescribe sacrifices [56:00]

- **Human tendency:** a person generally wants to live a lazy, cosy life — and modern society and culture are increasing this. There is no need to wash clothes, less need to cook, cleaners for most things; earlier a person had to make great effort.
- **So _yajña_, _dāna_ and _tapas_ involve some discomfort:**
  - austerity is difficult;
  - giving is difficult;
  - sacrifice is a little difficult, because it means not using something for oneself.
- **But they purify.** By them a person is purified and can engage properly in life; they are a sign that connects one to _bhakti_. [Recitation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **Accepting an authority.** When someone accepts another's leadership, he follows that person's rules. Following these rules of sacrifice is like following the Lord's rules.

#### _Vimokṣa_: the same act, a different direction [58:00]

- The activity a person could have done for himself, he does for the Lord's pleasure.
- **The same activity**, done for one's own enjoyment, binds; done for the Lord's pleasure, it opens the path of liberation. The same possessions he could have used for himself, he now uses for the Lord's pleasure.

#### The purport: work with body, mind or intelligence [58:00]

- The purport explains that these sacrifices are described in the Vedas because different people have different tastes; according to their nature they prefer different sacrifices.
- The teacher reads the reason given: because men are so deeply absorbed in the bodily concept, the sacrifices are arranged so that one can work with the body, the mind or the intelligence — but all are recommended for ultimately bringing about liberation from the body. [Purport reading flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **The purpose, in his words:** so that a person can rise at least a little above bodily consciousness. Today it is very common that a person cannot think beyond his own life and family — he is completely hooked. By performing sacrifice he can come out of that at least a little.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The many sacrifices come from the Vedas and are all forms of action, prescribed because a person naturally drifts toward a lazy, cosy, bodily life. _Yajña_, _dāna_ and _tapas_ cost some discomfort, and that is what purifies. The same act that binds when done for oneself liberates when done for the Lord's pleasure; the variety exists so that people of every nature can rise at least a little above the body. [54:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.33 [58:00 – 68:00]

<!-- verse:4.33 -->

**Context.** The concluding verse of the section. After describing so many sacrifices over the previous five or six verses, Krishna says that the sacrifice performed in knowledge is better than the sacrifice of material possessions. [58:00 – 60:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śreyān dravya-mayād yajñāj jñāna-yajñaḥ_: Better than the sacrifice of possessions is the sacrifice in knowledge. (The teacher catches and corrects his own slip in reading the order.) [58:00 – 60:00]
- _Sarvaṁ karmākhilaṁ pārtha_: All actions, in their entirety. [60:00]
- _Jñāne parisamāpyate_: Culminate in knowledge — the purpose of all of them is to bring a person to the level of knowledge. [60:00]

#### Why knowledge is the culmination [60:00]

- When knowledge arises, the person's fruits of _karma_ are destroyed.
- The purpose of all actions is that the person comes to the level of self-realization — realization of knowledge of the self — and then the reactions of _karma_ go away.
- **The thread of the chapter:** until now the Lord has been telling Arjuna to act without selfish desire. Why? Because then there will be no reaction of _karma_, and he will come to the level of knowledge — of self-realization — with no reaction attached.
- **Prabhupada writes from the point of view of _bhakti_.** So the Lord is saying to Arjuna: you are doing the work of fighting — very good; do it without selfish desire; and at the level of _bhakti_, do it for My pleasure. Then knowledge will be obtained and nothing else needs to be done.

#### Every action has two portions [60:00 – 62:00]

- **The gross portion** — the activity itself: giving possessions, making a donation.
- **The subtle portion** — the consciousness in which it is given; this is the knowledge-portion.
- The Lord says the knowledge-portion is superior. An activity like _yajña_, _dāna_ or _tapas_ exists at the level of activity and also at the level of consciousness.

#### Same activity, different consciousness [62:00]

- This is why the Eighteenth Chapter will describe charity, sacrifice and austerity in each of the three modes — goodness, passion and ignorance. They become different according to the consciousness.
- **Charitable people of very different consciousness:** Jarāsandha was very charitable; Rāvaṇa was charitable; Karṇa was charitable.
- **The best is both — activity and consciousness.** Bali Mahārāja gave in charity, and the consciousness was also there.
- **A simple thing, given with knowledge — good feeling — greatly pleases Krishna:**
  - Vidurānī fed the Lord banana peels, but with devotion.
  - Śabarī — the teacher names her in the same line.
  - Duryodhana was offering _chappan bhog_, and the Lord refused.

#### _Yajña_ in one line [62:00 – 64:00]

- **The definition:** sacrifice what one can enjoy for oneself, for the pleasure of the Lord.
- **The mood to carry:** "What can I do for the Lord?" From this, satisfaction and happiness come into life. The opposite mood — "what can I do for myself?" — leads to unhappiness. The teacher calls this mood very important; it is the whole point of sacrifice.
- **The second formulation:** sacrificing what I can enjoy for a higher cause — best of all, for Krishna's cause. To give, and to give in knowledge, is best — as Bharata Mahārāja performed sacrifices. [64:00]

#### Knowing why [64:00]

- The purport: the purpose of all sacrifices is that the person is freed from material bondage and material suffering, engages in the Lord's service, and comes to the level of _bhakti_.
- **The contrast he gives:** at Patañjali-style centres today, _prāṇāyāma_ and everything is taught, but not its purpose — there is little talk of sense control, and nothing beyond it.
- **What is best:** when someone does any sacrifice — giving, _yoga_, anything — knowing why: "what I am doing, what I am giving — why am I doing it?" That is best: the activity, together with the knowledge of why it is done. This is the knowledge the Lord praises here — knowledge of the Lord, transcendental knowledge. [64:00 – 66:00]

#### Where the chapter has reached [66:00]

- **The first ten verses** — the Lord told a little transcendental knowledge about Himself, and its application.
- **Then** — how to do _karma_ at the level of knowledge, that is, without selfish desire.
- **This section** — such sacrifices lead to _jñāna_: sacrifice leads to transcendental knowledge.

#### Knowledge does not settle without offering the fruit [66:00 – 68:00]

- **The principle:** if a person does not offer the fruits of his work, knowledge does not become steady in his heart.
- **The listener with no realization:** someone hears a great deal — on one topic he has heard four or five top speakers. He can listen and he can speak, but he will have no realization, because he is not offering the fruits of his work; he is not acting without selfish desire.
- **This applies to all four _āśramas_:**
  - **_Brahmacārī_** — if he is not serving in the guru's _āśrama_, sleeping eight to ten hours and then studying six or seven hours, nothing will go inside him. When he offers the fruits of his work, acting without selfish desire and detached, for the pleasure of the guru, then knowledge settles in him. With service, his _sādhana_ goes inside; otherwise he becomes a presenter — a speaker. [66:00 – 68:00]
  - **_Gṛhastha_** — if he does no charity, accumulating wealth that is not engaged in the Lord's service, nothing much will go inside; memorizing verses will achieve nothing if he has spent his entire wealth only on serving his family. [68:00]
  - **_Vānaprastha_** — he will have to give up comfort. [68:00]
  - **_Sannyāsī_** — if he is not engaged in preaching: his _dharma_ is to preach for the Lord's pleasure. [68:00]
- **The meaning of "without selfish desire":** offering the fruit of work. And the best form of it is _bhagavat-arpita niṣkāma karma_ — selfless work offered to the Lord.
- **The final point:** all these different sacrifices must come to transcendental knowledge. [68:00]
- **What comes next:** from BG 4.34, how transcendental knowledge is to be obtained, and then its qualities — a long section, for the next class. [68:00]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every sacrifice has a gross portion — the thing given or done — and a subtle portion, the consciousness and knowledge behind it, and Krishna declares the knowledge-portion superior. That is why the same charity can be Jarāsandha's or Bali Mahārāja's, and why Vidurānī's banana peels pleased Krishna while Duryodhana's feast did not. In one line, _yajña_ is to sacrifice what one could enjoy for oneself for the pleasure of the Lord; and unless one offers the fruits of work, knowledge never settles in the heart, in any _āśrama_. [58:00 – 68:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

**1. How are the first four chapters connected? [68:00 – 72:00]**

- **Chapter 1 to 2.** In the First Chapter Arjuna began to weep; the Second Chapter begins with the Lord consoling him.
- **Chapter 2 to 3.** In the Second Chapter the Lord spoke of _buddhi-yoga_, _bhakti-yoga_ and selfless action. But in the _sthita-prajña_ section, BG 2.54–2.72, the word _jñāna_ appears many times and the word _śānti_ is used repeatedly — in three or four verses. [Recitations flagged; see Verification Flags.]
  - So Arjuna felt: the Lord is speaking of peace, yet telling me to fight — and he was confused.
  - Arjuna's working definition, which continues right up to the Fifth Chapter: _karma_ means I will be trapped in bondage, and _jñāna_ means peace.
  - Hence his question in BG 3.1: if knowledge is better, why are You engaging me in this terrible action?
- **Chapter 3 to 4.** In the Third Chapter the Lord explained _niṣkāma karma-yoga_ — but the biggest obstacle to acting without selfish desire is _kāma_. So the last section, from BG 3.36, explained how dangerous the enemy in the form of lust is, and the Lord said to bring it under control and defeat it by knowledge and intelligence. [70:00 – 72:00]
- **So the Fourth Chapter** begins giving that transcendental knowledge, and it is continuing; the Fifth Chapter will also come. [72:00]

**2. Should we say _oṁ tat sat_ on completing a task? [72:00 – 76:00]**

- A listener had heard in another Gita class that saying _oṁ tat sat_ on completing any work makes it complete, and asks when to say it — for example, on removing the plate after offering _bhoga_.
- The teacher: _oṁ_, _tat_ and _sat_ each indicate something different, and that will be discussed in detail in the Eighteenth Chapter.
- **The point:** devotees do not need to say it — it is not that each time we make an offering we must say it.
- **The reason — _oṁ_ is included in the Hare Krishna _mahā-mantra_**, as Prabhupada explains.
  - In the Seventh Chapter (BG 7.8) the Lord says He is the _praṇava_ in all the Vedas. Vedic mantras generally begin with _oṁ_ — _oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya_, _oṁ namaḥ śivāya_, and so on.
  - But it is included in the Hare Krishna _mahā-mantra_; Rūpa Gosvāmī and others have explained this in different ways.
- **The listener's objection:** where does _oṁ_ come in Hare Krishna? It appears in Prabhupada's _praṇāma-mantra_, but not in Hare Krishna.
- **The teacher's answer — the banknote.** "In a two-thousand-rupee note, do you see a one-rupee note?" "No, Prabhuji." "That's it." It is included. He directs the class to the purport of BG 7.8. [Figure flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **Why the _mahā-mantra_ needs nothing added:**
  - _Mahā_ — the _ācāryas_ explain one meaning as _mahābhāva-ṭhākurāṇī_, Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī.
  - _Mantra_ — a mantra indicates its _mantra-devatā_, and the _mantra-devatā_ here is Krishna.
  - And who is Krishna? The Bhāgavatam's First Canto states _kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam_ — Krishna is the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
  - Therefore all those other mantras are included, because the _mantra-devatā_ of this mantra is Keśava.
- The listener confirms the takeaway — devotees need not say _oṁ tat sat_ — and the teacher agrees.

**3. Isn't _yajña_ really the traditional fire ceremony? [76:00 – 78:00]**

- A listener notes that from childhood, and in many traditions, _yajña_ has meant lighting a fire and making offerings into it, and that definition seems set. How does the broader meaning fit?
- **The teacher distinguishes the gross form from the concept.**
  - The gross form is more prominent in our minds because we have seen it more — a marriage with a fire-pit, a function with a _yajña-kuṇḍa_.
  - The concept is what he has been explaining: whatever we do for the Lord. Chanting the holy name is also _yajña_, and there is no _kuṇḍa_ in it; reading _śāstra_ is also _yajña_, with no _kuṇḍa_.
- **His reductio:** if _yajña_ could happen only in a _kuṇḍa_, how would all activities become _yajña_? One would have to carry a _yajña-kuṇḍa_ around like a tiffin box.
- **The scriptural basis:** BG 3.9 — work must be done as sacrifice, otherwise work binds. [Recitation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **The skill of action:** how to make whatever action we are doing related to the Lord, and done for His pleasure. That is the understanding to have. The class continues the next day. [78:00]
