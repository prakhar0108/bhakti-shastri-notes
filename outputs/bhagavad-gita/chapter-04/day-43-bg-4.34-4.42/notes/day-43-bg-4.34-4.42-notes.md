## Continuity & Recap from the Preceding Class (BG 4.25 – 4.33) [02:00 – 04:00]

- **The previous section.** Krishna described many kinds of _yajña_, and concluded that all the different works one performs culminate in knowledge — the captions render this as _sarvaṁ karmākhilaṁ jñāne parisamāpyate_. [02:00 – 04:00]
- **Knowledge-sacrifice is better.** Krishna also said that sacrifice performed in knowledge is better than the sacrifice of material possessions. The teacher distinguishes two parts of any religious act:
  - **The gross part** — donating things, performing austerity, keeping a vow, conducting a ritual.
  - **The subtle part** — the consciousness in which it is done.
- **Why knowledge matters.** When work is done in knowledge, without desire for the result, the person comes to knowledge and does not incur the reaction of work.
- **The bridge to today.** The natural question is: how is that knowledge obtained? This section answers it — how knowledge is received, its glories, the qualification needed, and who is fit (_adhikārī_) and unfit to receive it. [02:00 – 04:00]

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 4.34 [04:00 – 24:00]

<!-- verse:4.34 -->

**Context.** Knowledge can be acquired in many ways, but here Krishna gives the standard and most recommended way. The teacher calls it a famous verse that most people will have heard before. [04:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tad viddhi_: Try to understand that — learn the method (_vidhi_). The teacher's point is that before entering anything one must understand its science, and understanding the science of spiritual life is very important; a person who acts without it makes many mistakes. [04:00]
- _Praṇipātena_: _Praṇipāta_ is _prakṛṣṭa-rūpa nipāta_ — falling down completely, the way a rod falls, as in offering _daṇḍavat_. He stresses that this is not only external prostration; it means **internal submission**. [04:00]
- _Paripraśnena_: Inquiry made humbly, in a favourable frame of mind. [06:00]
- _Sevayā_: Service, done for the pleasure of the guru and of the Lord, not for oneself. [06:00]
- _Jñāninas tattva-darśinaḥ_: The great souls who have seen the truth; approaching them with these three, a person receives knowledge. [06:00]

#### Following used to be part of culture [06:00]

- The principle is simple, but today it is rare.
- In earlier times following someone was part of the culture: a younger brother listened to his elder brother, children obeyed their parents.
- Today following is rare, and the word itself has a wrong connotation.
- The teacher's observation: people who followed could sometimes make mistakes — someone might follow the wrong person — but not following today creates far more trouble.

#### The universal principle of learning [06:00 – 10:00]

- Even for ordinary things the method is: **go to the expert, surrender to him, ask relevant questions, and serve.**
- His examples: maths, science, music, art, cooking — whatever you want to learn, go to an expert and submit to him a little.
- With a mood of service, the expert will reveal even the finest details, everything he knows.
- **The old gurukula pattern:** someone who wanted to learn the tabla, or music, went and lived with the teacher and served there, whatever family he came from.
- If this is the rule for ordinary skills, it is even more important in spiritual life.

#### All three are needed, not one or two [08:00 – 12:00]

The teacher insists that **three** things are needed, and goes through what happens when one is missing:

1. **Submission without inquiry → blind following.** Two kinds of people ask no questions: one who already understands everything, and one who understands nothing. A person who submits but has no curiosity may not even be listening to what the guru is saying.
2. **Service without submission and inquiry → incomplete.** To serve, one must at least know the master's mood, because service is done for the master's pleasure.
3. **Inquiry without following → will not work.** A person sits attentively, makes notes, asks a great many questions — but follows nothing; he only wants answers.

- **Ignorant versus stubborn.** An ignorant person can inquire and come to know. But someone who knows and still does not follow is called stubborn — rigid.
- **The goal differs.** In the material world the goal of learning is to become an expert; in spiritual life the goal is to serve. When all three are combined, it is the perfect combination for receiving knowledge. [10:00]

#### Why a guide is needed at all [12:00 – 14:00]

- **Lust covers knowledge.** The enjoying tendency (_kāma_) covers knowledge, as the Third Chapter explained; that is why one needs to approach a spiritual master. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- The teacher maps the three words onto a doctor and a patient:
  - **_Praṇipāta_ — feeling the need.** First understand: "I don't know; I need someone." Until a person feels the need, nothing works. If an MD doctor stopped you on the road, checked your pulse and announced a disease, you would not care. It works when the patient goes to the doctor himself.
  - **_Paripraśna_ — wanting to know.** "I don't know the way, and I want to know." A person may be ill but not want medicine at all; then there is no point going to the doctor.
  - **_Sevā_ — following the instructions.** It is not enough to visit the doctor; the medicine must also be taken. Going to the doctor and returning without taking what he prescribed achieves nothing.
- **Practice changes vision.** As a person follows guidance, relevant inquiries arise; as practice continues, vision changes and growth follows. [14:00]

#### The purport: why the guru, and which guru [14:00 – 18:00]

The teacher reads through Srila Prabhupada's purport, which he calls very beautiful and long, and comments line by line. [Purport readings flagged; see Verification Flags.]

- **The path is difficult, so a guide is needed.** An expert teacher makes it easy; with guidance, devotion becomes easier.
  - "Behind every champion you will find a good teacher" — behind Arjuna stands Droṇācārya.
  - A person who feels he needs no guidance at all is either in ignorance (_avidyā_) or in false ego.
- **The bona fide spiritual master is in disciplic succession.** He is not presenting his own ideas; he gives what comes down the _paramparā_. No one becomes realized by manufacturing his own process. [16:00]
- **Not by independent study of books.** Spiritual life is a matter of **application, not information.** The teacher describes levels of understanding:
  - some things are understood by reading;
  - some only after reading and contemplating;
  - some after reading, contemplating and discussing with others;
  - and some only with time.
- **The South Indian brāhmaṇa.** He was illiterate, yet understood the essence of the Gita because he followed the instruction of his guru. [18:00]
- **Senior Vaiṣṇavas too.** Someone may think he will check everything directly with the guru — but how often does one actually meet him? Learning and submission are a basic principle, and one also needs the help of senior Vaiṣṇavas. [16:00]

#### Surrender, service and the right kind of questions [18:00 – 22:00]

- **Full surrender and menial service.** Once a guru is accepted, the mood of service and surrender towards him should remain, without false prestige. [18:00]
- **Questions change with the stage.** Before initiation the question is whether to accept this guru — that is personal. After initiation the question is how to follow his instructions. [Example flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **The guru's satisfaction is the secret of advancement.** Every line of the purport is important; unless there is submission and service, inquiries will not be effective — mere questioning without submission and service has little meaning.
- **The Rāmānujācārya anecdote.** The teacher recalls a class at Radha Gopinath temple, Chowpatty: a scholarly disciple asked Rāmānujācārya the meaning of _sarva-dharmān parityajya_. Rāmānujācārya sent him to Goṣṭhīpūrṇa, who lived far away, to learn it. The teacher's comment: if today someone in a Bhāgavatam class were told, "to understand this, go from Bombay to Kanpur," he would say, "Leave it, Prabhu, I'll ask another question." [20:00] [Names and distance flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **Passing the test.** The disciple must pass the test of the spiritual master; when the master sees the genuine desire, he automatically blesses the disciple with genuine understanding.
- **The link with the guru and with Srila Prabhupada.** [20:00 – 22:00]
  - First: do we feel a connection with Srila Prabhupada, and with our own spiritual master?
  - Second: within the institution, wherever we serve, we are serving for him.
  - **The sense of belonging makes a person strong.** A man works in Bombay while his family lives in Bihar, but he knows he is working for them. A disciple should feel that way for Srila Prabhupada's mission and the spiritual master's value system, and try to serve so that everyone blesses him — and through those blessings spiritual understanding comes.
- **Blind following and absurd inquiry are both condemned.** Blind following means not even knowing what principles the guru is teaching; absurd inquiry means useless questions with little to do with spiritual life. This is why _paripraśna_ is given. [22:00]

#### Perfect teacher, perfect student [22:00 – 24:00]

- When the student is submissive and always ready to render service, the exchange of knowledge and inquiry becomes perfect.
- The teacher's pairs: **Krishna** the best teacher and **Arjuna** the best student; **Śukadeva Gosvāmī** and **Parīkṣit Mahārāja**; **Caitanya Mahāprabhu**, who discussed with **Rūpa Gosvāmī** for ten days. [Further pairs flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- His principle: whenever there is a self-realized teacher and a sincere disciple, the transmission of transcendental knowledge becomes very effective.
- **Our position.** By Srila Prabhupada's mercy we are in ISKCON, we have his books, and many of his disciples live by them. Srila Prabhupada is a perfect teacher and the Bhaktivedanta purports are perfect; we have to see from our side how we can improve as disciples.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Transcendental knowledge is received the way any real skill is learnt — go to the expert, submit, ask, and serve — but in spiritual life all three must be present together. Submission without inquiry becomes blind following, service without inquiry misses the master's mood, and inquiry without following achieves nothing. Because lust covers knowledge and spiritual life is a matter of application rather than information, one needs a teacher in disciplic succession, senior Vaiṣṇavas, and a sense of belonging to the guru's mission; the guru's satisfaction is the secret of advancement. [04:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.35 [24:00 – 28:00]

<!-- verse:4.35 -->

**Context.** This verse gives the **fruit** of knowledge. [24:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yaj jñātvā na punar moham_: Once knowledge is obtained, one will not fall into illusion again. [24:00 – 26:00]
- _Evaṁ yāsyasi pāṇḍava_: You will not fall again into illusion as you are now. [26:00]
- _Yena bhūtāny aśeṣāṇi_: All living entities without exception. [26:00]
- _Drakṣyasy ātmany atho mayi_: You will see them in connection with Me — see the whole world spiritually, and how everything belongs to Me. [26:00]

#### What we now call knowledge is not yet knowledge [26:00]

- The first implication the teacher draws: **we are not yet in knowledge.** What we now call knowledge is not it; having information is not knowledge.
- Once knowledge is attained, a person sees all living entities in connection with the Lord.
- The present illusion — "I am the enjoyer, the controller, the proprietor" — exists because we cannot see things in connection with the Lord, and so we want to enjoy everything.

#### The dog and the lion [26:00]

- Throw a bone before a dog, and the dog looks at the bone. Throw it before a lion, and the lion looks behind to see who threw it.
- Similarly, after receiving knowledge a person is able to see **who is behind**. A devotee sees that behind every situation there is Krishna, and his outlook changes.

#### The person behind the bike [26:00 – 28:00]

- Someone sees a beautiful bike and feels he should enjoy it. Then he sees that someone is standing behind it — and the tendency to enjoy it goes.
- In the same way the three false notions change once knowledge comes:
  - **Enjoyership** gives way once he sees who stands behind the object.
  - **Controllership** becomes **responsible but detached**.
  - **Proprietorship** — "it is mine" — becomes "it is not mine; I am a caretaker."
- His summary: when we see this world **disconnected** from the Lord, we are attracted by it; when we see it **in connection** with the Lord, it does not affect us.
- The purport calls this the result of receiving knowledge from a realized source — knowing things as they are. [28:00]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The fruit of transcendental knowledge is a permanent change of vision: one no longer falls back into illusion, because one sees every living being and every object in connection with Krishna. Like the lion that looks for who threw the bone, the person of knowledge looks behind the object to its owner — so enjoyership falls away, control becomes responsible detachment, and ownership becomes caretaking. [24:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.36 [28:00 – 38:00]

<!-- verse:4.36 -->

**Context.** Krishna now describes the **power** of transcendental knowledge — how much potency it has. [28:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Api cet_: Even if — the same construction as in the verse _api cet su-durācāro_, which the teacher cites as BG 9.30. [30:00]
- _Pāpebhyaḥ sarvebhyaḥ pāpa-kṛt-tamaḥ_: Even the greatest sinner among all sinners. [30:00; 34:00]
- _Jñāna-plavena_: In the boat of knowledge — if he accepts it. [30:00]
- _Vṛjinaṁ santariṣyasi_: He will cross the ocean of miseries — the ocean of material existence (_bhava-sāgara_). [30:00]

#### The past background need not be checked [30:00 – 32:00]

- Once a person receives transcendental knowledge — becomes a devotee — there is no need to look at his past background.
- The teacher cites the principle that seeing a Vaiṣṇava in terms of birth is an offence: do not ask whether he was formerly a _kṣatriya_ or anything else.
- **Srila Prabhupada's revolutionary organization.** In ISKCON many devotees do not even know which caste others come from. Outside, many religious institutions look at caste first.
- **The temple meeting.** The teacher recounts visiting a Kali temple with a devotee, Dr. Rahul Prabhu, to plan a programme. The organizers were preparing for Paraśurāma Jayantī and discussing the need for big numbers. When he was introduced and asked his name, he gave his pre-initiation name, which carried a brāhmaṇa surname — and the reply was, "Good — only a brāhmaṇa can bring revival." His point: caste is very prominent there, but in bhakti the principle is different; once a person comes to devotion there is nothing to check. [30:00 – 32:00]

#### Potency is accessed only by application [32:00 – 34:00]

- **This is the power of transcendental knowledge** — however many sins a person has committed.
- But **to access the potency of spiritual knowledge, it has to be applied.** The formulas for accessing spiritual life are given in these verses and in Srila Prabhupada's books; whoever applies them accesses that power to the same degree.
- Otherwise a person stays in illusion even after knowing everything — even after memorizing 700 verses.
- The ocean is genuinely hard to cross: the attachments and the craving for sense objects are very strong. But once a person accepts spiritual life through _praṇipāta_ and _paripraśna_, tries to obey and engages in service, transcendental knowledge is illuminated in the heart, and its results are what this verse states.

#### Why the guarantee is such a big offer [34:00 – 36:00]

- Krishna is giving a guarantee: even the greatest of all sinners will be purified if he sits in the boat of knowledge. More such verses on the power of bhakti will come later.
- The offer is big because so many impressions (_saṁskāras_) have accumulated. The teacher names three sources:
  1. **Upbringing** — what one received from one's parents.
  2. **Previous births.**
  3. **Friends and culture** — the kind of friends one had and the culture one grew up in.
- Together these create many wrong _saṁskāras_ and _vāsanās_ — yet the boat of knowledge purifies all of it.

#### How can a great sinner receive knowledge? Two readings [36:00 – 38:00]

A question can be raised: if a person is so sinful, how will he obtain transcendental knowledge at all? The teacher gives two understandings from the _ācāryas_:

1. **A hypothetical that glorifies knowledge.** It is like someone saying, "If I were a millionaire, I would have sponsored all of Srila Prabhupada's books, and your whole Janmāṣṭamī festival by myself" — which means he is not one. In the same sense Caitanya Mahāprabhu told Śrīvāsa Ṭhākura that even if Lakṣmī herself had to beg from door to door, his house would never lack anything. The statement strengthens the principle rather than describing a likely case.
2. **Mercy.** If a person receives mercy, it can happen: Mṛgāri the hunter belonged to exactly that category of sinners, but received the mercy of a bona fide guru and accepted it. [Name flagged; see Verification Flags.]

- **No swimmer can cross the ocean.** However expert the swimmer, he needs a strong, good boat — and the boat of knowledge is what carries a person out of the ocean of material existence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Transcendental knowledge is a boat strong enough to carry even the greatest sinner across the ocean of material existence, which no swimmer can cross by his own skill. That is why a devotee's past background is never checked. But the potency is accessed only by application — one can memorize 700 verses and still remain in illusion. Whether read as a hypothetical that glorifies knowledge or as a promise fulfilled by a bona fide guru's mercy, the verse is an enormous offer to souls loaded with impressions from upbringing, past lives and association. [28:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.37 [38:00 – 48:00]

<!-- verse:4.37 -->

**Context.** Krishna continues to glorify knowledge, now with an example. [38:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yathā_: Just as. [38:00]
- _Edhāṁsi samiddho 'gniḥ_: A blazing fire with firewood placed in it. [38:00]
- _Bhasma-sāt kurute_: Turns it to ashes. [38:00]
- _Jñānāgniḥ_: The fire of knowledge. [38:00]
- _Sarva-karmāṇi_: All works — meaning **all reactions of works**, which are destroyed once a person becomes situated in knowledge. [38:00]

#### Situated in knowledge, everything changes [38:00 – 40:00]

- Whatever is put into a blazing fire is burnt to ashes; once a person is situated in knowledge, all reactions go.
- When the soul is situated in knowledge it acts in its constitutional position — its **identity, purpose and motivation** all change.
- **Before knowledge: _ahaṁ mameti_.** "I and mine" is called ignorance, a symptom of _avidyā_. Because of it a person:
  - considers himself the body;
  - considers only his family and relatives his own, and his relationship with them his identity — "mother, father, brother, sister, wife, children: this is my world";
  - takes satisfying them as the purpose of life, and that becomes his motivation.
- **After knowledge:** "I am the Lord's servant; pleasing the Lord is the purpose of my life; everywhere else I do my duty." Everything changes. [40:00]

#### Even happiness binds: the golden handcuff [40:00 – 42:00]

- Cutting the reactions of karma is very hard, and whatever happiness and distress we now experience in this world comes prominently because of karma.
- Someone may say distress is understandable, but what is wrong with happiness? The teacher's answer: **even with happiness one must stay in bondage.**
  - A handcuff may be gold or iron — but either way it has to be worn.
  - If someone gave charity with desire for the result, he will have to come in the next birth to receive that result — and how much suffering there is in coming, including entering a mother's womb.
- Until the account is cleared, one stays stuck in trouble. For so many births we served relatives, family and society, and the account was never cleared. **The account first begins to clear when one engages in the Lord's service.** Any intelligent being should feel concern about this.

#### The EMI and the hospital bill [42:00 – 44:00]

- Taking a loan is almost a social status today, but then there is the tension of the EMI, and if it is not paid the amount keeps growing. Imagine someone saying, "Come to me — I will pay off your whole loan."
- Or you are admitted to hospital and the bill keeps rising, faster than you recover — the tension about the bill becomes greater than the concern about getting well. Imagine someone paying the whole bill.

#### "Paid in full with a glass of milk" [44:00 – 48:00]

The teacher tells a story:

- A boy who went selling small items on a very hot day knocked at a house and asked for some water. The lady saw how thirsty he was and gave him a glass of milk. When he asked how much he owed, she said, "My mother told me never to charge for kindness." [44:00]
- Time passed. The boy became a famous doctor, Dr. Howard Kelly. The lady grew old and fell ill with a rare disease; the village doctor could not understand it, and the city doctors could not diagnose it either, so the case was referred to Dr. Kelly. [44:00 – 46:00]
- When he saw her name he remembered her. He took up the case himself and had her admitted to the city hospital. She was not a rich lady, and as treatment went on and she slowly recovered, her worry about the bill kept increasing. [46:00]
- Dr. Kelly had asked for the final bill file to be given to him. On the day of discharge she was afraid even to open it — and found his signature with the words: **"Paid in full with a glass of milk."** [46:00 – 48:00]
- The teacher's lesson: **an act of selflessness is small, but its penetration is very high.** Think how grateful that lady must have been. We should feel crores of times more gratitude to Krishna — the doctor settled the bill for one disease, but the Lord burns the entire reaction of karma. [48:00]
- **How much the Lord values surrender.** The problem is that we do not surrender. The teacher criticizes a loose, casual approach to bhakti in which "we don't want to offer our best for Krishna." Material examples like the doctor's are understood very quickly; but as soon as a person surrenders, the Lord closes all the files of so many births and so many sins. [48:00]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The fire of knowledge burns all reactions of work just as a blazing fire turns firewood to ashes. Situated in knowledge, the soul's identity, purpose and motivation all shift from "I and mine" to being the Lord's servant. Since even pious happiness is a golden handcuff that brings one back for another birth, this is like having an unpayable loan or hospital bill settled in full — and, as in the glass-of-milk story, it should inspire immense gratitude to Krishna, who closes the files of countless births the moment a soul surrenders. [38:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.38 [48:00 – 52:00]

<!-- verse:4.38 -->

**Context.** The glorification of knowledge continues: nothing in this world is as purifying as it. [48:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Na hi jñānena sadṛśaṁ pavitram iha vidyate_: In this world there is nothing as purifying as knowledge. [48:00]
- _Tat svayaṁ yoga-saṁsiddhaḥ_: One who becomes situated in yoga in this way. [48:00]
- _Kālenātmani vindati_: In due course of time he attains divine bliss within. [48:00 – 50:00]

#### Purifying the heart is the hard purification [50:00]

- There are many purifying things in this world, but they purify **externally**. Purifying from within is much harder.
  - A dirty room — add some detergent and clean it.
  - Clothes get dirty and are washed.
- Every object has its own method of purification:
  - some things are purified with water;
  - gold is burnt; iron is heated in a blast furnace;
  - some things are purified by heating.
- Purifying the **heart** is very difficult — and this knowledge purifies the heart of all contamination. Then the person, purified in yoga, relishes that knowledge.
- The teacher links this to the previous verses: one will have no more illusion (4.35), and even the greatest sinner will be purified (4.36).

#### Advertised guarantees versus the Lord's [50:00 – 52:00]

- The world is full of advertisements that are actually cheating. The teacher cites an insurance company's slogan about carrying the burden of _yoga_ and _kṣema_: you keep paying as long as you live, and then you die — so what has been insured? [Slogan flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- Srila Prabhupada said it is not life insurance but **death insurance**.
- His comparison: you die in an accident but the car was insured — what does that mean for you? No one except the Lord can actually give a guarantee.
- _Kālenātmani vindati_: with time, the person attains the divine experience.
- **The four verses of glory.** The teacher sums up that 4.35, 4.36, 4.37 and 4.38 — four verses — describe the glory of knowledge. Next comes who is qualified to receive it. [52:00]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Most purifying agents in this world work on the outside, and each object needs its own method; the heart is the hardest thing to purify, and nothing purifies it like transcendental knowledge. Unlike worldly guarantees — "life insurance" that is really death insurance — the Lord's guarantee is real: one who becomes situated in yoga relishes this knowledge and, in due course of time, attains divine bliss within. [48:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.39 [52:00 – 58:00]

<!-- verse:4.39 -->

**Context.** Who is qualified (_adhikārī_) to receive this knowledge? [52:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śraddhāvān labhate jñānam_: The person of faith obtains knowledge — first of all there must be faith, which Krishna mentions again and again. [52:00]
- _Tat-paraḥ_: Commitment — readiness to act on that faith. [54:00]
- _Saṁyatendriyaḥ_: Sense control — the senses must be restrained. [52:00 – 54:00]
- _Parāṁ śāntim_: Deep, supreme peace. [54:00]
- _Acireṇādhigacchati_: Attains it soon — it will not take long. [54:00]

#### Three qualifications, together [52:00 – 54:00]

- **Faith alone is not enough.** "Prabhuji, I have faith" — but there must also be readiness to bring that faith into one's life.
- The three are:
  1. **_Śraddhā_ — faith.**
  2. **_Tat-paraḥ_ — commitment** to act on it.
  3. **_Saṁyatendriyaḥ_ — sense control.**
- Knowledge is not obtained merely by making notes or PPTs. Only when the three are combined is it attained.
- The teacher observes that these verses are very clear: by studying even one such verse we can see why there is no progress in bhakti.

#### The brain-tumour diagnosis [54:00 – 56:00]

- The first step is for the soul to understand that **enjoyment is my biggest problem — I am stuck in the material world.** Other problems exist, but this one is the biggest.
- **The illustration:** a person is doing everything — caring for his family, working his job. Suddenly the doctor says, "You have a brain tumour; you have only four or five months."
  - His family, relatives, business and friends are all still there.
  - But where does he focus? **On the treatment** — because if the project itself is over in five months, what is left?
- **We are fortunate** to have been diagnosed in spiritual life: "this is the main disease, it is serious, and this is the main thing to do." Yet a person is unfortunate if, even after this, it is still business as usual.
- **Only the fortunate take the treatment seriously.** Many may come to spiritual life, but the fortunate take it seriously.

#### How faith arises [56:00]

- Faith arises as one practises: **hearing, reading, chanting, and devotee association.**
- Without faith a person does not even make a proper start: only when there is faith does a person step forward. [58:00]

#### _Tat-paraḥ_: awake, committed, not casual [56:00 – 58:00]

- _Tat-paraḥ_ means a person engaged and fully committed — awake in spiritual life, not asleep, not loose, **not a casual person**.
- He knows how important this is: if the opportunity of the human birth passes, it is gone, and he will keep wandering.
- So many impressions are stuck to the mind that must be removed, and that requires one's **best effort** — that is _tat-paraḥ_.
- **His illustrations of eagerness:** [58:00]
  - "Stay tuned," as YouTube channels say.
  - Children with a Sunday holiday and a cricket match between two teams: everyone wants to give their best, wakes early and arrives ready.
  - A person going to a movie theatre goes fully prepared, mentally ready.
- The teacher notes he has taken detailed classes on 4.34 and 4.35 earlier, and that these verses can be studied in much more detail.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge comes to the person who has faith, commitment and sense control together — faith alone, or notes and slides, will not do. Like a patient told he has five months to live, the fortunate soul who understands that enjoyment is his biggest problem focuses on the treatment. Faith grows from hearing, reading, chanting and association, and _tat-paraḥ_ means bringing to spiritual life the eagerness children bring to a Sunday cricket match — and then supreme peace comes soon. [52:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.40 [58:00 – 68:00]

<!-- verse:4.40 -->

**Context.** Having said who is qualified, Krishna now says who is **not**. The teacher asks the class to stay alert: this is a very important verse, describing three kinds of people. [58:00 – 60:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ajñaḥ_: One who has no knowledge of the scriptures — ignorant, has read nothing, living almost like an animal. [60:00]
- _Aśraddadhānaḥ_: One who has knowledge of the scriptures but no faith. [60:00]
- _Saṁśayātmā vinaśyati_: The doubting soul is destroyed. [60:00]
- _Nāyaṁ loko 'sti na paro na sukhaṁ saṁśayātmanaḥ_: For the doubter there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness. [66:00]

#### Three kinds of unqualified persons [60:00 – 62:00]

1. **_Ajña_ — the ignorant.** He does not know the scriptures at all. The teacher adds that he still incurs sin — it is not that sin does not touch him because he did not know. [62:00]
2. **_Aśraddadhāna_ — the faithless scholar.** He reads the scriptures but has no faith in them.
   - Many study religion at universities, or study Sanskrit.
   - In Vrindavan too, some study Sanskrit and the Bhāgavatam with the goal of becoming a _Bhāgavata_ _kathākāra_, and start by practising the harmonium.
   - He reads, but has no belief in it — the way one reads a novel or fiction; to him it is mythology.
3. **_Saṁśayātmā_ — the doubter.** This is the third category, and Krishna says he is destroyed. The teacher calls it very dangerous: such a person has received the shelter of a Vaiṣṇava _sampradāya_, is connected with a bona fide guru-_paramparā_, has Srila Prabhupada's books — and still doubts. [60:00 – 62:00]

#### The doubter's doubt: "Will it work for me?" [62:00 – 64:00]

- The _saṁśayātmā_ knows the scriptures and even has some faith in them. His doubt is: **will it work for me?**
- "Prabhuji, that's just how it is — it's written, but not written to actually be followed."
- Krishna says many have come to His abode by following this — yet this person, with faith in the scriptures, doubts whether it will work for him. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **The law of gravity.** One man fell from a building and died; a second fell; a third is thinking, "I'll jump — but will the law of gravity work for me?"
- This is a useless and dangerous category: having received everything, a person still stays here, and progress becomes very slow. We should first check ourselves to avoid it. [64:00]

#### Following one, not every baba [64:00 – 66:00]

- Mṛgāri chanted the holy name; so many people chanted and so many were delivered — and still the doubt "will it work for us?"
- **The modern form of doubt.** Even after coming into the _paramparā_, a person listens to this baba, then another, then a third; listens to someone's kīrtana; watches the reels and shorts of whichever baba has become famous.
- The teacher's principle:
  - **Many people can be pure** — Srila Prabhupada is not the only sādhu.
  - **But you have to follow one**; otherwise you will not be purified.
  - If one follows any one _tattva-darśī_ guru properly, he becomes purified. The doubter follows no one properly: he likes one person's kīrtana, another's lecture reels, a third's smile. **Bhakti will not happen by such drama.**
- **Mixing worship.** "Jai Mata Di" in Navaratri, then "Ganapati Bappa," and "Krishna is God, but keep the lower ones happy too" — the teacher calls this drama. At least in a devotee's life this should be very clear. **We respect everyone, but one's _niṣṭhā_ (fixed allegiance) must be clear.** Srila Prabhupada has given a very clear path, and we are very fortunate; others go around in confusion without even knowing which goddess is which. [64:00 – 66:00]

#### Why the doubter is the worst case [66:00]

- _Nāyaṁ loko 'sti na paro_: neither in this world nor in the next is there happiness — the doubter stays disturbed, because **he cannot surrender.**
- Comparing the three:
  - The **ignorant** can be given knowledge.
  - The **faithless** person, coming into devotee association, will gain some faith.
  - The **doubter** had faith in the scriptures from the start, but lacks the faith that it will work for him.
- Hence: in bhakti, **increasing one's faith** is very important.

#### How faith grows: two routes [66:00 – 68:00]

The teacher recalls a principle he gave earlier: faith comes either in the association of those who have it, or by direct experience of the higher reality.

1. **Association.** When a great devotee — a guru mahārāja, a senior disciple — comes to the temple, a kind of faith arises in their association; seeing their practice, one feels, "yes, this is possible."
2. **Direct experience.** One must **apply** spiritual things; without application there is no direct experience. Apply the ninefold bhakti — reading, hearing, chanting, service, surrender — all of it. [68:00]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Three people are unfit for transcendental knowledge: the ignorant, who can still be taught; the faithless scholar, who reads scripture as mythology but may gain faith in association; and the doubter, who has the scripture, the paramparā and the books and still asks, "will it work for me?" — like a man wondering whether gravity applies to him. The doubter cannot surrender, so he has happiness neither here nor hereafter; his modern form is sampling every famous baba without following one. The remedy is to increase faith, through association with those who have it and through direct experience gained by actually applying the practices. [58:00 – 68:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.41 [68:00 – 70:00]

<!-- verse:4.41 -->

**Context.** The teacher extends the class by about ten minutes to complete the chapter today, since during Kārtika classes may be fewer. He notes that this verse and the next are what confuse Arjuna, and that confusion opens the Fifth Chapter. [68:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yoga-sannyasta-karmāṇam_: Renouncing action by performing it in the spirit of yoga. [68:00]
- _Jñāna-sañchinna-saṁśayam_: One whose doubts are cut by knowledge. [70:00]
- _Ātmavantam_: One situated at the level of self-realization. [70:00]
- _Na karmāṇi nibadhnanti dhanañjaya_: O Dhanañjaya, works do not bind him — he is not caught in the bondage of karma. [70:00]

#### Renunciation in action, not renunciation of action [68:00 – 70:00]

- After glorifying knowledge so much, Krishna says _yoga-sannyasta-karmāṇam_.
- **What Krishna means:** not _sannyāsa_ of work, but renunciation **in** action — engage in yoga and do the work; what is to be renounced is the **desire for the result**, while performing one's own work.
- **What Arjuna hears:** "All actions have to be given up" — renunciation **of** action. This is where Arjuna becomes confused.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Krishna asks for renunciation in action — doing one's work in the spirit of yoga while giving up the desire for its result — not renunciation of action. One whose doubts are cut by knowledge and who is situated in the self is not bound by works. Arjuna, however, hears it as "give up all action," and this misreading sets up the Fifth Chapter. [68:00 – 70:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

**1. We are not perfect students — what qualities can we develop to receive the guru properly? [72:00 – 76:00]**

- **Perfect knowledge, not necessarily a perfect person.** Someone once asked Srila Prabhupada whether he was perfect; he replied that he was not perfect, but the knowledge he was giving was perfect. For us Srila Prabhupada is a perfect guru.
- The three qualities for a disciple are the ones in the verse — _praṇipāta_, _paripraśna_, _sevā_ — and anyone can experience their effect.
- Whoever we take knowledge from may not be perfect, but the knowledge is. Our mood should be one of **submission**.
- **Submission means a desire to apply.** Whatever I am told, I want to apply it.
- **The _kathā_ mindset.** In some villages people attend a _kathā_ with their minds firmly set: "Nothing here is to be applied, not even by mistake; at the end we get the Lord's prasāda." Doing bhakti with that mindset wastes a lot of time and slows everything down. [74:00]
- **_Paripraśna_ is how to apply** — discussion about how to bring it into life.
- **_Sevā_ is to please** the spiritual master and Srila Prabhupada — and **one who does this is the most joyful person.**
- The teacher cites Bhakti Tirtha Maharaja: most people today are in great misery because they want to manipulate the whole world according to their desires instead of becoming genuine servants; a genuine servant is always joyful. Misery has increased because people want to use all of creation to fulfil their desires, and there is no satisfaction in that. The more we try this combination of three in life, the more satisfaction we experience.

**2. What is transcendental knowledge? [74:00 – 76:00]**

- Transcendental knowledge is that **we are the Lord's servants** — the constitutional position of the living entity is to be Krishna's eternal servant — and to be situated in that knowledge.
- **_Niṣkāma karma-yoga_ as a comparison:**
  - its _sādhana_ is performing _varṇāśrama_ duties without desire;
  - its _sādhya_ is realizing "I am not the body, I am the soul" — and the person comes to the level of knowledge.
- He connects this to a recent Srila Prabhupada lecture giving the definition of liberation as being situated in one's constitutional position. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]

**3. We hear and try to apply, but cannot — what then? [76:00 – 78:00]**

- That is exactly why the verse says _tat-paraḥ_.
- **Quality association.** If we compromise on association, there is difficulty. The teacher recommends reading Bhakti Tirtha Maharaja's last letter, written before he left his body. Quality association has two parts:
  1. **One's own _sādhana_** must be very good.
  2. **At least one or two sincere devotees** with whom one has a heart-to-heart relationship — at least one person, engaged very sincerely, with whom the connection is very strong.
- **_Tat-paraḥ_ means not leaving the path.** There may be difficulty, but one must not leave it; one keeps at it with determination. [78:00]
- **"What is normal is actually insane."** "Everyone does it this way" — but if you want to do what everyone does, why did you come to bhakti? Krishna Himself says only one among thousands of human beings endeavours for perfection. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **Not a pious materialist.** A devotee should not settle as a pious materialist: a lamp in the morning and "Jai Jagadish Hare," a little water offered in the evening, the newspaper, tea, a TV serial at relatives' houses, reels and news on YouTube. "This is not the life of a devotee." A devotee's life is one of commitment.

**4. Is giving up the fruit harder than giving up the work — and isn't internal purity what matters in both? [78:00 – 84:00]**

- The questioner reflects that the mind tends to read spirituality as "renounce the work," that giving up the fruit seems harder than giving up the work, and that either kind of renunciation can be merely external — so the inner motive seems decisive in both cases.
- **The level of the teaching.** Krishna is speaking here from the level of _niṣkāma karma-yoga_, as He does in the first six chapters; the essence will be given in the middle six. Srila Prabhupada has already given it in his purports: above _niṣkāma karma-yoga_ offered to the Lord is _bhakti-yoga_, and _bhakti-yoga_ already includes _niṣkāma karma_.
  - _Niṣkāma_ action means no attachment to the fruit of work.
  - _Bhakti-yoga_ means the **desire to please the Lord** — a further stage.
- **Action cannot be given up.** The _karma-sannyāsa_ of the path of knowledge is repeatedly discouraged, because it is not natural and is very difficult — renouncing action is not easy. Unless the senses are fully purified, one cannot take up the path of knowledge, which is why Krishna keeps directing Arjuna to _niṣkāma karma-yoga_. [82:00]
- **Which action?**
  - In _niṣkāma karma-yoga_: work according to _varṇāśrama_ and offer the fruit to the Lord.
  - In _bhakti-yoga_: act with the Lord at the centre, with hearing, chanting and the rest of the ninefold bhakti prominent in life.
- **From gross to subtle.** The questioner is right that someone may say he is offering the fruit without doing so internally — but everything begins gross and becomes subtle. First one offers grossly: engaging one's resources, body and wealth in service. He may still think, "I am engaging **my** wealth."
  - Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura's reply to someone who wanted to renounce everything: **the only thing you have to renounce is the false conception that you own anything.** [82:00 – 84:00]
  - That mixed mood is acceptable as a beginning; as one engages property, time, body and family in the Lord's service, purification comes and the mood itself becomes pure.
- **Why sādhana does not stay in the heart.** When one offers the fruit of work to the Lord, sādhana begins to settle in the heart. We hear and read so much, yet it does not stay — because there is no sense of desirelessness; everything is being used for one's own enjoyment, and so the knowledge does not stabilize. That is the block. [84:00]

**5. Doubt still arises despite reading and hearing — how do we deal with it? [84:00 – 86:00]**

- **The opposite of doubt is faith.** The nine stages of bhakti are a gradual development of _śraddhā_ — faith is present at every stage (_śraddhā_, _sādhu-saṅga_, _bhajana-kriyā_, _anartha-nivṛtti_ and onward) and keeps deepening. [Sequence flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- So the question is how our faith develops. His steps:
  1. **Fix the quantity** of reading, hearing and chanting, then gradually increase it.
  2. **Improve the quality** — we are reading, but how? We are hearing, but how?
  3. **Engage practically in the Lord's service** — because faith comes from practice.
- **The salary analogy.** Someone has worked in a company for ten years and his pay has not increased at all — is something wrong? Naturally he is not working properly. In the same way, if faith is not increasing in bhakti, there is a problem, and one should think about it. It does not happen merely with time; the person has to make the effort. [86:00]

**6. What is the difference between renunciation of action and renunciation in action? [86:00 – 89:21]**

- **Renunciation of action (_karma-sannyāsa_),** as described in the path of knowledge: the person gives up all action and goes to the forest. He performs no _varṇāśrama_ duty — a _brāhmaṇa_ does not do a _brāhmaṇa_'s work, a _kṣatriya_ not a _kṣatriya_'s, a _brahmacārī_ not a _brahmacārī_'s, a _gṛhastha_ no duties towards his family.
  - In the forest he performs the bare minimum — he cannot do zero — eating whatever dry leaves have fallen in the direction he walked.
  - He makes no arrangement for himself: if he finds an apple, he will not make a fruit salad. "We can, the _jñānī_ cannot." [88:00]
- **Renunciation in action:** the person keeps acting but gives up the desire for the fruit — he acts for the Lord, or for some higher purpose; for example, one who acts without desire for the purpose of realizing the self.
