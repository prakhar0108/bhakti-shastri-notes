## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 1.21 – 1.22 [08:00 – 22:00]

<!-- verse:1.21 -->

**Context.** With both armies poised and the conches sounded, the most important warrior on the Pandava side unexpectedly asks to be driven into the open ground between them. [12:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ubhayor madhye_: Between the two. The teacher notes that _ubhaya_ recurs throughout the Gita whenever two things are being contrasted. [10:00]
- _Acyuta_: The infallible one — the first of three occurrences of this name in the Gita. [16:00]
- _Yāvad etān nirīkṣe_: Until I have properly surveyed them. [10:00]
- _Yotsyamānān_: Those eager to fight. [10:00]

#### Why the request is so striking [12:00 – 14:00]

- Up to this point everything has proceeded as expected, and expected things do not hold anyone's attention. The teacher compares it to a serial that ends each episode at a climax precisely to keep people watching.
- Here something genuinely unexpected happens: with everyone's weapons already drawn, the principal warrior asks to be taken into the middle.
- He describes the scale — a vast field with the armies drawn up at a distance from each other — and the Lord calmly placing the chariot at the centre of it.
- He reads this as the Lord's own arrangement of the setting, comparing it to street theatre, where a performance begins abruptly in the middle of a crowd and draws everyone in before the real conversation takes place. He recalls processions where a drama would start suddenly and a crowd would gather around it.

#### What Arjuna is actually doing [16:00]

The teacher's formulation: rather than asking what he should do, Arjuna wants to assess the situation on the basis of direct observation. He notes that this is where most people become bewildered.

#### Acyuta — the Lord who never fails His devotee [16:00 – 22:00]

The teacher gives the purport's first paragraph the heading "glorious master" and treats the name as the key to the whole section.

- _Acyuta_ means one who never falls from his position, and specifically here, one who never fails in showing affection to His devotees.
- His application: whatever calamity comes, whatever plan fails, whatever close relative fails us, the Lord does not fail one who has taken shelter of Him.
- **The redefinition of God.** He contrasts the common conception of God as a severe senior figure passing judgment and consigning people to one destination or another, with how the Lord actually enters the Gita: as one who takes His devotee's instruction and drives his chariot. His phrase for it is a God who loves to serve, and who does not delight in being God.
- Summarizing the purport: the Lord accepted the position of His devotee's charioteer without hesitation, and His supreme position remained untouched by it. The relationship between master and servant is sweet and transcendental; the servant is always eager to serve, and the Lord too looks for some service He can render, taking special pleasure in being given an order by a pure devotee rather than giving one.

#### Why this is the closest of all relationships [20:00]

Drawing on Brahma's prayers in the Tenth Canto, the teacher traces the ordinary chain of affection:

- A living being loves himself most of all.
- He loves parents and relatives because they are connected to him.
- He loves his body because the soul is within it — so the affection is really for the soul.
- And the soul is loved because it is part of the Supersoul.

Therefore the living entity's closest relationship of all is with the Lord. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]

#### The Lord never rejects [20:00 – 22:00]

- In the world there is competition, and people remain with the successful. The teacher's phrase is that failure is always an orphan.
- In devotion it is not so: the Lord is always ready to accept. He notes that even Ravana was given opportunities until the very end, and would have been forgiven had he come.

#### The search for a real master [22:00 – 24:00]

The teacher cites a verse of Yamunacharya from the Stotra-ratna, quoted in the Mukunda-mala-stotra, and draws out its argument:

- The living entity in this world is actually searching for a master, but has had the misfortune of finding wrong ones. Hence the derogatory conception of a boss as one who torments and exploits.
- If he found an ideal master, he would no longer want to become a master himself. The whole reason we are here is the mentality of proprietorship and lordship, and we continue attempting it even in devotional life.
- The prayer is to remain the Lord's constant servant, at which point the mind — which now runs in every direction — becomes entirely peaceful.
- He adds Prahlada Maharaja's statement to Nrisimhadeva in the Seventh Canto: that their relationship is fixed, the Lord being the eternal master and himself the eternal servant, and that he wants no inducement of any kind. [Quotations flagged; see Verification Flags.]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Gita introduces God not as a judge but as one who accepts an order from His devotee. _Acyuta_ names the guarantee behind that: He never fails in affection, whoever else does. [08:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.23 [24:00 – 26:00]

<!-- verse:1.23 -->

**Context.** Arjuna specifies what he wants to look at — those who have come to fight on behalf of Dhritarashtra's evil-minded son. [24:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yotsyamānān avekṣe 'ham_: I wish to see those who are ready to fight. [24:00 – 26:00]
- _Dhārtarāṣṭrasya durbuddheḥ_: Of the evil-minded son of Dhritarashtra. [24:00 – 26:00]
- _Yuddhe priya-cikīrṣavaḥ_: Those wishing to please him in battle — well-wishers. [26:00]

#### An objection and its answer [24:00 – 26:00]

Following Baladeva Vidyabhushana, the teacher stages the exchange:

- The Lord could object that Arjuna is a fighter, not a spectator, and that the time for looking has passed.
- Arjuna's reply is that he is not proposing any reconciliation. He knows they have come to fight. What he wants to see is _who_ is supporting the evil-minded son.
- The teacher notes how many words for seeing appear in this chapter.

#### Why the question of supporters matters [26:00]

- The purport's point is carried by the phrase about well-wishers. Arjuna wants to see who Duryodhana's friends are, and whose intelligence has become such that they would support irreligion.
- The teacher's supporting proverb is that birds of a feather flock together: people's associates resemble them.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna is not looking for a way out of the fight. He is asking a diagnostic question — whose judgment has become so disordered that they would stand on that side. [24:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.24 [26:00 – 34:00]

<!-- verse:1.24 -->

**Context.** Sanjaya reports that the Lord, addressed as Hrishikesha, drew up the chariot between the two armies as Arjuna had asked. [26:00 – 28:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Hṛṣīkeśaḥ_: Master of the senses. [28:00]
- _Guḍākeśena_: An epithet of Arjuna, which the teacher unpacks three ways. [28:00 – 32:00]
- _Senayor ubhayor madhye_: Between the two armies. [28:00]

#### The irony the teacher draws out [28:00]

If the Lord is the master of the senses, then we are supposed to carry out His order — yet here Arjuna is giving Him one. His application: we give the Lord a great many orders. He has a master plan, and we want Him to move according to ours, which is where the difficulty starts.

#### Three readings of Gudakesha [28:00 – 32:00]

- **Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura's reading.** Taking _guḍa_ as sweetness and analysing _ākeśa_ as comprising Vishnu, Brahma, and Shiva, the name describes one who gives those three the experience of sweetness. The teacher explains how: Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva are themselves those whose order cannot be denied, and yet they wait upon the Lord's order — so when they see that same Lord accepting the position of a charioteer out of affection for His devotee, their hearts are sweetened. Arjuna is therefore the cause of that sweetness.
- **Baladeva Vidyabhushana's reading.** One who has conquered sleep. Srila Prabhupada writes in the purport that a devotee can conquer both sleep and ignorance by remembering Krishna, and the teacher connects Arjuna's conquest of sleep to the depth of his absorption in remembrance of the Lord.
- **The literal reading.** _Keśa_ meaning hair and _guḍa_ meaning curled — one with curly hair. The teacher notes the acharyas focus on the first two.

#### How low the charioteer's position actually was [30:00 – 34:00]

The teacher develops this at length, because it is the section's point:

- A modern driver's role is already menial — he is told little, waits without knowing when he will be needed, and turns left or right as instructed.
- A charioteer's position was lower still. He sat below while the warrior stood above, and in the noise of battle spoken directions were useless.
- Directions were therefore given by striking the charioteer's shoulder with the foot — the right shoulder for right, the left for left.
- His conclusion: the Lord is prepared even to take His devotee's foot on His shoulder. That is the humility in the Lord of innumerable universes, and it is why Arjuna is addressed by a name meaning one who sweetens the hearts of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva.

#### Why the Lord submits at all [32:00 – 34:00]

- He is the master of the senses, but He comes under the control of His devotee — voluntarily.
- The teacher notes another acharya's point that the Lord renders this kind of service to a devotee so dedicated that he is not a servant of sleep or laziness but only of the Lord, standing beyond ignorance.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The name pairing is the whole teaching: the master of the senses takes an order, and the one who gave it is a devotee who serves nothing else. Divine greatness expresses itself as willingness to occupy the lowest position. [26:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.25 [34:00 – 42:00]

<!-- verse:1.25 -->

**Context.** The Lord halts the chariot in the presence of Bhishma, Drona, and all the chiefs of the world, and tells Arjuna to behold the Kurus assembled there. [34:00 – 36:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bhīṣma-droṇa-pramukhataḥ_: In the presence of Bhishma and Drona. [34:00 – 36:00]
- _Sarveṣāṁ ca mahī-kṣitām_: And of all the rulers of the earth. [36:00]
- _Pārtha_: The Lord's most affectionate address to Arjuna. [36:00]
- _Paśyaitān samavetān kurūn_: Behold these Kurus assembled — the teacher notes that _kuru_ covers both Pandavas and Kauravas, so the word itself recalls the family bond. [36:00]

#### Why that exact spot [34:00 – 36:00]

- With sixty-four crore people present, the chariot could have been halted anywhere — before Shakuni, before Duryodhana. The Lord placed it precisely before Bhishma and Drona.
- The teacher's application: in devotional life the Lord similarly brings us face to face with our own attachments and shortcomings, the things we feel we cannot deal with. Only then does one have to take shelter of Him and listen to His instruction in order to conquer them.

#### Why these two in particular [38:00 – 40:00]

These were the two people to whom Arjuna was most attached:

- **Bhishma** is senior even to Dhritarashtra — the great-grandsire, who had held Arjuna on his lap as a child. The teacher recalls that when Arjuna called him grandfather, Bhishma would reply that he was his grandfather's grandfather.
- **Drona** had taught these boys from childhood, teaching him even how to hold a bow, and Arjuna was his most favoured student.

Seeing both at once, with all the emotional associations flashing at once, Arjuna became emotional and began to wonder how he could possibly fight them.

#### What the word Kuru is doing [36:00 – 38:00]

- By using a word that includes both parties, the Lord is reminding Arjuna of the family bond.
- The teacher observes the nature of attachment: where there is affection, faults do not register — a mother sees no fault in her child, because the governing sense is that this one is mine.
- In material life this is negative, since it prevents us from correcting or helping. Spiritually it is positive, because the Lord relates to a surrendered devotee in just this way, overlooking small lapses and inefficiencies.

#### The Lord knows what is happening [40:00 – 42:00]

- Srila Prabhupada's purport opens by noting that as the Supersoul in everyone's heart the Lord knew exactly what was passing in Arjuna's mind.
- Baladeva Vidyabhushana's comment, as the teacher relays it, is that the Lord knew Arjuna was about to give up the desire to fight — and brought him before these two anyway.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The chariot stops where it will do the most damage to Arjuna's resolve, and the Lord's own word recalls the family tie. This is arranged, not accidental. [34:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.26 [42:00 – 46:00]

<!-- verse:1.26 -->

**Context.** Arjuna sees, standing on both sides, fathers, grandfathers, teachers, maternal uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, fathers-in-law, and well-wishers. [42:00 – 44:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pitṝn_ and _pitāmahān_: Those in the position of fathers, and grandfathers — he names Bhurishrava among the former and Bhishma and Somadatta among the latter. [44:00]
- _Ācāryān_: Teachers — Drona and Kripacharya, the family preceptor and the instructing teacher from whom he learned archery. [44:00]
- _Mātulān_: Maternal uncles — Shalya and Shakuni. [44:00]
- _Bhrātṝn_, _putrān_, _pautrān_: Brothers, sons, grandsons — the teacher notes that Duryodhana is a brother just as Bhima is, and names Abhimanyu among the sons and Lakshmana among Duryodhana's. [44:00]
- _Śvaśurān_ and _suhṛdaḥ_: Fathers-in-law and well-wishers. [44:00]

#### Five generations at once [42:00 – 44:00]

- The teacher counts the generations: Arjuna's own, his father's, his grandfather's, his sons', and his grandsons'.
- He asks the class to consider the emotional turmoil in that, noting that five generations rarely assemble at all today, and that people generally gather in such numbers only at weddings and at deaths.
- Here they have assembled on a battlefield, so many of them are about to die — which intensifies the emotion further.

#### Why separation hurts so much here [44:00 – 46:00]

- These are not merely personalities; with every one of them there have been exchanges of selfless dealings, which the teacher notes are rare today.
- His principle: where the exchanges have been selfless, the separation is extremely painful.
- He points to the First Canto chapters describing the Lord's departure from Hastinapura and His entry into Dvaraka as descriptions of exactly this kind of intense emotion. [Reference flagged; see Verification Flags.]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
What Arjuna sees is not an enemy army but a family reunion on a killing field. The depth of the coming grief is a measure of how selfless the past dealings were. [42:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.27 [46:00 – 50:00]

<!-- verse:1.27 -->

**Context.** Having surveyed all his relatives, Arjuna is overcome with compassion and speaks in despondency. [46:00]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tān samīkṣya_: Having thoroughly surveyed them. [46:00]
- _Kṛpayā parayāviṣṭaḥ_: Overwhelmed with compassion. [46:00]
- _Viṣīdan_: Lamenting. [46:00]

#### What actually weakened him [46:00 – 50:00]

- Arjuna sees people he has been with since birth, with whom he played, connected by birth and by education.
- His thought becomes that whoever dies, the loss is his own — his dynasty will be destroyed, his relatives killed.
- **The crucial distinction the teacher makes:** Arjuna was not weakened by the sight of Bhishma's and Drona's strength. He was weakened emotionally, by affection.
- He supports this with the earlier battle at Virata, which he describes as more dangerous, where Arjuna had no one with him — not even Krishna, only an inexperienced charioteer — and defeated everyone. [Details flagged; see Verification Flags.]

#### Why the Lord placed him under illusion [48:00 – 50:00]

The teacher quotes Baladeva Vidyabhushana's comment in substance: the merciful Lord of all living beings, desiring to deliver the universe through instruction to His dear associate, placed Arjuna under illusion.

- Arjuna was not in illusion; he was put there, because the Gita had to be given to the whole world.
- The teacher's illustration of why a pure devotee is used this way is a household one: a mother-in-law who wants to convey something to a newly arrived daughter-in-law, with whom no relationship is yet established, says it loudly to her own daughter instead — and the message is received without anything breaking. In the same way the Lord reveals things to the world through one with whom the relationship is already secure.

#### Strong emotion makes action difficult [50:00]

- The teacher's closing point: when emotions become strong, acting becomes hard.
- His comparison is a surgeon, who generally becomes unemotional through practice, but who would find it very difficult to operate on his own son. Here an operation has to be performed on a battlefield.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's collapse is affectionate, not fearful — and it is arranged. A pure devotee is placed in difficulty so that the answer given to him can be given to everyone. [46:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

### 1. If Krishna is present, how can there be illusion? [50:00 – 52:00]

- **Question:** The Caitanya-caritamrta compares Krishna to the sun and maya to darkness, so where Krishna is there can be no maya. How does that square with Arjuna's bewilderment in Krishna's presence?
- **Answer:** The teacher points back to Baladeva Vidyabhushana: Arjuna was not in illusion; the Lord placed him there in order to deliver the universe, using the devotee with whom the relationship was already established — the daughter through whom the daughter-in-law is addressed.
- The quoted principle nonetheless stands. The more Krishna conscious a person becomes, the more clearly this is seen and the more naturally attachment declines. As attachment to Krishna and surrender to Him increase, worldly relationships are carried out as duty without becoming binding. [Quotation flagged; see Verification Flags.]

### 2. Why are there so many scriptures when one is not yet finished? [52:00 – 56:00]

- **Question:** First one learns there is a Bhagavatam and a Gita; then that there is a Caitanya-bhagavata; now that there is an Uddhava-gita. Why are more needed when the Bhagavatam already exists in so many parts?
- **Answer:** The teacher grants the feeling — in a technical course one knows how many subjects there are, whereas here books keep appearing.
- The reason is that the Lord's pastimes are unlimited. He returns to Sridhara Swami's verse, saying again that Ananta Shesha has been describing the Lord's pastimes for unlimited time without repeating anything and without exhausting even one of His qualities — and promises once more to bring the verse to class. [Verse flagged; see Verification Flags.]
- **But no one is required to complete everything.** Devotion is a path of relishing. His analogy: countless dishes and sweets can be prepared, but one's daily rice, dal, vegetable, and chapati give taste, satisfaction, and strength.
- The acharyas therefore identified a core so that people would not become bewildered — which is why the course studies the Gita as the essence of the Vedas, and a single short Upanishad out of a hundred and eight. What matters is reading, understanding, and implementing as much of that core as one can.
- Beyond that the treasure is unlimited and anyone who wishes may go further. And a scripture does not become stale on completion: these texts remain ever-fresh because they are living, and the Lord reciprocates each time.

### 3. How does a householder reduce attachment to family? [56:00]

- **Question:** Arjuna's illusion was removed when the universal form was shown. Householders have similar attachment to their own people and suffer greatly when something is taken away. Is the remedy to become Krishna conscious and offer everything to the Lord, so that attachment gradually reduces?
- **Answer:** The teacher confirms that this is exactly how it works.

### 4. Is _acyuta_ here the same as the term used in the Fifteenth Chapter? [56:00 – 60:00]

- **Question:** A participant notes the Fifteenth Chapter's discussion of two classes of living entities and asks how the term relates.
- **Answer:** The teacher separates them. The Fifteenth Chapter's distinction is between the fallible and the infallible — those who can fall and those who do not, the eternally liberated associates of the Lord — with the Lord being superior to both. We fall in the conditioned category.
- Here _acyuta_ is a name of the Lord, meaning He never falls from His position. He notes that the infallible living entities also do not fall, yet a difference remains, and the Fifteenth Chapter establishes that the Lord is above both.
- In this verse the specific sense is the one already explained: He never fails in showing affection to His devotees. [Verse references as spoken; see Verification Flags.]
