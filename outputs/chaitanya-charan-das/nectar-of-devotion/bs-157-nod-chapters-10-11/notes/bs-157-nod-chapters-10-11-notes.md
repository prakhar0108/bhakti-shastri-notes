## Chapter-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Three kinds of hearing [00:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:nod/10 -->

**Context.** We normally think of hearing (_śravaṇa_) as hearing _kṛṣṇa-kathā_, but it can be of different kinds `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nāma-śravaṇa_ — hearing the holy name `06:00`.
- _Carita-ādi-śravaṇa_ — hearing the character and pastimes of the Lord `06:00`.
- _Guṇa-śravaṇa_ — hearing about the Lord's transcendental qualities `06:00`.

#### Hearing and chanting overlap [00:00 – 02:00]

- When there is _kīrtana_ of Kṛṣṇa's names and we hear it, that is also _śravaṇa_.
- Generally, when something heard is repeated, it can be called _kīrtana_; the same applies when we speak about and hear about Kṛṣṇa.
- The book gives three references, each about one specific kind of hearing `02:00`.

#### The snakebite example [02:00 – 06:00]

- A person made unconscious by a snakebite can be brought back to consciousness by the sound of a mantra.
- Similarly, we are spiritually unconscious, bitten by the snake of illusion.
- Practically every sense object is like a snake's fang: when we meditate on sense objects, an enjoying mentality is injected into our consciousness and poisons our spiritual awareness `04:00`.
- The more one is injected with this mentality, the more unconscious one becomes of spiritual reality — and this has happened over many previous lives, not just this one `04:00`.
- The effect of sound on the body is increasingly researched by scientists; people listen to "relaxing music," and a specific mantra, chanted in a specific way, can counteract poison `04:00`.
- So too the chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra brings us back from spiritual unconsciousness — an example of hearing the name `06:00`.

#### Pastimes and qualities [06:00 – 08:00]

- The next reference concerns the Lord's transcendental activities: the nectar flowing like a river from the moon-like faces of great devotees frees the hearer from material miseries.
- A proper philosophical background for hearing is important.
- The last reference concerns hearing the Lord's transcendental qualities.
- The categories overlap and are not sharply differentiated: there is both continuity and distinction among hearing the name, pastimes and qualities `06:00`.

#### Watering the creeper of devotion [06:00 – 08:00]

- In the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_, Lord Caitanya instructs that hearing and chanting are like watering the seed of the creeper of devotion: once it starts growing it still needs water `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sense objects poison us with an enjoying mentality, making us spiritually unconscious; hearing Kṛṣṇa's name, pastimes and qualities is the mantra that revives us and the water that keeps the creeper of devotion growing. [02:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Expecting the Lord's mercy [08:00 – 16:00]

**Context.** The next item is expecting the mercy of the Lord. The teacher calls it interesting because _bhakti_ is a path of grace `08:00`.

#### Endeavour and grace [08:00]

- We cannot make advancement just by our individual endeavour.
- Endeavour is very, very important, but along with it we need Kṛṣṇa's mercy.
- Our endeavours basically show Kṛṣṇa that we want His mercy.

#### Brahmā's prayer and the billionaire's child [08:00 – 10:00]

- The teacher cites a famous verse from Brahmā's prayers in the Tenth Canto: the devotee looks for the Lord's mercy, accepts his sufferings as reactions to his own past deeds, and offers obeisances with body, speech and heart `08:00 – 10:00`.
- One who lives like this becomes entitled to liberation as an inheritance `10:00`.
- The child of a billionaire will inherit the property; what does the child have to do? Simply stay alive.
- Similarly we simply have to stay alive in _bhakti_. Life's tribulations may tempt us foolishly to give up _bhakti_, but we just have to hold our position `10:00`.

#### Desire, not demand [12:00]

- The teacher gives an example of a bird that keeps crying out forcefully and repeatedly, and is given more and more food; but the giver is completely free to give — the bird cannot make demands.
- Kṛṣṇa has already given us mercy:
  - the fortune to practise devotional service is itself great mercy;
  - the association of devotees is great mercy;
  - getting taste in devotion is greater mercy still.
- Devotees are grateful for whatever mercy is given and long for more.
- Desiring mercy is natural because we want devotion; demanding is not in the mood of devotion — Kṛṣṇa is the master and gives as He wants.

#### Calling out with our whole life [14:00]

- We express our longing by calling out — attentive, serious chanting is one way.
- But our whole life can be a calling out: by doing our services responsibly and committedly we show Kṛṣṇa, "I am serious about my relationship with You."

#### An intangible but essential item [14:00]

- Many items discussed so far have tangible activities associated with them, such as deity worship or hearing in a group.
- This item is intangible — not tied to any specific form of service — yet essential; because it is distinct, it can often be missing: I can sit in class or take _darśana_ and just do it dully.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti is a path of grace: our endeavour shows Kṛṣṇa we want His mercy, gratitude recognises the mercy already given, and longing — expressed through attentive chanting and committed service — asks for more without ever demanding it. [08:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Remembrance and meditation on the Lord's form [16:00 – 22:00]

**Context.** The next item is remembrance; Śrīla Prabhupāda quotes verses on how remembering the Lord brings all auspiciousness `16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Rūpa-dhyāna_ — meditation on the Lord's form `18:00`.
- _Līlā-dhyāna_ / _krīḍā-dhyāna_ — meditation on the Lord's pastimes `16:00`, `22:00`.
- _Sevā-dhyāna_ — absorption in service `24:00`.

#### Remembrance cleanses [16:00]

- The famous verse recited at initiation speaks of remembering Lord Viṣṇu; remembrance is extremely important in cleansing — nothing else can cleanse like it.
- Meditation is a much deeper remembrance; Prabhupāda describes three or four kinds of meditation and discusses them one by one.

#### Concentration versus meditation [16:00]

- Concentration: there is a possibility of distraction and an attempt to focus the attention.
- Meditation: one goes so deep inside that one becomes unaware of anything else — as people become completely absorbed while watching a film.

#### Meditating on the form [18:00]

- When we go to the deity, we look at the full form.
- The Third Canto describes how the yogi meditates on each limb of the Supreme, starting from the lotus feet and moving up — knees, thighs, belly, the chest that is the abode of Lakṣmī-devī, nose, eyes, forehead — to the complete form.

#### Worldly beauty versus Kṛṣṇa's beauty [18:00 – 20:00]

- In the material world, attraction between man and woman is spontaneous, and one can meditate on it constantly.
- Worldly beauty agitates; Kṛṣṇa's beauty pacifies.
- The film industry focuses on depicting worldly beauty — it is attractive, but the end result is lust.
- The more we behold Kṛṣṇa's beauty, the more lust is pacified. The spiritual senses do become eager to behold the Lord's form, but that eagerness is spiritual — and spiritual hankering is wonderful because it purifies material hankerings `20:00`.

#### Nārada Muni's single darśana [20:00]

- In the First Canto, Nārada Muni in his previous life lost his mother, began meditating and had a brief experience of the Lord; his senses were enlivened by the beautiful _darśana_.
- When the Lord disappeared, he was extremely agitated, and a voice said: because you are impure in heart and incomplete in service, you will not be able to see Me again; but this one _darśana_ will create a constant hankering for Me, and by that hankering your worldly hankerings will go away.
- The Lord wants to reveal Himself, but with an impure heart we cannot see Him — just as I cannot see a person while my eyes are closed. Impurity is like closed eyes; as we are purified, the eyes open.

#### Crowding out, not emptying [20:00 – 22:00]

- We have the power to desire; it is to be directed towards Kṛṣṇa.
- The process of _bhakti-yoga_ is not to empty the heart of desire but to crowd material desires out, by filling the heart with spiritual desire until material desire has no place left.
- So the focus should not be on saying "no, no," but on saying "yes" to Kṛṣṇa and becoming eager for the different processes of devotional service; material desires then automatically go `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Meditating on Kṛṣṇa's form awakens a spiritual hankering that pacifies material hankerings; bhakti does not empty the heart of desire but fills it with desire for Kṛṣṇa, so material desires are crowded out. [16:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Meditation on pastimes and on service [22:00 – 30:00]

**Context.** After meditation on the form come meditation on the pastimes and absorption in service `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### Caitanya Mahāprabhu at the Garuḍa pillar [22:00]

- Caitanya Mahāprabhu would stand far away in the Jagannātha temple, near the Garuḍa pillar — a place pilgrims can still see — from where it is difficult to see the Lord clearly; yet He was completely absorbed in beholding the Lord's form. He is an example of _rūpa-dhyāna_.

#### Hearing pastimes again and again [22:00 – 24:00]

- _Līlā-dhyāna_ means becoming absorbed while hearing the Lord's pastimes.
- A devotee wants to hear the pastimes of Dhruva Mahārāja and Prahlāda Mahārāja a hundred times — again and again.

#### Absorption in service [24:00 – 26:00]

- We should practise meditation on the name and form, but we cannot do it very long — the mind wanders.
- _Sevā-dhyāna_ means absorption in service:
  - in deity worship — how can I cook for the Lord, decorate Him, celebrate His festival?
  - in any service — how can I speak, how can I preach, how can I distribute more books?
- Whatever the service, it can become a source of absorption, and through that absorption we stay connected with Kṛṣṇa — constantly thinking how to share Kṛṣṇa consciousness with people.
- The teacher gives an example from San Francisco: where others would see only an ordinary flatbed vehicle, a mind absorbed in service saw a way to place Jagannātha, Baladeva and Subhadrā on it and hold a festival `24:00`.
- Even meeting people can be done in the mood of serving Kṛṣṇa — "How can I help this person come closer to Kṛṣṇa?" — so the meeting itself becomes devotional service `26:00`.
- That is why _bhakti_ is translated not just as devotion but as devotional service `26:00`.

#### The brāhmaṇa's mental worship [26:00 – 28:00]

- The references in the book are not sharply divided: one refers to _līlā-dhyāna_; one to remembering the Lord's qualities — and since beauty is a quality, it can also apply to _rūpa-dhyāna_; and the last illustrates _sevā-dhyāna_ `26:00`.
- That last is the story of a _brāhmaṇa_ who performed worship in his mind: he prepared sweet rice for the Lord mentally, and to check whether it had cooled he put his finger into it — all in his mind — while Lord Viṣṇu, with Lakṣmī, was watching and smiling `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Mental service and its limits [28:00]

- Mental service is something we can always do when we cannot serve physically.
- Vasudeva wanted to give much charity but could not because he was imprisoned, so he gave it in his mind; later, when he was in a position of power and glory, he actually gave the charity.
- Principle: when able, express the desire in action; when unable, desire in the heart, "Let me do this service" — and in this way stay Kṛṣṇa conscious all the time.
- Mental worship is acceptable only when physical service is not possible; when the opportunity comes, translate it into action. Mental worship is the heart that wants to serve the Lord.
- The teacher cites a special concession described in the First Canto: one does not get a reaction for merely thinking bad things, but does get the benefit of good things thought in the mind — and we can tap into that `28:00 – 30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Since the mind cannot stay long on the name and form, absorption in service — planning, cooking, preaching, even meeting people for Kṛṣṇa — keeps us constantly connected; service done in the mind is valid when action is impossible, but should become action when possible. [22:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Offering work to Kṛṣṇa [30:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:nod/11 -->

**Context.** The next chapter, "Aspects of Transcendental Service," covers further items, including the last three of the nine forms of devotional service `30:00`.

#### Work within varṇāśrama offered to the Lord [32:00]

- Depending on the person's consciousness, the offering mostly is _karma_: a person does activities within _varṇāśrama_ but offers the work to the Supreme Lord.
- This is technically considered _bhakti_, but in the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_, when Rāmānanda Rāya proposed satisfying Lord Viṣṇu through _varṇāśrama_, Caitanya Mahāprabhu replied that it is external.
- So offering one's _varṇāśrama_ work to Kṛṣṇa is not exactly pure devotional service — it is one level. People who offer their work because they have some feeling for Kṛṣṇa are part of _bhakti_, but not pure, and their devotional disposition will eventually develop `32:00`.

#### Levels in Gītā Chapter 12 [34:00]

- The twelfth chapter of the _Bhagavad-gītā_ (from 12.8 onwards) describes various levels at which _bhakti_ can be — one level is working for Kṛṣṇa, another is offering the fruits of one's work.
- Duties offered to Kṛṣṇa are not necessarily _bhakti_ in themselves, but they are favourable (_anukūla_) to _bhakti_ and help us advance; we have to do our duties, but they alone do not constitute _bhakti_.

#### "Work is worship"? [34:00 – 36:00]

- People say work is worship. If work is worship, then the donkey is the greatest worshipper — it works so hard.
- "Work is worship" is not taught anywhere in the Vedas.
- To make work worship, we need a relationship with Kṛṣṇa developed separately through _sādhana-bhakti_ — a morning programme, hearing and study.
- Then when we go to work with that foundation of spiritual consciousness, the work also becomes worship `36:00`.

#### Offering the items of devotion [36:00]

- The other part of this item is offering the items of devotional service themselves to Kṛṣṇa — understanding "I am doing this for Kṛṣṇa."
- Prahlāda Mahārāja's verse on the nine processes says they have to be offered to Viṣṇu; coming to the temple to hear about Kṛṣṇa is such an offering of _bhakti_.
- Worldly work for Kṛṣṇa is a step towards _bhakti_ but not exactly _bhakti_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Offering one's duties to Kṛṣṇa is favourable to bhakti but not yet bhakti; work becomes worship only when it rests on a separately cultivated relationship with Kṛṣṇa through daily sādhana. [32:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Servitorship and friendship [36:00 – 46:00]

**Context.** Higher than offering activities is servitorship — offering oneself to Kṛṣṇa; then comes friendship, in two aspects `36:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kiṅkara_ — a servant: one who asks the master, "What shall I do? What do You want me to do?" `36:00`.
- _Viśvāsa_ — confident faith in the Lord as one's well-wisher and protector `38:00`.
- _Maitrī_ — very intimate friendship with the Lord `38:00`, `42:00`.
- _Śraddhā_ — the beginning stage of faith, the first of the stages of _bhakti_ `40:00`.

#### Servitorship [36:00 – 38:00]

- It is not that I do an activity for Kṛṣṇa; I offer myself to Kṛṣṇa.
- It involves identification: "I am a servant" — which we express in how we address one another.

#### Confident faith: Draupadī [38:00 – 40:00]

- In _viśvāsa_ one has great confidence that the Lord is one's well-wisher, benefactor and protector — somewhat like an elder's protective mood.
- Draupadī called out to the Lord with _viśvāsa_: "the Lord will come and protect me."
- Her relationship with Kṛṣṇa is not exactly conjugal like the _gopīs_' of Vṛndāvana, since she had her husbands; she is actually a friend. The point is her intensity of calling out and her confidence in His protection `40:00`.

#### From śraddhā to prema [40:00]

- _Viśvāsa_ is a deeper result of faith; _śraddhā_ is the beginning, tender stage, and faith grows stronger as we progress through the stages.
- Example: I trust a good doctor, but that does not mean I love the doctor; love is much deeper — though trust is implied in love, for without it how can there be love?
- The essential process of _bhakti_ is the deepening of faith from _śraddhā_ to _prema_.
- Not only is the Lord our well-wisher; when the devotee starts sharing Kṛṣṇa consciousness, the devotee becomes the Lord's well-wisher too.

#### Intimate friendship: the cowherd boys [42:00 – 44:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that sometimes a devotee lies down in the temple of the Lord out of confidential friendship. Normally we lie down in the temple only to offer obeisances, not to sleep there at night; but as the relationship becomes more intimate, the devotee does what others normally would not.
- Normally it is a great fortune to get the dust of the Lord's feet on our head. But in the _Gopāla-campū_, Jīva Gosvāmī describes how, after Kṛṣṇa defeated Kaṁsa's huge wrestlers, He wrestled with His cowherd friends — and was defeated by them `44:00`.
- The loser had to carry the winner, so a cowherd boy's feet touched the Lord's body. Normally we would not let our feet touch any sacred object — not even a book, what to speak of the deity or the Lord Himself — but in an intimate relationship this is part of the _līlā_ `44:00`.

#### What we can practise now [44:00]

- This intimacy is very advanced; we cannot jump to it.
- But at the level of faith we can cultivate the confidence: "My friend will come to help me."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Servitorship offers the self, not just activities; friendship grows from confident faith that the Lord will protect us to intimate closeness — and bhakti is the gradual deepening of faith from śraddhā to prema. [36:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Offering oneself and one's favourite things [46:00 – 50:00]

**Context.** Next comes offering oneself (_ātma-nivedana_); what is offered depends on what "self" refers to `46:00`.

#### Three levels of self-offering [46:00]

- Surrender of the body, surrender of the mind, and surrender of the soul — all three are glorious.
- Bali Mahārāja: Lord Viṣṇu asked for three steps, then expanded and covered the whole universe with two, leaving nothing. Bali Mahārāja offered his body — he offered his head for the third step.
- Bali's offering of the body carried the mood of offering his entire existence; but essentially what he offered there was the body.
- Further examples are given of offering the mind, and of offering the self such that one is "no longer concerned with my maintenance, because I have given my body to You" `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Not hair-splitting [48:00]

- It is not that "I will offer my body but not my heart."
- These distinctions help us understand self-offering analytically; generally in _ātma-nivedana_ everything is offered, but there can be different levels of offering.

#### Offering one's favourite article [48:00 – 50:00]

- An important principle of surrender is offering one's favourite article — offering the best thing in one's possession.
- For example, food items one likes can be cooked and offered to Kṛṣṇa; one can offer decorations.

#### All endeavours for Kṛṣṇa [50:00]

- Performing all one's endeavours for the service of the Lord, and offering Him the fruits of one's work.
- The teacher also raises how _ātma-nivedana_ differs from _śaraṇāgati_ discussed elsewhere `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Self-offering can be of body, mind or soul, and generally includes everything; a practical expression of surrender is giving Kṛṣṇa our favourite things and dedicating all our endeavours to Him. [46:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Serving Tulasī-devī [50:00 – 54:33]

**Context.** A reference from the _Skanda Purāṇa_ describes the glory of serving Tulasī-devī `50:00`.

#### Simple acts of service [52:00]

- Simply by touching Tulasī, praying before her, hearing about her or showing her to others, one is always auspicious.

#### Who Tulasī is [52:00]

- There is a festival of Tulasī–Śālagrāma marriage: Śālagrāma is a form of Viṣṇu, and Tulasī a form of the Lord's consort.
- Ultimately the _gopīs_ in the spiritual world are expansions of Rādhārāṇī, and Tulasī is also one of her expansions.
- So Tulasī–Śālagrāma represents the union of the Lord and His greatest devotee.
- Tulasī is the giver of _bhakti_; by worshipping Kṛṣṇa and Tulasī-devī we make much spiritual advancement.

#### Medicine or mercy? [52:00 – 54:00]

- Some people value Tulasī for her medicinal value; devotees take Tulasī not for that but because she is dear to Kṛṣṇa — it is Kṛṣṇa's mercy manifesting.
- Should we then not chew Tulasī, since she is an expansion of Rādhārāṇī? The teacher answers that it is not necessarily like that: Kṛṣṇa has different forms and we reciprocate with them in different ways — the _capātī_ offered as _prasāda_ is also Kṛṣṇa, yet we eat it.
- So we can take Tulasī and respectfully honour her when taking _prasāda_ `54:00`.
- Through such services one can make swift spiritual advancement; the discussion continues next class `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Tulasī is dear to Kṛṣṇa and the giver of bhakti; devotees serve and honour her not for her medicinal value but as Kṛṣṇa's mercy, reciprocating with each of His manifestations in its own appropriate way. [50:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
