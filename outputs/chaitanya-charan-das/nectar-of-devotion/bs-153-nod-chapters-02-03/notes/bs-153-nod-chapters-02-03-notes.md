### Three stages of devotion and the meaning of "taste" [00:00 – 02:00]

<!-- vedabase:nod/2 -->

**Context.** Chapter 2, "The First Stages of Devotion," opens by describing three stages of devotional service and then begins with the first of them, devotional service in practice `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sādhana-bhakti_ — devotional service in practice; it has two qualities `00:00`.
- _Bhāva-bhakti_ — devotional service in ecstasy; it has four qualities `00:00`.
- _Prema-bhakti_ — devotional service in pure love of Godhead; it has six qualities `00:00`.
- _Taste_ — here, attraction to Kṛṣṇa; a person eligible for devotional service is classified by this taste `00:00`.

#### Attraction to Kṛṣṇa as the measure of everything [00:00 – 02:00]

- The greater the attraction to Kṛṣṇa, the more advanced the devotee.
- The teacher cites the _Bhāgavatam_'s point that if one's performance of _dharma_ does not increase attraction to the Supreme Lord, it is a waste of time.
- All activities — spiritual, and even material _dharmic_ ones — are ultimately meant to increase attraction to Kṛṣṇa.

#### How attraction first appears [02:00]

- Even the first stage of _bhakti_ is a kind of attraction, which Prabhupāda renders as faith and also as "favourable curiosity" — "What is this? I want to know about it."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Devotion is graded by one thing — attraction to Kṛṣṇa — and every practice, even worldly duty, is meant to increase it; the first sign is simply a favourable curiosity to know more. [00:00 – 02:00]
</Callout>

### How bhakti begins: the mercy of devotees, not piety [02:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda writes that no one can take to devotional service without some previous connection with it. The teacher draws on Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's _Mādhurya-kādambinī_ — not a direct commentary but a further analysis of the same subject as the _Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu_ — to ask how _bhakti_ starts at all `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Puṇya_ — pious credit; material, not spiritual `06:00`.
- _Pāpa_ — sin; with _puṇya_, "two sides of the same coin" — one positive, one negative, both material `06:00`.
- _Sukṛti_ — pious activity that predisposes one toward devotion `08:00`, `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### The problem of infinite regress [04:00]

- Attraction leads to practice, and practice increases attraction — a circle that, once started, is self-sustaining.
- But if devotion needs previous devotion, when does devotion originally begin?
- Answer: not by any past activity, but by the mercy of devotees. Devotees intervene in our life and give the opportunity, impetus and inspiration to practise — whether in this life or a previous one, through a pure devotee or a pure devotee's representative.

#### Mercy from outside and inside [06:00]

- Externally, the spiritual master comes as the representative of Kṛṣṇa's mercy; internally, Kṛṣṇa prompts the devotee — "go here, listen, understand."
- So the Lord works for the living being's welfare both ways; He is ultimately the bestower of mercy.

#### Why mercy cannot be based on puṇya [06:00 – 08:00]

- If the Lord gave _bhakti_ because a person had _puṇya_, then _puṇya_ would become the cause of _bhakti_.
- But _puṇya_ is material, and nothing material can lead to spiritual devotion.

#### What BG 7.28 and 7.16 actually say [08:00 – 10:00]

- In 7.28 Kṛṣṇa says that when sinful reactions end over many lifetimes, one becomes fixed in devotion. The teacher explains this is about the _determined, steady_ practice of _bhakti_, not its beginning — sinful desires keep dragging one away, so steadiness needs freedom from sin.
- In 7.16 Kṛṣṇa names four kinds of people — the distressed, the seeker of wealth, the inquisitive and the seeker of knowledge — with no special qualification other than being pious; they _begin_ worshipping Kṛṣṇa, while real surrender comes much later (7.19).
- These impetuses are below the level Rūpa Gosvāmī discusses: he is describing pure devotional service and the means to approach it.

#### Why the Lord is not partial [10:00]

- If the Lord simply decided independently, "I'll give mercy to this person," He would be arbitrary and partial; but the Lord is supremely fair.
- After elaborate analysis, the conclusion: the Lord's mercy follows the mercy of His devotees — to whomever the devotees give mercy, the Lord gives mercy.
- _Bhakti_ cannot emerge by itself; by contact with a _bhakta_ in whose heart _bhakti_ is present, the _bhakti_ in our heart awakens.

#### Attraction to the devotee leads to attraction to Kṛṣṇa [10:00 – 12:00]

- Often it is attraction to the devotee that leads to attraction to Kṛṣṇa — Prabhupāda was a fascinating person for his disciples, and through him they became attracted to Kṛṣṇa.
- In India many know Kṛṣṇa as a cultural or religious figure, even as God, with some sentiment of devotion, but are not serious seekers.
- Serious spiritual seeking requires association with a seer, or at least a serious seeker — without devotees one cannot even start serious spiritual life, leave alone become advanced.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti is not purchased by piety: piety may make one steady or predisposed, but bhakti itself is passed on by the mercy of devotees, and the Lord's mercy follows theirs — so association with serious devotees is where spiritual life truly begins. [04:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Sādhana-bhakti: reviving a dormant potential [12:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** Rūpa Gosvāmī next gives a "slightly technical" definition of _sādhana-bhakti_, which the teacher unpacks into three points `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### The three points of the definition [14:00 – 18:00]

1. **It is eternally present in the heart as a potential.** Practising devotional service is not acquiring something foreign or imposing something artificial; it is the soul's original spiritual energy and natural state.
2. **It is performed with the senses** — not only internally `16:00`.
3. **Its goal is that the dormant _bhāva_ rises** — like the sun rising: something that already exists comes into our perception at a particular time `18:00`.

#### Potential versus not potential [14:00]

- A mother helps her baby learn to walk, but the baby already has the capacity to walk.
- A human baby cannot learn to fly, while a bird's baby can — what is not potential cannot be made kinetic; what is not dormant cannot be made active.
- So _bhakti_ is not foreign to us.

#### The "brainwashing" objection [16:00]

- Prabhupāda often pointed out that people who had never heard of Kṛṣṇa for twenty-odd years take up chanting sixteen rounds within months of hearing about Him. External brainwashing could not last so long or transform so deeply.
- Prabhupāda's reply to the charge: brainwashing means our brain is filled with dirt, and we are washing the dirt out — "you should thank us," not take us to court.
- Once the dirt around the soul is washed away, its natural attraction for Kṛṣṇa awakens.

#### Why practice uses the senses [16:00 – 18:00]

- At an advanced stage a devotee may simply absorb the mind in remembering Kṛṣṇa — that happens when natural love is present.
- At our stage, practice is executed with the senses; that is why we chant aloud, not just in the mind.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sādhana-bhakti does not create love for Kṛṣṇa — it uncovers it. Like a baby who already can walk, the soul already can love; practice with the senses simply washes away the dirt so that dormant bhāva can rise like the sun. [14:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### How sensory practice spiritualises consciousness [18:00 – 28:00]

**Context.** A question follows: the senses are material and their actions often mechanical — how can mechanical repetition of a sound awaken the soul? `18:00`

#### From imperfection, perfection [18:00 – 20:00]

- The lotus rises from mud; similarly, though we are imperfect at first, perfection can arise from imperfection.
- The soul, mind and body are connected; right now the soul's consciousness is channelled almost entirely at the mental and physical levels. Kṛṣṇa is next to us in the heart, but we cannot perceive Him or hear His voice.

#### Spiritual sense objects [20:00 – 22:00]

- "Sense objects" usually means things like the opposite sex or delicious food, but literally it means anything pursued by the senses.
- In that sense the Deity is also perceived by our senses — a _spiritual_ sense object, which directs consciousness toward Kṛṣṇa rather than the material realm.
- Even if we cannot yet perceive the Deity's non-difference from Kṛṣṇa, we can at least see that the Deity reminds us of Kṛṣṇa.
- Examples of spiritual sense objects: the holy name we utter aloud, the Deities we see, the scriptures we read, the holy places we visit.

#### Revival, not development [22:00 – 24:00]

- The engagement of the senses is not what awakens _bhāva_ mechanically; it provides a _pathway_ for redirecting consciousness toward the spiritual.
- The soul is covered by material contamination; perceiving material reality keeps adding layers, while contact with spiritual objects gradually removes them, until the soul's natural attraction to Kṛṣṇa appears.
- Hence _bhakti_ is technically a _revival_, not a development; "development" is only a loose, non-technical usage.

#### Practice without longing [24:00 – 26:00]

- Engaging the senses without the desire for revival — uttering words mechanically, without longing for Kṛṣṇa — does not move consciousness much, and sometimes not at all.
- The teacher's example: some professional singers sing devotional songs vigorously, sweetly and repeatedly, but for money, applause, DVDs and a brand name; because they long for fame and money rather than Kṛṣṇa, their singing does not revive their attraction to Him.
- If fame is the primary desire, _bhakti_ awakens very gradually, if at all — it may take many lifetimes.

#### Chanting seriously [26:00]

- Chanting sixteen rounds mechanically does not lead to much advancement.
- "Serious" chanting does not mean a grave face; it means fixing the mind on the holy name and developing longing: "Kṛṣṇa, I have left You for so long; I want to return to You; please accept my chanting as an offering for Your pleasure."
- Even without strong attraction, we should try to have some longing; associating with senior devotees whose eagerness for Kṛṣṇa "rubs off" sparks eagerness in us.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Holy name, Deity, scripture and holy place are spiritual sense objects that open a pathway for consciousness — but the pathway is travelled only with longing for Kṛṣṇa; chanting for fame or by rote barely moves the heart. [20:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Sādhana as treatment for a double amnesia [26:00 – 32:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda compares the prescribed methods to a child practising walking, and to a psychiatrist's directions curing mental disease: _sādhana-bhakti_ cures the conditioned soul of madness under _māyā_'s spell `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Two levels of illusion [28:00 – 30:00]

- At one level our madness is amnesia: like a film character who hits his head and cannot remember his own name — a horrible, agitating experience.
- Even worse is forgetting that we have forgotten.
- The cinema example: first the lights in the hall are switched off (perception of who and where we are is cut off), then the lights on the screen come on and the illusion begins.
- So our illusion is twofold: we have forgotten our true identity, and we have assumed a false one — "I am male, female, Indian, American."
- Like a madman who thinks he is normal and that those calling him mad are mad — a "double installment" of the disease.

#### Stimulus of the past as treatment [30:00 – 32:00]

- A person with amnesia is often treated by stimuli from their past life; likewise, the process of _bhakti_ centres on stimuli from our eternal life with Kṛṣṇa.
- Prabhupāda writes that it is the duty of the _ācārya_ to find ways and means for the disciple to fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa — and that is the beginning of _sādhana-bhakti_.
- The whole purpose of everything in _bhakti_ is to help us remember Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We have not only forgotten Kṛṣṇa but forgotten that we forgot, and taken on a false identity; sādhana-bhakti is the therapy of repeated reminders of our eternal life with Him, arranged by the ācārya to fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa. [28:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Vaidhī and rāgānugā; the rule of always remembering Kṛṣṇa [32:00 – 40:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda next gives the two divisions of _sādhana-bhakti_ `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vaidhī_ — practice impelled by scriptural rules and regulations: "I chant because I have been told to," following the scripture and the spiritual master `32:00`.
- _Rāgānugā_ — practice impelled by spontaneous attraction `32:00`.

#### Vaidhī-bhakti is not devotion-less [34:00]

- These forms are _potential_ pure devotional service — they lead to it, but are not themselves pure devotional service.
- Still, _vaidhī-bhakti_ should not be minimised: why would one submit to the instruction of Kṛṣṇa through scripture and the spiritual master unless there were some devotion? That submission itself is a sign of devotion.
- It is simply not yet sustained or spontaneous — we do not feel like chanting every time.

#### The example of Parīkṣit Mahārāja [34:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda's example: if you want to be fearless in meeting death next week, you should hear and chant.
- The teacher clarifies that this does not imply Parīkṣit Mahārāja was at the _vaidhī_ level — he was very advanced. The point is the _injunction_: hear and chant _for the purpose of_ fearlessness from death, which comes by absorption in Kṛṣṇa. That instruction is an example of _vaidhī-bhakti_.

#### Bhakti is never lost; always remember Kṛṣṇa [36:00]

- Everything else will be lost, but devotional credits stay with us forever.
- Prabhupāda gives the _Padma Purāṇa_ statement: somehow or other always think of Kṛṣṇa and never forget Him in any circumstance — a regulative principle applicable to all _varṇas_, _āśramas_ and occupations.

#### Varṇāśrama and the purpose of duties [36:00 – 38:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes the Eleventh Canto: the four _varṇas_ and _āśramas_ come from the Lord's universal form, and one who does not worship the Lord, the source of all, falls from one's position.
- Point: whatever our _varṇa_ or _āśrama_, without fixing the mind on Kṛṣṇa we fall away.
- Prabhupāda then describes _varṇāśrama_ — the different _varṇas_ cooperating in their duties — and quotes that if their activities are offered in worship of Kṛṣṇa, they become happy in this world and the next. As the Gītā teaches, prescribed duties are meant to worship Kṛṣṇa.

#### Compass and road [38:00 – 40:00]

- The chapter's two parts: how _bhakti_ begins (the mercy of devotees), and the basic regulation of _bhakti_ (always remember Kṛṣṇa).
- The principle is like a compass and the rule like a road: the road is generally the best way to go in a direction, but the road itself does not carry us — we must move along it.
- So we neither neglect the rules nor emphasise them so much that we forget their governing principle: always remember Kṛṣṇa, never forget Him — by steady remembrance our love awakens.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even rule-based practice is already devotion, because submission is devotional; yet every rule is a road pointed by one compass — always remember Kṛṣṇa and never forget Him. Keep the rules, but never lose sight of what they are for. [32:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Chapter 3: eligibility — kaniṣṭha, madhyama, uttama [40:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:nod/3 -->

**Context.** Chapter 3 concerns _adhikāra_, the eligibility of the candidate for devotional service. Since the three levels were discussed earlier, the teacher reviews them briefly `40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Adhikāra_ — eligibility; Prabhupāda's English "eligibility" renders this term `40:00`, `54:00`.

#### Faith and knowledge at three levels [40:00 – 42:00]

- _Kaniṣṭha_ — weak faith and weak knowledge; some knowledge, but cannot offer arguments.
- _Madhyama_ — strong faith, good knowledge, but not enough to defeat opposing opinions.
- _Uttama_ — strong faith and strong knowledge; can defeat opposing opinions.
- All three are moving progressively forward on the spiritual path.

#### Do not trivialise the kaniṣṭha [42:00]

- The _kaniṣṭha_ has weak faith but still some faith — and that faith has the right objective: developing devotion for Kṛṣṇa.
- If that objective is steadily maintained — "everything else is meant to contribute to my devotional service" — then, as the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ defines faith, by serving Kṛṣṇa all other duties and purposes are served. _Kṛṣṇa-bhakti_ is completely inclusive of all one's responsibilities; this is the level of pure devotion.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Eligibility grows with faith and knowledge, but even the kaniṣṭha is on the path as long as the objective — devotion to Kṛṣṇa, which includes every other duty — is steadily held. [40:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### The four kinds of pious people and the role of sukṛti [42:00 – 50:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda gives a further classification of neophyte devotees — a pre-_kaniṣṭha_, almost conditional level — and Rūpa Gosvāmī shows how each attained pure devotion `42:00 – 44:00`.

#### Examples of the four [44:00]

- Dhruva Mahārāja wanted a kingdom; Gajendra was in distress; the four Kumāras were in the _jñānī_ category; Śaunaka Ṛṣi is named among the inquisitive.
- Rūpa Gosvāmī's point: whatever their motive, through it they attained the association of a pure devotee, and by that association they elevated themselves.
  - Dhruva may not have met anyone in this life, but he was already a devotee from a previous life.
  - The Kumāras had been instructed by Brahmā about the Supreme Lord and had the opportunity to meet the Lord Himself.

#### Distress alone does not cause bhakti [44:00 – 46:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that without pious activity a distressed man becomes an agnostic, a communist or something similar, thinking he can adjust his distress by disbelieving in God.
- So distress is not the cause of _bhakti_: many people are distressed, yet few come to Kṛṣṇa; distress can even aggravate disbelief — "God has put me in so much trouble; there is no God."
- To understand that the cause of suffering is one's own past _karma_, and that Kṛṣṇa is right here helping one endure and transcend it, one needs some _puṇya_.
- Without it, one may become anti-devotional: atheists simply do not believe; antitheists aggressively denigrate theism and preach atheism, often as fanatical as religious fanatics.

#### Kinds of sukṛti [46:00 – 48:00]

- _Sukṛti_ can come unknowingly: someone who loves gardening accidentally waters a _tulasī_ plant; someone who likes helping others happens to help a devotee.
- Such service done without intending devotion is very favourable for the awakening of devotion.
- As established earlier, _sukṛti_ does not produce _bhakti_, but it can _predispose_ one toward it: a distressed person turns to God for relief, meets a devotee, and is encouraged to start practising.

#### From approaching to surrendering [48:00]

- Prabhupāda paraphrases 7.19: moving from these categories to real surrender may take many lifetimes, but it can happen much faster by the association of devotees.
- Those who come this way are _mahātmās_ — liberal, broad-minded souls.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Distress, desire for wealth, curiosity or philosophical inquiry can bring a pious person toward God, but it is the association of a pure devotee that turns that motive into devotion; sukṛti prepares the ground, devotees plant the seed. [44:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Three kinds of happiness; enjoyment and liberation as the "ghosts" [50:00 – 54:00]

**Context.** Most people remain on the material platform; some reach the spiritual platform. Rūpa Gosvāmī distinguishes three kinds of happiness `50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bhukti_ — the desire for material enjoyment `50:00`, `54:00`.
- _Mukti_ — the desire for liberation `50:00`, `54:00`.

#### Three kinds of happiness [50:00]

- Material happiness — sense gratification.
- Spiritual happiness — here Prabhupāda uses "spiritual" for Brahman realisation, "I am Brahman," which he calls an imperfect spiritual concept.
- Devotional happiness — the perfect concept: we are parts and parcels of the Supreme Lord, meant for His service.

#### The ghosts within [50:00 – 52:00]

- Desires for _bhukti_ and _mukti_ are like the black art of a witch: they keep distracting us from pure devotion — we may move slowly, but not smoothly and swiftly.
- Many quotations follow on how a devotee does not aspire for liberation.
- Ordinary enemies attack from outside; a ghost haunts from within — you cannot take a gun to shoot it. Our enemy is our own wrong desires for enjoyment or liberation, which stop us acting in our ultimate interest: loving and serving Kṛṣṇa and returning to Him.

#### The paradox and its resolution [52:00 – 54:00]

- People haunted by ghosts are sometimes relieved when exposed to devotional stimuli, such as chanting in a haunted house.
- Paradox: because of the haunting we cannot practise _bhakti_ seriously, yet by practising _bhakti_ the haunting goes away.
- Resolution: it begins again with a devotee — we develop affection for a devotee, follow their instructions, perform devotional service, and the haunting departs.

#### Chapter summary [54:00]

- Even the _kaniṣṭha_ is not a very low level: an aspirant for pure devotion, even with incomplete faith.
- The class discussed the four kinds of people, how they move toward more serious devotion — _madhyama_ and ultimately _uttama_.
- Qualification is a quality; the disqualifications are the desires for enjoyment and for liberation — to the extent we become free of them, we move smoothly and swiftly toward Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The real disqualification for bhakti is internal — the haunting desires for enjoyment and liberation; we cannot exorcise them first and then practise, but by affection for a devotee and practising under their guidance, the haunting itself goes away. [50:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

No separate audience Q&A appears in the captions. The teacher raises and answers several questions himself:

- **If devotion needs previous devotion, where does it begin?** By the mercy of a pure devotee or a pure devotee's representative, in this life or a previous one `04:00`.
- **Does the Lord give mercy on the basis of puṇya, or arbitrarily?** Neither — _puṇya_ is material and cannot cause _bhakti_, and arbitrariness would make the Lord partial; His mercy follows His devotees' mercy `06:00 – 10:00`.
- **Doesn't BG 7.28 make freedom from sin a precondition?** It describes steadiness in _bhakti_, not its beginning `08:00`.
- **How can mechanical sensory practice awaken the soul?** It is a pathway: spiritual sense objects redirect consciousness and remove coverings — but only when practised with longing for Kṛṣṇa `18:00 – 26:00`.
