## Chapter-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Knowledge and renunciation are not integral to bhakti [00:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:nod/14 -->

**Context.** In Chapter 14, "Devotional Qualifications," the focus shifts to knowledge and renunciation. Some scholars recommend them as important for elevating oneself to devotional service, but Śrīla Prabhupāda says this is not actually a fact `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jñāna_ — knowledge `02:00`.
- _Vairāgya_ — renunciation `02:00`.
- _Rāga_ — attachment; the teacher notes this has nothing to do with _rāgānugā-bhakti_, which is a different category discussed later `02:00`.

#### Important on the spiritual path, but not integral to bhakti [02:00]

- _Jñāna_ and _vairāgya_ are not directly included in the 64 items of devotional service.
- They can be important on the general spiritual path, but the subject here is the devotional path, _bhakti-yoga_.
- In _bhakti-yoga_ they are not integral elements: they can be helpful, but they are not always necessarily helpful.
- From the impersonalist perspective, the essence of spiritual advancement is to cultivate renunciation.

#### Knowledge traditionally leads to renunciation [02:00]

- After Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's elder brother took _sannyāsa_, His father wanted to stop Nimāi's studies, reasoning that if He studied the scriptures He would realise the world is a place of misery, renounce it, and they would lose Him too.
- So in the traditional sense, _jñāna_ leads to _vairāgya_: we understand the world is temporary and that we need to go beyond it.

#### Knowledge today leads to attachment [02:00 – 04:00]

- In today's culture, most of the time knowledge leads not to renunciation but to _rāga_ — attachment; most knowledge today is concerned with how to enjoy the material world more.
- Example: someone who knows how to operate a computer may use that knowledge to surf undesirable sites; filters may be put on the computer, but a knowledgeable person can bypass them without being detected.
- Prabhupāda writes in one of his letters that we are meant to use our intelligence to control the enjoying mentality, but we use it to enjoy "intelligently"; the teacher explains there is really no such thing as intelligent enjoyment — intelligence is being used to further enjoyment `04:00`.
- Much technology — such as screens with very high-clarity images — is used for this purpose `04:00`.

#### The impersonalist idea [04:00]

- In the impersonal path, knowledge brings renunciation, which brings further knowledge: _Brahman_ is true and the world is false; knowledge shows that _Brahman_ is true, and by renunciation one gives up the false world.
- The teacher says this is true in one sense, but the philosophical foundation is incomplete: the world is not false but temporary, and _Brahman_ is not just impersonal oneness — the Absolute is a person, and we are meant to develop love for that person.
- So we cannot approach Kṛṣṇa merely through knowledge.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge and renunciation may help on a general spiritual path, but they are not integral to bhakti; today knowledge more often fuels attachment than renunciation, and the Absolute is a person to be loved, not merely understood. [00:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### Our real problem: detachment from Kṛṣṇa [06:00 – 10:00]

**Context.** The teacher recalls the earlier example of the analytical and aesthetic approaches to a flower to explain why _jñāna_ and _vairāgya_ are not always favourable for _bhakti_ `06:00`.

#### Analytical versus aesthetic approach [06:00]

- The _jñānī_ takes an analytical approach to the Absolute Truth; it may lead to some passive meditation, but not to active attraction.
- Active attraction needs an aesthetic appreciation of Kṛṣṇa as a person — one who loves and who loves to be loved.
- Kṛṣṇa is _bhāva-grāhī_ and _bhakta-vatsala_, hungry for His devotees' devotion; we express our love by serving Him.
- Too much analysis makes the Absolute Truth an object of analysis, not of devotion; too much renunciation can make one say "no" to all emotion — not just material emotion — leaving one detached from _māyā_ but also detached from Kṛṣṇa.

#### Attachment, not detachment, is the cure [06:00 – 08:00]

- Many people think attachment is the problem; actually our problem is not attachment to _māyā_ but detachment from Kṛṣṇa.
- Because we are detached from Kṛṣṇa, our pleasure-seeking propensity looks for other sources of pleasure, and then we become attracted to _māyā_ and its temptations.
- So what we need is not detachment but attachment to Kṛṣṇa — along with which comes detachment from matter.

#### Four categories of people [08:00]

The teacher recalls this from an earlier Īśopaniṣad class:

- Attached to matter, detached from Kṛṣṇa — materialists.
- Detached from matter, attached to Kṛṣṇa — devotees.
- Attached to matter and attached to Kṛṣṇa — the _sahajiyās_, who are not really authentic devotees.
- Detached from matter and detached from Kṛṣṇa — the impersonalists, for whom there is no _rasa_.

Too much _jñāna_ and _vairāgya_ often takes a person toward the impersonal orientation; a heart that is "dry" is not very suited to feeling an emotional relationship with Kṛṣṇa `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Our core problem is not attachment to māyā but detachment from Kṛṣṇa; the cure is attachment to Him, which brings detachment from matter as a by-product, whereas dry knowledge and renunciation can leave one detached from both. [06:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Knowledge favourable and unfavourable to bhakti [08:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Jīva Gosvāmī, in his commentary on _Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu_, explains the difference between knowledge favourable to _bhakti_ and knowledge unfavourable to it `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bhakti-anukūla jñāna_ — knowledge favourable to devotion `08:00 – 10:00`.
- _Bhakti-pratikūla jñāna_ — knowledge unfavourable to devotion `08:00 – 10:00`.
- _Aṇu_ and _vibhu_ — we are tiny; Kṛṣṇa is all-pervading `10:00`.
- _Māyādhīna_ and _māyādhīśa_ — we are controlled by _māyā_; Kṛṣṇa is the controller of _māyā_ `10:00`.

#### Three criteria [10:00]

- _Intention_ — what the goal is.
- _Constitution_ — what the knowledge is made up of.
- _Destination_ — where it takes one.

#### Comparing the two kinds [10:00 – 12:00]

| Criterion | Unfavourable knowledge | Favourable knowledge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intention | Minimise the difference between us and God; focus on oneness ("I am Brahman") | Highlight the difference, so we understand how wonderfully lovable Kṛṣṇa is |
| Constitution | The qualityless, formless, activityless _Brahman_; dries up the heart, since there is no need for relationship or emotion | Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, qualities and glories |
| Destination | At best, the impersonal _Brahman_ | Increases _bhakti_ and takes us to the spiritual planets, to live forever in love with Kṛṣṇa and His intimate associates |

#### No separate process needed [12:00]

- To acquire favourable knowledge we do not need a separate _jñāna_ process; the _śravaṇa_ and _kīrtana_ we already do will develop it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge is favourable to bhakti when its intention, content and destination all point to Kṛṣṇa's glory and our loving difference from Him; such knowledge comes naturally through hearing and chanting. [10:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Markaṭa, phalgu and yukta-vairāgya [12:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** Turning to renunciation, Rūpa Gosvāmī defines different kinds of _vairāgya_, a concept discussed earlier in the course `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Markaṭa-vairāgya_ — "monkey renunciation": an external show of renunciation with internal attachment `12:00 – 14:00`.
- _Yukta-vairāgya_ — using whatever is required in Kṛṣṇa's service, without attachment for oneself `14:00 – 16:00`.
- _Phalgu-vairāgya_ — renouncing things without seeing their spiritual potential, when they could be used in Kṛṣṇa's service `16:00`.

#### Monkey renunciation [12:00 – 14:00]

- Monkeys have no home and wear no clothes, but Prabhupāda would say they sometimes have dozens of mates — so they are not really renounced; it is only an external show.
- The teacher digresses on the claim "I want to live the way nature produced me": nature itself changes us as we grow (especially at puberty, when sexual attraction increases), which is why civilised society prescribes clothes; we should honour nature's changes and dress accordingly `12:00 – 14:00`.
- Similarly, many act as renunciants but are pseudo-renunciants, bringing a bad name to the renounced order; Kṛṣṇa speaks of such hypocrisy `14:00`.
- _Markaṭa-vairāgya_ is definitely not recommended.

#### Yukta-vairāgya: technology as an example [14:00 – 16:00]

- We use the internet, cell phones and various gadgets — not for our own enjoyment, but for Kṛṣṇa's service. That is _yukta-vairāgya_.

#### Phalgu-vairāgya: the sannyāsī who could not sleep [16:00 – 18:00]

- Prabhupāda would quote the story of a _sannyāsī_ so detached that he never accepted money; someone put a coin under his mat, and he could not sleep until his follower searched, found it and removed it.
- People said, "How renounced he was — the mere presence of money took away his sleep."
- Prabhupāda said this is _phalgu-vairāgya_: seeing money as material and rejecting it is a partial vision. Seeing money as meant for our enjoyment is even more incomplete. The complete vision is to see money as Lakṣmī-devī and unite her with Nārāyaṇa — use money in Kṛṣṇa's service.

#### Prabhupāda and money [18:00]

- Prabhupāda would humorously say that, far from being renounced, if you gave him a donation in one hand he would put out his next hand — not out of greed, but because he wanted to serve Kṛṣṇa and recognised money's value in that service.
- Once someone gave him a suitcase full of cash; his face lit up, and a guest was surprised. Within a few minutes he had distributed it — this for printing books, this for temple construction, this to be sent somewhere. Money was a tool; Kṛṣṇa was dear to his heart.

#### The internet: avoiding phalgu-vairāgya [20:00]

- Some say the internet is full of garbage, so never go there. This can be _phalgu-vairāgya_.
- People are on the internet; if devotees do not make their presence felt, impersonalists, other religions and shallow teachers will, and people will be attracted there.
- Someone who sees the outreach potential and starts tapping it is practising _yukta-vairāgya_ — though there is danger, and one must be cautious.

#### Vairāgya must accompany the yukta [20:00 – 22:00]

- Without detachment, one will not be able to keep the _yukta_: we will take things meant for service and use them for our own enjoyment.
- Early days of the movement: after Prabhupāda started the Sunday love feast in New York, the resident devotees once ate all the _prasāda_ cooked for the guests before the programme; when Prabhupāda began his lecture the temple manager panicked — nothing was left. The feast was meant mainly for new people; this was shocking and displeasing, and the rule became: do not be selfish — cook more so everyone gets enough, but keep the purpose in mind `20:00 – 22:00`.
- With technological gadgets: if we intend to use them rightly but tend to misuse them, we must either stop using them or take precautions (such as internet filters) so we do not easily misuse them `22:00`.
- We cannot simply reject many things in this world, or we will fall behind; _yukta-vairāgya_ means recognising things' potential for Kṛṣṇa's service and using them accordingly `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Monkey renunciation is a show; dry renunciation misses the spiritual potential of things; yukta-vairāgya uses everything — money, technology, the internet — in Kṛṣṇa's service, and it needs real detachment, with precautions where we tend to misuse. [12:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Good qualities, knowledge and renunciation come from bhakti [22:00 – 28:00]

**Context.** After knowledge and renunciation, Prabhupāda's summary study turns to good qualities (_sad-guṇa_), such as non-violence `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### A devotee is naturally non-violent [24:00]

- Prabhupāda says a devotee is naturally non-violent and need not practise non-violence separately.
- Some seek purification by joining a vegetarian movement, but a devotee is automatically vegetarian.
- The soul has good qualities, covered by conditioning; as _bhakti_ purifies us and the conditioning is removed, the good qualities naturally manifest.

#### Higher happiness removes lower cravings [24:00 – 26:00]

- Some societies try to free people from alcohol addiction, which is a serious and worthy endeavour.
- In _bhakti_ that happens as a by-product: practising seriously, one gets a higher happiness, and with it the lower cravings go away.
- From the beginning of spiritual life we follow four regulative principles, which take us away from self-defeating activities that trouble many people.

#### The hunter from the Skanda Purāṇa [26:00]

- Prabhupāda tells the story of a hunter, an "enemy of animals," who would half-kill animals and leave them to suffer.
- After he became a devotee, he would not even step on an ant: before offering obeisances to his spiritual master he gently brushed the ants away, although someone might rationalise that a higher purpose justifies ignoring them.
- That sensitivity and non-violent mentality comes by the practice of devotion.

#### Devotion produces knowledge and renunciation too [26:00 – 28:00]

- Devotion does not depend on good qualities; good qualities develop from devotion. Likewise devotion does not depend on _jñāna_ and _vairāgya_, yet they develop in one who has devotion.
- The teacher explains a verse with a poetic metaphor: when man and woman unite, children are born; when _bhakti-yoga_ is united with Lord Vāsudeva, the "children" born quickly are renunciation and knowledge.
- That knowledge is _ahaitukī_ — unmotivated: not driven by a desire for liberation, as the impersonalists' knowledge is, but simply knowledge that deepens our love for Kṛṣṇa `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti is the root, not the fruit: non-violence, freedom from bad habits, knowledge and renunciation all grow automatically from devotion to Kṛṣṇa, because they are the soul's own qualities uncovered. [24:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Practising one limb or many limbs [28:00 – 32:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda continues with _ekāṅga_ and _anekāṅga_ devotion — _aṅga_ means limb `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ekāṅga_ — practising one limb of _bhakti_ `28:00`.
- _Anekāṅga_ — practising many limbs of _bhakti_ `28:00`.

#### The nine limbs and their exemplars [28:00 – 30:00]

- Hearing (_śravaṇa_) — Parīkṣit Mahārāja.
- Chanting (_kīrtana_) — Śukadeva Gosvāmī.
- Remembering (_smaraṇa_) — Prahlāda Mahārāja.
- Serving the lotus feet (_pāda-sevana_) — Lakṣmī-devī.
- Worship (_arcana_) and offering prayers (_vandana_) — the exemplars' names are not clear in the captions; for _vandana_ the teacher mentions beautiful prayers.
- Servitorship (_dāsya_) — Hanumān.
- Friendship (_sakhya_) — Arjuna.
- Full self-surrender (_ātma-nivedana_) — Bali Mahārāja.

These devotees attained perfection by practising one limb `30:00`.

#### Ambarīṣa Mahārāja: many limbs [30:00]

- Ambarīṣa Mahārāja engaged all his senses: his mind on Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, his words in glorifying the Lord, his eyes beholding the Lord, his feet going to holy places, dancing in _kīrtana_.
- His _rasa_ is more exalted and sweeter — not in the sense of a more elevated _rasa_, but in experiencing the richness of _bhakti_.

#### What should a sādhaka do? [30:00 – 32:00]

- The principle: by whatever means, fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa.
- Some people go so completely into one thing, like _kīrtana_, that it becomes their whole universe — which tune, how the _karatālas_ should be played, whether background music helps; by such absorbed analysis one can move toward perfection.
- One limb or many — it depends on what best helps us fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa `32:00`.
- Liberation then becomes like a maidservant of the devotee: liberation, with folded hands, serves the devotee `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Perfection can come through one limb of bhakti or many; what matters is absorbing the mind in Kṛṣṇa by whatever practice best fixes it on Him. [28:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Spontaneous devotion: rāgātmikā and rāgānugā [32:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:nod/15 -->

**Context.** Here the discussion of _vaidhī-bhakti_ ends and _rāgānugā-bhakti_ begins. _Vaidhī-bhakti_ is serving the Lord in awareness of His glory; Prabhupāda begins spontaneous devotional service by saying its examples are easily seen in Kṛṣṇa's direct associates in Vṛndāvana, whose spontaneous dealings with Him are called _rāgātmikā_ `32:00 – 34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ātmikā_ — related to _ātmā_, one's essence. In BG 2.43, _kāmātmānaḥ_ are those who think the essential purpose of their existence is to enjoy _kāma_ `34:00`.
- _Rāgātmikā_ — those for whom _rāga_ is the very essence of their being: the liberated devotees whose nature, purpose and perfection is to serve the Supreme Lord eternally `34:00`.
- _Anugā_ — following; _rāgānugā_ devotees follow the path of _rāga_ `34:00`.
- _Rāga_ — also means colour `44:00`.

#### Vaidhī versus rāgānugā [34:00 – 36:00]

- Both are parts of _sādhana-bhakti_.
- In _vaidhī-bhakti_ the impetus for service comes primarily from _vidhi_ — scriptural rules.
- In _rāgānugā-bhakti_ the impetus comes primarily from one's own inspiration.

#### The Caitanya-caritāmṛta definition [36:00]

- The teacher reads a verse from the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ (Madhya-līlā): _rāgātmikā-bhakti_ shines and is fully manifest primarily in the residents of Vṛndāvana; devotion that follows in their footsteps is called _rāgānugā_.
- So the _rāgātmikā_ devotees are those in the spiritual world; the _rāgānugā_ devotees are those here who follow in their footsteps `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### How rāgānugā devotion develops [38:00 – 40:00]

- At a very advanced stage, as described in the elevated Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava scriptures, a devotee meditates on the Lord's associates, finds a particular associate most inspiring, and a bond forms centred on spiritual attraction to Kṛṣṇa.
- Example: Yaśodā-māī serves Kṛṣṇa in _vātsalya-rasa_; a _sādhaka_ in the material world aspires to follow in her footsteps and serve Kṛṣṇa in that mood.
- The devotee's present male or female form does not matter — bodies here are results of past _karma_ and have nothing to do with the soul's original _svarūpa_.
- Gradually the devotee develops in that mood, and eventually enters the spiritual world as an assistant of Yaśodā-māī.

#### The "peacock" in Vṛndāvana [40:00 – 42:00]

- Rāgānugā is a very advanced stage; we cannot prematurely jump into it.
- When Prabhupāda's disciples first came to Vṛndāvana, excited by its pastime places, they once heard strange noises from a locked room and found a man moving his neck back and forth and making peacock sounds.
- He explained that an "advanced guru" in Vṛndāvana had told him he was a peacock in the spiritual world, so he was practising, to be a well-seasoned peacock when he returned.
- Prabhupāda said this is nonsense — prematurely and artificially imagining such things.

#### When the svarūpa is revealed [42:00]

- Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura describes in _Jaiva-dharma_ that when a devotee becomes very advanced, the spiritual master reveals the devotee's _svarūpa_ in the spiritual world.
- This need not happen for everyone in this life; for some, _svarūpa-siddhi_ can happen after death.

#### A second definition of rāgātmikā [42:00 – 44:00]

The teacher explains the terms of another verse:

- _Iṣṭa_ — the desired object of life, Kṛṣṇa.
- _Svārasikī_ — natural, of one's own flavour.
- Supreme absorption (_paramāviṣṭatā_) in Him, and devotion full of that attachment, is called _rāgātmikā_ — a very deep sense of devotion and strong attraction for Kṛṣṇa.
- One who models his own life on such an eternal associate's devotional service is a _rāgānugā_ devotee.

#### Rāga as colour [44:00]

- If I wear red glasses, everything seems red.
- _Rāgātmikā_: the colour is deeply internalised. Yaśodā's _vātsalya-bhāva_ is so deep that she naturally sees everything, including Kṛṣṇa Himself, from the motherly perspective — not as God, but as a child who needs her protection, discipline and milk, or He will stay hungry.
- _Rāgānugā_: one is trying to imbibe that colouring of consciousness.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Rāgātmikā devotees in Vṛndāvana are coloured through and through by love for Kṛṣṇa; rāgānugā devotees follow in their footsteps, but this is an advanced stage that grows from genuine inspiration and guidance, not from premature imitation. [34:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Sambandha-rūpā and kāma-rūpā: seven ways of absorption [44:00 – 52:00]

**Context.** Rūpa Gosvāmī describes two kinds of _rāgātmikā-bhakti_ — _sambandha-rūpā_ (based on relationship) and _kāma-rūpā_ (based on attraction). To understand them, the teacher reads a famous _Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_ verse (7.1.31) that Rūpa Gosvāmī quotes, which describes different ways people absorbed their minds in Kṛṣṇa and attained perfection `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### The categories in the verse [46:00 – 50:00]

- **The gopīs — by _kāma_ (attraction).**
- **Kaṁsa — by fear.** Kaṁsa was so fearful that when his own wife came near at night, he thought Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma were coming. The teacher contrasts this with how material thoughts arise when ordinary people see a spouse — Kaṁsa was "Kṛṣṇa conscious" in fear `46:00`.
- **Śiśupāla and other kings — by envy.** Kṛṣṇa had given Śiśupāla's mother the benediction that He would tolerate up to a hundred blasphemies. At the Rājasūya sacrifice Kṛṣṇa was offered the first worship; Śiśupāla, outraged, began blaspheming. Kṛṣṇa counted, and when the count exceeded the mark, His Sudarśana cut off Śiśupāla's head `46:00 – 48:00`.
- **The Vṛṣṇis — by _sambandha_ (family relationship)** — Kṛṣṇa as their child, cousin, nephew, grandson `48:00`.
- **The Pāṇḍavas — by affection (_sneha_).** Nārada, speaking to Yudhiṣṭhira Mahārāja, says "you are related by affection" `48:00`.
- **Nārada and his like — by devotion.** Putting himself in a lower position, Nārada says "we are related through devotion" `48:00`.

#### Fear and envy are not favourable [46:00, 50:00]

- The point is not that fear is glorified. Recalling the definition of _uttamā bhakti_, devotion must be _ānukūlyena_ — favourable, with the intention to please Kṛṣṇa. Kaṁsa did not intend to please; the point is only that he was absorbed in Kṛṣṇa.
- Kaṁsa is not eternally present in the spiritual world fearing Kṛṣṇa, so fear and envy are left out.

#### Rūpa Gosvāmī's reduction to two [50:00 – 52:00]

- **Vṛṣṇis** — very intimate, elevated and sweet affection; they are eternal associates serving in the spiritual world, so this is _rāgātmikā_.
- **Pāṇḍavas** — very sweet relationship, but their feelings are quite often filled with awe and reverence; not an eternal, distinct category.
- **Nārada's devotion** — more at the level of duty and obligation, and not eternal; at the eternal level, _bhakti_ is in a particular _rasa_.
- So the seven categories are reduced to two: _sambandha_ and _kāma_ `52:00`.

#### Why kāma is separate [52:00]

- In one sense _kāma-rūpā_ is a type of _sambandha-rūpā_, since _kāma_ (attraction) also involves relationship.
- Technically it is placed separately because it is so pre-eminent, so extraordinary and special.
- One can model oneself on the Yadus and Vṛṣṇis too (_sambandha-rūpā_), but the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas especially exalt the devotees of Vṛndāvana: no one worships like the young women of Vraja — the gopīs are the topmost devotees.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Many absorbed their minds in Kṛṣṇa — by attraction, fear, envy, kinship, affection or devotion — but only favourable, eternal moods count as rāgātmikā; Rūpa Gosvāmī distils them into sambandha-rūpā and the pre-eminent kāma-rūpā of the gopīs. [44:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### The gopīs' kāma is pure prema [52:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** Rūpa Gosvāmī distinguishes _kāma_ from _prema_, a point discussed earlier in the course `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kāma_ — the desire for one's own gratification `52:00 – 54:00`.
- _Prema_ — the desire for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure `54:00`.

#### Apparently selfish, actually selfless [54:00]

- The gopīs sometimes appear selfish — very careful about their appearance, decorating and dressing themselves — but their purpose is Kṛṣṇa's pleasure: they know that if they are beautiful when He beholds them, He will be pleased.

#### When the flute calls [54:00 – 56:00]

- Contrast: an ordinary couple must go out; the husband keeps waiting while the wife is so concerned with her appearance that she is not concerned about reaching on time.
- The gopīs never tell Kṛṣṇa "we are not ready, wait." When He plays the flute they rush immediately — mascara on one eye and not the other, clothes meant for one part of the body put on another, ornaments in only one ear.
- Most people would find appearing like this in public horrible and would resist; but the gopīs' only concern is Kṛṣṇa's pleasure — sometimes dressing very beautifully for it, sometimes leaving everything and running for it. Their love is completely pure and transcendental `56:00`.

#### Why the word kāma is used [56:00]

- Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu was very strict; He would never be attracted to anything involving material sexuality. This love is transcendental.
- Recalling the Nectar of Instruction discussion of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī's love: male–female attraction lies on a negative axis; zero is no attraction; on the positive axis is the attraction between Kṛṣṇa and His devotees.
- This _kāma_ is not material; it is very pure desire. Because that desire cannot be understood at the material level, the word _kāma_ is used to give us a glimpse of its intensity.

#### Looking ahead [56:00]

- _Rāgānugā_ devotees model themselves on the _rāgātmikā_ devotees: at the advanced stage, a devotee thinks, "this is the particular associate, and I want to serve Kṛṣṇa the way they serve." More on _rāgānugā-bhakti_ in the next class.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The gopīs' "kāma" is the purest prema — whether adorning themselves or running half-dressed at the flute's call, their sole concern is Kṛṣṇa's pleasure; the word kāma is used only to convey the intensity of that transcendental desire. [52:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

No separate audience Q&A appears in the captions. The teacher raises and answers several questions himself:

- **If knowledge and renunciation are important on the spiritual path, why are they not among the 64 items?** Because the subject is the devotional path, where they are helpful at times but not integral `02:00`.
