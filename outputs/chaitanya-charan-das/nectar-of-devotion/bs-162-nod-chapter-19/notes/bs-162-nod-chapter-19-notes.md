## Chapter-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Prema: condensed bhāva [02:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:nod/19 -->

**Context.** Now the course moves to the fourth wave, _prema-bhakti_ — devotional service in love of God, Chapter 19. The description in Chapter 19 is not very detailed, because _prema_ and _bhāva_ are similar developments `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sāndra_ (as the teacher glosses it) — condensed, concentrated: _bhāva_ that is concentrated is called _prema_ `04:00`.

#### The definition [04:00]

- When _bhāva_ softens the heart completely and becomes condensed, it is called _prema_.

#### Accepted by great authorities [06:00]

- It is clearly stated that when love is transferred to the Supreme Lord and the conception of possessiveness elsewhere is completely removed, this is accepted as pure love of God by great authorities such as Bhīṣma and Prahlāda, and Nārada (the _Nārada-pañcarātra_ is cited).
- A famous prayer asks: as a young man is attracted to a young woman and a young woman to a young man, similarly let my mind be attracted to You.
- The principle of love: reposing one's affection completely on one person while withdrawing one's affinities from others.

#### Exclusive, but not narrow-minded [06:00 – 08:00]

- Love for Kṛṣṇa is exclusive, but not narrow-minded. In this world, "I love you" may mean "I must cut everyone else out of my love circle."
- When we love Kṛṣṇa, because He is connected with and intimately loves everyone, our love flows through Him to everyone. The focus is on one person, and through Kṛṣṇa love goes to all.
- So a devotee is not insensitive: he is very sensitive about various issues and tries to deal with them properly, not divorced from everything else.

#### Primary and secondary characteristics [08:00]

- Primary: _bhāva_ that has become condensed and intensified — very intense feelings of love.
- Secondary: complete softening of the heart.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prema is bhāva condensed until the heart is completely softened; it reposes all affection in Kṛṣṇa, yet, because He loves everyone, that exclusive love flows through Him to all. [04:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Mamatā: "I am Yours, and You are mine" [08:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** A very significant point in the definition is an intense sense of _mamatā_ — "mine-ness." There are two moods in relationship, and _prema_ is based on _mamatā_ `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mamatā_ — the sense of "mine" `08:00 – 12:00`.

#### "I am Yours" versus "I am independent" [10:00]

- "My dear Lord, I am Yours" is the level of a surrendered person. The opposite feeling — "I will do what I want; I am free; I am independent" — is celebrated by many moderns, but it is not conducive to devotion; it leads to continued material existence.
- In independence we may have a feeling of freedom, but not the fulfilment of reciprocated love. Relationship implies subordination: people do not "stand" in love, they "fall" in love.

#### "You are mine" [12:00]

- Beyond "I am Yours," _mamatā_ also implies "You are mine." Yaśodā thinks that Kṛṣṇa is going around stealing butter from everyone — what will happen to His reputation?
- _Mamatā_ here is a very elevated stage of love, although in other contexts possessiveness is called bad.
- In the Bhagavad-gītā (2.71) we are told to give up _mama_ — the sense of "mine" — and false ego: possessiveness and misidentification in relation to matter. Their reversion to their original forms comes through purification by devotional service `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Possessiveness that pleases Kṛṣṇa [14:00]

- Directed toward Kṛṣṇa, _mamatā_ makes the relationship very sweet. When Kṛṣṇa has been with Candrāvalī and later comes to a gopī, she is angry: "If You want to be with her, don't come here." This possessiveness is very pleasing to Kṛṣṇa.
- It indicates, most importantly, a very personal and intimate relationship with God — not just a sense of His greatness ("God is great; I am a small part"), but intense possessiveness.
- The Ratha-yātrā festival carries this mood: from Kurukṣetra, the gopīs are taking Kṛṣṇa back to Vṛndāvana, pulling Him on a cart — the sentiment being "Kṛṣṇa belongs to us, the Vrajavāsīs, and we want Him back."

#### Two-sided [16:00]

- The relationship is not one-sided: "I am Yours, I give myself completely to You," and "You are mine — if I have You, what else do I need? You are the supreme possession."

#### Intoxicated with love [18:00]

- A famous verse from the Eleventh Canto, quoted and glorified at length, describes the mood of one completely intoxicated and maddened by love of God; that intoxication is the perfection of love.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The hallmark of prema is mamatā toward Kṛṣṇa — "I am Yours" and "You are mine" — the same possessiveness that binds us when aimed at matter becomes the sweetest intimacy when aimed at Him. [08:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Practice, mercy, and not demanding mercy [20:00 – 26:00]

**Context.** As with _bhāva_, a couple of points are revisited: how _prema_ comes by practice and by mercy `20:00`.

#### Emotions carried from a previous life [20:00]

- Sometimes a young _sādhaka_ shows special spiritual emotions without much connection to devotional service in this life. This can come from previous lives: the person was attracted because of past practice.
- _Sādhana_, _bhāva_ and _prema_ can be a multiple-lifetime process.

#### The normal path and the exception [20:00 – 22:00]

- The normal process for a PhD is kindergarten, primary, secondary, college, graduation, post-graduation and then a doctorate.
- Some exceptional person may write an extraordinary novel that becomes globally acclaimed, win the Nobel Prize in literature, and receive an honorary doctorate.
- That is exceptional; an ordinary student cannot think, "I will get it that way too."

#### Mercy and our endeavour [22:00]

- A devotee does not depend on or demand _kṛpā_ as the only factor for perfection. It is not our _bhakti_ that leads to advancement but Kṛṣṇa's mercy — yet along with that our endeavour is also important.
- A person outside a well pulls someone out, but the person in the well has to hold on.
- When we see someone very advanced, we should understand they may have endeavoured in previous lives.

#### The bargaining mood [22:00 – 24:00]

- A business mood bargains: give as little as possible and get as much as possible.
- Thinking "I won't endeavour; let Kṛṣṇa give mercy and I'll get everything" is non-devotional. Demanding that Kṛṣṇa give _darśana_ is basically a business mood, not devotion.
- Real mercy is love that floods and fills our life; a devotee does not demand it.

#### Two roads [24:00]

- The path from _sādhana-bhakti_ to _bhāva-bhakti_ has two ways — _vaidhī_ and _rāgānugā_ — like two roads coming together and continuing as one.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prema may come by practice over lifetimes or by exceptional mercy, but a devotee never bargains for mercy; like someone being pulled out of a well, we must hold on with our own sincere endeavour. [20:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### The gopīs: perfection by Kṛṣṇa's association [26:00 – 30:00]

**Context.** By Kṛṣṇa's mercy — how did the gopīs get _prema_? They did not execute regulative principles or undergo austerities; simply by association with Kṛṣṇa they attained the highest perfection of devotional service `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Different categories of gopīs [28:00]

- Some are _nitya-siddha_ gopīs descended from the spiritual world; others, as discussed earlier, were sages in previous lives who appear in Kṛṣṇa-līlā later.
- When Kṛṣṇa called the gopīs for the _rāsa_, not all could come; some faced obstacles. Some broke free; others, unable to overcome the obstacles, became desperate, meditated on the Lord and longed intensely to be with Him — and by that longing their impurities came out.
- This verse refers to such gopīs: simply by Kṛṣṇa's association they developed love of God.

#### No pilgrimage, no rituals [28:00]

- The gopīs did not go to Vṛndāvana on pilgrimage; it was simply the place where they were born and lived. They did not practise the rituals of _dharma_ in this sense.
- What made them exalted was their association with Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The gopīs reached the highest prema not by rules, austerity or pilgrimage, but by Kṛṣṇa's association — and even those blocked from Him were purified by their desperate longing. [28:00]
</Callout>

### Love with awareness of greatness, and kevala prema [30:00 – 42:00]

**Context.** Their attraction to Kṛṣṇa was so spontaneous and deep. The chapter distinguishes _mahimā-jñāna-yukta prema_ — love with knowledge of the Lord's greatness — from _kevala prema_ — unmixed, pure love `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mahimā-jñāna_ — knowledge of the Lord's greatness `30:00`, `38:00`.
- _Kevala_ — unmixed, pure `30:00`.

#### The Dāmodara pastime and the footwear analogy [32:00]

- In the Dāmodara-līlā Kṛṣṇa is being bound by a devotee. For one in _mahimā-jñāna_, how could that be conceivable?
- Footwear is normally considered contemptible: kept outside the temple. Footwear inside the temple, what to speak of at the altar, what to speak of touching the Deities, what to speak of on top of the Deities — one who understands the rules would never allow it. Similarly, one aware of the Lord's greatness could never bind Him.

#### Stars by day [34:00]

- The Vrajavāsīs think Kṛṣṇa is not God but lovable and special. If He does something extraordinary, they may think it is because of some special power, or for a moment become aware He is God — but afterwards that awareness is covered.
- Stars are there in the daytime but not visible, because the sun is so bright. The awareness of Kṛṣṇa's greatness is like the stars; the awareness of His sweetness — their love — is like the sun.
- Because of their love they do not care for Kṛṣṇa's divinity: as discussed earlier, the Vrajavāsīs are dearer to God than anyone, yet they do not think Kṛṣṇa is God. Nanda Mahārāja worships Nārāyaṇa and thinks that, because of his worship, Nārāyaṇa is blessing and protecting Kṛṣṇa — when demons come, Nārāyaṇa enters Kṛṣṇa and gives Him power.

#### The perfection of existence [36:00]

- This _kevala prema_ is the perfection of life — the perfection of existence in terms of the relationship between devotee and Lord, in which the Lord Himself becomes extraordinarily delighted.
- The teacher refers to a prayer whose first verses describe the pastime and then that Kṛṣṇa is crying, and which uses the terminology of _mahimā-jñāna_ and _kevala prema_ — an ocean of happiness through the pastime `36:00`.

#### A prayer of pure love [38:00]

- Those who follow great religious rules are told that this is fine, but without love they will not win the Lord; winning the Lord is possible only by _bhakti_.
- Rūpa Gosvāmī offers such a prayer not in a mood of interference but due to the spontaneity of love flooding his heart. The pastime contrasts love in reverence (_mahimā-jñāna prema_, _aiśvarya_) with pure love (_kevala prema_); in _mahimā-jñāna prema_ the relationship with Kṛṣṇa is not so close.

#### How the miracle happens [40:00]

- Yoga-māyā alone is not responsible; the devotees' purity and intensity of love is also responsible. Together the miracle happens: the devotee remembers God and forgets He is God.

#### Three levels of knowing God [40:00 – 42:00]

- **Nominal religious people** — "know God without God": people born in a Kṛṣṇa culture, such as many in India, know about Kṛṣṇa as a Hindu god who danced with the gopīs, but do not really know _kṛṣṇa-tattva_ or think about Him; their knowledge is superficial.
- **Sādhakas** — must "know God with His Godhood": come to know His greatness, position and power, and that He is supreme. The difference from nominal religious people is intense love for Kṛṣṇa, which the nominalists lack.
- **Vrajavāsīs** — "know God without His Godhood": love is their defining characteristic; it makes theirs a very special kind of love.
- This is the love our Gauḍīya tradition is offering; by practising Kṛṣṇa consciousness through the process of _bhakti_ in the scriptures, we will ultimately attain this kind of exalted love `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Love aware of Kṛṣṇa's greatness keeps a reverent distance; kevala prema, like the sun hiding the stars, so floods the Vrajavāsīs that they forget He is God — knowing God without His Godhood — and this is the love our tradition offers. [30:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### The progression, and why prema cannot be judged from outside [42:00 – 52:00]

**Context.** Rūpa Gosvāmī describes the progression of devotion, discussed implicitly earlier, from the beginning of practice up to the appearance of _prema_ `42:00 – 44:00`.

#### Transcendental experience [44:00]

- Such a person's experience is transcendental — not something of the body or mind.
- Normally we judge others' happiness by external expression — a smile, a face; but this cannot be done for _prema_ `44:00`.

#### Externals can mislead [46:00 – 48:00]

- To the extent we remember Kṛṣṇa we are completely satisfied and need nothing else; yet that remembrance may sometimes lead to external crying.
- After Kṛṣṇa left Vṛndāvana, the gopīs were constantly crying in separation. One might think: "I want to love Kṛṣṇa and become happy, but the topmost devotees are crying constantly — why go through all the regulative principles and _sādhana_ for that?"
- Remembering a beautiful sunrise from long ago is a passive memory of an object. Remembering Kṛṣṇa is not like that, because Kṛṣṇa is a person; it is active remembrance of an active subject, and emotions change depending on whether one feels His presence or His absence. Underlying even separation is intense remembrance of Kṛṣṇa `48:00`.
- So we cannot judge by externals. Generally a devotee is happy because of remembering Kṛṣṇa, but not always outwardly `48:00`.

#### Furious on the outside [50:00]

- Once Śrīla Prabhupāda was furious at a construction site because the temple was not ready. That anger was not material: in remembering Kṛṣṇa, he was going to offer Him the temple, and it was not ready — intense remembrance of Kṛṣṇa.
- So even if there is fury externally, there may be internal remembrance of Kṛṣṇa. We cannot know precisely who is how advanced.

#### Bliss can be faked [52:00]

- Of all the characteristics associated with connection to the Lord, mere bliss is not listed as one, because it can be faked.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prema is a transcendental, active remembrance of a Person, so its outward face may be tears, separation or even fury; external signs, which can be faked, are no measure of how advanced someone is. [44:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Closing review of the Bhakti Shastri course [52:00 – 58:00]

**Context.** The teacher concludes the study of the exaltedness of _bhakti_ and the pathway to it, and reviews the whole course, offered as service to Śrīla Prabhupāda's books `52:00`.

#### Why study Prabhupāda's books [52:00]

- Prabhupāda's books give the message of Kṛṣṇa as explained by many _ācāryas_ through history, properly presented so that it is relevant to us.
- Studying them, we receive Prabhupāda's mercy; when the message goes through our head and enters our heart, we become intellectually convinced about what we are doing.

#### The four books [52:00 – 56:00]

- **Śrī Īśopaniṣad** — focuses on understanding the inconceivable position of _īśvara_, who alone is the object of our life. In any spiritual path we need to know _sambandha_, _abhidheya_ and _prayojana_; the four books help us understand them. We should not be distracted by material progress, demigod worship or the impersonal aspect, but focus on the personal form of the Absolute; there is an indication of _bhakti_ in the prayer asking the Lord to remove the covering of His real face — showing that knowledge of God is not merely theoretical but meant to transport us, and the process of that transport is _bhakti-yoga_ `52:00 – 54:00`.
- **The Nectar of Instruction** — how _bhakti-yoga_ is practised: identifying a guru; guidelines on what is favourable to cultivate and what to avoid; most importantly, avoiding bad association and positively having good association, and how to associate; how not to have a mundane mentality toward devotees' bodies; and how to practise _bhakti_ more and more in the mood of Vraja, ultimately reaching Rādhā-kuṇḍa, the arena of the practice of the most exalted love `54:00 – 56:00`.
- **Bhagavad-gītā** — a huge, full course in which the various paths are discussed: _karma-yoga_ is compared with _jñāna-yoga_ and shown superior; the early chapters show how _karma-yoga_ leads through _dhyāna-yoga_ to _bhakti_; the middle six chapters glorify _bhakti_; the last six again move from _jñāna-yoga_ to _bhakti-yoga_. After comparing the paths, the Gītā establishes the supremacy of _bhakti_ `56:00`.
- **The Nectar of Devotion** — does not compare paths; having established _bhakti_ as supreme, it details the _sādhana_ with its 64 elements, and then gives pictures of the destination `56:00`.

#### Pictures of the destination [56:00 – 58:00]

- If you must travel for three months and are shown pictures of a beautiful island with beautiful scenery and beaches, those pictures attract you: "I want to go there; however much trouble there is, I will go."
- Similarly, after the pathway is delineated, looking at _prema_ makes us say: "Yes — no matter how difficult, this is where I want to go."
- On this long journey, Śrīla Prabhupāda is our guide, and by Lord Caitanya's mercy we will be able to go through it and ultimately attain life's supreme perfection. The teacher thanks everyone for joining this small step forward in the Bhakti Shastri course, meant to deepen conviction and inspiration to accept Prabhupāda's gift `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The four books form one map — Īśopaniṣad shows the goal, the Nectar of Instruction the practice, the Gītā the supremacy of bhakti among paths, and the Nectar of Devotion its detailed sādhana and the attractive picture of prema that keeps us travelling under Prabhupāda's guidance. [52:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
