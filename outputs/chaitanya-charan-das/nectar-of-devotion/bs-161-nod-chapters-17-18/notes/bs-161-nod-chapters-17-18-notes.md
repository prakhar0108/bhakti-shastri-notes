### From sādhana to bhāva: the definition [00:00 – 04:00]

**Context.** By executing regulated devotional service one is elevated to the transcendental stage beyond the material modes; as the devotee advances he reaches the platform of the _uttama-adhikārī_. Prabhupāda then uses the terms "executive" and "effective," which the teacher explains by going back to the verse defining _bhāva_ `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śuddha-sattva_ — pure goodness `02:00`.
- _Ātmā_ — can mean the soul, but also "nature": here, that whose nature is pure goodness `02:00`.
- _Aṁśu_ — a ray; _sūryāṁśu_ is a ray of the sun `02:00`.
- _Sāmya_ — similar `02:00`.

- That whose nature is pure goodness.
- That which is like a ray of the sun of _prema_ — _prema_ is the final stage, like the sun; _bhāva_ is the penultimate stage, like a ray.
- That which softens the heart, along with various tastes.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhāva-bhakti is defined as pure goodness that is a ray of the sun of prema, filling the heart with tastes and softening it. [02:00 – 04:00]

### Śuddha-sattva: the potencies of knowledge and pleasure [04:00 – 10:00]

**Context.** The teacher unpacks "pure goodness," recalling the modes discussed in the Gītā, especially Chapter 14 `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sattva_ and _śuddha-sattva_ — _sattva_ (goodness) is still material and considered contaminated; _śuddha-sattva_ (pure, uncontaminated) is transcendental `04:00`.
- _Saṁvit_ — the Lord's knowledge-giving potency; "cognition" `04:00 – 06:00`.
- _Hlādinī_ — the pleasure potency — not sensual but devotional pleasure; "affection" `04:00 – 06:00`.
- _Sandhinī_ — the potency that provides facility `08:00`.

#### Material goodness versus pure goodness [04:00 – 06:00]

- When Kṛṣṇa introduces material goodness, He says it has two characteristics, happiness and knowledge; both are material and bind the soul.
- _Śuddha-sattva_ is composed of pure knowledge and pure happiness: pure knowledge is knowledge about Kṛṣṇa, and when it manifests the devotee's heart is illumined with it.

#### Three things in any activity [06:00 – 08:00]

- Playing a sitar requires knowledge of how to play, the instrument (facility), and the desire to play — which comes from some pleasure, whether one enjoys playing, or wants appreciation or payment. That binding desire is roughly "affection."
- Joining an online course requires knowledge of how to use the internet (_saṁvit_), a connection and device (_sandhinī_), and some pleasure from it, which comes when we like something about it (_hlādinī_).

#### What changes at the stage of bhāva [08:00 – 10:00]

- These are material examples; in the spiritual world pure love and happiness come by Kṛṣṇa's special mercy — and that mercy is personal: _saṁvit_, _sandhinī_ and _hlādinī_ are all His mercy, enabling us to serve Him better.
- Facilities are already present in the material world and can be used in His service — land can become a temple; the tongue can glorify Kṛṣṇa.
- What needs to change is our knowledge of how things are meant to be used, and our affection — redirected from the things of this world to the transcendental Lord.
- At the stage of _bhāva_, one's cognition and affection are redirected from matter to spirit — not merely the _brahmajyoti_, but the Supreme spiritual reality, Kṛṣṇa. As the conditioned soul spontaneously delights in worldly things, one in _bhāva_ is attracted to and delights in Kṛṣṇa `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every action needs knowledge, facility and affection; in bhāva the facilities stay the same, but our knowledge and affection — Kṛṣṇa's own saṁvit and hlādinī mercy — are redirected wholly toward Him. [06:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### A ray of the sun, a seed of love, a softened heart [10:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** The teacher explains the ray-of-the-sun comparison and the softening of the heart `10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Premāṅkura_ — a word Rūpa Gosvāmī also uses: _aṅkura_ means seed; as a whole tree blossoms from a seed, love of God will soon manifest from this `10:00`.

#### Same quality, different intensity [10:00]

- Between _bhāva_ and _prema_ there is qualitative identity but a difference in quantitative intensity.
- A ray gives a little light and warmth; the full sun gives complete illumination. The emotions in _bhāva_ are pure, spiritual and rich, connected with Kṛṣṇa, but not yet as intense.

#### Very exalted from our perspective [10:00 – 12:00]

- Compared with the sun a ray seems small, but _bhāva_ is not "nothing great" — it is a very exalted stage just before _prema_; lesser only from the perspective of _prema_. That is why Prabhupāda calls this the level of the _uttama_.

#### Softness of heart [12:00]

- Whenever there is any devotional stimulus, the devotee is immediately flooded with devotional emotion.
- Illustration: Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu would see some river, think it was the Yamunā, and want to run and jump in to get Kṛṣṇa's touch through His beloved river. The teacher notes Mahāprabhu is at the highest level of _prema_, not _bhāva_; the example only illustrates softness of heart.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhāva is one in quality with prema but less in intensity — a ray of the same sun and the seed of the same tree — yet from our position it is an extremely exalted stage, marked by a heart that melts at every reminder of Kṛṣṇa. [10:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Executive and effective; endeavours, tastes and steady ecstasy [12:00 – 20:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda says that within devotional service there are two divisions, executive and effective `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Executive_ — _sādhana-bhakti_: we execute devotional service so that by practice we gradually move forward `12:00`.
- _Effective_ — Prabhupāda uses the word in a slightly unusual sense: not "producing an effect" but "being the effect"; the effect of _sādhana_ is _bhāva_ `12:00 – 14:00`.
- _Ceṣṭā-rūpa_ and _bhāva-rūpa_ — _bhāva_ expressed primarily through endeavours, or primarily through emotions `14:00 – 16:00`.
- _Sādhana_ and _sādhya_ within _ceṣṭā-rūpa_ — endeavours that cause _bhāva_, and endeavours that are the effect of _bhāva_ `14:00`.
- _Samādhi_ — the continuation of ecstasy within the mind after the external symptoms appear `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### Not ordinary endeavour [14:00 – 16:00]

- The endeavours here are a different category from material endeavours for material results: one already has very rich, intense emotions for Kṛṣṇa — this is beyond _rāgānugā_, which itself is spontaneous devotion.
- Whether the actions or the emotions are primary, the important point is that it is all _bhāva-bhakti_.

#### Primary and secondary characteristics [16:00]

- Primary: the descent of pure goodness (_śuddha-sattva_).
- Secondary: the softening of the heart.

#### Three kinds of ruci [16:00 – 18:00]

- _Prāpti-abhilāṣa_ — the desire to attain Kṛṣṇa. _Abhilāṣa_ is best translated "aspiration": a cherished desire held on a high pedestal in the heart, deeper than ordinary desires.
- _Ānukūlya-abhilāṣa_ — the desire to please Kṛṣṇa. The desire to please is present from the beginning, but here realisation is much deeper: one perceives Kṛṣṇa as a person and wants to do what will please Him.
- _Sauhārda-abhilāṣa_ — the desire to keep Kṛṣṇa close to the heart. Earlier one tried, as a rule, not to forget Kṛṣṇa; now one cherishes the desire that He never go away from one's heart.

#### The iron rod in the fire [18:00 – 20:00]

- Put a metal rod in fire: it becomes hotter and hotter, acquiring the fire's characteristics not only externally but also internally.
- Similarly, as a devotee comes close to Kṛṣṇa, symptoms manifest, and with them internal ecstasy appears and stays; when that internal ecstasy becomes steady it is called _samādhi_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sādhana is the executive cause and bhāva its effect; in bhāva the desires to attain, please and keep Kṛṣṇa close become cherished aspirations, and like an iron rod glowing in fire, the devotee's ecstasy becomes steady within. [12:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Attaining bhāva by practice: Nārada and a dancing devotee [20:00 – 28:00]

**Context.** _Bhāva-bhakti_ is attained in two ways — by practice and by mercy. Practice means _sādhana_, either _vaidhī_ or _rāgānugā_; Rūpa Gosvāmī gives an example of each, which Prabhupāda elaborates `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Avyartha-kālatva_ — _kāla_ is time, _vyartha_ is to waste; not wasting any time at all `28:00`.

#### Nārada Muni: by vaidhī-sādhana [20:00 – 24:00]

- Nārada's story, told to Vyāsadeva in the First Canto of the _Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_ (Chapters 4–6) when Vyāsa was still dissatisfied after compiling the Vedas, illustrates many principles the _Bhāgavatam_ later develops.
- He was born as a maidservant's son, with no mention of a father; his mother had a service disposition and served visiting devotees, and seeing her he served them diligently, heard their instructions carefully and took their remnants.
- After his mother passed away he went north, detached from everything material, and practised rigidly. Kṛṣṇa appeared once and disappeared; desperate, he tried to replicate his meditation, but could not see Him again.
- A voice told him to keep practising diligently throughout this life; in his next life he would have Kṛṣṇa's constant association.
- By determined practice he attained such perfection that he can move between the material and spiritual worlds at will — unlike aeroplanes or spacecraft needing fuel, checks and planning, he can transfer himself instantly from one planet to another `24:00`.
- So by diligent _vaidhī-sādhana_ he attained ecstatic love.

#### The power of hearing [24:00 – 26:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that a realised soul speaking in the Lord's service can inject spiritual life into the audience; one should seek the association of pure devotees, and by such association and service a neophyte will develop attachment, love and devotion.
- Nārada heard only for four months during _Cāturmāsya_, but he took those words like treasures into his heart; they echoed there and became the map of his life.
- Hearing inspires us consciously — "I should do this" — and subconsciously: the great devotees' desires are transferred to us and purify our own desires.

#### A devotee who danced all night: by rāgānugā-sādhana [26:00 – 28:00]

- From the _Padma Purāṇa_: a lady devotee came to a temple, saw the beautiful Deity of Kṛṣṇa and was so overwhelmed that she began dancing with divine madness.
- We may dance in _kīrtana_ for 5, 10 or 15 minutes before tiring; she, infused with spiritual energy, danced the entire night — not because anyone told her to, as when we dance out of discipline, but because she was captivated by Kṛṣṇa's beauty.
- She got the mood of the gopīs dancing for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure; Rūpa Gosvāmī explains that her dancing indicates she had come to the level of ecstatic love.
- Her example shows a characteristic discussed in the next chapter — _avyartha-kālatva_: she did not want to waste a moment in anything else `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhāva can grow from practice: Nārada reached it through diligent vaidhī-sādhana built on what he heard from devotees, and the devotee who danced all night shows the spontaneous absorption it brings. [20:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Attaining bhāva by mercy: words, glance and heartfelt blessing [28:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** _Bhāva_ can also come by mercy. Prabhupāda explains: simply by speaking, simply by glancing, and simply by good wishes — _vācika_, _darśana_ and _hārda_ `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vācika_ — by words `28:00 – 30:00`.
- _Darśana_ — by glance or sight `30:00`.
- _Hārda_ — by heartfelt blessing, which can come from the Lord or from a devotee `32:00`.
- _Performative utterance_ — words whose speaking causes the action `30:00`.

#### By words [28:00 – 30:00]

- The Lord may say "I bless you," and those words carry the potency by which the devotee becomes infused with _bhakti_. A discussion is quoted in which Kṛṣṇa tells Nārada, "I bless you with unalloyed devotion."
- Does this contradict Nārada's attaining perfection by practice? No — the quote illustrates the principle, not Nārada's case.
- Kṛṣṇa's words are performative utterances: when a judge says "let this prisoner be hanged," it happens; an ordinary citizen's identical words do nothing. Being omnipotent, Kṛṣṇa's words have that power.

#### By glance: the jungle province [30:00 – 32:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes: when the inhabitants of the jungle province saw Kṛṣṇa, they were so stricken with feeling that they could not withdraw their glance from Him.
- "Stricken" is usually negative ("grief-stricken"), but here it indicates extreme intensity of positive feeling.
- They did not have _bhāva_ before; simply by beholding the Lord's all-attractive beauty they developed it.

#### By heartfelt blessing of the Lord: Śukadeva [32:00 – 34:00]

- In a quoted passage Nārada tells Vyāsadeva that his son, without following regulative principles, is already enriched with symptoms that come after many births of devotional service.
- Śukadeva stayed in the womb a long time, fearing that māyā would entrap him if he came out; Vyāsa assured him otherwise, but he would not come. Vyāsa then besought the Lord, who came and assured him that he would not be entangled.
- His love, equal to that developed over many lifetimes, came by the Lord's blessing.

#### By heartfelt blessing of a devotee: Prahlāda and the hunter [34:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that this natural attraction is caused by the special grace of the Lord or of a great devotee like Nārada.
- When Nārada instructed Prahlāda's mother, Prahlāda was in the womb; Nārada, well-wisher of both, wished that this child become a great devotee — and Prahlāda's great devotion manifested.
- The hunter Mṛgāri half-killed animals; Nārada desired that he be reformed, and he became so transformed that he would not even hurt ants.

#### Chapter 17 summary [36:00 – 38:00]

- Definition of _bhāva_; primary characteristic (descent of _śuddha-sattva_) and secondary (softening of the heart, with various tastes).
- Attained by _sādhana_ (_vaidhī_ or _rāgānugā_) or by mercy (_vācika_, _darśana_, _hārda_).
- _Hārda_ differs from _vācika_: in the cases of Nārada and Prahlāda, Nārada and Mṛgāri, and the Lord and Śukadeva, there is no direct speech to the devotee — Prahlāda and Śukadeva were in the womb. The desire alone is enough for ecstatic love to manifest.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhāva can also be given by mercy — through the Lord's performative words, through the sight of His beauty, or even through an unspoken heartfelt blessing of the Lord or His pure devotee. [28:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Why tears are not a reliable sign [38:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:nod/18 -->

**Context.** The next chapter, on the characteristics of one in ecstatic love, is very important because it helps differentiate real from apparent advancement; it describes nine characteristics, with Rūpa Gosvāmī's Sanskrit terms `38:00`.

#### Two dangers in equating tears with love [38:00 – 40:00]

- Softness of heart can lead to tears, and one may think, "This person has so many tears — he must be very advanced."
- First danger: imitators may somehow produce tears, not from love of God but for prestige. When Haridāsa Ṭhākura was in ecstasy, a snake charmer's performance had him dancing; another man began imitating, and the snake charmer beat him until he ran away. That person was discerning, but recognising imitation of love of God may not be so easy `38:00 – 40:00`.
- Second danger: there may be real inner devotion without external symptoms. Expressions of love of God are private; a devotee does not parade devotion and may cry only in private `40:00`.

#### Sustained symptoms as the guide [40:00 – 42:00]

- So this chapter gives symptoms that are exhibited over a period of time and are not easily imitable — a much more reliable guide to spiritual advancement, for ourselves and for others.
- Shadow emotions (_ābhāsa_ and _chāyā_) are discussed later in the chapter.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Tears can be imitated, and real love can stay hidden; the reliable marks of ecstatic love are sustained qualities, shown over time, that no imitator can keep up. [38:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### The nine characteristics of one in ecstatic love [42:00 – 54:00]

**Context.** The teacher goes through each characteristic with its example `42:00`.

#### 1. Avyartha-kālatva — never wasting time [42:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that such a devotee is always anxious to use his time in devotion, does not like to be idle, and wants to serve twenty-four hours a day without deviation.
- The Gosvāmīs barely slept a few hours so they could constantly glorify Kṛṣṇa in every possible way — their divine madness.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda too slept only a few hours; all day he spoke about Kṛṣṇa and arranged for His glorification, and at night he would wake and dictate his books.

#### 2. Kṣānti — perseverance [42:00 – 46:00]

- One who remains undisturbed despite causes of disturbance is called reserved and perseverant (_Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_ 1.19.15).
- Parīkṣit Mahārāja, cursed to die, says: let the snake-bird created by the _brāhmaṇa_'s son come and bite me — but please keep speaking the glories of Viṣṇu, and by hearing them I will become transcendental.
- Normally when trouble comes we give up a desire; when the desire for Kṛṣṇa is intense, no obstacle stops one. "Nothing holds a devotee back; a devotee holds nothing back." Kuntī prays in the First Canto (1.8.42) that as the Gaṅgā flows to the ocean, her consciousness may flow toward Kṛṣṇa `44:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda's obstacles: an uncooperative family; a business that failed; a magazine people were not interested in; an institution he was driven out of by political conspiracy; Godbrothers with a narrow-minded vision; running out of funds while writing books, and people not taking them seriously; a heart attack on the way to America; his typewriter stolen; not being allowed to speak where he stayed; a roommate who came at him with almost violent intent, forcing him to flee. Yet he was determined to keep moving forward `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### 3. Virakti — detachment [46:00]

- Bharata Mahārāja had opulence and a beautiful wife, but, desiring the Supreme Lord, he left everything worldly like stool — the graphic point being that we do not even look back at it.

#### 4. Māna-śūnyatā — pridelessness [46:00 – 48:00]

- A great king in the _Padma Purāṇa_ became a mendicant and was so humble that he respectfully bowed even to his political enemies and to untouchables.
- Prabhupāda gives a recent example: Lāla Bābu, a big landlord, became a Vaiṣṇava and lived in Vṛndāvana.
- The point is not that everyone must become a beggar, but that one who loves Kṛṣṇa cares only to please Him; for that one does not mind even being dishonoured. Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, the Supreme Lord Himself, went around begging people to chant, and Haridāsa Ṭhākura did the same.
- Imitators may easily get tears, but to put aside pride and ego day in and day out, year after year, requires genuine love of God `48:00`.

#### 5. Āśā-bandha — hope [48:00 – 50:00]

- Tremendous hope that, despite reversals outside and limitations and _anarthas_ inside, Kṛṣṇa will one day bestow mercy and one will attain perfection.
- The _ācāryas_ say the devotee should have such hope that even if Kṛṣṇa Himself said there is no chance for him, he would continue practising — because Kṛṣṇa is supremely merciful and nobody is hopeless.
- Without hope we lose the battle before it begins: hope is what keeps us fighting temptations, distractions and tribulations; without it one thinks, "What is the use of fighting? Let me just fall into sense gratification."
- We get hope by seeing how great devotees went through great tribulations and Kṛṣṇa eventually delivered them `50:00`.
- Rūpa Gosvāmī's own example: he says he has no love for Kṛṣṇa, nor the means of developing it — hearing, chanting and _bhakti-yoga_ are lacking, he has no knowledge or piety, nor high birth — he simply hopes somehow to approach the Lord's lotus feet, and that hope gives him pain because he feels incompetent `50:00`.

#### 6. Samutkaṇṭhā — intense eagerness [50:00 – 52:00]

- Bilvamaṅgala Ṭhākura: "I am eagerly waiting to see that boy of Vṛndāvana," whose bodily beauty captivates the universe and whose lips produce a sound that drives one madder than a mad elephant.
- This resembles the intense greed discussed earlier. One may act eager for a day or two, but without deep eagerness one cannot continue month after month, year after year — these are non-imitable, reliable symptoms.

#### 7. Nāma-gāne sadā ruci — constant taste for chanting [52:00]

- From the _Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta_: an associate of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī tells Govinda that the daughter of King Vṛṣabhānu is shedding tears and anxiously chanting His names — exhibiting Rādhārāṇī's taste for the holy name.

#### 8. Guṇākhyāne āsakti — eagerness to describe the Lord's qualities [52:00]

- This refers to the Lord's qualities and pastimes generally. Again from the _Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta_ — a very ecstatic book by Bilvamaṅgala Ṭhākura describing Kṛṣṇa's pastimes and the feelings of His devotees as they long for Him, unite with Him and serve Him.

#### 9. Prīti for His places — attraction to living where Kṛṣṇa had pastimes [52:00 – 54:00]

- The example remembers the place where Dāmodara, who cuts the knots of material existence, was tied by His mother: "I want to go there; I want to be there."
- Earlier, residing in the _dhāma_ was mentioned as a rule of _vaidhī-bhakti_; here it is a spontaneous, intense, almost intolerable craving to be in the holy place.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The marks of ecstatic love are sustained, not momentary: never wasting time, perseverance through every obstacle, detachment, pridelessness, unshakeable hope, intense eagerness, taste for the name, eagerness to glorify, and longing for Kṛṣṇa's places. [42:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Reflection and shadow: ecstasies that are not yet real [54:00 – 60:00]

**Context.** Rūpa Gosvāmī then discusses _ratyābhāsa_ — a semblance of _bhāva_ — of two kinds, _pratibimba_ and _chāyā_. Prabhupāda points to it when he writes that this attachment is confidentially kept by Kṛṣṇa and bestowed only on pure devotees, so it cannot be achieved by those whose hearts are contaminated by fruitive action and mental speculation `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ābhāsa_ — a reflection or semblance of _bhāva_ `54:00`.
- _Pratibimba_ — reflection `54:00`.
- _Chāyā_ — shadow `54:00`.
- _Nāmābhāsa_ — specifically, the semblance experienced while chanting the holy name `56:00`.

#### Both point to reality without being it [54:00]

- A reflection means the object is somewhere, but it is not in the reflection; a shadow means the object is somewhere, but the shadow is not the object.

#### Pratibimba: impure motive [56:00 – 58:00]

- People motivated primarily by success in _karma_ or _jñāna_ may use _bhakti_ as a tool and practise it intensely; in devotees' company they may chant, dance and seem to experience ecstasy.
- Because the motive is impure, the ecstasy is contaminated and incomplete — a reflection — though the undiscerning may take it for very advanced emotion.
- Example: when Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī came to meet Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, a companion who seemed a great devotee of Rāma — constantly chanting Rāma's names, practising yoga, wanting to carry the luggage — received little regard from Mahāprabhu, because though he chanted Rāma's name, his goal was to become Rāma. Such impersonal aspiration is not pleasing to the Lord `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Chāyā: unpurified but sincere curiosity [58:00]

- One not yet purified, without serious practice, comes among devotees out of social convention or curiosity, starts chanting, dancing and singing — and seems to feel ecstasy.
- Sometimes at a _harināma_ long-time devotees do not feel much ecstasy, while a newcomer's eyes widen and face brightens as if feeling more. That is a shadow — a glimpse, because the potency present in the devotees' association rubs off.

#### From shadow to substance [58:00 – 60:00]

- Neither is the real thing, but both can be precursors to it if they purify the heart.
- The shadow is relatively better, because there is no wrong motive — one is not using _bhakti_ for another purpose; Prabhupāda calls shadow attachment better than reflective attachment because there is less ulterior motive.
- The way to rise from shadow or reflection to the substance is association with devotees: they inject pure desires into our heart, and by those desires pure love awakens.
- Prabhupāda concludes that such attachment, even seen in a common man, can by pure devotees' association bring him to perfection — but it cannot be invoked without being sufficiently blessed by that association `60:00`.
- The next class will discuss _prema_ and conclude the study of The Nectar of Devotion `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Ecstasy with an ulterior motive is only a reflection, and ecstasy caught from others' potency is only a shadow; neither is bhāva, but association with pure devotees can purify both into real love. [54:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

No separate audience Q&A appears in the captions. The teacher raises and answers several questions himself:

- **If bhāva is only a ray compared with the sun of prema, is it a small thing?** No — it is very exalted, lesser only from the perspective of _prema_ `10:00 – 12:00`.
- **Isn't the desire to please Kṛṣṇa present from the beginning?** Yes, but in _bhāva_ realisation is much deeper — one perceives Kṛṣṇa as a person `18:00`.
- **Nārada attained perfection by practice — so why is he an example of mercy too?** The quote illustrates the principle of _vācika_ mercy, not Nārada's own path `30:00`.
- **Are tears a sign of advanced love?** Not necessarily — they can be imitated, and real devotees may hide them; sustained symptoms are the reliable guide `38:00 – 40:00`.
- **Must everyone become a beggar to be prideless?** No — the point is caring only for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure, even at the cost of honour `46:00 – 48:00`.
- **Why do newcomers sometimes seem more ecstatic than long-time devotees?** That is _chāyā_ — a shadow from the potency of the devotees' association, not the real thing `58:00`.
