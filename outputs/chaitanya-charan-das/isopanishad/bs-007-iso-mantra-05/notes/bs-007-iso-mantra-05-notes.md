## Mantra-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Three paradoxes and the plan of the purport [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/5 -->

**Context.** The teacher introduces the fifth mantra, which attributes three pairs of seemingly contradictory qualities to the Absolute Truth, and notes that Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation makes the contradiction even more apparent `00:00`.

#### The three pairs [00:00]

- He walks, and He does not walk.
- He is far away, and He is very near.
- He is within everything, and yet He is outside everything.
- At first glance this appears absurd and contradictory; the class sets out to understand "how this works out."

#### Five parts of the purport [02:00]

1. The Supreme must be inconceivable.
2. How the Absolute Truth is both far and near.
3. In connection with His nearness, the Lord's manifestation as the Deity.
4. The common Māyāvāda notion that the Absolute Truth has no qualities — the meaning of _nirguṇa_.
5. The mantra's last line: the Lord pervades everything and is yet separate from and beyond everything.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The fifth mantra deliberately describes the Lord through opposite-seeming statements; Prabhupāda's purport unpacks them in five steps, from inconceivability to the Lord's simultaneous pervasion and transcendence. [00:00 – 02:00]
</Callout>

### Logic as minister, not master [04:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Following Jīva Gosvāmī in the _Sandarbhas_, the teacher explains that logic cannot fully understand the Absolute Truth — not because the Absolute is illogical, but because it is trans-logical `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Illogical_ — below the level of logic: something so senseless that it cannot stand the scrutiny of logic, e.g. an ordinary person claiming "I am God" or "I am the Queen of England" `04:00`.
- _Trans-logical_ — above the level of logic: that in whose presence logic cannot stand `04:00`.

#### Why the Supreme must be inconceivable [04:00 – 06:00]

- Supreme means superior to our logical faculty.
- If logic could understand and comprehend God completely, God would no longer be supreme — logic would become supreme.
- Do not make logic into a "secular god" — the attitude of people who do not value God and say logic is greater than Him.
- "Let logic be our minister, not master": logic is valuable and useful in many cases, but it is subordinate to God; within its jurisdiction we use it to examine what is what, but there are many places where it does not apply `06:00`.

#### The torchlight and the sun [06:00]

- A torchlight helps us see the way in the dark and is very valuable.
- But if I point it at the night sky to find the sun, I will not find it — the torch leads to futility there.
- The sun is far greater; when it rises, its glory is seen by its own light, without any torch.
- Likewise, God is like the sun and logic like the torchlight; we do not deny logic's role — in darkness, without it we would be completely confused.

#### Logic cannot substitute for experience [08:00 – 12:00]

- The Vedic scriptures also use logic; the whole _Bhagavad-gītā_ and the _Vedānta-sūtra_ are based on logic in many cases `08:00`.
- **Doctor and medicine:** logic helps me judge whether what the doctor says makes sense, but merely understanding how the medicine works will not cure me — I must take the treatment `08:00`.
- Similarly, logic helps us distinguish authentic spirituality from shadow spirituality, but beyond that we must practise devotional life to get the supreme result: divine perception of reality `08:00 – 10:00`.
- **The blind patient:** a doctor may explain the cause of blindness and the cure, but logically understanding the process does not grant vision; the person must undergo the treatment `10:00`.
- We currently lack spiritual vision; rather than rejecting the spiritual as illogical, we should accept it as trans-logical and develop the higher vision by which it is seen `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Absolute is not illogical but trans-logical; logic is a useful torch for discrimination, but the sun of the Supreme is seen only by His own light, through the "treatment" of devotional practice. [04:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Paradox, not contradiction: far and near [12:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** Applying this to "far and near," the teacher distinguishes a contradiction from a paradox `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Contradiction_ — opposite statements that do not make sense together, e.g. "X is fat and X is thin" `12:00`.
- _Paradox_ — opposite-seeming statements that make sense at a deeper level and are meant to grant a deeper understanding `12:00`.
- _Juxtaposition_ — bringing contradictory-seeming words close together, which forces us to think `12:00`.

#### The least corrected papers [12:00 – 14:00]

- "The least corrected papers are the most correct": superficially, "least corrected" and "most correct" do not fit together.
- The higher meaning: the teacher had to correct those papers least because what the student wrote was most correct.
- Similarly, scripture gives contradictory-seeming statements with a deeper meaning that must be understood by proper contemplation — not rejected at a superficial level.

#### Near as Paramātmā, far as Bhagavān [14:00 – 16:00]

- The Absolute Truth manifests as Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān.
- As Paramātmā He is right next to us in the heart — closer than the closest; even someone embracing us tightly cannot be as close as the Paramātmā. The teacher cites Kṛṣṇa's statement in the fifteenth chapter of the Gītā that He is seated in everyone's heart `14:00`.
- As Bhagavān, Kṛṣṇa performs pastimes in the spiritual world and is far away. The teacher cites the _Brahma-saṁhitā_: sages travelling for millions of years at the speed of mind and wind still cannot touch even the front part of the Lord's feet (the toes); Brahmā offers his obeisances to that inconceivable Govinda `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### No hands and legs, yet He comes and accepts [16:00]

- Another scriptural description: the Lord has no hands and no legs, yet when someone offers Him food He immediately comes and accepts it; He has no eyes yet sees, no ears yet hears.
- Resolution: He has no _material_ eyes or ears; He has transcendental eyes and ears.
- What seems a contradiction at the immediate, material level of perception conveys a deeper reality.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Scripture's opposites are paradoxes, not contradictions: the Lord is nearer than any embrace as Paramātmā and beyond the reach of sages as Bhagavān, and "no hands, no eyes" means no material ones. [12:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Deity worship: the elephant sits down [18:00 – 28:00]

**Context.** In discussing how the Lord is near, Prabhupāda brings up Deity worship `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Iconoclast_ — "icon" means symbol; iconoclasts destroy symbols. The term refers to religionists who reject Deity worship and delight in destroying Deities, saying all symbols are false and must be destroyed to focus on the "real God" `18:00`.

#### Who is really limiting God? [18:00 – 20:00]

- Impersonalists and iconoclasts say God is infinite and unlimited, so He cannot appear in a stone form — that would limit Him.
- The teacher's reply: by saying God *cannot* appear in a form, we are limiting Him.
- The Vedic scriptures, especially the _Pāñcarātra_ scriptures, describe that God can appear in form: He is so unlimited that He can appear in a limited-seeming form and still stay unlimited.

#### The elephant and the child [20:00 – 22:00]

- The teacher attributes this example to Piḷḷai Lokācārya, a prominent Śrī Vaiṣṇava _ācārya_ in the line of succession from Rāmānujācārya (the other line being Vedānta Deśika's).
- A small child sees a majestic elephant on the street and wants to touch its head, but no matter how much it stretches — even if it grows up — it can never reach.
- If the elephant sits down, lies down and brings its head close, the child can touch it very happily.
- Similarly, we souls are like the child: with material senses we cannot access the spiritual level of reality. When the Lord descends from the spiritual realm and manifests as the Deity, He becomes accessible — the elephant sitting down.
- The elephant still has its height and power; it has *chosen* to sit down.

#### Mercy over majesty [22:00 – 24:00]

- In the Deity, Kṛṣṇa is not limited; He manifests His unlimitedness differently — not in terms of largeness but in terms of mercy and accessibility.
- God does not delight in exhibiting His greatness so much that He remains inaccessible; His primary concern is to make Himself accessible.
- Prabhupāda explains that matter and spirit are both God's energies, so He can convert matter into spirit: the Deity may be made of material elements, but by Kṛṣṇa's presence it becomes spiritualised and is no longer material.

#### "The Deity can't even move a fly" [24:00 – 26:00]

- Some object: the Deity cannot move, cannot talk, cannot even move a fly from its head — how can it be God?
- The Deity has not manifested to show omnipotence or majesty; it has manifested to show accessibility, and we must keep that purpose in mind.
- A devotee who sees a fly on the Deity thinks: "I am not serving properly; I am negligent; I must be more attentive and keep everything so clean that no flies come."
- The Deity gives an opportunity for service. Whenever the Lord manifests through matter, that manifestation generally follows the laws of material nature.

#### The scripture that burns [24:00 – 26:00]

- Some people from other religions desecrate Deities and ask, "Where is God?" — yet they consider their own holy book sacred.
- If someone burns that book, it burns; its sacredness is not demonstrated by being fireproof.
- A scripture's sacredness is understood through its wisdom — when one studies it, understands it, applies it and experiences the transformation.
- In scripture Kṛṣṇa gives accessibility through wisdom; in the Deity He gives accessibility through form — so that we can serve Him.

#### Omnipotence is shown through death [26:00 – 28:00]

- Every manifestation of the Lord has a particular purpose; He manifests His omnipotence in this world as time, in the form of death.
- However defiant, arrogant, powerful or autocratic a person may be, when Kṛṣṇa comes as death no one can do anything.
- The teacher recounts that a famous dictator, while dying, lifted his fist towards the sky in defiance — and nothing happened; defiance will not save us at death.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Deity is the elephant sitting down: the unlimited Lord chooses a limited-seeming form not to display power — which He shows through death — but to become accessible so that we can serve Him. [18:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Three levels of seeing the Deity [28:00 – 44:00]

**Context.** A Deity differs from a statue first because it is made according to scriptural description; the teacher then presents Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's three levels of vision of the Deity `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jaḍa_ — seeing the Deity as just a statue `28:00`.
- _Manomaya_ — seeing the Deity as a symbolic, mind-made representation of the Absolute Truth `28:00`.
- _Cinmaya_ — seeing the Deity as spiritual and non-different from the Lord `40:00`.
- _Iṣṭa-devatā_ — the cherished form of the Lord most dear to us, with whom we have an eternal relationship `30:00 – 32:00`.
- _Prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā_ — the ceremony in which devotees invite the Lord to manifest as the Deity `40:00`.

#### Jaḍa: just a statue [28:00]

- People make statues of politicians or film stars; the statue has nothing to do with the person — if the person is dead, the soul has gone to another body according to _karma_.
- At this level one thinks the Deity is just stone in a shape.

#### Manomaya: the flag [28:00 – 32:00]

- At this level the Deity is a symbol: God is ultimately formless, but one ascribes a form because one cannot feel emotion for, or connect with, something formless.
- **The flag:** looking around my country I may not feel patriotic, but when I see the tricolour, salute it and hear the national anthem, patriotic feelings well up. The flag is a concrete symbol of something abstract — nationhood.
- So at this level the Deity is like a flag meant to evoke sublime devotional feeling. The teacher says the idea underlying this is wrong.
- Impersonalist conclusion: since the Absolute is formless and the form only evokes devotion, people can make or choose their own forms.

#### Iṣṭa-devatā, rightly and wrongly understood [30:00 – 34:00]

- The concept of _iṣṭa-devatā_ is valid in Vedic scripture: there are many forms of the Lord — Viṣṇu, Kṛṣṇa, Rāma, Nṛsiṁha; Vṛndāvana Kṛṣṇa, Dvārakā Kṛṣṇa — and one focuses on worshipping the form most dear, with whom one has an eternal relationship.
- Distorted version: the _iṣṭa-devatā_ is just a tool, to be worshipped until one goes beyond form to "light." Some then say, "I'll worship my guru," and ultimately "I'll worship myself."
- This reduces religion to absurdity — like the T-shirt slogan "I was an atheist until I discovered I was God." The teacher calls it a caricature and mockery of impersonalism, to be discussed in detail in future classes.
- Some spiritualists teach: meditate on a Deity until, by the power of concentration, it crumbles into powder — proof of going "beyond the form to the light." The teacher calls this a monstrous idea; unfortunately some actually beat a Deity into powder and claim enlightenment `34:00`.

#### Ascending symbolism: no intrinsic connection [34:00 – 36:00]

- Another manomaya view: the Absolute may have a form, but the Deity is just a medium with no intrinsic connection to it.
- **Changing the flag:** if the Indian government decided agriculture was no longer important and removed the green colour, Parliament could pass a resolution and the tricolour could become a bicolour — the connection between nation and flag was only in our conceptualisation.
- This is called _ascending symbolism_: from a concrete symbol, by imagination, one moves upwards to the higher reality, though there is no intrinsic connection.
- The teacher cites a Vedic statement that there is no such representation of the Absolute — we cannot ascribe forms to Him from our own imagination, which keeps changing.

#### Descending symbolism: the photo in the wallet [36:00 – 40:00]

- A man carries his wife's photo in his wallet. One day his wife finds another woman's picture there; he says, "I'm thinking of you only — I just decided to change the picture." She replies, "Then I'll change my husband."
- A photo is directly connected to the person, because the person has an original form that the photo depicts — this is _descending symbolism_: the representation is based on an original reality.
- Kṛṣṇa has an original form in the spiritual world, and that form is depicted through the Deity — not a mental concoction.
- Seeing the Deity as a "photo of God" is better than calling God formless, but it is not complete: a photo and a person are separate, whereas Kṛṣṇa is all-pervading and the Supreme Lord, so He can manifest wherever He wants.

#### Cinmaya: the Deity is non-different [38:00 – 44:00]

- When the Deity is made according to scriptural description and the devotees pray, "Please manifest so that we can serve You," and perform _prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā_, the Lord personally appears and resides in the Deity, which is then non-different from Him `38:00 – 40:00`.
- At the _cinmaya_ level of consciousness one understands this; the Absolute Truth has chosen to become accessible `40:00`.
- The teacher compares this with the holy name: like the name, the Lord cannot be understood with gross senses; He reveals Himself to one who serves, beginning with the tongue — chanting the holy name `40:00 – 42:00`.
- Practice: approach the Deity with reverence and faith based on philosophical understanding — Kṛṣṇa is all-pervading, omnipotent and, most importantly, merciful and wanting to be accessible `42:00`:
  - with our eyes we take _darśana_;
  - with our hands we do worship;
  - with our tongue we glorify the Lord.
- Pleased, the Lord elevates us to spiritual consciousness and reveals Himself; Prabhupāda said that when you become qualified, this very Deity will talk to you. Until then we strive for the qualification by practice `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Deity is not a statue (jaḍa) or an imagined symbol like a flag (manomaya) but the Lord Himself, invited through prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā (cinmaya); approached with faith and served with eyes, hands and tongue, He reveals Himself. [28:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Nirguṇa and saguṇa [42:00 – 52:00]

**Context.** After recapping — far as Bhagavān, close as the Deity — the teacher follows Prabhupāda's citation of the Gītā (4.11): for the surrendered soul the Lord is always within reach, for the unsurrendered He is far away; Kṛṣṇa is revealed according to our attitude, and He is not manifest to everyone, being covered by _yogamāyā_ `42:00 – 44:00`. Prabhupāda then discusses _saguṇa_ and _nirguṇa_ `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Guṇa_ — can mean "quality" or "mode"; there are three modes of material nature: ignorance, passion and goodness `44:00`.
- _Nirguṇa_ — impersonalists take it as "without qualities"; it can also mean "beyond the modes," unaffected by them `44:00 – 46:00`.
- _Saguṇa_ — taken as "with qualities" `44:00`.

#### No qualities means nothing [46:00]

- The teacher refers to Jīva Gosvāmī's discussion in the _Sandarbhas_ (and a reference to the _Govinda-bhāṣya_).
- Whenever we describe the Absolute Truth at all, we ascribe qualities: even impersonalist descriptions — pure consciousness, unlimited, all-pervading, full of bliss — are qualities.
- If the Absolute had absolutely no qualities, nothing would be left; it would be reduced to an absolute nothing, a zero.
- So _nirguṇa_ understood as "beyond the modes" is more logical, and also more scriptural.

#### Gajendra's prayer [48:00 – 52:00]

- Background: Gajendra, in danger and caught by a crocodile, prays to the Supreme Lord.
- In his prayer he says the Lord has no birth, activities, name, form or qualities; impersonalists read this as "the Lord has no form or qualities."
- Yet in response, a person comes — with form, qualities and activities — rescues Gajendra, who goes back to the spiritual world. Did Gajendra pray to one person and someone else come? No.
- Resolution: the prayer denies the material qualities and forms others in the world have; the Lord has transcendental qualities.
- The same prayer offers obeisances to the Lord as the supreme controller (the teacher notes the same word _īśa_ as in the Īśopaniṣad's opening), the Absolute with unlimited energy, of amazing activities — and as _arūpa_ and _uru-rūpa_ at once `50:00`:
  - _arūpa_ — no material form;
  - _uru-rūpa_ — many, many spiritual forms.
- Conclusion: _nirguṇa_ refers to the modes; on the transcendental level the Absolute Truth has qualities and form — a living, loving, all-attractive person filled with qualities, with the most attractive form `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Nirguṇa means beyond the material modes, not without qualities — a quality-less Absolute would be a zero; Gajendra's prayer shows the Lord as formless materially yet possessing many spiritual forms. [44:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Within everything, outside everything [52:00 – 58:00]

**Context.** The last line of the mantra: the Lord is inside everything and outside everything `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pervade_ — to be inside this world, present everywhere `52:00`.
- _Transcend_ — to be beyond this world; His existence is not limited to it `52:00 – 54:00`.
- _Pantheism_ — everything is God: the world is God, the table is God `56:00`.
- _Panentheism_ — everything is sacred because God pervades everything, but God also exists beyond everything `56:00`.

#### Prahlāda and the pillar — pervasion [52:00 – 54:00]

- Prahlāda says the Lord is everywhere, even in the pillar; Hiraṇyakaśipu strikes it and Nṛsiṁhadeva emerges with a frightening sound.
- After Hiraṇyakaśipu is killed and the demigods are victorious, Prahlāda does not offer prayers to the pillar. He respects the pillar because the Lord was present there, but he understands the Lord is different from it, and offers prayers to the Lord seated on the throne.

#### Yaśodā sees the universe — transcendence [54:00]

- When Kṛṣṇa, accused of eating dirt, opens His mouth, Yaśodā sees the whole universe — including Vṛndāvana and herself — inside Him.
- She was with Kṛṣṇa within the universe, yet the whole universe was within Him: He is separate from and outside the universe.

#### Surrender to the source [54:00 – 56:00]

- The teacher cites the Gītā: after many lifetimes the wise surrender to Vāsudeva, understanding Him to be everything.
- They do not surrender to "everything"; they surrender to Vāsudeva, because He is the source of everything and beyond everything.

#### Panentheism, not pantheism [56:00]

- Prabhupāda states there is nothing but God within and without, but in different energies, like light emanating from a fire; there is oneness among His diverse energies.
- Yet the Lord in His personal form still enjoys unlimitedly, whereas the tiny living entities enjoy minutely — this will be discussed later.
- Kṛṣṇa pervades everything but still has His own life beyond this world, in the spiritual world.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord pervades everything — so all is sacred — yet transcends everything and has His own life in the spiritual world; like Prahlāda, we respect His presence everywhere but offer our prayers to Him personally. [52:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
