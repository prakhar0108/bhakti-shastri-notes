### Vidyā and avidyā: why context decides meaning [00:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/9 -->

**Context.** The words _vidyā_ and _avidyā_ appear across Mantras 9–11, and a similar pair of words appears in the next three mantras, but their meanings differ from mantra to mantra `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Avidyā_ in Mantra 9 — ignorance, the ignorant pursuit of sense gratification `02:00`.
- _Vidyā_ in Mantra 9 — not real knowledge but so-called knowledge: knowledge that leads people into even greater sinful activities `02:00`.
- _Avidyā_ in Mantras 10–11 — knowledge for body maintenance: maintaining basic family stability and social harmony so that we can live in this world harmoniously, without too much disruption `04:00 – 06:00`.
- _Vidyā_ in Mantras 10–11 — knowledge for the pursuit of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, by which the eternal destination is attained `06:00`.

#### Both are negative in Mantra 9 [06:00]

- In Mantra 9, _vidyā_ and _avidyā_ are both basically negative — both are pursuits related to the senses.
- In Mantras 10–11 their meanings change; the teacher notes that the comparison may seem a little confusing now but will become clearer as the mantras are studied.

#### Words need the whole picture [06:00 – 08:00]

- We cannot understand scripture just by knowing the words of the text; text alone is not enough for comprehension.
- A dictionary gives a basic understanding, but complete understanding needs a broader understanding of the context.
- The Upaniṣads especially need careful study: their words have multiple meanings in different contexts, which makes them more difficult than other books in the Vedic library.
- The Upaniṣads are meant for the _jñānīs_, and this kind of analysis is what _jñānīs_ like to do `08:00`.

#### Three categories of "knowledgeable" people [10:00]

- The purport describes people who have knowledge, but whose knowledge does not take them to the real destination. The teacher groups them into three broad categories, which structure the rest of the class:
  1. Godless materialists.
  2. Religious materialists.
  3. Impersonalists — especially the _Māyāvādīs_, who consider themselves to be God.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Vidyā and avidyā change meaning from mantra to mantra; in Mantra 9 both are sense-oriented pursuits, and only the whole context — not a dictionary — reveals what scripture means. [00:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Godless education: comfort without happiness [10:00 – 16:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda writes that modern civilisation has advanced considerably in mass education, yet people are more unhappy than ever before, because of the stress placed on material advancement to the exclusion of the most important part of life, the spiritual aspect `10:00 – 12:00`.

#### More unhappy than ever [12:00 – 14:00]

- Compared to the past, stress, depression, addiction and suicide have all increased; the teacher notes a declaration that mental illness and mental health would become the greatest problem.
- Happiness cannot be quantified, but statistics of well-being compared with a few decades or a century ago show these four rising.
- People are more comfortable, but not happier: someone may have a flashy office, yet inside it the officer is stressed by tension.
- The teacher mentions that he discusses such statistics in his own books `14:00`.

#### Not against material progress [14:00]

- The point is not specifically against material progress; the problem is that material power has outrun spiritual power.
- "We have controlled machines and uncontrolled minds" — the tragedy of modern society.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Education that excludes the spiritual aspect makes people more comfortable but not happier; when material power outruns spiritual power, we end up with controlled machines and uncontrolled minds. [12:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### The worship of avidyā [16:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** The mantra speaks of those who worship _avidyā_. The teacher explains what such "worship" actually is `16:00`.

#### Forgetting the controller [16:00]

- The practical implication of knowing God as the controller is to take only what is meant for us, nothing beyond that.
- When I forget that God is the controller, the idea becomes: "whatever I can grab and get my hands on, I can enjoy."
- The immediate result of rejecting God is the pursuit of enjoyment; Kṛṣṇa also speaks about this mentality.

#### What "worshipping avidyā" means [16:00 – 18:00]

- We don't have to make a deity of _avidyā_ and start offering _ārati_ and fifty-six offerings to it.
- _Avidyā_ means forgetfulness of God; worshipping _avidyā_ means that, having forgotten God, we pursue sense gratification and take sense perception itself as the ultimate reality.

#### Secular gods [18:00 – 22:00]

- The teacher recounts a story: a famous music icon stayed in a hotel and used a towel in the washroom; the hotel owner auctioned it in sixteen pieces, and people bought each piece at a huge price.
- Just as devotees consider the small cloth that has adorned the Deity sacred, here people consider a cloth used once in a washroom almost sacred.
- People make sense gratifiers into secular gods and start worshipping them.
- But these are temporary gods: when the object of adulation can no longer provide, someone else is brought in and given a temporary "worship certificate."
- Worship meant to be directed toward God is directed toward people — the Īśopaniṣad calls this _avidyā_, the ignorance by which movie stars are put on a pedestal as gods. The teacher calls it a tragic misuse of human intelligence `20:00`.

#### Imitation and absorption [22:00]

- People try to become like their idols: if a film star has a particular hairstyle, everyone wants it.
- Thinking constantly about movie stars keeps people on the materialistic level, because that is the current flowing through such adulation.

#### Darkness [22:00]

- The mantra's word for darkness refers to the darkness of material existence.
- The spiritual world is beyond this darkness, full of life; we are meant to come from darkness toward the light, but such people stay in the darkness of material existence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Worshipping avidyā means forgetting God and treating sense pleasure as ultimate — which turns celebrities into temporary secular gods and keeps their worshippers in the darkness of material existence. [16:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### "I don't know the way, but I'll show you" [22:00 – 32:00]

**Context.** Having described the worshippers of _avidyā_, the teacher turns to what is so bad about the so-called _vidyā_ of modern education `22:00`.

#### Worse than ignorance [24:00]

- Not knowing is bad, but claiming to be in knowledge is worse.
- If I don't know the way, yet I think I know, and when someone asks me I say, "Come with me, I'll show you" — that is the atheistic scientist's position toward God.

#### The designer behind the mechanism [24:00]

- Looking at the world, we see such artistry and harmony that it cannot have come about by chance.
- There may be a mechanism by which things happen, but the mechanism itself is not automatic — there is a designer behind it.
- To drive God out of the world, focus only on studying and improving it, and claim "you don't need God" is like saying, "I don't know the way, but I'll show you the way."

#### Condemning those on the right way [26:00]

- Not only am I going the wrong way and claiming it is right — when someone is on the right way, I say they are wrong, condemn them, and try to drive them onto the wrong way.
- Today, people who live with faith in God are often labelled "right-wing" religionists, and many believe the solution to the world's problems is to keep people away from religion.
- Such godless education keeps people in great ignorance and away from God — the misfortune of today's educational advancement.

#### Power without morality [26:00 – 30:00]

- The result is a loss of moral values: people have a lot of technological power but very little moral power and willpower.
- Knowledge and technology joined with sense-gratification education make people more and more greedy.
- Children today often have unfiltered access to content on the internet `28:00`.

#### The "better idiot box" [30:00]

- The teacher recalls studying electronics engineering to understand how electronics works; in the fourth year, studying applications such as television and entertainment, he asked whether he really wanted to spend his hard work just to entertain people and make a better "idiot box."
- Technology not integrated into a God-centred worldview that promotes holistic living gives an addicted life.
- Like a vacuum cleaner, technology can suck up a person's mind; hyper-stimulated by multiple varieties of sense gratification, people can hardly respond to anything else.
- This, the teacher says, is the result of this so-called _vidyā_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Claiming to know the way while denying God is worse than plain ignorance; godless knowledge gives technological power without moral power, and technology outside a God-centred worldview produces addiction. [24:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Religious materialists: religion with a materialistic face [32:00 – 40:00]

**Context.** The second category is those who study or practise the Vedas but stop at their material side `32:00`.

#### Mistaking the side issue for the goal [32:00 – 34:00]

- The Vedas do speak of material sense gratification for those who want it, but that is only the beginning topic; afterwards they take people to higher spiritual knowledge.
- Some people mistake this side issue in the Vedas for the ultimate purpose.
- They reduce religion to just another form of materialism — "materialism with a good face on it."
- Having no conception of anything beyond matter, they cannot achieve anything beyond matter.

#### Kṛṣṇa's analysis in the Gītā [34:00]

- Kṛṣṇa first describes this mentality in Gītā 2.42–43 — a lack of discrimination between matter and spirit.
- In Gītā 9.20–21, Kṛṣṇa explains that such people may reach the heavenly planets and prosperity, but that prosperity is not eternal, because nothing in this world is eternal.
- The human form can be used to attain something eternal; using it to attain something temporary in the name of religion deprives one of the gift of religion.

#### Examples [34:00 – 36:00]

- Many religionists today give only lip service, and worship for worldly things.
- The teacher describes a temple in India (he mentions Chennai) associated with visas: many Indians think the goal of life is to go to America, the biggest obstacle is the visa department, so they pray to the remover of obstacles — "I will do whatever pleases You, please arrange a visa for me" — and long queues form there.
- Other-worldly enjoyment can be sought in the same spirit: the teacher cites suicide bombers who act on one twisted interpretation of the Quran, having been told they will be rewarded with heavenly enjoyment for dying in jihad.
- Some say they want to study the Vedas not to know who God is, but only to learn how to do material things better, and want to separate the material knowledge in the Vedas from the spiritual `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### The Gītā's parallel [38:00 – 40:00]

- In Gītā 2.42 Kṛṣṇa speaks of people absorbed in Vedic rituals meant for material enjoyment; the teacher points out that a common word links Gītā 2.42 and this mantra `38:00`.
- He also refers to people engaged in rituals who rejected Kṛṣṇa's own request `38:00`.
- _Vāda_ means not just one argument but a school of thought — a system that makes a proposition, advances arguments and establishes itself `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Vedas begin with material rewards only to lead people higher; religious materialists stop at that side issue, turning religion into materialism with a good face and trading the eternal for the temporary. [32:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Impersonalists: rejecting the real as counterfeit [40:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** The third category is the impersonalists, whose ultimate focus is Brahman and who do not know about Bhagavān `40:00`.

#### Fortunate and unfortunate impersonalists [40:00 – 42:00]

- Some fortunate impersonalists become attracted to Bhagavān — as happened to the Kumāras and Śukadeva Gosvāmī when they came in contact with the Lord.
- The unfortunate ones say the Lord's form and pastimes are _māyā_: this world is a temporary manifestation of Brahman, Brahman is the ultimate reality, and from formless Brahman a form sometimes manifests, which is Bhagavān — so they imagine one must go beyond Bhagavān as well `42:00`.

#### The unique gift of Vedic literature [44:00 – 46:00]

- This is a fundamental misunderstanding about the nature of God, not just another philosophy.
- Outside the Vedic literature there is very little positive description of God — what He looks like, what His personal pastimes are.
- God's personal descent and pastimes are the unique contribution of the Vedic literature, and impersonalism undermines exactly this; the teacher adds that impostors also misuse it `46:00`.

#### The counterfeit-note analogy [48:00]

- A person who has been cheated by counterfeit notes may reject even a real note when he gets one.
- Impersonalists have seen worldly activities lead to bondage, so they think the Lord's pastimes are also mundane and must be rejected and gone beyond.
- But the Lord's pastimes are the revelation by which He becomes wonderfully attractive to us; considering them _māyā_ means mistaking the real for the counterfeit.

#### Why is Kṛṣṇa black? [48:00 – 52:00]

- The teacher writes a spiritual column in the _Times of India_; once the editor asked him to write about why Kṛṣṇa is black — "don't tell the usual stuff, tell something new" — so he inquired into the different theories.
- Some intellectuals say one has to go beyond Kṛṣṇa's black form to white formless light, i.e., Brahman: the form is temporary, the formless light eternal. Many people are impressed by this, but the teacher calls it nonsense.
- Kṛṣṇa's beauty is not limited by normal conventions of beauty; He is supremely beautiful `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### The danger of this view [52:00]

- Rejecting the Lord's pastimes as illusion is a great danger.
- If the Lord's form is just a temporary form taken by formless light, then I too am such a form — and with a little worldly success I can claim to be God and have people worship me; such a person is overwhelmed by ignorance.

#### The right vision [54:00]

- We must remember that Kṛṣṇa's form is eternal and His pastimes are also eternal; by taking shelter of His pastimes we become liberated from material existence.
- This is the vision needed when hearing Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, as in Gītā 4.9 — knowing them in truth, not superficially for entertainment value.
- The teacher notes that how God can descend will be discussed further later.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Impersonalists reject the Lord's form and pastimes as māyā, like rejecting a real note after being cheated by counterfeits; Kṛṣṇa's form and pastimes are eternal, and knowing them in truth liberates. [40:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Summary and what comes next [56:00 – 58:18]

**Context.** The teacher sums up the class `56:00`.

#### The three categories in review [56:00]

- _Vidyā_ and _avidyā_ mean different things in different contexts; in Mantra 9, people who have knowledge are still in ignorance. The three categories:
  1. **Godless education** — knowledge that keeps people in sense gratification and lets them pursue whatever lifestyle they like.
  2. **Religious materialists** — religion used for material ends.
  3. **Impersonalists, especially the _Māyāvādīs_** — spiritual in that they identify themselves with Brahman, but they come back down to the material level, and the material energy's attack can cause them suffering too.
- All three have been given intelligence but misuse it, and because of that enter greater darkness.

#### Next class [58:00]

- The next mantra will discuss how _vidyā_ and _avidyā_ lead to different results.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
