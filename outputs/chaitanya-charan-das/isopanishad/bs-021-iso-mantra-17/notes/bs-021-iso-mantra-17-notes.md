## Mantra-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Mantra 17: a prayer at the moment of death [00:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/17 -->

**Context.** The speaker asks that the body be let go and burnt, that the life-air merge with the total air, and that the Lord remember all that he has done for Him `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Krato_ — addressed to the Lord as the enjoyer of sacrifices, the supreme beneficiary of all that is done, as Kṛṣṇa is described in the Bhagavad-gītā `00:00`.
- _Kṛtam_ — what has been done by me; the prayer asks the Lord to remember it `00:00`.

#### Two distinct thoughts [02:00 – 04:00]

- **Gravity of death:** religion and spirituality are not casual pursuits or "social cosmetics" for looking good in public; they are of supreme importance in determining our post-mortem destiny, decided by how we related with God in this life.
- **Not the pure devotional level:** the mood here is "O Lord, I want to come back to You; please remember what I have done for You." At the pure devotional level one simply calls out, "Please let me serve You," as in the prayers of Vṛtrāsura and Queen Kuntī (who even prayed for dangers).

#### Don't look down on the prayer [04:00]

- That a person is so soberly preparing for death is itself instructive: we rarely think about death seriously.
- When we see death in the news, or more consequentially in our family, we think about it for a while — then the wheels of illusion turn and we forget our mortality.
- Recognising that what happens after death is in the hands of the Supreme Being naturally leads to prayer: "Have mercy on me."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even if Mantra 17 is not a prayer of pure devotion, its sober preparation for death teaches us to take death — and therefore our relationship with God — with the seriousness it deserves. [00:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### The purport's map and three attitudes to the body [04:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** The teacher outlines the purport's points, then expands on the first: the body is a foreign dress that must be given up `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### Points of the purport [04:00 – 06:00]

1. The material body is a foreign dress that must be given up.
2. Everyone gives up the body at death, but the best way is by surrender to the Lord, not by going to the _brahmajyoti_ — the _jñānī_ goes to the _brahmajyoti_; the _bhakta_ alone returns to Bhagavān.
3. The last thought at death is decisively important.
4. Kṛṣṇa never forgets our service, so we should always serve Him; His mercy is ever available through devotional service.
5. Prabhupāda connects the mantra with the _Bhāgavatam_ and its model devotees.
6. Prepare for death by devotional practice — practise in life to attain perfection at the end.

#### Karma: romanticising the body [06:00 – 10:00]

- On the path of material enjoyment people dream about their own good looks, fantasise about others' bodies and imagine endless pleasure — many think this is the very purpose of existence.
- Kṛṣṇa describes this mentality among the demoniac in Gītā chapter 16: they are absolutely certain that sense gratification is the supreme goal of life.
- **The sponge, not the well:** the body's pleasure capacity is not a well with unending reserves; it is like a sponge filled with water — a little touch and water comes, then one must squeeze harder, and finally only the pain of squeezing remains.
- **Road becomes roadblock:** a fallen tree on a road can be removed, but here the road itself turns into the roadblock — the body's capacity to enjoy is permanently and irreversibly limited.

#### Jñāna: demonising the body [10:00 – 14:00]

- The philosophically minded see the body as a trap whose senses drag us down to sin and suffering, make us forget our spiritual identity, and fill us with desires that perpetuate illusion.
- **Blind about our blindness:** a blind person who knows it walks carefully with a stick or asks for help; one unaware of being blind walks into a wall and breaks his nose. The body makes us forget our identity — and forget that we have forgotten.
- A Vaiṣṇava song calls the body a net of ignorance — not just ignorance, but a net that traps us deeper the more we struggle.
- In the _Bhāgavatam_, Purūravā, after being left by Urvaśī, comes to his senses and sings a song of lamentation describing the body as flesh, bone, blood and skin covering stool and urine: if I enjoy such a body, what is the difference between me and a worm enjoying stool? This is extreme rejection of the body `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Bhakti: utilising the body [14:00 – 18:00]

- Since we have the body, make the best use of it in Kṛṣṇa's service.
- Mahārāja Ambarīṣa engaged all his senses: mind in thinking of the Lord, tongue in glorifying Him, hands in cleaning the temple, feet in walking to holy places, head in bowing down.
- Prabhupāda wrote to a disciple who neglected his health that if he became very unhealthy he would not be able to serve Kṛṣṇa in future.
- We can serve with the mind alone, but so many exciting and fulfilling services require the body.

#### The pendulum [16:00]

- Romanticising and demonising are the two extremes of a pendulum; utilising is the normal point.
- Because most of us default to romanticising, scripture contains statements that forcefully push the pendulum to the other extreme — this is why devotee-_ācāryas_ also make statements that seem to demonise the body.
- The steady state is to utilise: we don't love the body, but we take care of whatever Kṛṣṇa has given for His service, as we would a computer or a vehicle.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Karma romanticises the body and jñāna demonises it; bhakti, the steady point of the pendulum, utilises and cares for it as Kṛṣṇa's instrument — while remembering it is a foreign dress. [04:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### The body as a foreign dress [16:00 – 22:00]

**Context.** "Foreign dress" means the body is different from me and alien to my nature `16:00`.

#### Sat-cit-ānanda comes from the soul [18:00 – 20:00]

- _Sat_ — all living beings try everything to keep living; this desire comes not from the body but from the soul.
- _Cit_ — everyone wants to know: some gossip about the neighbourhood, some read world news, some research the far reaches of the galaxy; experiments show even monkeys in a box will risk climbing a wall out of curiosity. Matter has no urge to know.
- _Ānanda_ — we all want happiness, but matter is dead: a _gulāb jāmun_ on the tongue doesn't think "I am delicious"; we experience its sweetness. It does not even become sweet by itself — we cook and sweeten it.
- Comparing the body's nature with the soul's shows that the body is a foreign dress to be given up `20:00`.

#### Dressed for and by our desires [20:00 – 22:00]

- **Dressed by** our desires: whatever desires we have determine the kind of body we get next — one who wants to eat meat gets an animal body.
- **Dressed for** our desires: as people are dressed for a party, sports, swimming or a hike; a lion or tiger is a soul that wanted to eat meat and is now dressed for that desire.
- Periodic contemplation of such sections pulls the mind back from the romanticised notion of the body that our culture constantly glamorises `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The urges to live, to know and to enjoy belong to the soul, not to dead matter; the body is only a dress, shaped by and for our desires. [16:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### The art of leaving the body [22:00 – 26:00]

**Context.** Utilising the body is the practice; ultimately we must leave it — and there is an art to doing so `22:00`.

#### The body gives us up; we don't give it up [22:00 – 24:00]

- By the force of time the body will give us up, but psychologically and emotionally we will not give it up — the attachments remain, so we get another body.
- Śaṅkarācārya describes an old man whose limbs are weak, hair grey, teeth gone, who walks with a stick — yet still does not give up the bundle of desire.
- The specific body goes, but the "field" (_kṣetra_) through which we enjoy the world does not leave us as long as the desire to enjoy remains.

#### Purifying the subtle body [24:00]

- The whole process of spiritual life is purifying the subtle body of material desires.
- When it no longer contains the desire to enjoy, then when the physical body falls the subtle body does not carry the soul to another body; it merges with the subtle elements in the coverings of the universe, and the soul returns to the spiritual world.
- Prabhupāda explains that the impersonalist path is difficult and unstable, with no certainty of the eternal goal; _bhakti_ is the preferred method.

#### Only in the human form [24:00 – 26:00]

- All living beings die by nature's course, but only humans have the intelligence to pursue a reality higher than bodily demands — a cat seeing a rat does not observe Ekādaśī.
- The soul passes through various species and comes to the human form, the opportunity to be freed from bodily attachment.
- Prabhupāda supports the idea of the soul's evolution from lower to higher species — spiritual evolution — not the scientific idea that body forms themselves change; after the deluge at the end of a _manvantara_ the universe is covered with water, souls first take aquatic forms, and gradually other forms appear `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Death separates us from the body, but only purification of the subtle body's desires frees us from embodiment — and that art can be practised only in the human form, best through bhakti. [22:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### The last thought and pseudo-shelters [26:00 – 32:00]

**Context.** At death, when coherent thought is difficult, the mind subconsciously goes to whatever we took shelter of most during life `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Examples [26:00 – 30:00]

- The cricket fan: whenever troubled, he turned on a match — his favourite moments, the match-winning six, India lifting the World Cup. That greatest relief will surface at death.
- The recovering alcoholic: the physical urge fades, but whenever reversal, depression or frustration comes, the mind's default says no problem is so big that alcohol or one shot of a drug can't solve it — hence the chance of relapse.
- We all have our pseudo-shelters; even as devotees we keep taking shelter of them. Kṛṣṇa is the true shelter.
- If we train ourselves, when troubled by life's inevitable problems, to consciously seek Kṛṣṇa's shelter instead, the mind will gravitate to Him at death too.

#### Watching the mind [30:00]

- When we leave the mind idle, it immediately goes wherever it thinks pleasure is available — revealing our attachments.
- We needn't sit idle to observe it — the mind is so seductive it will make us the actor, not the observer; we can see it going in wrong directions anyway. The point is to consciously retrain it toward Kṛṣṇa.

#### Not a magical last-moment trick [30:00 – 32:00]

- Some ask: "If I just remember Kṛṣṇa at death, can I enjoy all my life and then put a pistol to my head saying Hare Kṛṣṇa?" That would send one not "back to Godhead" but "back to ghost-head" — suicide leads to that destination.
- Kṛṣṇa's statement about the end of life speaks of remembering (_smaraṇa_) Him, not merely uttering His name; one attached to the body all life will not truly remember Kṛṣṇa at death. We cannot cheat our way back.
- Death may come by heart attack or in sleep; the only way is to practise _bhakti_ and surrender our life to Kṛṣṇa throughout `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
At death the mind runs to whatever it habitually sheltered in; only a life of consciously taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa — not a last-minute trick — makes remembering Him at the end natural. [26:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Kṛṣṇa never forgets our service [32:00 – 40:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda uses the mantra's prayer to show that Kṛṣṇa never forgets the service we do for Him `32:00`, `38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Animiṣa_ — one who never blinks `34:00`.
- _Nimiṣa_ — one who blinks, closes His eyes `34:00`.

#### The paradoxical names [32:00 – 34:00]

- A commentary on the Viṣṇu-sahasra-nāma explains the meaning of each name, including two contradictory ones: _animiṣa_ and _nimiṣa_.
- When a devotee renders service, the Lord keeps His eyes unblinking so as to notice it; when a devotee commits an accidental mistake, the Lord closes His eyes and overlooks it.

#### Not to catch but to coach [34:00 – 36:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's inner presence is not to catch us doing wrong but to coach us.
- A cricket coach notices a batsman moving his legs too fast or too slow, but his purpose is not fault-finding; it is to bring out the athlete's potential and remove whatever faults block it.

#### Draupadī's cloth [36:00]

- Kṛṣṇa, peeling fruit Draupadī had left with a knife, cut His finger; without a second thought she tore off a piece of cloth to bind it.
- Kṛṣṇa's body is transcendental — nothing can harm Him — but for the sake of _līlā_, to let devotees care for Him and reciprocate, He manifests whatever is required, even blood; it is not material blood.

#### Pūtanā's good intention seen, bad overlooked [38:00]

- Pūtanā came to poison and kill Kṛṣṇa, yet came as a nurse; in a previous life, as Bali Mahārāja's sister, she had felt maternal affection when Vāmanadeva came, which changed when He took a giant form.
- Kṛṣṇa closed His eyes — focusing only on her maternal feeling and overlooking her malevolence — purified her and let her become a nurse.

#### Kṛṣṇa is always watching our service [38:00 – 40:00]

- Even if no one notices — other devotees, our spiritual master, our counsellor — Kṛṣṇa notices and will reward every single service.
- We serve not for reward but out of love, yet we want reciprocation; we may not always get it from others, but Kṛṣṇa never fails.
- Kṛṣṇa is so conscientious that, far from forgetting our service, He never forgets us — even when we completely forget Him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa watches not to catch us but to coach us — unblinking toward our service, closing His eyes to our mistakes — so no service done for Him is ever lost. [32:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Ever waiting, ever willing, ever working [40:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** The teacher sums up Kṛṣṇa's love for the soul in three phrases `40:00`.

#### Ever waiting [40:00]

- We get irritated waiting a few hours for someone; a coach doing a favour for a student who keeps him waiting says, "Get lost."
- Kṛṣṇa comes for us and waits in our heart as Paramātmā life after life — not because He needs us; we need Him. We think we'll be happy without Him but suffer.
- He never says, "You neglected Me so long, I won't care for you."

#### Ever willing [40:00 – 42:00]

- Friends after a tiff may refuse to reconcile; Kṛṣṇa is not like that.
- We not only turn away from Him but turn against Him — blaming Him, becoming atheistic or offensive — yet no sin or offence can stop Kṛṣṇa from loving us.
- Nothing I do can stop the sun from giving light; I can only close my eyes and not see it.

#### Ever working [42:00 – 46:00]

- Gajendra, caught in the crocodile's jaws, prays: I surrender; please free me from this animal noose — meaning both the crocodile and the bodily concept.
- Why should the Lord free him? Because He is liberated and therefore can liberate, and because He is abundantly merciful.
- And if the Lord replies that He tried many times before? Gajendra calls Him one who never tires. The teacher cites Prabhupāda's purport and Viśvanātha Cakravartī: the Lord constantly strives to bring us back and never, ever gets tired.
- A mother whose child knocks the medicine glass away may try a few times and then get angry; Kṛṣṇa keeps offering the medicine of spirituality again and again.

#### Unrepayable love [44:00 – 48:00]

- Whatever little we do in devotional service is tiny compared to what Kṛṣṇa does for us.
- The _Bhāgavatam_ describes a soul awakened to Kṛṣṇa's glory saying that even a lifespan as long as Brahmā's would not be enough to express gratitude, because the Lord works externally (sending _guru_, _sādhu_ and _śāstra_) and internally (as the _caitya-guru_ guiding "do this, don't do this").
- Meditating on Kṛṣṇa's love lets us put aside the false loves of this world; worldly relationships can be wonderful, but they should not become substitutes for Kṛṣṇa's love.
- So we can pray "please remember me," but more important is that we keep remembering how much the Lord loves us; then we won't be distracted by worldly things.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is ever waiting in our heart, ever willing to accept us whatever we have done, and ever working tirelessly to bring us back; remembering His love is the strongest protection against worldly distraction. [40:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Model devotees and the urgency of now [46:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda gives examples from the _Bhāgavatam_ of devotees who perfected one limb of devotional service; the teacher then stresses that the time to practise is now `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Model devotees [46:00 – 48:00]

- Parīkṣit Mahārāja — hearing; Śukadeva Gosvāmī — speaking; Hanumān — servitorship; Arjuna — friendship; Bali Mahārāja — offering himself completely to Vāmanadeva.
- They practised other limbs too, but one limb was the defining attribute of their devotional lives; the _Bhāgavatam_ has many such examples of how people remembered Kṛṣṇa and returned to Him.

#### The present is not a practice run [48:00 – 50:00]

- The mind keeps going to the past ("that went wrong," "that was so nice") and the future, so we don't utilise the present.
- In cricket net practice some players don't take it seriously — "it's not the real thing." We often treat the present the same way: as a practice run for next time.
- That careless attitude doesn't only lose the present; it is carried into the future, which also becomes a "practice run" when it arrives.
- Nor will a magic moment come — e.g. the spiritual master touching one's head and an electric current making one a pure devotee. We must purify ourselves, and now is the best time, because now is all we have and all we will ever have.

#### Attitude, not time [50:00 – 54:00]

- Distractions will always be there, more at some moments and less at others; do material responsibilities and devotional activities wholeheartedly, because this is the only time to do them.
- Creating more favourable circumstances for the future is excellent, but don't postpone spiritual practice to a comfortable future.
- Work expands to occupy the time available; if the mind is habituated to material things, even when time comes some material engagement will expand to fill it. The problem was never lack of time but lack of attitude — and the attitude has to change now.
- With this urgent attitude, devotional life becomes deeper and richer, and remembering Kṛṣṇa at death becomes natural, leading back to Him `54:00`.

#### Summary [54:00 – 56:00]

- The body is a foreign dress; three attitudes: romanticise (_karma_), demonise (_jñāna_ — sometimes used to pull the mind from its default romantic concept), utilise (steady state).
- The body will give us up, but we give it up only when the subtle body is purified and attraction to the world becomes attraction to Kṛṣṇa — possible only in the human form, best through _bhakti-yoga_.
- Two motivations to remember Kṛṣṇa: "I am going to die" is fear-based and important; meditating on Kṛṣṇa's love — not catching but coaching, ever waiting, willing and working — is love-based. Both can be used at different times.
- Whatever I have, let me offer it to Kṛṣṇa in the present moment.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The present moment is all we have; treating it as a practice run loses both present and future, so offer whatever we have to Kṛṣṇa now — the urgency that makes remembering Him at death natural. [46:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
