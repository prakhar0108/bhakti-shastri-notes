## Mantra-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Mantra 14: balancing the eternal and the temporary [00:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/14 -->

**Context.** Everything material is temporary, but that does not mean it must all be rejected — it has to be utilised `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sambhūti_ — translated by Śrīla Prabhupāda as the Lord together with His name, form, qualities and pastimes `00:00 – 02:00`.
- The word for the temporary — in Mantra 13 one word was used; here it is replaced by a cognate, more or less equivalent word meaning "that which is temporary": the material creation with its variety of living beings `00:00 – 02:00`.
- Here "the temporary" does not mean temporary material things in general, but knowledge of this world that helps us become fixed in Kṛṣṇa's service — used in His service so that we can return to the spiritual world `00:00`.

#### The translation [02:00]

- By knowledge of the material world one can navigate through it; by knowledge of the Lord one attains immortality.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation: one should know perfectly the Personality of Godhead Śrī Kṛṣṇa with His transcendental name, form, qualities and pastimes, as well as the temporary material creation with its temporary demigods, men and animals; then one surpasses death and enjoys eternal life of bliss and knowledge in the kingdom of God.

#### Flow of the purport [02:00 – 06:00]

- Nothing — not even science — can make this world deathless; science cannot change its nature.
- Is there a realm beyond death? Yes: the Vedas give information about both the death-filled world (this material world) and the death-free world beyond.
- **BG 8.15 – 8.22** illustrate this: from Brahmā's planet down to the lowest, all are places of repeated birth and death; even Brahmā's lifespan ends; and from 8.20 Kṛṣṇa describes the spiritual world `04:00`.
- Only by surrender to Kṛṣṇa can we transcend death. The purport then adds, more specifically, that we must give up misconceptions — "I am the body," "I am God" — that keep us from God's shelter `04:00`.
- Attaining Kṛṣṇa automatically means attaining immortality: one who attains Him does not come back (BG 8.15). Attaining immortality means attaining love for Kṛṣṇa and thereby Kṛṣṇa `04:00`.
- Focus of this class: how the material world, with its varied time scales for different beings, is still ultimately mortal — studied through the concept of time, since Kṛṣṇa says He is time, the destroyer of the worlds `04:00 – 06:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Mantra 14 asks for balance: know the Lord fully, and know the temporary world well enough to use it in His service — because only by attaining Kṛṣṇa do we pass beyond a world that nothing, not even science, can make deathless. [00:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### The six transformations and the linear model of time [06:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Before discussing time, Śrīla Prabhupāda describes what he calls the six laws of transformation `06:00`.

#### The six transformations [06:00]

- Non-living things go through three phases: creation, deterioration, destruction.
- Living things go through three more: growth, maintenance and reproduction — along with birth, deterioration and death.
- Because these cannot be stopped, death cannot be stopped: everything material is moving unstoppably and irreversibly towards destruction.

#### The modern linear model [06:00 – 08:00]

- Mainstream society holds a Judeo-Christian-scientific model of time: linear, from a start to an end.
- The universe is created; unicellular organisms appear (exactly when and how is speculated); then human beings evolve. What comes next — a peak, devolution, destruction — no one knows. Researchers also say the world will ultimately be destroyed.

#### Science–religion wars in the West [08:00 – 10:00]

- The Catholic Church had adopted into its theology a pre-Christian Greek thinker's model in which the sun moves around the earth.
- Copernicus opposed it; Galileo's telescope and findings gave strong evidence that the earth moves around the sun, and the Church "took a big beating."
- The conflict continued up to Darwin's theory of evolution, where religion again seemed to lose.
- Yet despite nearly 400 years of conflict, the Judeo-Christian and the scientific world views share a key feature — both are linear `08:00`.

#### Linear time as a Christian idea, secularised [08:00 – 12:00]

- Augustine, a prominent Christian thinker, reshaped history into a linear model: all events progressed towards Jesus' appearance, and after his departure towards the Messiah's return — paradise on earth, sinners cast into hell.
- Before that, even in Europe, the Greek and Roman models were cyclic — and most of nature is cyclic: sunrise and sunset, the waxing and waning moon, the seasons, morning–afternoon–evening–night `10:00`.
- Science adopted the Christian arrow of time and replaced God and Jesus with a secular god — progress: from simple matter to microscopic organisms, animals, monkeys, humans and finally today's car-driving, phone-using modern person `10:00 – 12:00`.
- This is a self-serving model that places modern humans at the pinnacle of history `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The six transformations make death unavoidable for everything material, and the modern belief in linear progress is a Christian arrow of time with God replaced by "progress" — a model that flatters modern humanity. [06:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### The cyclic Vedic model and real progress [12:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** The Vedic vision differs from the linear model not only by being cyclic, but in placing the present brand of humanity at the bottom, not the top `12:00`.

#### The Vedic cycle [12:00 – 14:00]

- Of the four _yugas_ — Satya, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali — Kali-yuga is the lowest.
- The Vedic idea is cyclic: creation, maintenance, annihilation, again and again; understanding the soul, we see this also goes on life after life.
- There is a way out of the cycle: the slide shows the spiritual world with Goloka at the top, the spiritual sky around it, then the _brahmajyoti_, and below that the material world `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Is the cyclic model pessimistic? [14:00]

- It seems to leave no room for progress — after morning comes night; however much we evolve, Kali-yuga will come again — so many Western thinkers have called the Vedic literature pessimistic and life-denying.
- But the Vedic model has room for tremendous progress — of a different nature: spiritual progress.
- Material "progress" is like a wheel: what seems vertical is really going round; even reaching the top of the wheel, one comes down again. Real progress is getting out of the wheel into the eternal spiritual reality.

#### Humans need progress [14:00 – 18:00]

- A cow eats the same grass year after year and never asks for masala in it; it has no conception of progress. Humans, with developed intelligence, want to improve things.
- By denying the spiritual dimension, modern society restricts progress to the material level. Technological progress in recent centuries is undeniable `16:00`.
- But intelligence that could have taken us out of the cycle of birth and death is used to keep going around in it. Despite huge technology we have stressed, distressed, suicidal and addicted people — technologically improved, psychologically regressed, spiritually regressed further `16:00`.
- Spiritual progress means increasing philosophical understanding of spiritual reality and experiencing it through yoga, centred ultimately on _bhakti-yoga_ `16:00`.
- The mantra's balance: do whatever is needed for maintenance in the cyclic material world, but focus on going beyond it `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Vedic cycle is not pessimistic: material progress only turns the wheel, while real progress — understanding and experiencing spiritual reality through bhakti — takes us off the wheel altogether. [12:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Vedic time scales: even Brahmā's life ends [18:00 – 28:00]

**Context.** A slide on the Vedic units of time shows how everything in the material world is ultimately cyclic and mortal; the teacher asks students not to worry about the figures but to grasp the principle `18:00`, `22:00`.

#### The units from largest to smallest [18:00 – 22:00]

- **Life of Brahmā** — gigantic (the teacher gives a figure of about 311 trillion years); 360 days in his year, 36,000 _kalpas_ in his lifetime.
- **One _kalpa_** — one day of Brahmā, 4.32 billion years; it contains 14 _manvantaras_ and 15 _sandhyās_ (joining periods between and around them).
- **One _manvantara_** — the duration of one Manu, about 306.72 million years = 71 _divya-yugas_, with a juncture of 1.728 million years.
- **One _divya-yuga_** — one cycle of Satya, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali, 4.32 million years.
- **Kali-yuga** — of its 432,000 years, only about 5,000 have passed; it has a central period of 360,000 years with 36,000-year _sandhyās_ at the beginning and end — each _sandhyā_ one-tenth of the _yuga_ `20:00 – 22:00`.
- Brahmā is now in his 51st year, first month, first day; Kṛṣṇa appears in the 28th _divya-yuga_ of the 7th _manvantara_ `20:00`.
- Compared with the vastness of the universe, modern man is very small, in an insignificant corner `20:00`.

#### Devastations [22:00]

- At the end of each _manvantara_, a great flood wipes out most life on Earth.
- At the end of a _kalpa_, all life on Earth is destroyed, but the planets continue; souls not liberated remain to reincarnate in Brahmā's next day.
- At the end of Brahmā's life — the great devastation — the whole universe is destroyed. Everything in the world is destroyed.

#### Arithmetic in units of Kali-yuga [22:00 – 24:00]

- Taking Kali-yuga as K: Dvāpara is twice, Tretā three times, Satya four times — one _mahā-yuga_ is 10K.
- One day of Brahmā is calculated as 1,000 _mahā-yugas_; day and night alternate continuously.
- One second of Brahmā is about 100,000 human years — think how tiny our life is.
- The teacher also gives worked-out figures for the age of the Earth (about 1.97 billion years) and of the universe (about 155.5 trillion years) `24:00`.

#### Brahmā's dollar [24:00]

- A man asks Brahmā: "Is your one second our 100,000 years, and your one dollar our 100,000 dollars?" "Yes." "Then can I have your one dollar?" Brahmā replies: "Wait a second" — i.e. 100,000 years.
- Our whole life passes in a fraction of a moment of Brahmā; our vision is limited, and we must see through scriptural eyes.

#### The sage who would not build a hut [24:00 – 26:00]

- A sage had the benediction that one of his hairs would fall at the end of each Brahmā, and he would die only when all his hairs had fallen.
- Yet when his disciples wanted to build a hut for him, he said: "What is the use? I am going to die so soon."
- Even with so long a life he felt it was temporary and wanted to use all his time for self-realisation.

#### Life takes off when we see how soon life takes us off [26:00 – 28:00]

- Our life is not very long; the earlier we recognise it, the better.
- **The rented car** — suppose I must drive from Mumbai to Pune and will have the car only for two and a half hours; wherever I am then, I must leave it and hire another only when I have enough money. I will drive straight and fast. If I stop for sights, hotels and movies, the car will be taken away before I cover a fraction of the distance.
- Similarly, life will take us out of the car of this body into another; recognising this temporary nature fixes the mind on the eternal, whatever our _āśrama_ or situation `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Against the immense Vedic time scales even Brahmā's life ends and ours is a flicker; life truly "takes off" only when we realise how soon life will take us off, and drive straight towards the eternal. [18:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### How a devotee transcends death [28:00 – 44:00]

**Context.** The main point of the mantra is that a devotee transcends death; the teacher explains this logically through three phases — past, present and future — contrasting the materialist and the devotee `28:00`.

#### The cat, the kitten and the rat [28:00 – 30:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's favourite example: the same cat holds a kitten in its mouth out of love and a rat in its mouth for death. The holding looks the same, but the purpose and the destination are entirely different.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda would say: for a materialist Kṛṣṇa comes as time, but for a devotee time comes as Kṛṣṇa — through time the devotee returns to Kṛṣṇa `30:00`.

#### The trauma of death [30:00]

- Death happens to the body; as long as the soul is attached to the body, it suffers death's trauma.
- The _Purāṇas_ say the pain of death is like 40,000 scorpion bites. Such figures illustrate a point: one sting is agonising, so 40,000 is unbearably frightening; the actual pain depends on how attached the person is to the body.

#### Three phases of suffering for a materialist [32:00 – 34:00]

1. **Sorrow about the past** — everything and everyone one was attached to, even the body, is ripped away: "I built this house, I had this family…" No bereavement is greater.
2. **Pain in the present** — the body is breaking down (e.g. lung cancer, constant coughing, treatment not working); time itself, the greatest terror, destroys the body.
3. **Fear about the future** — not knowing whether one will exist, where, or how one will suffer — like being evicted and put in a van without knowing whether it goes to a lock-up, an asylum or a courtroom.

#### The devotee and the past [34:00 – 36:00]

- A devotee gives up material attachments and becomes attached to Kṛṣṇa; with the heart primarily with Kṛṣṇa, there is little lamentation at losing things and loved ones.
- A devotee loves family members as Kṛṣṇa's children and Kṛṣṇa's family, caring for them on His behalf, so they increase the devotee's dependence on Kṛṣṇa.
- What the devotee loses is the body — a source of physical misery and sensual distraction.
- There may be some sorrow at separation from devotees, but also eagerness to be reunited with Kṛṣṇa and His pure devotees in the spiritual world `36:00`.

#### The devotee and the present: problems versus suffering [36:00 – 42:00]

- For a devotee, bodily suffering is more an inconvenience: a fractured hand and a punctured car tyre are different for most people, but one who understands that the body too is external sees them as not so different. Kṛṣṇa also describes the body as a machine.
- Problems are unavoidable; suffering is avoidable — it depends on how emotionally entangled we are `38:00`.
- **The new car** — a proud owner screams when someone touches his bumper at a signal, as if his heart were torn. After selling it, he hears the same car was badly crushed by a truck, and feels only human concern for anyone injured — the attachment is gone `38:00`.
- The body is given by Kṛṣṇa through material nature, which uses it to give us karmic reactions; as detachment grows, pain becomes more an inconvenience `40:00`.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda's massage** — a servant writes in his diary that in Māyāpur, when no one else was present during his massage, Śrīla Prabhupāda would become silent and internally absorbed, as if he had left his body with the servant for overhauling — like leaving a car at a garage — and gone out to relish the _dhāma_ `40:00`.
- Pure devotees transcend the body by absorption in Kṛṣṇa, so their physical suffering is much less. Many of Śrīla Prabhupāda's disciples left this world gloriously and fearlessly, as compiled in the book _Many, Many Moons_ `42:00`.

#### The devotee and the future [42:00 – 44:00]

- A devotee welcomes the future, knowing "I am going to return to Kṛṣṇa — the event I have long awaited."
- At the physical level no one escapes, but raising consciousness above it minimises the pain.
- **The bride** — a bride in an arranged marriage feels apprehension going to a groom and in-laws she does not know; but after a long courtship with a beloved groom there is only eager anticipation. Devotional service is a courtship with Kṛṣṇa; for a devotee death is a take-off, a transition to a higher existence, not a termination.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A materialist meets death with sorrow for the past, pain in the present and fear of the future; a devotee, attached to Kṛṣṇa, detached from the body and long "courting" the Lord, experiences death as a transition home. [28:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### A disciple's fearless departure [44:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** The teacher narrates the departure of one of Śrīla Prabhupāda's _sannyāsī_ disciples as a living illustration of the mantra `42:00 – 44:00`.

#### The "Jolly Swami" [44:00 – 46:00]

- He was introduced to Kṛṣṇa consciousness by reading the _Śrī Īśopaniṣad_ itself, and came to India on hearing that Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted devotees to help preach there.
- Renounced and devoted, he was also always joking and cheerful; Indian life members, who expected a _sādhu_ to be grave and sober, called him the "Jolly Swami."
- Told by a doctor he would die soon of a severe liver problem, he accepted it as Kṛṣṇa's will and kept serving — and miraculously lived over a dozen more years, travelling and preaching vigorously.

#### The one-way ticket [46:00 – 48:00]

- In Canada, told his liver had completely failed, he first planned to stay with his disciples; a close _sannyāsī_ friend urged him to go to Māyāpur — "all your life you have given yourself to others; now focus undistracted on Kṛṣṇa," since the holy _dhāmas_ are meant for that.
- They met in London; seeing discoloured patches on his body, the friend asked about them: "The small blood vessels have gone; when the big ones go, I will go" — said casually and fearlessly.
- Checking tickets, the friend noticed he had only a one-way ticket and realised its meaning; the Swami laughed: "Kṛṣṇa has given me a one-way ticket back to Godhead."

#### On the plane and in the car [48:00 – 50:00]

- In great pain on the flight, he was moaning; when an air hostess asked whether something was wrong, he opened his eyes and said: "There is always something wrong in this material world," and preached that the best solution is to go back to God.
- She agreed it made sense; he reached out with a weak hand and gave her a book — preaching to the last.
- In the car from Kolkata to Māyāpur he thanked a leading disciple, and through him all his disciples: as a _sannyāsī_ he had renounced family joys, but they had given him many sons and daughters and made him proud; he asked them to continue with what Śrīla Prabhupāda had given.

#### Arrival in Māyāpur [50:00 – 52:00]

- The _Bhāgavatam_ class was cancelled; with GBC meetings on before Gaura-pūrṇimā, hundreds of devotees lined the way doing _kīrtana_ — like a nation welcoming home a warrior from glorious wars.
- He walked to take _darśana_ of the deities and up to his room, then almost fell unconscious with exhaustion and a gas-bloated stomach.
- When a doctor put a stethoscope to his belly, he opened his eyes and asked, "Doctor, is it a boy or a girl?" `52:00`.

#### Selfless to the end [52:00 – 54:00]

- He had three desires: to reach Māyāpur, to attend a deity installation, and to be there for Gaura-pūrṇimā — all fulfilled; though weak, he was on the altar helping in the _ārati_.
- He prayed not to depart on Gaura-pūrṇimā itself: it should remain Caitanya Mahāprabhu's festival, not shift attention to him.
- When the teacher visited him in Māyāpur and asked how he felt, he joked that there was so much gas in his stomach that India's national gas corporation could set up a profitable plant there.
- He departed early one morning on a holy day, beholding pictures of the deities in his room — fearless throughout, passing through death as if it were nothing `54:00`.

#### What this shows [54:00 – 58:00]

- The physical event of death happens, but the devotee transcends its pain because consciousness is fixed on a higher reality.
- Some devotees may not depart so gracefully; how they depart matters less than where their consciousness is. A lifetime of devotional service ensures an auspicious destination — open to all who practise systematically `54:00`.
- **The coaching class** — elite coaching classes for an entrance exam boast of toppers, but they admit only toppers through cut-offs. A class that took barely-passing students and made them toppers would be extraordinary. Śrīla Prabhupāda took aimless hippies who had broken all the regulative principles and made them exalted devotees who left this world remembering Kṛṣṇa `54:00 – 56:00`.
- Scripture is not just an ancient book but a living reality for those who live by it — and will be for us if we do `56:00`.

#### Many, many moons [58:00]

- One moon is better than hundreds of stars, because one moon illuminates the whole sky.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda used this saying: one glorious disciple is enough — but Kṛṣṇa had been so kind as to give him "many, many moons," many illustrious disciples.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The cheerful, preaching, selfless departure of Śrīla Prabhupāda's disciple shows the mantra as lived reality: by Prabhupāda's mercy even people from non-spiritual backgrounds can become "moons" who transcend death in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. [44:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
