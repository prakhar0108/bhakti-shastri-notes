### The mantra as a warning [00:00 – 04:00]

**Context.** The teacher first explains the mantra's sense: those who kill the soul, whoever they may be, enter worlds of the faithless (the demoniac), covered by darkness and ignorance `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Asura_ — the demoniac; their worlds are covered by darkness `00:00`.
- _Tamas_ — darkness of ignorance `00:00`.
- _Ātma-hā_ — "killer of the soul"; the word comes in the last line of the mantra `02:00`.

#### A warning, like a parent's [02:00]

- One who lives without awareness of his spiritual nature, neglecting his spiritual responsibilities, lives ignorantly and will go into still deeper ignorance in the future.
- Like a parent telling a child: "If you don't study, you'll fail; then you'll have no career, live in poverty and ignominy; nobody will respect or employ you."

#### Structure of the purport [02:00 – 04:00]

- Three sections, six paragraphs:
  1. Who is a demon — clarifying the two Sanskrit terms _asura_ and _ātma-hā_.
  2. The basic message: liberate the soul, don't kill the soul.
  3. How human beings misuse their facility.

### Who is an asura? A mentality, not just a form [04:00 – 10:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda explains _asura_ as one who is demoniac; the teacher unpacks the word through its etymology `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Etymology_ — the study of word origins; e.g. _lexicographer_: _lexico_ (dictionary) + _graph_ (to write) = one who makes a dictionary. Etymological meanings may not always be historically precise, but they are semantically illuminating `04:00`.
- _Asu_ — breath, life air; as in BG 2.11, where the wise lament neither for those whose life air has gone nor for those in whom it remains `04:00 – 06:00`.
- _Ra_ — to enjoy `04:00`.
- _Asura_ — one who enjoys the life air: one who wants to enjoy at the level of the body, in a godless, sensuous, materialistic way `06:00`.
- _Suras_ — those who always look toward the feet of Lord Viṣṇu; _asuras_ are exactly the opposite `06:00`.

#### Not just a species [06:00 – 08:00]

- We picture demons with fangs and horns; there can be such demons — in the _Rāmāyaṇa_, humans and monkeys fight _rākṣasas_ with terrible forms.
- But in the _Mahābhārata_ the battle is between humans and humans; _rākṣasas_ even fought on both sides — one joined the opposing side to take revenge on Bhīma.
- Duryodhana did not have a demon's body but had a demoniac mentality; those actively assisting him are also considered demoniac.
- In BG chapter 16 Kṛṣṇa describes divine and demoniac qualities within human society itself.

#### "Who is a demon?" [08:00]

- Prabhupāda wrote a _Back to Godhead_ article, "Who Is a Demon?": we may imagine a horned creature, but your local politician, your grocer, your neighbour — or you — might be a demon.
- All of us have a divine side and a demoniac side; if we let the demoniac side take control, we behave demonically.

#### Demoniac localities [10:00]

- Just as there are demoniac mentalities, there are demoniac localities in the cosmography of the universe.
- The fourteen planetary systems are often grouped into three _lokas_ — upper, middle (including the earthly system) and lower; demons often go to the lower systems.
- Those who live godlessly go to such places — the meaning of the mantra.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
An asura is anyone who seeks godless bodily enjoyment — a mentality present in all of us, not merely a monstrous form — and such a mentality leads to correspondingly dark destinations. [04:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### "Killer of the soul": a figurative meaning [10:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** The first lesson of the Gītā is that the soul cannot be killed (BG 2.17), so how can anyone be a "killer of the soul"? `10:00`

#### The Gītā's own example [10:00 – 12:00]

- In chapter 16 Kṛṣṇa says there are three gates to hell — lust, anger and greed — which destroy the soul, and the wise should avoid them.
- Kṛṣṇa is not contradicting His earlier teaching: He is speaking non-literally.
- "Killing the soul" means killing the soul's spiritual consciousness, not its existence. E.g. I may know I am a soul meant for spiritual realisation, but exposure to provocative pictures or circumstances arouses lust, which makes me forget I am the soul and awakens bodily consciousness `12:00`.

#### Primary and secondary modes of reading [12:00 – 14:00]

- Scripture can be understood through a primary, direct (literal) mode and through _lakṣaṇā_, a secondary, indicative (non-literal) mode.
- We cannot take non-literal meanings arbitrarily. Prabhupāda strongly opposed non-literal readings not grounded in scripture.
- **Example:** some Indian nationalist thinkers who used the Gītā in the independence struggle were embarrassed that it was spoken to make Arjuna fight, so they called the war a metaphor for a battle within consciousness. The historical context makes clear a real battle was fought; such unscriptural interpretation is wrong `14:00`.
- When scripture itself supports a figurative reading, it can be accepted.

#### Scripture explains ātma-hā: the boat analogy [14:00 – 18:00]

- Scripture cannot be understood in isolation; a _Bhāgavatam_ verse (11.20.17) explains who the killer of the soul is `14:00`.
- Material existence is an ocean in which we are struggling and drowning:
  - **The boat** — the human body, rare among millions of species, yet obtained naturally through progression through the lower species; it is well designed to cross the ocean.
  - **The captain** — the spiritual master, who "catches the ear" of the disciple and corrects him when he goes wrong.
  - **The favourable wind** — the scriptures given by the Lord.
- Studying scripture generates spiritual desires, thoughts, inspirations and convictions that move us toward Kṛṣṇa; mundane movies and magazines, especially provocative ones, create an opposite wind that takes us away from Him `16:00 – 18:00`.
- One who has all these facilities and still does not cross the ocean ruins his spiritual prospects — he is the _ātma-hā_ `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul cannot be killed, so "killer of the soul" means one who destroys the soul's spiritual awareness and wastes the human boat, the captain and the favourable wind of scripture; such figurative readings are valid because scripture itself supports them. [10:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Three levels of reading scripture [18:00 – 26:00]

**Context.** To show that figurative meanings must be grounded in scripture, the teacher presents Śrīpāda Madhvācārya's three levels of reading `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Literal reading_ — the events actually happened `18:00`.
- _Ethical reading_ — the events serve as moral benchmarks for present-day decisions `18:00 – 20:00`.
- _Metaphorical reading_ — the historical events also symbolise truths relevant to spiritual seekers `20:00`.
- _Hermeneutics_ — the branch of knowledge concerned with the study and interpretation of scripture `24:00`.

#### Literal reading [18:00]

- Sītā went with Lord Rāma to the forest; Rāma chased the deer; Rāvaṇa abducted Sītā — these actually happened.
- Simply hearing the Lord's pastimes literally purifies us and increases our attraction to Him; no further interpretation is needed.

#### Ethical reading [18:00 – 20:00]

- Sītā chose to accompany Rāma to the forest though she was not obliged to — an ethical lesson.
- She was attracted to the deer; when we are with the Lord, we should not be attracted to other things.
- Ethical readings are valuable but must be taken in proper context to avoid misjudgements.

#### Metaphorical reading [20:00 – 24:00]

- **Devakī's six children:** after six children were killed, Balarāma appeared, then Kṛṣṇa. One metaphorical reading: the six children are the six _anarthas_ (such as lust, anger, greed, illusion and pride); when they are removed, the spiritual master manifests in the heart — we become fixed in _guru-niṣṭhā_ and service — and then Kṛṣṇa appears.
- An _ācārya_ also reads elements of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes as our past conditionings coming back to haunt us `20:00 – 22:00`.
- **Rāvaṇa as the mind:** a later _ācārya_, a great logician who systematically established the logical foundation of the personal school, explains: the soul is with the Lord, but when it turns away for something else, the mind catches it. Rāvaṇa's ten heads are the mind with the ten senses; Laṅkā is the body; the mind traps the soul in the body because it desires to enjoy `22:00`.
- These meanings can be accepted — but it does not follow that there was no Sītā or Rāma; the events happened historically, and an additional meaning is given.

#### Supplement, not supplant [24:00]

- The key principle: the metaphorical should _supplement_ (add to) the literal reading, not _supplant_ (replace) it.
- Prabhupāda's objection: Kurukṣetra is a geographical place that exists today, and the war took place 5,000 years ago; calling it "just a metaphor" supplants the literal meaning.
- Yet Prabhupāda himself used metaphor: as described in a disciple's book, _The Hare Krishna Explosion_, at an early initiation in 1966 he told the devotees they need not lament, for they had handed the chariot of their life to Kṛṣṇa — Arjuna lamented until he took Kṛṣṇa's guidance; the body is the chariot and Kṛṣṇa the charioteer.

#### When the literal must yield [24:00 – 26:00]

- A non-literal reading may replace the literal when the literal leads to obvious absurdity.
- **"His house is on the river"** — a house cannot stand on water; it means the house is on the river's bank, though "bank" is not stated.
- Likewise _ātma-hā_ cannot be literal, because the soul is eternal; it means one who kills the soul's spiritual awareness and consciousness `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Scripture can be read literally, ethically and metaphorically; metaphor must supplement, never supplant, the literal meaning, and may replace it only where the literal is plainly absurd — as with "killer of the soul." [18:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### A soul-killing civilisation: form changes, purpose doesn't [26:00 – 32:00]

**Context.** The purport's message — liberate the soul, don't kill it. By living materialistically we deny the soul the benefit of its spiritual potential; if we don't use the higher facilities of the human form, we live worse than animals here and go to hell hereafter `26:00`.

#### Same purpose, new form [26:00 – 28:00]

Scientific advancement has changed the form, not the purpose, of our activities:

- **Eating** — a pig eats refuse from a garbage pile; humans titillate their tongues with processed, packaged foods like pizzas and burgers. The essential pleasure is the same.
- **Mating** — animals mate in the streets; humans cultivate erotic fantasies through magazines, television, movies and the internet before indulging. Surfing the net with sophisticated technology may serve the same purpose — seeking sexual stimulation.
- **Sleeping** — animals sleep on the hard street; humans sink into oblivion on a soft bed in an air-conditioned room. Both forget who and where they are.
- **Defending** — animals snarl and bare their teeth; humans brandish weapons of mass destruction. Both are shows of strength.

#### What drives advancement [28:00 – 30:00]

- Most research funding comes from two sources: defence (wars and threats of war) and the consumer industry (eating, sleeping and mating).
- So advancement largely furthers our animal propensities — e.g. advanced phone cameras used largely to attract the opposite sex; at premier institutes with fast internet, one of the top searches is sex.
- With all our progress we still eat, sleep, mate and defend, without crossing the ocean of material existence.
- Hence the purport's word: the modern civilisation is "soul-killing" — not one individual _ātma-hā_ but an entire soul-killing civilisation `30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Technology has changed the form of eating, sleeping, mating and defending, but not their purpose; a civilisation devoted only to these is soul-killing, because it never uses the human form to cross material existence. [26:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Worse off than animals even in this life [32:00 – 40:00]

**Context.** For those who do not believe in consequences in the next life, one can point to consequences in this life to encourage serious spiritual life `32:00`.

#### Eating, sleeping, mating and defending [32:00 – 34:00]

- **Eating** — animals do not starve because of artificial shortages created by their own species. The book _Food First_ studied famines over decades and found that in each case food was available in the same country but was withheld for political or business interests.
- **Sleeping** — no animal needs a sleeping pill; a dog sleeps in the middle of the street, while we cannot sleep in a comfortable bed, sometimes even with a pill `34:00`.
- **Mating** — animals don't suffer heartbreak from betrayed love; a dog doesn't sing songs of sorrow or commit suicide over it `34:00`.
- **Defending** — few fear a tiger entering their house, but many fear terrorists or thieves; our biggest danger is other humans. Which other species lives in mortal fear of its own members? `34:00`

#### Other kinds of suffering [34:00 – 38:00]

- **Stress** — no animal takes a stress-management course `34:00 – 36:00`.
- **Addiction** — a fish takes a bait, a mouse takes the cheese, but they have excuses: they don't know in advance, and the bait looks like food. No one thinks a cigarette is food, and everyone knows smoking is harmful, yet humans become addicted. An animal smokes only if a human master, not wanting to smoke alone, teaches it — bad association `36:00`.
- **Suicide** — about one million people kill themselves every year, more than are killed in war or violent crime; something is seriously wrong with how we live. It is a soul-killing and even a body-killing civilisation `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Misusing the higher for the lower [38:00 – 42:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that swine, dogs and camels have "economic necessities" — arranging for eating, sleeping, mating and defending — but they solve them only under nasty, unpleasant conditions `38:00`.
- **Eating under threat:** imagine someone standing over us with a stick as we sit to honour _prasāda_. A mosquito feeding on our skin may find its meal becomes its graveyard `38:00 – 40:00`.
- **The judge and the clerk:** both work in the same court, but the judge is paid and cared for far more because of his higher responsibility. Humans have privileges because of a higher responsibility — spiritual inquiry. If a judge doesn't do a judge's work, it is a serious flaw; if humans live animalistically, they are irresponsible in nature's system, and the consequence is suffering `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Despite all our advancement, humans suffer more than animals in eating, sleeping, mating and defending — and add stress, addiction and suicide — because the privileges of human life come with a higher responsibility we neglect: spiritual inquiry. [32:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### The Mercedes and the plough: three kinds of damage [42:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** The human body is an advanced facility meant for an advanced purpose `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### The analogy [42:00]

- A wealthy city man gifts his old farmer friend a Mercedes; not knowing its use, the farmer yokes it to plough his field. Three things happen:
  1. The Mercedes is spoiled.
  2. The field is spoiled.
  3. The farmer is frustrated.
- The human body is the Mercedes, meant for the expressway back to God; the lower species are like tractors or yoked bullocks. Conditioned by a culture that knows only eating, sleeping, mating and defending — and by our past in lower species — we use the human body for ploughing.

#### 1. The vehicle is spoiled — disease [42:00 – 46:00]

- Few animals suffer indigestion from overeating; humans suffer obesity and its many diseases.
- A saying: on one-third of what people eat, they live; on the remaining two-thirds, their doctor lives.
- Sexually transmitted diseases spread because people seek more and more pleasure from a body whose capacity is limited.
- The teacher calls AIDS an "acquired intelligence deficiency syndrome": thinking human life is meant for indiscriminate sexual enjoyment is deficient intelligence, acquired from culture — TV, movies and the internet.

#### 2. The field is spoiled — the environment [46:00 – 48:00]

- The biologist E. O. Wilson, among others, has said that every species contributes to ecological balance — if plants die out, herbivores suffer; if herbivores die out, carnivores suffer — except one: if humans became extinct, most ecological problems, such as climate change, would be minimised.
- This is jolting and humiliating for the supposedly most intelligent species — like the brightest student in a class being the most destructive.
- The reason: humans are meant to contribute to the ecology not materially but spiritually — by fulfilling the purpose of material nature, which is to elevate, educate and liberate conditioned souls `48:00`.

#### 3. The driver is frustrated — mind [48:00]

- People become stressed and depressed, fall into addictions and even suicide.
- People say they have no time for spiritual life because they must work to feed the stomach — yet many ready to work all day cannot even find jobs. An economic system focused only on economics fails even at economics, because we abuse the facility we have been given.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Using the human body for animal pleasures is like ploughing a field with a Mercedes: the body falls sick, the environment is damaged and the person becomes frustrated — because the human form is meant for the spiritual journey. [42:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### The human form as a spiritual IIT [48:00 – 54:00]

**Context.** A second analogy for misusing the human form `48:00`.

#### The student who only looks for food [48:00 – 52:00]

- Getting into IIT — India's premier educational institute, which every student dreams of — promises a lucrative career.
- Imagine a student who, once admitted, only searches the campus and neighbourhood for the best food, skips classes and experiments, and fails his exams. He may be expelled — and then, without a career, he may not even be able to eat.
- Eating has to be done, but it should not be the primary focus.
- The human form is a "spiritual IIT" for the soul: education here offers an eternal career — liberation from all material miseries and a life of endless love with Kṛṣṇa.
- All species — humans included — eat, sleep, mate and defend, but humans must not make these their primary focus.

#### Back to "dog-head" [52:00]

- Just as a student may be expelled from IIT, souls get expelled from the human body: instead of going back to Godhead, they go "back to dog-head" — they already have a dog's mentality and return to lower species.
- Even in this life, using higher intelligence for lower purposes creates bigger trouble than necessary. Such a civilisation entangles rather than liberates the soul — "killing" it by denying it the opportunity for spiritual advancement and happiness.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The human body is a spiritual IIT: if we make eating, sleeping, mating and defending our main pursuit, we fail the course and are "expelled" back to lower species, losing an eternal career with Kṛṣṇa. [48:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Even the attempt brings success [52:00 – 62:00]

**Context.** The purport's contrast: if I put all my energy into material life, I gain nothing spiritually and make things worse even materially; if I put even a little energy into spiritual life, that little advancement becomes a permanent, eternal asset of the soul `52:00`.

#### Birth in an auspicious family [54:00]

- BG 6.41–43: one who practises some spiritual life is born, in a future life, in a wealthy or learned family.
- Prabhupāda writes that simply by attempting to realise God one is guaranteed birth in a wealthy or aristocratic family — not even succeeding, just attempting.
- Those who make no attempt, wanting to stay covered by illusion and attached to materialism, enter the darkest regions of hell — living hellishly now and going to hell in future.

#### The failed exam with a free holiday [54:00 – 56:00]

- BG 6.41: one who deviates from the path — because of remaining desires to enjoy — goes to the heavenly planets, enjoys for a long time to exhaust those desires, and is then born auspiciously to continue spiritual life.
- Imagine a competitive exam where those who fail get six months' fully paid stay at a hill station — who would not sit for it? And if failure brings that, imagine success.
- Prabhupāda here speaks to materially minded people: even if you doubt heaven, at least you will be born auspiciously next life — so why not at least attempt? Otherwise one goes to the lower species.
- The mood of the mantra: live in harmony with God, according to the _īśāvāsya_ principle; merely materialistic living leads to disaster `56:00`.

#### Not on a tottering platform [56:00 – 60:00]

- Prabhupāda's "punching" conclusion: as human beings we are meant not simply for solving economic problems on a tottering platform, but for solving all the problems of material life into which we have been placed by the laws of nature.
- The "tottering platform" is material life: we are here for a while and then die; solving economic problems where existence itself is uncertain is a lost cause `58:00`.
- **The UNESCO conference:** at a conference the teacher attended, the head of UNESCO's India division regretted that, sixty years after the United Nations was established and after billions of dollars of sincere, intelligent effort in areas like child protection, maritime law, education and labour rights, conditions had only become worse `58:00`.
- Working only at the material level does not improve things; it makes them worse. As Albert Einstein said, problems cannot be solved at the same level of consciousness at which they were created — materialism created the problems, so spirituality, a higher consciousness, is needed `58:00`.
- Even today's social problems arise from lust, anger, greed and pride, and cannot be solved at the materialistic level `60:00`.
- So devotees practise spiritual life not selfishly or merely to avoid suffering: it is the most intelligent way to create an auspicious future and to contribute to the good of the world in this very life `60:00`.

#### Summary [60:00 – 62:00]

1. _Asura_ — not just a biological form but a mentality, which humans may also have.
2. _Ātma-hā_ — metaphorical meanings are valid as long as they do not replace the literal; three levels of reading (literal, ethical, metaphorical), with examples like Devakī's six children and Rāvaṇa as the mind.
3. Since the soul cannot be killed, "killing the soul" means destroying its spiritual awareness and its opportunity for liberation — the meaning the _Bhāgavatam_ also uses.
4. Modern society is soul-killing: advancement changes the form of eating, sleeping, mating and defending, not the purpose — and we suffer more than animals in each.
5. The Mercedes used for ploughing — body, environment and mind all suffer.
6. The IIT student who only looks for food — expelled back to lower species.
7. Even the attempt at spiritual life brings auspicious results and contributes to solving the world's problems.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even a small attempt at spiritual life is an eternal asset — failure itself is rewarded — while materialism alone tries to solve problems on a tottering platform; real solutions, personal and social, require a higher level of consciousness. [52:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
