### "No likeness of Him": negative descriptions in context [04:00 – 08:00]

**Context.** The word most likely to suggest an impersonal Absolute is _akāyam_, "without body." Before examining it, the teacher looks at a famous verse from another Upaniṣad that impersonalists quote to argue that the Absolute has no form `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### What the verse actually says [06:00]

- In its full context, the verse says no one can grasp Him and there is no likeness of Him — His name is great glory.
- It does not say He has no form; it says nothing can compare with His transcendental form.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation makes this explicit: nothing can compare with the transcendental form of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

#### Comparisons as indications [06:00 – 08:00]

- Scripture sometimes compares the Lord's features — eyes like lotuses, lips like a red fruit — only to give us some idea.
- In a drama by Rūpa Gosvāmī, a devotee king tells the Lord that he once thought he was glorifying Him with choice poetry; now that he has beheld His form, he sees it was an insult — like glorifying the emperor of the world as the landlord of a village.
- Great devotees who experience the Lord's beauty understand that nothing compares with it; so negative descriptions must be understood in their full context and heard from the _ācāryas_ `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Statements that the Lord has "no likeness" mean His form is beyond all comparison, not that He has no form; even the finest poetry minimises Him. [06:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Reconciling scripture by postulation (arthāpatti) [08:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** To read such statements properly, a hermeneutic — a method of understanding scripture — is needed `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Hermeneutics_ — the art of studying and understanding scripture `08:00`, `10:00`.
- _Arthāpatti_ — postulation: when two statements known to be true seem to contradict, one postulates a third statement that reconciles them `08:00`.

#### The humorous example [08:00]

- Statement 1: a person does not eat food throughout the day.
- Statement 2: his weight is increasing.
- Reconciling statement: he eats secretly at night — now both statements are true.
- The example is humorous, but the point is serious: when both statements are known to be true, a third statement must reconcile them.

#### Applied to scripture [08:00 – 12:00]

- Gajendra's prayers say the Lord has no form and also that He has many forms; reconciled: He has no material form but many spiritual forms `08:00 – 10:00`.
- In Vedic hermeneutics the Vedas give the Absolute Truth; if statements seem contradictory, our understanding is incomplete. With complete understanding — through _guru_, _sādhu_ and _śāstra_ — they are reconciled `10:00`.
- This is not something imposed on scripture: another Upaniṣadic statement says He sees though He has no eyes — He does not see with material eyes but with spiritual eyes `10:00`.
- Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu makes a similar point in His discussions with Sārvabhauma and Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī: the Vedas say the Absolute Truth glanced and then created `10:00 – 12:00`.
  - The senses, including all material eyes, appear only at a later stage of creation.
  - When the glance started creation, nothing material was yet manifest — the _mahat_ was still unagitated.
  - So the eyes that glanced must be transcendental.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Scripture is never self-contradictory; when two statements seem to clash, the ācāryas postulate the reconciling truth — the Lord has no material form or eyes, but has transcendental ones. [08:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Akāyam: unembodied, not disembodied [12:00 – 22:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda chooses a very specific translation for _akāyam_ — "unembodied" — and the teacher explains why the choice is meticulous `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Embodied_ — given a body; the soul becomes embodied from conception onward `12:00`, `14:00`.
- _Disembodied_ — having had a body taken away; ghosts, who lost their gross body (for example by suicide) and have not received a new one, are disembodied — the subtle body and soul remain without a gross body `12:00 – 14:00`.
- _Unembodied_ — never made embodied at all: the Lord eternally has His transcendental body, so there is neither embodiment nor disembodiment for Him `14:00`.
- _Denotation_ vs _connotation_ — the direct meaning of a word vs the implied meanings it acquires from context and culture `14:00`.

#### Why use "akāyam" at all? [14:00 – 16:00]

- Words acquire connotations from culture; the teacher's examples:
  - "Gay" fifty years ago simply meant cheerful; today it carries an entirely different meaning.
  - Śrīla Prabhupāda used the word "ass" for a donkey, while modern usage gives it another connotation.
- Connotations depend on the hearer's past conceptions; the word _kāya_ brings conceptions that do not apply to the Lord, so the Upaniṣad uses _akāyam_.

#### Connotations of "kāya" that do not apply to the Lord [16:00 – 20:00]

1. The body is separate from the real person, the soul — but the Lord's form is not different from Him.
2. The body will be physically separated from the soul at death — but the Lord's form is eternally with Him in the spiritual world, and descends from there.
3. The body degrades the soul by stimulating bodily desires (for example, sexual desire at puberty) — but the Lord's transcendental body is untouched by material desire.
4. The body is a product of past _karma_ — good-looking or not according to one's karma — but the Lord's body is not the result of karma; He is entirely beyond karma.

#### The internal evidence: "without veins" [20:00 – 22:00]

- An objector may say this is just a personalist's interpretation.
- But the mantra next says He has no veins. If He had no body at all, why add that?
  - If a person has no clothes, there is no point in saying he has no tie.
  - If a person has no house, it is implied he has no hall or bedroom.
- _Kāya_ (body) is the bigger set; veins are a smaller set within it. Saying "no veins" after "no body" shows _akāyam_ cannot simplistically mean "having no body."
- Hence: He has a special kind of body — transcendental, eternal, and without veins `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
"Unembodied" means the Lord was never given a material body as the soul is; His transcendental form is eternally His own — and the mantra's "without veins" confirms He does have a body, just not a material one. [12:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### The fulfiller of desires: qualification plus bestowal [22:00 – 34:00]

**Context.** The mantra says the Lord fulfils desires according to what a person offers. Śrīla Prabhupāda explains that results come only when qualification meets the Lord's bestowal `22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma_ — our endeavour `26:00`.
- _Daiva_ — the higher arrangement, destiny; ultimately the Supreme Lord `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Phala_ — the result, which comes when karma and daiva come together `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Karma-vāda_ — the idea that everything is in my hands `26:00`.
- _Daiva-vāda_ — the fatalistic idea that nothing is in my hands `26:00`.

#### Qualification plus bestowal [22:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's example: passing the exams qualifies a person to be a judge, but the government must then award the post.
- Likewise, acquiring qualification is necessary, but the Lord must bestow His grace for the result to come.

#### Prahlāda's three examples [22:00 – 26:00]

- Prahlāda Mahārāja prays to Lord Nṛsiṁha that the apparent protector is not the actual protector:
  - **Father and child** — a child takes shelter of the father, yet despite every intention the father may not protect it from sickness, kidnapping or death.
  - **Medicine and patient** — the best doctor may give medicine, yet the patient may grow sicker and die.
  - **Boat and drowning man** — a man may see a boat and not reach it, or the boat may capsize; the Titanic, considered unsinkable, sank on its first voyage.
- The higher sanction of the Lord is needed; the Lord is reciprocal, not arbitrary — He gives according to past karma and present endeavour `26:00`.

#### Neither karma-vāda nor daiva-vāda [26:00 – 28:00]

- _Karma-vāda_: "I will top the exam, win the championship, become the wealthiest businessman" — everything is in my hands.
- _Daiva-vāda_: "my fate is rotten, nothing works for me" — one becomes fatalistic and stops endeavouring.
- Both are required: the farmer ploughs and sows (karma), then waits for timely, adequate rain (daiva); together they yield the crop (phala).

#### Droṇa and Duryodhana [28:00 – 32:00]

- After Bhīṣma fell, Duryodhana made Droṇa commander; Droṇa, honoured, asked what he could do.
- Duryodhana asked that Yudhiṣṭhira be captured alive — so he could be defeated again at dice by Śakuni and exiled, rather than killed, which would make Bhīma and Arjuna wreak havoc.
- Droṇa tried with full vigour for two days and failed. When Duryodhana reproached him, Droṇa said he had exerted himself fully, but only endeavour is in our hands; results are determined by destiny — and the Lord of destiny sits on Arjuna's chariot.

#### Vidura and Dhṛtarāṣṭra [32:00]

- Vidura begged Dhṛtarāṣṭra to stop Duryodhana from the fratricidal war; the weak-hearted king said that if Destiny wills the dynasty's destruction, what can a tiny mortal do?
- Vidura replied: destiny determines the consequences of our actions, not our actions themselves — your actions are in your hands.
- A student's studying is in his hands; the marks are not.

#### Accepting results calmly [34:00]

- Understanding the daiva factor lets us accept things calmly: when things go wrong despite our best effort, the Lord is arranging something for our good.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda writes in the First Canto that when things go wrong beyond human control, there is nothing to regret.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Results need both our endeavour and the Lord's sanction; we control our actions, not their outcomes — so we strive fully and then accept the Lord's arrangement calmly. [22:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Śuddham: pure, prophylactic and antiseptic [34:00 – 44:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda connects the Lord's purity with His pastimes, especially the _rāsa_ dance, and with the mantra's words that He is beyond reproach and uncontaminated by sin `34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Antiseptic_ — what removes an existing infection, like ointment on a cut; curative `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Prophylactic_ — preventive medicine given before an expected infection, like medicine before a malaria epidemic `36:00`.
- _Bhava-roga_ — the disease of material existence: the soul's attachment to the body and bodily enjoyment, causing bondage and repeated birth and death `36:00`.

#### Beyond reproach [34:00]

- The Lord called the _gopīs_ with His flute and danced with them, yet He is beyond reproach — nobody can correct or chastise Him.
- He is not only pure but purifying.

#### Pure, preventive and curative toward us [36:00 – 38:00]

- Taking shelter of the Lord cures past contaminations — lust, anger, greed, envy, pride and illusion — by His potency (antiseptic).
- Strict _sādhana_ and remembrance of Kṛṣṇa also prevent new infection (prophylactic): after a good morning program, we can resist temptations in the day because we have cultivated a higher taste.
- What protects is not mechanical utterance or ritual but the connection with Kṛṣṇa; _sādhana_ is the means to that connection.
- The _Bhāgavatam_'s logic: by chanting and remembering Kṛṣṇa, great sages are purified of — even freed from — lust; so how could He be contaminated by lust? `38:00`

#### Pure in His own glory: the rāsa-līlā [40:00 – 42:00]

- The last verse of the _rāsa-pañcādhyāyī_ (end of the 33rd chapter) is a glorification in a prayerful mood.
- The Lord is called Viṣṇu there to remind us He is not an ordinary person but the Supreme Lord.
- One who hears and speaks of His play with the _gopīs_ with faith gets transcendental _bhakti_, and by it lust — the disease of the heart — is quickly and thoroughly destroyed.

#### Why hearing the rāsa removes lust [42:00 – 44:00]

- Ordinarily, hearing of a man and woman together privately at night raises suspicion, and intimate scenes in novels or movies contaminate us with lust; so something special must be happening here.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda often asks why great renunciants glorify it — Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, a strict _sannyāsī_, glorified the _gopīs_' pastimes and their exalted devotion.
- Three levels: material contamination, neutrality (impersonalism), and transcendental reality.
- Renunciants see this world as a reflection — perhaps real temporarily, but soon destroyed — and are not attracted to it; once they understand the personal reality beyond, they cannot but fall in love with it.
- They give up the reflection, not the reality. Seeing the _rāsa_, they think of the reality — Kṛṣṇa's intimate pastimes with His devotees — and that remembrance frees them from material attachment and enlivens them.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord is pure in Himself and purifying for us — curing past contamination and preventing new infection; hearing the rāsa-līlā with faith destroys lust because it is the reality of which worldly romance is only a reflection. [34:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### The purport: form, regret and spiritual intelligence [44:00 – 54:00]

**Context.** The teacher returns to the purport to bring out several of Śrīla Prabhupāda's statements `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vyavasāyātmikā buddhi_ — one-pointed spiritual intelligence (from Gītā 2.41), as opposed to intelligence scattered in many directions `48:00 – 50:00`.

#### Formless only in a material sense [44:00 – 46:00]

- A material body has anatomy and mechanism; the Lord's transcendental body has nothing like this, so He is sometimes described as formless.
- Foolish people consider the Deity worshipped by pure devotees to be made of material elements — their error comes from using material vision to see a spiritual object instead of spiritual vision.

#### The Lord regrets [46:00 – 48:00]

- Quoting the Gītā (9.11), Śrīla Prabhupāda writes that the Lord _regrets_ the fallen condition of people who deride Him because He descends like a human.
- Kṛṣṇa does not delight in chastising them; He doesn't want anyone to suffer, and the greatest suffering comes from misconceptions that alienate us from Him.
- Our fallen condition is due entirely to forgetting our relationship with God; to the extent we remember Him we are freed, which is why He manifests as the holy name and so many other facilities `48:00`.

#### Infinite longings or longing for the Infinite [48:00 – 50:00]

- Under _māyā_, living entities ask for many things, but spiritual intelligence is one: "please allow me to serve You."
- Two options:
  - **Infinite longings** — this dress, this car, this house, this spouse… happiness for a few days, then the next desire, without end; we work for them and also beg the Lord for them.
  - **Longing for the Infinite** — directing our power of desire to Kṛṣṇa as the only ultimate object; that desire purifies us of other desires.
- Without one-pointed intelligence, a person wanders asking for this from one and that from another — like "chewing the chewed."

#### "If a living being wants to go to hell" [50:00 – 52:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda says that if a living being wants to go to hell, the Lord allows him without interference; if he wants to go back to Godhead, the Lord helps him — because that is what the Lord also wants.
- Nobody consciously wants hell — but our wanting is spoken much louder through our actions; actions aimed at hellish enjoyment bring karmic reactions, and the Lord does not intervene.

#### "Lord" rather than "Kṛṣṇa" [52:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda repeatedly uses "the Lord" here rather than "Kṛṣṇa," because the Īśopaniṣad is presented to attract people with impersonal inclinations, who should not feel from the start that the presentation is biased toward Kṛṣṇa.

#### Liberation, real and imagined [52:00]

- Real liberation means going back to Godhead — being situated in our eternal, original position.
- Liberation as conceived by an impersonalist — "I will merge and become God" — is a myth; Brahman is part of the Absolute Truth, but this conception of it is not real. Without serving the Lord, begging for sense gratification continues and one stays trapped in infinite longings.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Spiritual intelligence asks only to serve the Lord; replacing infinite longings with longing for the Infinite is real liberation — and the Lord, who regrets our forgetfulness, helps everyone who truly wants to return. [44:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Conclusion: the seeker becomes the seer [52:00 – 55:10]

**Context.** Understanding all these words gives a clearer understanding of the Lord's properties, completing the section `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### How the seeker becomes the seer [54:00]

- The seeker who wants to harmonise with the Lord first gains spiritual vision toward the things of this world, then directs it toward the Lord.
- When he sees apparent contradictions, he understands there is an invisible reality beyond them which, when postulated, gives a conclusive understanding.
- The _mahā-bhāgavata_ understands the Lord to be the greatest, and so falls wholeheartedly in love with Him.

#### What comes next [54:00]

- Having understood how to understand the Lord, the next section describes the process to follow: _vidyā_ will be described and compared with _avidyā_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Spiritual vision directed at the world, then at the Lord, with apparent contradictions reconciled, turns a seeker into a seer who loves the Lord as the greatest of all. [54:00]
</Callout>
