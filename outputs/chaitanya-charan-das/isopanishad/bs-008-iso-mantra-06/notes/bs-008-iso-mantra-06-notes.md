## Mantra-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Seeing everything in relationship with the Lord [00:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/6 -->

**Context.** The teacher introduces the sixth mantra and gives Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation: one who sees everything in relationship with the Supreme Lord, who sees all living entities as His parts and parcels, and who sees the Supreme Lord within everything, never hates anything or any being `00:00 – 02:00`.

#### Two aspects of the vision [00:00 – 02:00]

- **Living beings as souls:** within every living being there is a soul; such a person does not see "those living beings" merely, but souls who are parts of Kṛṣṇa — as Kṛṣṇa describes in the Gītā (15.7) `00:00`.
- **The Supersoul within all:** amazingly, he also sees that the Supreme Being of whom everyone is a part is present inside every living being as the Supersoul `02:00`.

#### Not hating anyone [02:00]

- Such a person does not hate anyone or anything.
- The teacher explains that this means the person stays equipoised and undisturbed — an indication of being transcendental, beyond the material dualities.

#### How the purport is built [02:00 – 06:00]

- Prabhupāda correlates the mantra with verses from the _Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_ on the levels of devotees (_adhikārīs_):
  1. first paragraph — the _kaniṣṭha-adhikārī_;
  2. second paragraph — the _madhyama-adhikārī_;
  3. third paragraph — the _uttama-adhikārī_, who has equal vision, the vision of oneness.
- He contrasts that vision of oneness with the false conceptions of oneness others hold — so he describes not only the vision itself but the stages by which one reaches it and the misconceptions one can have on the way `04:00`.
- At the start of the purport Prabhupāda indirectly paraphrases a _Bhāgavatam_ verse spoken by one of the Yogendras to a king: the most advanced devotee sees all living beings as spiritual, parts of the Lord situated in spiritual reality, and sees everything situated within the Lord — and within everything he sees the Soul of all souls, the Supreme Personality of Godhead `04:00 – 06:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The sixth mantra describes a double vision — every being as a soul, part of Kṛṣṇa, and Kṛṣṇa as the Supersoul within every being — whose sign is that one hates no one and stays undisturbed. [00:00 – 02:00]
</Callout>

### The kaniṣṭha: God only in the Deity [06:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Moving from the _kaniṣṭha_ through the _madhyama_ to the _uttama_, the teacher looks at the _Bhāgavatam_ verses behind Prabhupāda's purport, showing how our consciousness must expand to perceive God's presence everywhere `06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kaniṣṭha_ — junior, beginning `06:00`.
- _Arcā_ — refers to temple worship `06:00`.
- _Prākṛta_ — from _prakṛti_, material nature (as in _puruṣa_ and _prakṛti_ in Sāṅkhya); a _prākṛta-bhakta_ is a materialistic devotee whose spiritual vision has not developed `08:00`.
- _Yukta-vairāgya_ — the heart attached to Kṛṣṇa while the body, talents and resources are all used in His service without attachment `12:00`.

#### Qualification and limitation [06:00 – 08:00]

- Qualification: he worships Lord Hari in the Deity with faith.
- Limitation: he does not respect devotees; his God consciousness is limited to the Deity.
- Such people come to the temple, take _darśana_, look around and go away — they do not associate with devotees, learn about God from them, or learn how to practise.
- Their mentality: "God is there, I am here, and I have a direct relationship with God" — the sign of a _kaniṣṭha_ `08:00`.
- Because _bhakti_ develops in the association of devotees, such a person cannot make much spiritual advancement; there may be some attraction to the Lord, but without devotee association there is no deep development of _bhakti_ `08:00`.
- Not seeing God's presence and connection with the people and things of the world, the _prākṛta-bhakta_ often quarrels: "My religious system is better than yours, my guru is better than your guru, my temple is better than your temple — I am better than you." Prabhupāda writes that this keeps the person in the mode of quarrel `08:00`.

#### Four categories by attachment [08:00 – 12:00]

The teacher sorts people broadly by their relationship with matter and with Kṛṣṇa:

- **Materialist** — attached to matter, detached from Kṛṣṇa; concerned with his own enjoyment, not with pleasing Kṛṣṇa `10:00`.
- **Māyāvādī** — detached from matter and also not concerned with pleasing Kṛṣṇa; hence a dryness: he cannot enjoy material pleasure, which he considers false, nor spiritual pleasure, because he has no relationship with Kṛṣṇa, the source of all pleasure `10:00`.
- **Sahajiyā** — attached to matter and also attached to Kṛṣṇa (correlating in many ways with the _prākṛta-bhakta_); the attachment to matter keeps dragging them down, so their attachment to Kṛṣṇa cannot be very deep, though they want to practise and may do some rituals quite diligently `10:00`.
- **Devotee** — detached from matter and attached to Kṛṣṇa. Devotees need not renounce everything material; they engage material things in Kṛṣṇa's service. If attached to those things, they would use them for their own gratification instead `10:00 – 12:00`.

#### Where we begin [12:00]

- Initially, in _bhakti_, we have some attraction to Kṛṣṇa but are also attached to _māyā_ — so in a sense all of us are _prākṛta-bhaktas_.
- Prabhupāda presented Kṛṣṇa consciousness with constant emphasis on devotee association, and by that emphasis he elevates devotees at least to the _madhyama_ level.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The kaniṣṭha worships the Deity with faith but limits God to the Deity and avoids devotees; since bhakti grows in devotee association, Prabhupāda's constant stress on association lifts us beyond this stage. [06:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### The madhyama: four relationships [12:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** The _madhyama_ level is characterised by recognising that God's presence is not limited to the Deity but extends beyond it `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Īśvara_ — the Supreme Lord; object of _prema_ (love) `12:00 – 14:00`.
- _Tad-adhīna_ — those attached to Him, His servants, the devotees; object of _maitrī_ (friendship) `14:00`.
- _Bāliśa_ — from _bāla_, child: innocent, childlike people who do not know about Kṛṣṇa; object of _kṛpā_ (mercy) — giving them knowledge of Kṛṣṇa extends His mercy `12:00 – 14:00`.
- _Dviṣat_ — the envious; object of _upekṣā_ (neglect) `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Reading "respectively" [12:00]

- In English, two lists are sometimes given and correlated by "respectively" — the first list of names, the second of the corresponding relationships. The verse works the same way.

#### Why neglect the envious [14:00]

- The more one glorifies Kṛṣṇa to the envious, the more envious they become; it is better for them not to come into contact.
- By _upekṣā_ the _madhyama_ protects them from committing further offences, and protects himself from hearing blasphemy, which would contaminate oneself.
- The _madhyama_ is a well-wisher of everyone; he serves even the envious by praying for them from a distance.

#### My relationship with Kṛṣṇa includes everyone [14:00 – 16:00]

- The basic difference: the _madhyama_ understands that his relationship with Kṛṣṇa does not depend on his relationship with Kṛṣṇa alone, but also on his relationship with everything else — because Kṛṣṇa is related with everything else.
- **The friend's family:** to please a friend I must behave nicely not only with him but with his parents, spouse and children; my relationship with him depends on how I relate with those related to him `16:00`.
- When the _kaniṣṭha_ understands that the Lord is a person with feelings whose presence pervades everywhere, and that everyone has a relationship with Him, his God consciousness expands and he comes to the _madhyama_ platform `16:00`.
- Discrimination is also part of this level: if someone blasphemes the Lord, we cannot just be silent — at least we can go away from that place and avoid hearing it `16:00`.

#### Three stages of seeing [16:00]

1. See the spiritual reality at least in the temple, in the Deity.
2. See the Lord and His relationship with everyone.
3. Higher still: do not see people as material at all, but spiritually — everybody as a devotee of Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The madhyama sees that pleasing Kṛṣṇa means relating rightly to everyone connected with Him — love for the Lord, friendship with devotees, mercy to the innocent and prayerful distance from the envious. [12:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### The uttama and the preacher [16:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** The highest stage, where everyone is seen in terms of spiritual essence `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### Everyone is dancing for Kṛṣṇa [16:00 – 18:00]

- Prabhupāda writes in the _Kṛṣṇa_ book, in the _rāsa-līlā_ pastime, that everybody is dancing to Kṛṣṇa's tune — some know it and some do not; some dance directly with Kṛṣṇa, others indirectly through _mahāmāyā_.
- The _uttama-adhikārī_ sees everybody this way — not by their external material covering, but by their spiritual essence `18:00`.

#### Why preachers come down to madhyama [18:00]

- At the _uttama_ level one sees everyone as a devotee, so one cannot preach.
- At the _madhyama_ level one sees, "This person is unaware of Kṛṣṇa's glories; let me share them and bring this person closer to Kṛṣṇa."
- Generally, therefore, the preacher comes down from the _uttama_ to the _madhyama_ level for the sake of preaching.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The uttama sees every being by its spiritual essence as a servant of Kṛṣṇa; out of compassion the preacher works on the madhyama level, where he can see who needs Kṛṣṇa's glories. [16:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### False oneness and real oneness [18:00 – 22:00]

**Context.** After this background Prabhupāda moves to another important point: the difference between false and real oneness `18:00`.

#### Bodily oneness does not work [18:00 – 20:00]

- The idea that everyone is equal at the bodily level does not work: people differ in talent, looks, species, physical abilities and intellectual abilities.
- The humanitarian idea — we are all part of the same human family, so all are equal — is valid as far as it goes, but humanism alone is not enough, because people are factually different.
- **Classroom:** even if the teacher treats everyone equally, not all students perform equally, since they have different intellectual calibres, talents and abilities `20:00`.
- We cannot artificially bring about or imagine oneness at the material level; there is diversity there.

#### Bodily diversity, spiritual unity [20:00]

- The process: accept bodily diversity and realise spiritual unity.
- Vedic culture's _varṇāśrama-dharma_ begins by accepting bodily diversity: "I have the propensities of a _vaiśya_ (or a _brāhmaṇa_), so let me serve in that particular way" — and in this way everyone makes spiritual advancement and comes to realisation of the soul.
- The principle underlying _varṇāśrama_: spiritually everyone is equal — all souls, parts of Kṛṣṇa, endowed with precious qualities; materially everyone is different.

#### The tiger [20:00]

- Prabhupāda's example: if I see a tiger as one with a human being, I may embrace the human — but if I try to embrace the tiger, I will end up inside its belly, and my relatives may come and embrace whatever skeleton is left.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Real oneness accepts material diversity and recognises spiritual unity; forcing equality on the bodily level is like embracing a tiger as if it were a human. [18:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Anupaśyati: seeing with the eyes of knowledge [22:00 – 26:00]

**Context.** In the last section of the purport Prabhupāda explains the mantra's word _anupaśyati_: oneness must be seen according to _śāstra_, not imagined artificially. He adds that only a liberated person's vision is truly beneficial; an unliberated person artificially imagining equality does not benefit anyone `22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paśyati_ — to see `22:00`.
- _Anu_ — to see systematically, with knowledge `22:00`; later, following Prabhupāda, "to follow" — one should not see with the naked eye but follow the previous _ācāryas_ `44:00`.
- _Jñāna-cakṣu_ — the eye of knowledge `24:00`.

#### We all see with knowledge [22:00 – 24:00]

- **Graph on a screen:** someone with knowledge of economics sees a downward curve and understands there is a recession — though on the screen there is only a line.
- **Bending light:** astrophysicists see light from a distant region bend instead of moving straight, and though they see no object there, they infer an object with very strong gravitational pull — some rays are absorbed completely into a black hole, others shifted.
- Even scientists must see with the eye of knowledge; with the eye alone we do not understand much.

#### "Seeing is believing" is a progress stopper [24:00 – 26:00]

- People ask, "Can you show me God?" But "seeing is believing" is a permanent progress stopper: if one believes only what one sees and never thinks about the unseen beyond the seen, one makes no advancement in knowledge.
- **Intellectually — Newton:** thousands of people saw fruit fall before and after Newton; most would think, "Let me pick it up and eat it." Newton asked why it fell, and inferred an invisible principle, gravity, from a visible observation.
- **Behaviourally — the mirage:** an animal in a desert believes the mirage it sees, chases it, gets only burning sand in its mouth, sees another mirage and keeps chasing, never reaching water `24:00 – 26:00`.
- So "seeing is believing" is a preliminary, animalistic level of intelligence — all the more so in the spiritual realm, where we must see with scriptural knowledge and systematic understanding `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Anupaśyati means seeing through knowledge, not the naked eye; just as science sees recessions in lines and black holes in bent light, spiritual oneness is seen only through scripture. [22:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### One message of the Vedas: omniform scripture [26:00 – 32:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda gives another point of oneness: scripture has one message `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Veda-eka-vākyatā_ — the Vedas have one message; the teacher cites Madhvācārya's _Viṣṇu-tattva-vinirṇaya_, one of his most important books: the Vedas seem to say many things, but the conclusive, essential message is one — Viṣṇu is the Supreme Person, to be glorified `26:00`.
- _Uniform / multiform / omniform_ — one form (all students in one dress) / many forms / all forms; scripture is omniform, giving instructions for people at all different levels `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Concession_ vs _instruction_ — a concession is what I want to do and am allowed to do; an instruction is what my authority and well-wisher wants me to do, and I agree to do it `30:00`.

#### The diabetic patients [28:00 – 30:00]

- A doctor tells a patient with acute diabetes, "Don't take sugar"; another patient who cannot give it up is allowed sugar in a regulated way.
- If the two patients meet, the first may complain: "The doctor is partial, his instructions contradict, he is useless."
- But for one who can live without sugar, sugar is not recommended; for one who cannot, it is given in a regulated way.
- Similarly the _Bhāgavatam_ gives the four regulative principles — no meat-eating, no gambling, no intoxication, no illicit sex — as the foundation for steady spiritual life.
- Some quote verses from the _Mahābhārata_ or the Vedas that talk of people eating meat; but in context it is always a concession, not an instruction — like a doctor allowing a diabetic sugar once a week because the patient wants it `28:00 – 30:00`.

#### Complementary, not contradictory [30:00]

- Because scripture is omniform rather than uniform, those who do not know it well feel it contradicts itself; actually its statements are complementary — together they complete the picture for people at different levels.
- This one message is seen only through _anupaśyati_ — by studying scripture under the guidance of proper authority and progressing in understanding.
- Prabhupāda cites the Gītā: by all the Vedas, Kṛṣṇa is to be known `30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Scripture is omniform: its varied instructions are customised for different levels and are complementary, not contradictory — their one message, Viṣṇu's supremacy, is seen only by studying under proper authority. [26:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Equal vision rightly understood [32:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** The teacher returns to "seeing everyone equally" with an exchange from Prabhupāda's time in India `32:00`.

#### "Swamiji, why don't you see equally?" [32:00 – 34:00]

- Some people told Prabhupāda that scripture says the wise see everyone equally, yet he discriminated — saying this is good, that is not good.
- The teacher cites Kṛṣṇa in the sixth chapter of the Gītā: one should see the pious and the sinful equally. How is that possible — a criminal in jail and a virtuous person receiving the Bhārat Ratna award, seen equally? Some conclude that scripture, written long ago, may contain mistakes.
- Prabhupāda's response: "First you were finding fault with me; now you are finding fault with Vyāsadeva. What is your qualification for judging the content of scripture?" `34:00`

#### A reality at a particular level of consciousness [34:00]

- In the path of _yoga_, a _yogī_ renouncing the world qualifies by seeing everything material equally — seeing beyond the body to the soul. That is a reality at a particular level of consciousness, not the level at which we function.
- Artificial equality — "all paths are equal, whatever anyone does is all right" — leads people to live in godless, sinful ways, and everybody suffers.
- The claim that all paths lead to the same goal will be discussed in a future class; the teacher notes that the ways most materialistic people live lead to much suffering, so we cannot say every way of living is the same — that kind of oneness is false.

#### The cobbler and the engineering professor [34:00 – 36:00]

- Oneness is from the point of view of everyone's spiritual potential, not the capability of teachers.
- A student who wants to study engineering cannot go to a cobbler on the street and say, "I see all beings equally, so I see you and the professor with a doctorate equally — please teach me engineering."
- With respect to learning, there cannot be equality in who is qualified to teach; one needs intelligent discernment to find who is spiritually realised and can help others realise — this is the _madhyama_ level.

#### The teacher's equal vision [36:00]

- From the student's side: be intelligent enough to check whether the teacher is qualified.
- From the teacher's side: the teacher may assess students' qualifications and talents and customise knowledge to their nature and level, but does not discriminate in wanting to give knowledge to everyone and desiring their growth — that is the teacher's equal vision.
- Prabhupāda said his equal vision was that everybody in all parts of the world has a right to become spiritually advanced, to love Kṛṣṇa and return to Him for a life of eternal happiness; with that vision he went to America, Africa, Russia, Australia — all the inhabited continents `36:00 – 38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Equal vision is not treating every path or teacher as the same; it is seeing every soul as having the same right and potential for spiritual life, while discerning carefully whom to learn from. [32:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Prabhupāda's equal vision in practice [38:00 – 44:00]

**Context.** Examples of how Prabhupāda saw everyone as a potential devotee `38:00`.

#### Bhakti Tīrtha Mahārāja and the civil rights era [38:00 – 40:00]

- He joined ISKCON at the time of the civil rights movement in America, when Black Americans, though freed from slavery about a hundred years earlier, were still discriminated against.
- Prabhupāda told him: if they think you are black and you also think you are black, what is the difference between you and them? They are acting in the bodily conception; if you respond in the same bodily conception, there is no difference.
- Prabhupāda saw no material discrimination with regard to spiritual potential, and encouraged him not to fight at the material level but to rise to a higher level of consciousness — and seeing him rise, others would also be inspired `40:00`.
- He became an exemplary devotee, known in the devotee community for the fearless and brilliant ways he distributed books `40:00`.

#### Africa [40:00 – 44:00]

- In Africa the devotees were going mostly to the Indians to share Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Prabhupāda said: this land belongs to the Africans; the Indians are immigrants — share with them too, but focus on the Africans.
- Society then had three levels — whites, blacks and Indians — and was segregated.
- The Hindu Indians, mostly well-off, were not taking it up very seriously, had built their own temples, and Black Africans never came to those temples `42:00`.
- Prabhupāda told Brahmānanda Prabhu, the leader there, to hold _harināma-saṅkīrtana_ and keep the temples open: when there is singing and dancing, passers-by will see it and want to come in `42:00`.
- His message for Africa: you want to develop, but do not develop only materially as the West has — in America there is prosperity but not happiness; develop holistically, spiritually `42:00`.
- To a reporter in Australia who asked his message for Australia, he said it was the same message for everyone: you have forgotten God, and by loving God you will become happy `42:00 – 44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prabhupāda's real oneness meant giving every person — whatever their colour, nation or status — the opportunity for spiritual life, and lifting them above bodily conflict rather than fighting on that level. [38:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Imitating the uttama [44:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda finally warns against those who imitate the _uttama-adhikārī_'s sense of oneness while behaving on the bodily platform — a false fellowship `44:00`.

#### One measure does not benefit everyone [44:00]

- We cannot do good to everyone by the same measures, because people are at different levels and must be benefited accordingly.
- A person of one nature given books in charity may say, "Why are you burdening me with books?"; a person of _brāhmaṇa_ nature given tools for menial work may say, "What are you giving me?"
- So artificial oneness does not work; fellowship will be discussed more in a later class.

#### Politicians quoting the Gītā [46:00]

- At the time of independence and after, many politicians pretended to be spiritual, quoted the Gītā and spoke of oneness.
- Prabhupāda said the _uttama_'s vision of oneness cannot be had by politicians who are always after material gain; because of that, they have dual vision — "this is good, this is bad."

#### Bodily welfare in the name of spirituality [46:00]

- There is the body and there is the soul; caring for the body is important, but it is not spiritual.
- Someone who takes care of others' bodies in the name of spirituality, considering that spiritual, is not doing good on the spiritual level, and confuses people into thinking material good is spiritual good.
- The _uttama_ sees the soul within the material body and serves him as spirit; then the material aspect is automatically served. This does not mean separating body and soul — focusing on the soul, the person is satisfied, and the body too becomes regulated and cared for. Social service will be discussed later.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Imitation oneness — identical treatment for all, politicians' slogans, or bodily welfare called spirituality — misses the uttama's vision, which serves the soul and thereby takes care of the whole person. [44:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>
