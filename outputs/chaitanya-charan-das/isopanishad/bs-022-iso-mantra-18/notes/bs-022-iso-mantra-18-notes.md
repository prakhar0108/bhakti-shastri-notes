## Mantra-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Mantra 18: a prayer to be led home [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/18 -->

**Context.** The Lord is glorified with various epithets and asked to lead the speaker on the right path and remove all hindrances, with repeated obeisances `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Agni_ — fire: as fire can burn everything, the Lord can burn all the impurities within the heart `00:00`.
- _Naya_ — kindly lead me `00:00`.
- _Supathā_ — the right path `00:00`.
- _Vidvān_ — the Lord is aware of all my actions `02:00`.

#### On behalf of all [00:00 – 02:00]

- The prayer is not just for oneself; it is offered on behalf of all living beings — "please take us to You."
- Prabhupāda's translation, as the teacher summarises it: the devotee offers all obeisances, falling at the Lord's feet, asks to be led on the right path to reach Him, and, since the Lord knows all he has done in the past, asks to be freed from the reactions to his sins so that nothing hinders his progress.

#### A spiritually evolved prayer [02:00]

- Most people feel God exists to help them in this world — true, God is ready to help here too — but ultimately He wants to take us back to Him.
- Many prayers ask for things needed here; this one asks to come back to God and for obstacles to be removed — devotional in mood and content.

#### Map of the purport [04:00]

- How obstacles on the spiritual path originate and must be overcome.
- Two ways to perfection — gradual and quick; Prabhupāda recommends the quick way.
- How the omnipotent Lord facilitates the quick, devotional way and guides the devotee.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Mantra 18 is not a prayer for worldly maintenance but a devotee's plea, on behalf of all, to be led home to the Lord and freed from whatever past sins obstruct the way. [00:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### How obstacles arise: external and internal reactions [04:00 – 14:00]

**Context.** The Lord is addressed as fire because He can burn anything to ashes, including the sins of the surrendered soul; meanwhile our past reactions come upon us to cause misery `04:00`.

#### Two kinds of reaction [04:00 – 06:00]

- **Physical reaction → external problems:** sickness, losing a job, problems in society, even having to relocate.
- **Psychological reaction → internal problems:** lust, anger, greed, envy, pride and illusion rising up and dragging us away from Kṛṣṇa.

#### "Why am I alone struggling?" [06:00]

- We see others' external problems but not their internal ones unless expressed in behaviour, so we feel that only we struggle while others practise happily.
- In fact we are all in the same boat of material existence; the waves of past conditioning hit everyone with a material mind.
- When inner passions are not controlled they come out physically, as a "slip" (almost falling, then regaining balance) or a "fall" (sprawling on the ground).

#### Gravity and the sinking boat [06:00 – 10:00]

- Blaming past conditionings for a fall is like blaming gravity for a ship's sinking: gravity pulls on all boats, without vendetta against any one; a carefully managed boat does not sink.
- Gravity is a common cause; the ultimate cause of everything is Kṛṣṇa, but He does not cause anyone's fall — there are laws of material nature, and if we don't protect ourselves we fall.
- If water enters through a hole, blame the one who made the hole or let it stay — not gravity.
- Devotional service is the boat: Gītā 4.36 says even the most sinful person can cross the ocean in the boat of transcendental knowledge; that knowledge culminates in devotion, the king of knowledge (the teacher cites 9.2) `10:00`.

#### Three ways to make a mess in the boat [10:00 – 12:00]

- **Jumping out** — giving up devotional service altogether.
- **Making holes** — secretly indulging in sinful activities while practising.
- **Steering into a storm** — deliberately entering tempting situations we know are dangerous. If a storm comes by itself, take shelter of Kṛṣṇa and He will protect; but a strong boat doesn't mean we can sail into storms.

#### We are not passive victims [12:00 – 14:00]

- We are not alone in the boat: Kṛṣṇa is there as the spiritual master and his representatives — _guru_, _sādhu_, _śāstra_ — and as the _caitya-guru_ within. When storms come, take greater shelter of them.
- Conditionings cannot overcome us unless we consent: the mind can only propose, not impose; ultimately we choose.
- Prabhupāda notes such _karma_, enacted by the illusioned living entity for sense gratification, becomes a hindrance on the spiritual path — like waves and holes obstructing the boat `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Past sins return as outer problems and inner conditionings that affect everyone; like gravity they are a common pull, but whether the boat of devotion sinks depends on our choices — so take greater shelter of Kṛṣṇa when the storms come. [04:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### The gradual way and the quick way [14:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda explains two ways to overcome these hindrances `14:00`.

#### The gradual way [14:00 – 16:00]

- Cultivating brahminical culture and good qualities, ideally in a spiritually oriented society.
- A long list: austerity, celibacy, following rules, cleanliness — striving vigorously to become purified.
- The teacher notes Prabhupāda will elaborate this gradual purification in the first verse of _The Nectar of Instruction_, to be covered in future classes.
- Important, but difficult, because it may take a very long time.

#### The quick way [16:00 – 18:00]

- Take shelter of Kṛṣṇa, connect with the spiritual master and learn devotional service from him; by Kṛṣṇa's mercy one is purified much faster than by one's own efforts.
- **Fog and sunrise:** the _Bhāgavatam_ compares material contamination to fog — you can't blow it away or catch and throw it aside, and a flashlight shows only a little; but when the sun rises the fog simply disappears. Likewise devotion to Lord Viṣṇu and chanting His names cleanse even the most sinful.
- Both ways aim at purification and manifesting godliness within, but the quick way relies more on the Lord's strength than on ours, so it is easier and more effective.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The gradual way cultivates good qualities by our own effort; the quick way invokes the Lord in the heart, and like the sunrise dispelling fog, His presence purifies far faster. [14:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Akarma, karma and vikarma [18:00 – 26:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda discusses how one may fall if careless; the teacher explains with a diagram of three trajectories `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Akarma_ — devotional service; like an elevator, it brings rapid elevation by Kṛṣṇa's mercy `18:00`.
- _Karma_ — here _karma-kāṇḍa_: following rules and regulations, going to the heavens; a slow way up that takes a very long time to liberation `18:00`.
- _Vikarma_ — sinful activity; it takes one down to lower species and planets, as described in Gītā chapter 14 `18:00`.

#### "Karmī" and contempt [18:00 – 22:00]

- Devotees often call materialists "_karmīs_" negatively, but in the Vedic context a _karmī_ (following _karma-kāṇḍa_) is positive; most materialistic people today are actually _vikarmīs_.
- Our goal is to laugh _with_ people, not _at_ them — not by joining their sinful activities, but by elevating them so we chant and dance together.
- If we are morally and spiritually better situated, it is by Kṛṣṇa's causeless mercy, not our deserving; had devotees laughed at us contemptuously, how would we have become devotees?
- We protect our _bhakti_ from being dragged away, but without moral superiority — the more we look down on people, the less we can help them; they will see us as religious fanatics.
- The example of humility: taking a blade of grass in one's mouth, falling at someone's feet and saying, "You are a great person — please take shelter of Lord Caitanya's mercy" `22:00`.

#### Spiritualising ordinary activities [22:00 – 24:00]

- Whether a devotee's familial, social, professional and physical activities count as _akarma_, _karma_ or _vikarma_ depends on consciousness.
- If the whole life is oriented to serving Kṛṣṇa — home as a temple, work as a means to glorify the Lord at home and serve His servants — other activities also become devotional service.
- _Vikarma_ such as breaking the four regulative principles is strictly avoided; some contamination is unavoidable in this world — Kṛṣṇa's example of fire covered by smoke.

#### When devotees slip [24:00 – 26:00]

- The teacher cites a Ṭhākura's explanation: a devotee's material activities (for bodily maintenance and social engagement) lie on a spectrum from inevitably sinful to exemplary; while doing _karma_ one may be dragged into _vikarma_.
- Protection comes from the practice of devotional service, which gives strength, determination and intelligence to pull oneself out of the world's traps.
- Until one is strong, occasional relapses may happen; if the devotee is steadily practising, they should be overlooked and the devotee encouraged to continue.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Akarma lifts us like an elevator while vikarma drags us down; consciousness turns ordinary duties into service, and a steadily practising devotee's occasional slips should be met with encouragement — never with contempt toward anyone. [18:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### The omnipotence of the Lord [26:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda gives a "fascinating understanding" of omnipotence: the Lord can make one born a _brāhmaṇa_ as degraded as a dog-eater, and a dog-eater superior to a qualified _brāhmaṇa_, simply on the strength of devotional service `26:00`.

#### Transformation of the heart [26:00 – 30:00]

- Omnipotence here is not lifting mountains or floating planets, but the transformation of the heart.
- **The rope again:** the one in the well holds the rope and is pulled out in minutes by the person outside. We may be amazed at how pure we have become — but it is Kṛṣṇa's grace, not our strength.
- Even near the top, if I let go of the rope, gravity still does its job. Our job is constant practice — Gītā 6.26: whenever the mind wanders, bring it back `28:00`.
- The dog-eater (_śvapaca_) is not a casteist judgement: eating dog indicates a very low consciousness; that such a person can become saintly shows the Lord's omnipotence `30:00`.

#### Why "make a brāhmaṇa degraded"? [30:00 – 32:00]

- The Lord doesn't deliberately make anyone fall; even _māyā-śakti_ is Kṛṣṇa's energy, so when _māyā_ pulls someone down, that too shows His power.
- A saintly _brāhmaṇa_'s saintliness is not independent; if he loosens his grip on the rope of Dharma and devotional service, he too will fall.

#### The Godlike power of the mind [32:00]

- When the mind troubles us, remember the Eleventh Canto's description of the mind's Godlike power — far greater than ours at present.
- It can be curbed only by taking shelter of the one who is not just Godlike but God; even the mind's power reflects His — "among the senses I am the mind."

#### When senior devotees fall [32:00 – 36:00]

- Seeing people from contaminated backgrounds purified increases our faith; seeing a senior devotee slip or fall can disturb us.
- But it should also increase our faith: no one's elevation is independent of _bhakti_'s power.
- **The lift:** a lift can take a person to the 200th floor, but if he jumps out there, gravity takes over; only outside Earth's gravitational pull would one float. Don't assume we or others are beyond the pull of material desires — we stay up as long as we stay in the lift, the boat or on the rope.
- Inquiry will show that some limb of devotional service was neglected; the problem is carelessness in tapping _bhakti_'s potency, not the potency itself.

#### More faith in His ability than doubt about ours [36:00 – 38:00]

- The omnipotent Lord in our heart is ready to use all His power to purify us.
- Being too self-conscious of our conditionings keeps us trapped; practising diligently with faith that Kṛṣṇa will help shifts our focus from our limitations to Him, which lifts and strengthens us.
- We are unable when we try alone; with Kṛṣṇa we can succeed.
- Prabhupāda explains the Lord helps the fighting devotee: when someone lovingly serves Him, giving up other inclinations, and occasionally falls, the Lord in the heart removes the impurities — He is there not to catch us but to coach us `38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord's omnipotence is His power to transform a heart; anyone who holds the rope of devotion rises, anyone who lets go falls — so have more faith in His ability than doubt about our own. [26:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Four stages in the battle against anarthas [38:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** Understanding the battle against _anarthas_ analytically helps us see where we are and how to move forward `38:00`.

#### The four stages [38:00 – 42:00]

1. **Helpless defeat** — we don't even try; told to give up meat-eating, gambling, intoxication and illicit sex, we say it's impossible. Even practising _sādhakas_ may be helplessly defeated by some conditioning.
2. **Struggle and defeat** — we resolve, pray and chant, fight when temptation comes, but still fall; better than the first stage because we put up a fight.
3. **Struggle and success** — remembering that the goal is Kṛṣṇa, not worldly enjoyment, we struggle and gradually succeed.
4. **Effortless success** — the inclination for the temptation is simply gone; the inner call of the Lord draws us, and we cannot but practise devotional service.

- Most devotees, within a few years, give up gross sinful activities, then oscillate between stages two and three `40:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa sends help [42:00]

- In "struggle and success," we may waver and consult devotees, but once we decide and try to apply the decision, Kṛṣṇa sends help — through books, devotees, the holy name, the spiritual master or a counsellor, and the _caitya-guru_.
- The real adventure of a devotee's life is continuous self-improvement — the excitement and magic are in the heart, in gradually conquering the _anarthas_.

#### The weakling and the bodybuilder [44:00]

- Initially we are like a weakling facing lust, a powerful bodybuilder who knocks us out with a single punch — helpless defeat.
- But if the weakling exercises and eats properly, he grows stronger; daily hearing and chanting are the soul's food.
- The key to moving through the stages is devotional nourishment: chanting, devotional service, rendering the best service we can to Prabhupāda and his followers.

#### Effortless success in practice [44:00 – 46:00]

- Few devotees struggle with no meat-eating even when a neighbour on a train or plane eats meat — the resolve made when taking up Kṛṣṇa consciousness seriously holds.
- Merely saying "no" to our pleasure-seeking nature makes it hungrier; _bhakti_ instead says "yes" in a more enlightened way — pleasure by loving and serving Kṛṣṇa, which requires humility.
- Rather than feeling discouraged at a lower stage, understand where we are and keep rising; never stop the nourishment, or we will become weak.

#### Never put down the sword [46:00 – 48:00]

- We never lose until we lose hope; Kṛṣṇa is always in the heart giving opportunities.
- A warrior who finds his sword too heavy and puts it down to rest his hand has his hand chopped off; a warrior who sees his weapon as a burden ends up in the graveyard. If we see devotional practice as a burden, we put aside both our sword and our shield.
- With our weapons we may be wounded but never defeated — a devotee may be checked but never checkmated.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The battle against anarthas moves from helpless defeat to effortless success through steady devotional nourishment; we lose only if we put down the sword of practice and lose hope. [38:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Never lose hope; pray to stay on the path [48:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** The class closes by grounding hope in Kṛṣṇa and returning to the mantra's prayer `48:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa hasn't lost hope in us [48:00 – 50:00]

- _Māyā_ can drag us down but cannot keep us down; it is we who keep ourselves down.
- Kṛṣṇa has accompanied us in the heart for many lifetimes, ever waiting, willing and working — if He hasn't lost hope in us, why should we?
- The short Kali-yuga life is in one sense a blessing: by serious practice for one lifetime, by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa, Lord Caitanya and Prabhupāda, we can be elevated, even liberated. The struggle is possible and glorious, because its fruit is eternal happiness.

#### Look at Kṛṣṇa, not at the world or the mind [50:00 – 52:00]

- Prabhupāda wrote to a struggling devotee that devotees should not become discouraged, because they operate on a different platform — loving and serving Kṛṣṇa; problems come and go.
- Looking at the world shows endless opportunities for enjoyment; looking at the mind shows endless problems — both discourage.
- Looking at Kṛṣṇa: "How long will I deprive myself of loving and serving such a wonderful person, who already loves me?" The world and mind remain, but their power to trouble us lessens.
- Advancement comes by Kṛṣṇa's grace, but grace requires our basic determination — like holding the rope `52:00`.

#### The time-honoured path [52:00 – 54:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport paraphrases the second chapter of the First Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_: by devotional service the devotee becomes fully enlightened, comes to the path of the Lord, and as all doubts diminish becomes a pure devotee.
- That section, roughly 1.2.6 to 1.2.21, is the basis of Prabhupāda's lecture series published as _The Way of Transcendence_: by devotional practice the lower modes subside, attachments and doubts go, and one gets spiritual vision and becomes a pure devotee.
- This path has been walked by many and laid out by the _ācāryas_ for us.

#### The concluding prayer [54:00]

- We too can pray: "My Lord, among the many paths in which I could have got lost, by Your mercy I have come to this good, supremely auspicious path. Please let me move on it, protect me when my conditionings and obstacles come, keep me fixed in Your service, and give me the intelligence and strength to keep moving toward You."
- By praying, seeking His help and doing our best, we can all be purified and return to Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Since Kṛṣṇa has never lost hope in us, we must never lose hope in Him; looking at Him rather than at the world or mind, we pray — as Mantra 18 does — to stay on the auspicious path until we reach Him. [48:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
