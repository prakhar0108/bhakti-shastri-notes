### "People are my God": the popular contrast [00:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/13 -->

**Context.** The idea that serving people is serving God is very popular today; movies and social critics contrast money spent inside temples with beggars starving outside `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mānava-sevā_ — service to human beings `02:00`.
- _Mādhava-sevā_ — service to God `02:00`.

#### A selective contrast [02:00 – 06:00]

- The poor should certainly be fed — no doubt. But is money spent inside temples the cause of starvation outside? That view is superficial.
- Beggars are everywhere — even outside the houses of billionaires, but there security drives them away; critics look at starvation only outside temples.
- Why not contrast starvation with the millions spent on watching a single cricket match, or with what the European Union and America spend on perfume — which, the teacher says, could feed the world's starving?
- Contrasting starvation only with temple worship emotionally manipulates people against God; it makes the critic feel superior to those who worship, and it exempts him from his own responsibility `06:00`.
- It is indeed the duty of the prosperous to help the poor; but manipulating emotions against God is misleading `06:00`.

#### Where the money really goes [06:00]

- The teacher shows data (a little old, he says) comparing the cost of meeting needs such as sanitation and food with spending on perfume.
- People who identify with the body want to decorate the body; people who identify with the mind want to titillate it — watching cricket and movies, spending billions on entertainment.
- If people rose to the spiritual platform, they would not identify so much with body and mind, would not spend so excessively on them, and that money could be redirected.

#### God in the beggar — and in the animal [08:00]

- Critics ask: "Why do you see God only in the temple? Don't you see God in the beggars outside?"
- One can reply: "Don't you see God in the slaughterhouse, where animals are being slaughtered? Is God not present in the hearts of animals too?"
- The aim is that people become truly God-conscious, instead of minimising God consciousness in the name of serving the poor.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Blaming temple worship for starvation is a selective, emotionally manipulative contrast; far more is spent on body and mind — perfume, sport, entertainment — and true God consciousness would redirect that, not reduce worship. [02:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### The real causes of starvation [08:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** Having set aside the temple contrast, the teacher examines what actually causes starvation `08:00`.

#### Meat-eating [08:00 – 10:00]

- In many countries, more than half the land that could grow grain for humans is used to grow fodder for animals.
- This is very inefficient: much grain fed to an animal yields only a little flesh.
- Statistics vary (the teacher mentions a range of about five to fifteen), but all agree the same land yields far more vegetable food.
- Implication: if people are serious about removing starvation, they should become vegetarian; land used to feed animals could feed humans.

#### Intoxication [10:00 – 12:00]

- Drinking is now fashionable; land worldwide is used to grow sugarcane and other crops for intoxicating drinks instead of grain, and so people starve.
- If people stopped drinking alcohol, much of that land could be used for grain.

#### Greed and mismanagement [12:00 – 16:00]

- The point is not that God should not be worshipped; in an ideal culture God is worshipped and God's children are also cared for. That is possible when people become selfless — godlessness makes people more selfish and materialistic `12:00`.
- The real problem is neither God consciousness nor a shortage of natural resources, but greed; the teacher cites figures on the concentration of wealth among the richest billionaires compared with the combined income of countries holding a large share of the world's population `12:00`.
- Starvation is not mainly due to droughts and famines but to misuse of food production for economic and political motives; he cites a source quoted by devotees in a book `14:00`.
- Many more people can be fed if they are properly cared for with God consciousness — a culture that existed in India thanks to its spiritual foundation `14:00`.

#### The solution: purify the heart [16:00 – 18:00]

- People become godless and greedy, and exploit nature and fellow human beings for enjoyment.
- Feeding the starving helps only a little, for a little time; the solution is to purify people's hearts of greed.
- The problem is not a shortage of food but a shortage of compassion; that comes from an excess of greed; greed comes from thinking material possessions will make me happy — therefore we need to connect with God.
- When we chant Hare Kṛṣṇa we participate in a process that transforms us positively and transforms others when we share it — a tangible contribution to the world `18:00`.
- Everyone is a child of God; Kṛṣṇa teaches equal vision towards living beings. Spiritual advancement is good for us and for the whole world `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Starvation is caused not by worship or scarcity but by greed — expressed in meat-eating, intoxication and the misuse of resources — and greed is removed only by purifying the heart through God consciousness. [08:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Why material help alone is not enough [20:00 – 30:00]

**Context.** The teacher looks at starvation from two perspectives: the greedy who exploit others, and the poor themselves `20:00`.

#### Two perspectives [20:00 – 24:00]

- **The greedy** — their greed is reduced by spiritual education and the experience of higher happiness, which lessens their infatuation with material things.
- **The poor** — they too have their own problems; even family members who know drinking hurts the family keep drinking. This self-defeating mentality results from the inability to control oneself `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### The leaking pot [24:00]

- If people are not in a position to be helped, they will misuse the help because they lack self-mastery.
- Helping them is like pouring water into a leaking pot — however much water you pour, it simply drains away.
- If you give money to a beggar, what will he do with it? Good intentions do not necessarily produce good results.

#### Psychological needs before physical ones [26:00]

- The teacher gives an everyday family example about food bought from the market.
- We need a process that transforms the needy when they cannot use what they have to fulfil their needs, because psychological needs can feel more urgent than physical ones — a person may buy alcohol before food or clothing.

#### The devotees' contribution [28:00]

- Devotees can contribute to the world in a remarkable way — something even great material power could not achieve.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Without inner transformation, material help is water in a leaking pot; spiritual education addresses both the greed of the exploiters and the self-defeating habits of the needy. [20:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### The Vedic system and the right place of social service [30:00 – 42:00]

**Context.** The teacher looks at the overall Vedic system and how social service fits within it `30:00`.

#### Social service that attracts people to Kṛṣṇa [30:00]

- Social service can be used to attract people to Kṛṣṇa: when food is distributed, people's perceptions of devotees change and they may eventually come to Kṛṣṇa.
- Material welfare is not unimportant; the teacher refers to a forest-fire episode in which those caught cried out "please help us."

#### Kṣatriya and brāhmaṇa responsibilities [30:00 – 32:00]

- Providing material welfare is primarily the _kṣatriya_ responsibility — social, cultural and political arrangements for people's material needs.
- Spiritual welfare means arranging for and directly giving spiritual education.
- Both work together, so material and spiritual welfare are both taken care of.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda said he was looking for brahminical people — the head of society; when he wanted full _varṇāśrama_, he wanted bodily needs taken care of too.

#### Our distinctive contribution [32:00]

- Many institutions provide material welfare (such as hospitals) and they are needed; very few offer spiritual welfare — that is our distinctive contribution.
- At the same time, devotees may open hospitals, run charitable food programmes and other community work, integrated with devotional service.

#### What is wrong — and what is not [34:00]

- The problem is not social service; the problem is making social service into God.
- The statement "people are my God" falsifies God and redirects society's energy away from Him — that is what must be strongly refuted.
- Devotees must not become insensitive human beings in the name of devotion either.

#### Charity to the poor is not a waste [34:00 – 38:00]

- The teacher discusses those who say: "I won't give charity to anyone except the temple — my money is for Kṛṣṇa, not for the poor."
- Such people show symptoms of wretchedness, cruelty and lack of compassion; they should not consider giving charity to the poor as mere fruitive activity — otherwise their hearts become hard and they suffer from greed.
- If we think all our money is reserved only for Kṛṣṇa and show no compassion, we may become so greedy that we will not spend it even on devotional service.
- If we cannot show kindness to those around us, how can we show affection to God? Giving some money to the poor is not a waste.
- The poor may have become poor by their karma, yet they are still part of the Lord's family, and it is the duty of every honest person to help them; we are not here to judge people on how they acted in the past `38:00`.

#### Soft heart and hard heart [38:00 – 40:00]

- **Hard heart** — one's emotions are restricted to one's own interests; one cannot think of others in terms of their emotions.
- **Soft heart** — one is sensitive to the emotions of others.
- Not only "feel for Kṛṣṇa" but "feel like Kṛṣṇa": Kṛṣṇa feels sorry seeing His children suffer and wants to help them; so we feel that Kṛṣṇa's children should be taken back to Godhead.
- Soft-heartedness is extended to other people and, through them, further on to Kṛṣṇa.
- Even if people suffer because of bad karma, we want to remove their suffering at the deepest possible level — through Kṛṣṇa consciousness — while our own hearts become softer and more sensitive `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Social service has a real place — as the kṣatriya duty, as outreach and as compassion — but it must not be made into God; and devotees who refuse all kindness to the poor in Kṛṣṇa's name only harden their hearts. [30:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Integrating social service with spiritual service [42:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** The teacher contrasts the material and the devotional perspectives on social service and shows how to integrate them `42:00`.

#### Two perspectives on social service [42:00 – 46:00]

- Do not think social service itself is the best thing in life.
- **Material perspective** — ordinary people concerned only with their own eating and sleeping; social workers are better than them.
- **Devotional perspective** — pure devotees, situated in transcendence, are constantly compassionate to people. Compassion for the soul does not mean "I take care of your soul but ignore your body."
- The problem arises if social work creates a wrong attitude towards devotion — "they only chant and dance and do nothing practical; I am doing much better" — and treats religious functions as mere ceremony. The teacher calls this attitude demoniac and says it is definitely condemned `44:00 – 46:00`.
- Such people may be concerned for more people than others, materially speaking, but their negative attitude to devotion is the problem `46:00`.

#### Expanding the circle of compassion [48:00]

- If we help people become God-conscious, they in turn will help many others.
- The aim is not to condemn social work but to integrate it with spiritual work, with spiritual work as the superset.

#### The anti-corruption movement and Gopāla [48:00 – 50:00]

- The teacher refers to a movement demanding a strong judicial body (a _lokpāl_) to punish the guilty, because corruption is eating up the social body. Punishing the guilty is important.
- But does punishment change people's hearts? It only makes them more fearful and cautious about corruption.
- Kṛṣṇa is called _Gopāla_: _go_ means the senses, so Kṛṣṇa is the protector of the senses.
- When the senses are used in Kṛṣṇa's service, people experience a higher happiness than the sinful, selfish allurements of the world provide; by this higher happiness Kṛṣṇa protects them, and they become free from greed `50:00`.
- This has to happen at three levels: educational, devotional and administrative `50:00`.

#### Connecting mānava to Mādhava [50:00 – 52:00]

- **Connecting people to God** — book distribution, _harināma-saṅkīrtana_ and outreach programmes give people the possibility to connect themselves with God.
- **Making Mādhava accessible to _mānava_** — beautiful temples in which the Lord is glorified; people are automatically welcomed.
- Recently in Pune, the President of India came to see a temple: if God is there, it is worth coming to see. People are attracted by material things, and using material things for God is a way of attracting them to God.
- Similarly, Śrīla Prabhupāda held a press conference in a big five-star hotel in Hong Kong `52:00`.

#### Summary [52:00 – 56:00]

- Mantra 13 connection: people worship something other than God, considering it the Absolute Truth, and go into darker regions because they are misled.
- Starvation is not because so much money is spent on God, nor because nature is short, but because resources are mismanaged through people's greed.
- Greed can be removed only through purification — the experience of higher happiness through spiritual education.
- People in and of themselves are not God; we should not make them God and think there is no need to worship God.
- At the same time we should be compassionate and soft-hearted towards people.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The best service to humanity integrates social service within spiritual service: connect people to God through outreach, make God accessible through beautiful temples, and let higher happiness free hearts from the greed that causes suffering. [42:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
