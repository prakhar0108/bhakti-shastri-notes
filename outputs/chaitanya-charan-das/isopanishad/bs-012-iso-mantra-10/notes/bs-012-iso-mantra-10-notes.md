### Contrast as a teaching method [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/10 -->

**Context.** Mantra 10 sets the results of _vidyā_ against those of the previous mantra's darkness `00:00`.

#### Why scripture uses contrast [02:00]

- Contrast is one of the most important methods of description for convincing a message: it helps us decide what we want to do.
- Kṛṣṇa uses it repeatedly in the Bhagavad-gītā, making things crystal clear — for example: if you become conscious of Me, you will pass over all obstacles by My grace; if you do not hear Me, you will be lost.

#### The purport's approach [02:00 – 04:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda explains _vidyā_ as a set of desirable qualities, and in the final paragraphs relates it to society.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Like Kṛṣṇa in the Gītā, the Īśopaniṣad teaches by contrast — setting the result of real knowledge against the darkness of ignorance so that we can decide which path to take. [00:00 – 02:00]
</Callout>

### Vidyā as qualities: humility, respect and non-violence [04:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda explains _vidyā_ on the basis of the qualities of knowledge listed in Gītā 13.8–12 `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ahiṁsā_ — normally translated as non-violence; Śrīla Prabhupāda gives a deeper translation: one should not become a source of anxiety to others by the actions of one's body, the thoughts of one's mind, or one's words `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### Humility shown as respect [04:00 – 08:00]

- One of the qualities is to learn to give proper respect to others.
- Saying "I am so fallen, I am good for nothing" may not really be humility; humility is more directly expressed in how we respect others.
- Getting rid of pride is difficult, but we can surely start respecting others — a positive way to cultivate humility, because in respecting others we give them thought, attention and time.
- Pride means we are so filled with ourselves that no one else matters.
- Humility is not so much thinking less of ourselves; it is thinking of ourselves less — "I am not an important person in the world, and I don't have to think about myself all the time."
- Even if I have good qualities, I don't demand or expect respect; demanding respect without the qualities is worse still `06:00`.

#### A profound definition of ahiṁsā [10:00]

- Non-violence means not only avoiding physical harm, but not becoming a source of agitation or anxiety to others through thoughts, words and actions.

#### "Maximum mileage" [10:00]

- Among the items listed is worship of the spiritual master.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda says one should use whatever knowledge and function one has with "maximum mileage" — like a car that gives maximum mileage per litre.
- The teacher notes Śrīla Prabhupāda's vivid English: he wanted to convey the profound wisdom of the Vedic literature in a way people can apply and benefit from.

#### A genuine spiritual master [12:00]

- One must go to a spiritual master — not simply an "_āśīrvāda_ guru" who will smile and raise his hand in blessing, but a person who will gradually lead one to spiritual realisation.
- One approaches with submission, service and inquiry.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Vidyā is visible as qualities: humility expressed by respecting others, non-violence in body, mind and words, and approaching a genuine spiritual master who leads one to realisation. [04:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### The real problems and the real solution [12:00 – 20:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda points out that knowledge means seeing the problems of repeated birth, death, old age and disease, and finding the means to become spiritual again `12:00`.

#### Specific problems and fundamental problems [12:00 – 14:00]

- We face specific problems — "I lost my job," relationship problems, health problems, tension with someone, trouble with a laptop.
- These specific problems come and go, but the actual problems are four bigger ones: birth, old age, disease and death.
- There is no use making plans to get rid of these on the material level.

#### Raising consciousness [14:00]

- These problems are solved by raising our consciousness: on the material level of consciousness they remain, but when we understand "I am the _ātmā_," old age does not apply to the soul.
- Spiritual culture gives a training for our consciousness that a materialistic psychology cannot.

#### Science, philosophy and spiritual knowledge [16:00 – 18:00]

- Some in the humanities argue that science only makes things faster and better, while the humanities speak of what makes human beings human; there were even "science wars" — intellectual battles in books and papers, written and counter-written, between philosophers of science and scientists.
- Through philosophy one can research and come to spiritual knowledge; through science also one can come to spiritual knowledge. Whatever one studies — physics or anything else — should ultimately lead to spirituality.

#### Spiritual knowledge is permanent [18:00]

- Material knowledge ends with the death of the body, but spiritual knowledge stays.
- Quoting Arjuna's question in Gītā 6.37–39 and Kṛṣṇa's reply: spiritual knowledge comes back, and one becomes attracted to spiritual principles almost helplessly, making further spiritual advancement.
- Today, asked about their education, people simply list degrees.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Our real problems are birth, old age, disease and death, not the passing troubles of the day; they are solved by raising consciousness to the soul — and spiritual knowledge, unlike material knowledge, is never lost. [12:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Four changing conceptions of knowledge [18:00 – 28:00]

**Context.** The teacher traces how the idea of knowledge has changed, using three figures: Prahlāda Mahārāja, Socrates and Francis Bacon `18:00`.

#### Knowledge is devotion — Prahlāda Mahārāja [20:00]

- Prahlāda Mahārāja lists the nine processes of devotional service and says this is the best education.
- These nine activities increase our attraction to the Supreme Lord, and that is the best knowledge — the transcendental understanding of knowledge.
- The _Bhāgavatam_ says that if our attraction to the Supreme Lord does not increase, whatever else we do is simply a waste of time and labour.
- In Gītā 10.8, Kṛṣṇa describes what happens to the wise person who knows Him as the source of everything — such a person becomes devoted.

#### Knowledge is virtue — Socrates [22:00]

- Knowledge should be reflected in a person's character; if it is not, what was acquired is not of much use.
- The idea that knowledge is virtue is consistent with the Vedic understanding: knowledge arises from goodness, and knowledge leads one to live in goodness.
- Knowledge is cultivated not as information in the head but as transformation of character; in Gītā 13.8–12 Kṛṣṇa speaks of developing these qualities.

#### Knowledge is power — Francis Bacon [22:00 – 24:00]

- In European history, one pioneer of empiricism thought about developing science and technology to control and change the world in future, and said "knowledge is power."
- This is clearly in the mode of passion: the mentality of "how can I make everything happen according to my will."
- Technology is essentially about bending nature to the human will.
- Gadgets — pressing a button to send an email or make a call — are useful, not bad; but when knowledge is reduced to this one aspect, we gain only external, technological power, not internal power or willpower.
- "Knowledge is virtue" means knowledge leads to willpower, and willpower enables one to use technology properly. If my knowledge does not develop good qualities, the more power I have, the more my bad qualities will use it to do harm.

#### Knowledge is information — today [28:00]

- Today knowledge has become simply information, much of it irrelevant — such as who a film star's daughter is; someone wins a quiz and gets a crore, yet such information does not change the person's life.
- Because irrelevant information is valued so much, contexts like quizzes are created to give it value.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge has descended from devotion (Prahlāda) to virtue (Socrates) to power (Bacon) to mere information (today); real knowledge transforms character and increases our attraction to the Lord. [18:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Education divorced from transformation [24:00 – 40:00]

**Context.** "The divorce of education from transformation leads to disruption" — the teacher's central thesis for the second half of the class `24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Education_ — from a root meaning "to bring out" what is within; in the Vedic view, godliness is to be brought out through education `24:00`.

#### Disruption [24:00 – 28:00]

- When education becomes merely a skill for external control, it is divorced from transformation, and this causes disruption in the social structure, because the person lacks the self-mastery to contribute properly.
- The teacher mentions young students in the United States and elsewhere taking guns and shooting, including at universities.
- The modern measure of success in education is just the capacity to hold information; transformation is not considered.
- Value education is now offered in many universities, and it is good and important — but values must be coupled with culture. If the whole culture is sarcasm and sardonic speech, it counters whatever value education teaches.
- Even from a material perspective, campus culture includes smoking and drinking: much information in the head, but no effective transformation `28:00`.

#### What you know vs how you live [28:00 – 30:00]

- What you know is not as important as how you live.
- A famous superstar who had met several spiritual teachers asked Śrīla Prabhupāda how to recognise a true spiritual teacher. Śrīla Prabhupāda replied: see who is most _addicted_ to Kṛṣṇa — not just attracted, not just devoted, but addicted; the word is normally negative, but in relation to Kṛṣṇa it is precise and profound.
- One may talk a lot about Kṛṣṇa, but is one attracted to Him in one's life?

#### Double standards [30:00 – 32:00]

- People say animals should be treated kindly and ethically; if a dog is beaten, a complaint about cruelty is made — while the complainants are eating a breakfast of animals and saying animals shouldn't be tied so tightly.
- Such double standards arise from the divorce between one's learning and one's living.

#### Learning is for living [32:00]

- Learning is for living — not just for earning.
- Euclid, the pioneer of geometry, was asked by a student what practical use geometry had; Euclid told his servant to give the fellow a coin, since he wanted to gain something from learning.

#### The cleverness of fault-finding [32:00 – 34:00]

- Students caricature teachers, talk behind their backs and make fun of them; whoever cracks the most satirical joke is considered cleverest.
- Kali-yuga is an ocean of faults; finding fault in it needs as much cleverness as finding water in the ocean.
- If you are in an ocean and want to show cleverness, find the land, which gives shelter. Real cleverness is finding the good in others.

#### Self-mastery and sense control [34:00 – 36:00]

- A century or so ago, most education in the West was based on a culture of self-mastery: a student who wants to study seriously must control the senses, and the greatest distraction of the mind is created by contact with the opposite sex — so education and social life were kept separate.
- Sexual feelings create the illusion "I am a big hero"; regulation and discipline are required for real education.
- Without this training come free sex, abortion, sexually transmitted diseases, teenage pregnancy, heartbreaks and divorce.
- Student life is meant to focus energy on character development, including respectfulness toward elders.

#### Intelligence abused [36:00 – 40:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda speaks of scientists developing weapons of destruction; using intelligence to develop weapons that destroy others is a tragic abuse of God-given intelligence.
- Before the Second World War, many scientists knew what kind of weapons could be developed; the arms industry creates a catch-22 — if one country develops weapons, others must too, and intelligence is diverted toward defence.
- Albert Einstein, considered the greatest brain of the last century, sent the letter to the US President that led to the Manhattan Project and the atom bomb dropped on Japan, destroying two cities; he said, roughly, that his fear was that Hitler might develop it first `38:00`.
- His best insight, the interchangeability of mass and energy, led to the worst destruction in human memory in a single attack `40:00`.
- The culture of _ahiṁsā_ is absent and a culture of violence is cultivated. Scientists may say they only develop technology, but tomorrow it can fall into the hands of terrorists — such destructive use of intelligence is bred by misunderstanding the values and purpose of life `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Education divorced from transformation breeds disruption — double standards, fault-finding as cleverness, sensual indiscipline and the abuse of brilliant intelligence for destruction; learning is meant for living, not just for earning. [24:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Pseudo-religion [40:00 – 46:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda next speaks of pseudo-religion; one of the qualities of knowledge is not wanting to show off `40:00`.

#### Religion for display [40:00 – 42:00]

- A pseudo-religionist performs activities publicly so that people can see how religious he is; the goal is not to be devoted to God but to be seen by people.
- If it is socially advantageous to appear religious, such a person will maintain the appearance without cultivating substance.
- Festivals with huge _paṇḍals_ — for example, Gaṇeśa festivals in Maharashtra and that part of India — where the size of the Gaṇeśa image becomes an expression of the size of the funds collected; there is appearance without genuine religious culture or spiritual understanding.

#### Cost-free initiation [42:00 – 44:00]

- Some who want to be spiritual masters say the guru's mercy costs nothing: water from washing the guru's feet is sprinkled, and whoever is fortunate to have a drop fall on them is "initiated."
- Everybody wants to be initiated, since who wouldn't want something that costs nothing?
- Such initiation has no commitment, no regulative principles and no rules — a mutual self-serving arrangement: the guru wants disciples, the disciple wants the honour of having a guru, and the guru does not perform the real function of religion with substance.
- This leads to undesirable qualities.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Pseudo-religion seeks to be seen rather than to be devoted; display without substance and initiation without commitment are mutual self-service, not spiritual life. [40:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Nationalism and the substance of things [44:00 – 52:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda next mentions nationalism as an undesirable quality. The teacher acknowledges the topic is sensitive and asks that it be understood carefully from a broad, universal perspective `44:00`.

#### The universal perspective [44:00 – 46:00]

- In the vastness of space there are so many planets and galaxies floating; seen from a transcendental perspective, nationalism on one planet is not very intelligent.
- Because of a bad system of education, people acquire bad qualities like pride and nationalism.
- Most people consider patriotism a good quality; a nationalist is one who is proud of his nation and thinks "my nation is the best."
- Śrīla Prabhupāda calls it a bad quality because it is ultimately based on the understanding "I am the body; this body was born in this country, so this country is special and I have a special bond with it" `46:00`.

#### The Bhāgavatam's cow-and-ass verse [48:00 – 50:00]

- Things normally considered good are, from proper spiritual understanding, like the behaviour of a cow or an ass. The verse the teacher paraphrases describes one who:
  - identifies with the body made of material elements,
  - considers bodily relatives to be his own,
  - considers his land of birth worshipable,
  - goes to holy places just to take a bath, without associating with the enlightened people there.
- The _Bhāgavatam_'s vision is penetrating — it goes to the substance. It rejects not only bodily attachment and family attachment but even nationalism and superficial religion without substance.
- The substance is associating with enlightened people and, in their association, making genuine spiritual advancement `50:00`.

#### Living in a nation without bodily identification [50:00]

- Living in a particular nation, we follow its laws and offer the respect expected; but this should not fix our consciousness in the bodily conception.
- Identifications like "I am American" or "I am Pakistani" are undesirable; we must rise to a higher understanding of life to make genuine advancement.
- As discussed in an earlier class, we don't have to reject everything Indian: whatever belongs to one's cultural tradition can be understood as something to share with the world.

#### Caitanya Mahāprabhu's instruction [50:00 – 52:00]

- Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu says that one born as a human being in India should make his life successful and share it with the world.
- The implication is not nationalistic, patriotic sentiment; it is that Indians have many spiritual inclinations, should practise spirituality and share it. The focus is not on India but on the spirituality latent within India and Indians.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Nationalism rests on bodily identification; the Bhāgavatam looks to the substance — association with the enlightened — and Mahāprabhu's call to Indians is to share spirituality, not to glorify a nation. [44:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### "Acquired by chance" and learning from the sober [52:00 – 60:49]

**Context.** Real knowledge begins by hearing from a sober person who is not deluded by material things. The teacher turns to the last paragraph of the purport `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dhīra_ — the sober person from whom _vidyā_ is to be learned; an _adhīra_ cannot be an instructive leader `52:00`, `56:00`.

#### Why "by chance"? [54:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda writes that the material body and mind are acquired by chance. The phrase is significant, because the first lesson of spiritual life is that nothing happens by chance — everything happens by the will of God.
- "By chance" does not mean there is no plan, order or pattern; it conveys that there is no intrinsic connection between the soul and the body and mind.
- The soul and body are essentially different and have come together because of circumstances — not disorderly circumstances, but past _karma_. I have had bodies like this in the past and may have many in future unless I purify myself and become liberated.

#### Making the best of a bad bargain [56:00]

- The body and mind are foreign elements; we simply make the best use of a bad bargain.
- The body is a bad bargain because it causes limitations, and through it the three kinds of miseries (_ādhyātmika_, _ādhibhautika_ and _ādhidaivika_) come to us.
- Even when we want to do good or serve, the body limits us physically, distracts us psychologically and gets tired; we use its energy in service to whatever capacity we can.

#### Learn from the sober, not the merely learned [56:00 – 58:00]

- _Vidyā_ must be learned from a _dhīra_; an _adhīra_ cannot be an instructive leader.
- Politicians may quote the Gītā and appear learned, but most are simply busy with remuneration in "dollars and cents" — how to earn more and more. A few are genuinely concerned for society, but the majority are concerned with their own interests, and so cannot do good to others.

#### Class summary [58:00 – 60:49]

- _Vidyā_ leads to the development of good qualities.
- In this mantra, _avidyā_ refers to anything that fosters ignorance and sense gratification, and _vidyā_ to anything that fosters spiritual advancement; they lead to different results.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda explains _vidyā_ through the twenty elements of knowledge from the Gītā, with "maximum mileage."
- The changing conceptions of knowledge: knowledge as devotion (Prahlāda), as virtue (Socrates), as power (Bacon), and today as irrelevant information.
- The divorce of education from transformation leads to disruption: untrained students cannot become moral, responsible citizens, and violence, wastefulness and destructiveness follow.
- The _Bhāgavatam_ goes to the substance of things: love for one's country, love for one's family, and information, without proper spiritual knowledge, remain at the animal level; we must purify ourselves and realise Kṛṣṇa, which happens only when we learn from the right source `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul has no intrinsic connection with the body it acquired through karma; making the best of that bad bargain requires learning vidyā from a sober person, which alone purifies us and leads to realising Kṛṣṇa. [52:00 – 60:49]
</Callout>
