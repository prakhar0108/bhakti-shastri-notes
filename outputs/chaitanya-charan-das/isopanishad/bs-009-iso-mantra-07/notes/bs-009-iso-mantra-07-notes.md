## Mantra-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### The mantra and the plan of the purport [00:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/7 -->

**Context.** The teacher reads the mantra: the person who knows all living beings systematically — seeing them on the spiritual platform, as spiritual — has no more illusion (_moha_) or lamentation (_śoka_), because he sees oneness. As in Mantra 6, the emphasis is on _anupaśyati_, seeing with knowledge `00:00 – 02:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's translation [02:00]

- One who always sees all living entities as spiritual sparks, in quality one with the Lord, becomes a true knower of things; what then can be illusion or anxiety for him?
- The teacher notes that Prabhupāda explains "oneness" not only in the purport but already in the translation — "in quality one with the Lord."

#### Why the purport elaborates oneness [02:00 – 04:00]

- The whole purport elaborates the mantra's word for oneness, because that word is "like a feast for the impersonalists."
- The Upaniṣads are generally considered books about impersonalism, so impersonalists use them a lot; however, they also speak of the personal features of the Absolute Truth, though not as their emphasis.
- Prabhupāda explains oneness on the basis of the broader understanding of the Vedic scriptures.

#### Outline of the purport [04:00]

1. Energy and energetic: the example of fire, heat and light — one, yet different.
2. Drop and ocean: one in quality, different in quantity.
3. Oneness in interest — in inclinations, desires and intentions: a family whose members are different but united in their interests is happy; if each pursues only his or her own interests, the family is chaotic. A nation of individual citizens can likewise be one cohesive whole.
4. In the spiritual realm too, when everyone is one in interest there is enjoyment.
5. Separate interests cause lamentation and illusion; therefore we should become one in interest with God — making the will of God our own.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The mantra promises freedom from illusion and lamentation through seeing oneness — and the purport insists that oneness means one in quality and one in interest with the Lord, not a featureless sameness. [00:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### Detachment and humility [04:00 – 10:00]

**Context.** Complete realisation of oneness has two aspects `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### Oneness in quality brings detachment [06:00]

- When I understand "I am not inert matter; I am spirit, a conscious being," I see that dead matter cannot give me happiness and temporary things only cause misery, because I am eternal.
- Understanding that I am in quality one with the Absolute, I become detached from matter and seek happiness on the spiritual level.
- This realisation is desirable, but not complete.

#### Subordination in quantity brings humility [06:00 – 08:00]

- Thinking only "I am spirit" can bring pride — especially "all these other people are attached; I am detached."
- But understanding "I am spirit, yet there is a Supreme Spirit and I am tiny" brings humility.
- On the impersonal path, oneness in quality is emphasised; on the path of devotion, subordination in quantity is emphasised. Mahāprabhu taught that we are servants of Kṛṣṇa — the devotees' primary emphasis `08:00`.
- Devotees also know they are not the body; but the soul is not formless spirit — it has a real, living relationship with the Absolute Truth based on love and service. "God is great and I am small" — small not only materially but even spiritually — brings humility `08:00`.

#### Why humility must come first [08:00 – 10:00]

- If detachment comes first and is the exclusive focus, it leads to pride.
- If humility comes first — "I am not the enjoyer; I am the servant; let me serve" — service gives a higher taste, and the detachment that comes from that higher taste does not bring pride, because the foundation of humility is already established.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka:** in the third verse Mahāprabhu exalts humility — humbler than a blade of grass, more tolerant than a tree — and only then, in the fourth, speaks of detachment. Both verses conclude with advancement in devotion: first humility, which brings us closer to Kṛṣṇa and fixes us in service, which then makes detachment easier `08:00 – 10:00`.
- The error of impersonalism: focusing only on detachment makes one vulnerable to pride, and the greatest of all pride is "I am God." That aspect of impersonalism — Māyāvāda — will be discussed later `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowing "I am spirit" gives detachment, but knowing "I am a tiny servant of the Supreme Spirit" gives humility; when humility comes first, the detachment that follows from higher taste does not turn into pride. [06:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Energy and energetic: the sun's three energies [10:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** Oneness is not absolute; there are gradations and subtleties. Jīva Gosvāmī in the _Sandarbhas_ analyses Prabhupāda's fire example further `10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Antaraṅga-śakti_ — internal energy; spiritual; the _svarūpa-śakti_ `12:00 – 14:00`.
- _Bahiraṅga-śakti_ — external energy; material `12:00 – 14:00`.
- _Taṭastha-śakti_ — marginal energy; also spiritual. All of us souls are the marginal energy: our position is variable, and we can go towards the external or the internal energy `14:00`.

#### The limit of the fire example [10:00 – 12:00]

- Wherever fire is, heat and light are there; the fire is the organic whole spreading its influence through heat and light — so the energetic and the energies are interconnected yet different (fire burns, fire illuminates).
- But the fire example cannot show that some energies are closer to the energetic and some further away — the gradation of energies.

#### Sun example: not the usual one [12:00]

- Normally the sun illustrates Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān: the all-pervading effulgence is the _brahmajyoti_; the orb reflected in many rivers is like the one Supreme Lord manifest as the Supersoul in many places; entering the sun planet and seeing the sun-god is like Bhagavān.
- Here Jīva Gosvāmī uses the sun not for the three aspects of the Absolute but for His various energies.

#### Radiance, rays and reflection [12:00 – 16:00]

- The sun-god, the predominating deity of the sun, corresponds to Kṛṣṇa.
- **Radiance** — the intense, blazing effulgence just around the orb: the _svarūpa-śakti_ (internal energy), which manifests the Supreme's consciousness.
- **Rays** — shooting far away from the sun: the _taṭastha-śakti_. They carry the sun's heat and illumination, but less so; going further and further away they eventually become cold and perish. Likewise, the further the soul goes from the Lord, the more it comes under material influence and the less it shows its spiritual nature.
- **Reflection** — in a river or a pot: the _bahiraṅga-śakti_. Neither the sun's heat nor its illuminating power is manifest in it; so matter, also an energy of the Absolute, does not exhibit consciousness and is not eternal.
- All three come from the sun and are its energies, yet they do not manifest its power to the same degree.

#### One and different, and different among themselves [16:00]

- The energies are non-different from the Lord, yet different from Him; and they are also different among themselves — different kinds of energy.
- The teacher calls this a very profound concept explaining the Lord's relationship with the world, its components and the souls: there is oneness, because everything depends on the sun, but within the oneness there is variety.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
All energies come from the Lord and are one with Him, yet they manifest Him in different degrees — like the sun's radiance, rays and reflection — so oneness always contains gradation and variety. [10:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Unity in diversity, diversity in unity [16:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** "Unity in diversity" is a popular slogan — a nation of many races, religions, languages and cultures seeking national integration. It has importance, but spiritual reality also has diversity in unity `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### From variety to unity to diversity [18:00]

- **Material variety:** male and female, _brāhmaṇa_ and _śūdra_, wealthy and poor.
- **Unity:** all are souls, and as souls all are equal — the first step in spiritual realisation.
- **Diversity within that unity:** souls are not "zero copies" or clones of each other; every soul is an individual, unique, with a unique relationship with Kṛṣṇa.
- **Unity again:** they all love Kṛṣṇa, serve Kṛṣṇa and delight in loving and serving Him.
- _Bhakti_ takes us beyond unity in diversity to diversity in unity.

#### The rāsa dance [20:00]

- When Kṛṣṇa called with His flute, all the _gopīs_ came — united in their love for Him.
- Yet every _gopī_ and her love are uniquely individual; each adds a special flavour to Kṛṣṇa's relationships that would not be there without her. Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of all _rasas_, enjoys a distinct _rasa_ with each.

#### The gopīs' prayers of separation [20:00 – 24:00]

- In the Tenth Canto, chapters 29 to 33 are the _rāsa-pañcādhyāya_, considered the climax of the _Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_: if the Tenth Canto is the face of the Lord, the _rāsa-pañcādhyāya_ is His beautiful smile `20:00`.
- Within it, the 31st chapter — the _gopīs_' prayers of separation — is the most intense in devotional fervour. Śukadeva Gosvāmī introduces them generically: "the _gopīs_ said." One famous prayer describes how glorifying the Lord extinguishes the blazing fire of material existence `20:00`.
- Did all the _gopīs_ rehearse and recite in chorus? No — Jīva Gosvāmī in the _Gopāla-campū_, knowing each devotee's heart, attributes particular prayers to Lalitā, Viśākhā, Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, Campakalatā and others `22:00`.
- Unity: all are heart-broken in separation and long for Kṛṣṇa to return; every prayer calls Him back. Diversity: each prayer comes forth from one _gopī_'s own heart.
- **A symphony of love:** different instruments under one orchestrator produce a magnificent performance `22:00`.

#### Fertile, not barren, oneness [24:00]

- The _gopīs_' oneness is not barren; it is a fertile, rich reciprocation — what the Vedic scriptures ultimately lead to.
- Where is this in the Īśopaniṣad? The Upaniṣads are transitional literature, taking people from material forms towards spiritual forms; they hint at the personal aspect. In the last section, Mantras 14 to 18, the soul prays to the Absolute to remove the covering and reveal His face — which implies a personal form not otherwise described.
- So Prabhupāda gives the full understanding of oneness from the whole body of Vedic literature, not only from what the Upaniṣads say.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Spiritual oneness is diversity in unity: like the gopīs, each soul offers a unique love, yet all are united in wanting to please Kṛṣṇa — a symphony, not a monotone. [18:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### The river meets the ocean [24:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda's drop-and-ocean example (one in quality, different in quantity) prompts the teacher to examine the related river-and-ocean metaphor that impersonalists often use: the river rushes into the ocean and merges, so the goal is to merge into the Absolute `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### Meeting, not merging [26:00]

- The metaphor does not speak only of merging; it speaks of meeting. The aspect impersonalists neglect is the _flow_ — the devotees pray, "Let my heart flow towards Kṛṣṇa."
- The metaphor appears twice in the _Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_:
  - **3.29.11** — Kapila, instructing His mother, describes His devotees: as the Gaṅgā's water moves towards the ocean, when they hear His glories their consciousness flows to Him without distraction or obstacle. It is the mind that flows; the devotee does not merge `26:00 – 28:00`.
  - **1.8.42** — the same image from the devotee's side: "My dear Lord, let my consciousness flow towards You as the Gaṅgā flows to the ocean" `28:00`.

#### Nothing holds back [28:00 – 30:00]

- A commentator on 1.8.42 gives an insight: when a river flows to the ocean, the river holds nothing back and nothing holds the river back.
  - **The river holds nothing back** — it does not keep some water for itself; all its water flows to the ocean. Likewise a devotee holds nothing back — internal commitment.
  - **Nothing holds the river back** — it goes below, around, over or even through an obstacle, eroding it. Likewise nothing holds a devotee back — external obstacles.

#### Nothing holds a devotee back [30:00 – 34:00]

- In any situation a devotee finds some way to keep serving and remembering Kṛṣṇa: unable to serve physically after an accident, he finds another service; unable to speak, he can chant in the mind and do _mānasa-pūjā_.
- **The jail Sunday feast:** in an American city the mayor had allowed book distribution, but while the mayor was out of station the sheriff, with whom he had a quarrel, had the devotees arrested on a complaint that they were creating a nuisance. It was Sunday morning; crestfallen at missing the Sunday feast, they decided Kṛṣṇa accepts _mānasa-pūjā_ and held a mental Sunday feast in jail — each "cooking" a preparation, a senior devotee giving class, real _kīrtana_, and a "feast" with no item in short supply and no digestive problems. The guards remarked that the devotees inside the jail were happier than they were outside. The temple president contacted the mayor, a release letter came, and they reached the real Sunday program `30:00 – 34:00`.
- **Prabhupāda's last days:** so old and sick that his voice was practically inaudible, but with clear consciousness he kept dictating his commentary on the _Bhāgavatam_ into a Dictaphone held at his lips `34:00`.

#### A devotee holds nothing back [34:00]

- As the river offers all its water, a devotee offers everything to Kṛṣṇa; the teacher cites the Gītā (5.17): offering one's intelligence, faith, mind and goal, one moves swiftly on the path back to Godhead.

#### The yajña-brāhmaṇas' wives [34:00 – 38:00]

- In the Tenth Canto, chapter 23, the wives of the sacrificial _brāhmaṇas_ come to Kṛṣṇa with food; He accepts it and asks them to go back.
- Kavi Karṇapūra, elaborating from his realisations, describes their reply: a river that has left the mountain and reached the ocean cannot go back to the mountain.
- Kṛṣṇa, moved, says their hearts will always be with Him; He has accepted their offering of love, but since they are married He wants them to serve by returning to their duties — the meeting of hearts is forever, and He will be with them in their hearts.
- So oneness is dynamic: two people in love are two as individuals yet one in heart, each wanting to please the other. This is the meaning revealed in the _Bhāgavatam_ and _Purāṇas_, which illuminate what the Upaniṣads teach `36:00 – 38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The river metaphor teaches meeting, not merging: the devotee's heart flows to Kṛṣṇa holding nothing back, and no obstacle — not even a jail cell or old age — holds it back. [26:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Object, vision and consequence [38:00 – 50:00]

**Context.** Seeing oneness alone is not difficult — in the dark everything looks one; when light comes we see a wall, a computer, a chair. Seeing only oneness is preliminary spiritual vision; _anupaśyati_ means seeing the variety within the oneness `38:00`. The teacher then sets out how objects and visions combine `38:00`.

| Object | Vision | Consequence | Underlying principle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Material | Material | Bondage | "It is for my enjoyment" |
| Material | Spiritual | Purification | Utility is the principle |
| Spiritual | Material | Offence (_aparādha_) and suffering | Reducing the transcendental to the material |
| Spiritual | Spiritual | Purification, and in advanced stages ecstasy | Seeing the Deity as Kṛṣṇa |

#### Material object, material vision → bondage [38:00 – 40:00]

- A man sees a woman not as a soul but only as a good-looking form; a woman sees a man only as a male form — "a man sees a woman as a sex machine, a woman sees a man as an ATM machine."
- Attraction to sense objects with material vision leads to bondage; this is what predominates for most people.

#### Material object, spiritual vision → purification [40:00 – 42:00]

- A laptop, a projector and other devices are material, but seen as Kṛṣṇa's energy meant for His service, they can be engaged in His service, and that brings purification. Utility is the principle.
- Prabhupāda wrote that this movement was practically "invented" by him — not the scriptures, which were already there, but how to get people practically to serve. How to engage people "whose only regulative principle was to break the regulative principles"? `40:00 – 42:00`
- **Ratha-yātrā:** the hippies loved to dance and sing wildly; dancing in a public street would have got them arrested, but at Ratha-yātrā, a cultural festival from an ancient tradition, they danced escorted by the police. It began when Prabhupāda, chanting on the balcony of his quarters in San Francisco, saw a flatbed truck and saw it as a pedestal for Jagannātha, Baladeva and Subhadrā, to be pulled by people `42:00`.

#### Spiritual object, material vision → offence [42:00 – 46:00]

- A famous verse in the _Padma Purāṇa_ says that seeing the Deity as stone is a hellish mentality; it is offensive and leads to suffering `42:00 – 44:00`.
- "But the Deity is made of material elements!" — yes, but it is no longer material, because Kṛṣṇa has manifested there. The offence is to not see the transcendental at all and reduce it to the material platform `44:00`.
- **Gurus by birth:** saying "my spiritual master is more advanced because he was born in a _brāhmaṇa_ family and yours was not" is offensive vision. Advanced devotees have practised for decades; to rank them by a background from long ago — skin colour, family — sees only the external shell and misses a heart sold out to Kṛṣṇa for decades `44:00 – 46:00`.
- At best, such offence means missing spiritual advancement (I won't pray to the Deity or receive His blessings to the same extent); at worst, one commits grievous offences that bring further suffering `46:00`.

#### Spiritual object, spiritual vision → purification and ecstasy [46:00 – 48:00]

- Seeing the Deity as Kṛṣṇa leads to purification in the initial stages of _bhakti_ and to ecstasy in the advanced stages.
- When the curtain opens, advanced devotees need no rule to offer obeisances — beholding the Deity, non-different from Kṛṣṇa, they faint in ecstasy and fall down. We don't faint, but we should at least bow down.
- The Deity is spiritual, because Kṛṣṇa has manifested through it; seeing it spiritually unlocks its great potency for rapid advancement `48:00`.

#### The pattern [48:00]

- Material vision creates problems in both cases — bondage towards material objects, offence towards spiritual ones.
- Spiritual vision is recommended in both cases — it leads to purification and elevation.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Material vision binds us to matter and offends the spiritual, while spiritual vision purifies us — engaging matter in Kṛṣṇa's service and seeing the Deity as Kṛṣṇa Himself. [38:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Spiritual vision is not whitewashing [48:00 – 58:00]

**Context.** Seeing everything spiritually does not mean a simplistic whitewash, calling everything spiritual; the philosophy begins by differentiating matter and spirit `48:00`.

#### Applying "I am not this body" [48:00 – 50:00]

- Before Prabhupāda went to America, many spiritual teachers had emphasised "I am spirit" (_ahaṁ brahmāsmi_), and it was a popular saying even among the hippies.
- Prabhupāda was the first to emphasise categorically its practical application: "You are not the body — therefore give up sense gratification."
- If I keep enjoying bodily pleasures and stay caught up in material desires, I remain in bodily consciousness, and repeating "I am spirit" will not benefit me much.

#### The ABC: discriminate, then engage [50:00 – 52:00]

- I cannot say the body is spiritual or all food is spiritual; I must discriminate — "this is _prasādam_, this is not." The ABC of spiritual life is to discriminate, on the basis of knowledge, between matter and spirit.
- With advancement, one sees in the material the potential to be engaged in the service of Kṛṣṇa — so devotees do not reject technology.
- Some impersonalists reject money, but Prabhupāda said money is Lakṣmī, meant for Nārāyaṇa.
- **Hanumān and Sītā:** Hanumān did not reject Sītā as material; he found her, brought her message back to Rāma and fought a war so that she could be united with Him. Likewise a devotee works actively to engage material things in Kṛṣṇa's service `50:00 – 52:00`.
- Not everything is usable: poison is also material energy, but taking it is not favourable to _bhakti_, so it is not used. What can be engaged varies with our advancement, specific service and circumstances `52:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's vision of the world [52:00 – 56:00]

- Prabhupāda saw the whole world as filled with potential devotees: everybody is a soul, actually a devotee of Kṛṣṇa, with some covering; remove the covering and everyone becomes a devotee.
- Some of Prabhupāda's godbrothers, advanced devotees, went to Europe; they met elite people, had photographs taken and gave much-appreciated discourses, but were not very successful in transforming hearts. They told their spiritual master, Bhaktisiddhānta Ṭhākura, that the Europeans were too rooted in their culture to change `52:00`.
- Arriving in Boston Harbor on the _Jaladūta_, Prabhupāda wrote a song. Where most Indians would see America as a land of dreams, he did not see wealthy, happy Americans but forgetful, suffering souls pulled by the modes of passion and ignorance, and prayed for words that would help them understand the Lord's message and become happy `54:00`.
- He did not think "I am Indian, they are American"; he saw their cultural and social conditioning keeping them from Kṛṣṇa, but saw beyond it to their souls `54:00`.
- So thousands who knew nothing of Kṛṣṇa a few weeks earlier gave up everything and dedicated their lives to Him: he saw their spiritual potential, kindled the spark, and it blazed, spreading Kṛṣṇa consciousness miraculously all over the world `54:00 – 56:00`.
- For us: in book distribution and outreach we don't focus on how people look; we offer Kṛṣṇa consciousness to everyone and guide anyone with a spark of interest in the best way available to them `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Spiritual vision first discriminates matter from spirit, then sees in every thing and every person the potential for Kṛṣṇa's service — as Prabhupāda saw suffering souls, not Americans, and kindled their spark. [48:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
