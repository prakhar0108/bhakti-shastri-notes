## Mantra-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Meaning of the mantra: working "in this way" [00:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/2 -->

**Context.** Mantra 2 continues the previous mantra: "in this way" refers to accepting one's own quota and not encroaching on the quota of others `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- "To do certainly in this way" — working in the manner described in Mantra 1 `00:00`.
- One may desire to live for "hundreds of years" — the teacher explains the word as a form of the word for a hundred `02:00`.
- "No alternative" — there is no other way for human beings `02:00`.
- "Bound" — working in this way, one does not become bound by _karma_ `02:00`.

#### Why desire a long life only if one lives this way? [02:00 – 04:00]

- Everyone wants to live long, so why make long life conditional on living in this way?
- If a person lives ignorantly or sinfully, the longer he lives, the more karmic bondage he creates — a longer life simply prolongs the miseries of material existence into future lives.
- If a person lives harmoniously, he creates no bad future for himself and also contributes to the good of society.
- For such a person a long life is a blessing — desirable for him, and others also wish him a long life.

#### "There is no other way" [04:00]

- People obviously do live in other ways, including godlessly; the mantra means there is no other _intelligent_ way to live.
- **The 20th-floor example:** if the staircase is the only way down, someone may say you could also jump from the terrace — you will come down, but never come up again. Any other way is bound to end in disaster.
- Likewise, not living harmoniously is the way to disaster; this way brings no karmic bondage.

#### Five sections of the purport [04:00 – 08:00]

1. Karmic bondage (_karma-bandhana_) causes death — connected to the mantra's point that nobody wants to die.
2. Human life offers liberation through _akarma_.
3. The Vedas teach many things, but their conclusive teaching is _akarma_ — essentially pure devotional service.
4. Since people cannot begin _akarma_ immediately, they start by bringing God into a life of sense gratification — doing it in a God-conscious rather than irreligious way.
5. Without God everything is in vain, so all "isms" should be made into _yoga_ — spiritualised, so their benefit lasts instead of being fleeting.

- The theme of the whole purport: acknowledge the _īśvara_ and live according to the _īśāvāsya_ principle `06:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A long life is worth desiring only when it is lived in harmony with the Lord; living any other way only lengthens karmic bondage, like choosing to leave a tall building by jumping off the terrace. [02:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### Why the eternal soul suffers death [08:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda opens the purport with the observation that no one wants to die; the teacher explains how karmic bondage causes the experience of death `08:00`.

#### The soul and the body [08:00]

- The Bhagavad-gītā (13.34) compares the soul to the sun: as the sun illuminates the whole universe, the soul illuminates and activates the body with consciousness. Kṛṣṇa also uses the example of ether pervading nature.
- The soul is eternal (_sat_) and does not have to die; the body is temporary.
- The soul experiences the misery of death only because of attachment to the body.

#### The movie-theatre analogy [08:00 – 12:00]

- An audience becomes emotionally attached to a heroic character; when the villain strikes the hero dead on screen, everyone is shocked — yet nothing has happened to them in their seats.
- The soul is like the audience; the body is like the character on screen.
- **Who suffers?** Not the body — a dead body cannot suffer; not the soul in itself — it is _sac-cid-ānanda_. The one who suffers is the soul who identifies with the body and mistakes the body's events for its own. We suffer because of misidentification `10:00`.
- To the extent we desire to enjoy material things, to that extent we become bound by _karma_: the soul came to "watch the movie" — to seek enjoyment through materialistic activity — and material life turns out to be a horror movie `10:00`.
- The more we identify with the body, the more our suffering increases; the ultimate suffering is death, when the body is destroyed and the soul feels traumatised.
- The immortal soul suffers the pain of mortality because of the desire to enjoy matter, which is the cause of _karma_ and _karma-bandhana_ `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul cannot die, yet suffers death because it identifies with the body, like a spectator shaken by a character's death on screen; the root is the desire to enjoy matter, which creates karmic bondage. [08:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Human life offers liberation through akarma [12:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** All living beings must die, but in the human form one can perceive the eternal beyond the temporary and the spiritual beyond the material, and realise it through _akarma_ `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Four meanings of the word _karma_ `12:00`:
  1. Action of any kind.
  2. The reaction to action ("I'm suffering because of my karma").
  3. The system of action and reaction ("the law of karma").
  4. Within action, one of three types of activity — good action.
- _Karma_ / _sukarma_ — actions performed according to one's prescribed duties in the revealed scriptures; acting morally and responsibly `14:00`.
- _Vikarma_ — bad action, the misuse of freedom, which directs one to lower life forms; e.g. one who eats meat may be given the body of a lion or tiger, better suited for it `14:00`.
- _Akarma_ — not inaction, but action that produces no reaction; the prefix negates _karma_ in the sense of reaction, not action. Such actions free one from the cycle of birth and death `14:00`.

#### How akarma frees us [14:00 – 16:00]

- When we act for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure, we suffer no reactions, because Kṛṣṇa, staying in our heart, is working for our purification and wants to liberate us.
- **Patient and doctor:** a patient who cooperates with the doctor recovers more smoothly, quickly and fully; working for Kṛṣṇa is cooperating with Him.
- **Back to the movie:** _akarma_ is like the spectator deciding, "I don't want to watch any more — it's illusion; let me get out." Whatever happens on screen then no longer affects him.

#### Acting on someone else's behalf [16:00]

- A soldier who shoots enemy soldiers while serving in the army is not held responsible — he may even get a medal; if he shoots a neighbour after an argument, he is punished.
- Acting on our own account binds us; acting on Kṛṣṇa's behalf liberates us.
- The whole purpose of the Vedic literature is ultimately to teach _akarma_. Prabhupāda says such liberating work is described in the Īśopaniṣad and immediately correlates it with the Bhagavad-gītā — something he does repeatedly throughout these purports.

#### Supplement, complement or replacement? [18:00 – 20:00]

- Prabhupāda states that factually no one has to do anything more than render devotional service to the Lord — but in the lower stages one can neither immediately adopt devotional activities nor completely stop fruitive work `18:00`.
- **Supplement** — something extra, like a dietary supplement; **complement** — what completes something by supplying what is essential.
- Spiritual life is ultimately neither: it is a _replacement_ for material life. We are spiritual; material life is unnatural for us, and in our pure stage we return to the spiritual world to live entirely spiritually.
- To think of spirituality as an optional extra (a supplement) is an initial, ignorant vision; even at the material level it is a complement — without God our lives remain incomplete `20:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Akarma is not inaction but action on Kṛṣṇa's behalf, which brings no reaction — like a soldier acting for his army; spiritual life begins as the essential complement to material life and ultimately replaces it. [12:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### The Vedas lead gradually to akarma: spiritualise all isms [20:00 – 26:00]

**Context.** The Vedas speak of religion at multiple levels. Prabhupāda makes a striking statement about altruism, socialism, communism, nationalism and humanitarianism `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma-bandhana_ — karmic bondage; the "isms" are "very attractive forms" of it `20:00`.
- _Karma-kāṇḍa_ — sense gratification plus God; religious materialism `22:00`.

#### An iron fist in a velvet glove [20:00 – 22:00]

- Altruism, humanitarianism and nationalism appear very good, soft and attractive, but they still bind.
- If I am attached to my nation, I will not be liberated; I may be reborn in that country — as some other species, depending on my _karma_, or as a human — but I continue in material existence.
- Therefore God must be added to whatever we do; without acting by the _īśāvāsya_ principle one cannot remain free of karmic bondage.

#### Two intersecting circles [22:00]

- Picture one circle of sense gratification and another of _dharma_; their intersection is _karma-kāṇḍa_ — dharmic sense gratification, religious materialism.
- Materialists want a good spouse, wealth and power; scripture says, "Do these rituals, worship this deity, perform this sacrifice, and you will get these things."
- Most people come to God not because they love God but because they love the things of this world and cannot get them themselves — that is the beginning of most people's spiritual life.
- Done dharmically, sense gratification brings gradual elevation; done without God, it is the cause of bondage. The same applies to altruistic work for society or nation — God must be kept in the centre.

#### Prabhupāda spiritualising isms [24:00 – 26:00]

- **Indians:** he encouraged them through pride in their identity — "The Gītā is your own cultural legacy; people all over the world are taking it up and becoming missionaries — why don't you?"
- **The whole world as Hindustan:** in the 1970s, when Bangladesh was separating from Pakistan, he told an audience concerned about losing part of the country that he, an old man alone, had made devotees all over the world; if all of them shared Kṛṣṇa consciousness, the whole world would become Hindustan.
- **Americans:** during a large Hare Kṛṣṇa _paṇḍāl_ festival in Mumbai, he told his American disciples, "You Americans are used to doing wonderful things — do something here people will never forget."
- He also predicted that when the Chinese take to God consciousness, they will take it up very fast.
- The principle: use people's existing attraction — to nation or identity — to engage them within the precincts of _dharma_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even noble "isms" are attractive forms of bondage unless God is brought in; the Vedic way, which Prabhupāda modelled, is to take people's existing attachments and steer them into the service of God. [20:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### The Gītā's strategy: pat the false ego onward [26:00 – 36:00]

**Context.** The same strategy of spiritualising all isms appears in the Bhagavad-gītā itself `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Parā-dharma_ — higher religion: pure devotional service — "I am the servant of Kṛṣṇa; I need do nothing except serve Him" `26:00`.
- _Aparā-dharma_ — lower religion: "I have a family, country, race and community, and duties toward all of them" — important, though not all-important `26:00`.
- False ego — the idea "I am the body"; the bond of the soul's misidentification with the body `28:00`.

#### Bodily designations right after "you are not the body" [26:00 – 28:00]

- BG 2.13 teaches that the soul inside the body does not change even as the body keeps changing — solid philosophy that, taken in a detached analytical way, would reject all bodily designations.
- Yet in the very next verse (happiness and distress come and go like summer and winter; tolerate them), Kṛṣṇa addresses Arjuna by two bodily designations: son of Kuntī and descendant of King Bharata.
- Kṛṣṇa is not contradicting Himself: though bodily designations are ultimately illusory, we do identify with the body and cannot wish that away. The Gītā uses that identity to elevate us — "You are born in a great family, so act in a great way; therefore tolerate."

#### Three ways to treat the false ego [28:00 – 32:00]

- **Massaging it** — "India is the greatest country; everything good in the world came from India; you are so great." This simply increases illusion.
- **Pinching it** — "India is dirty, corrupt and useless; you are a soul, you have nothing to do with India." Most people emotionally identify with their country, community, race or dynasty, so this is grating (jarring, like a harsh sound) and denigrating (insulting) `30:00`.
- **Patting it onward** — "You were born Indian, so learn and teach the Gītā"; "Americans do extraordinary things, so do something extraordinary for Kṛṣṇa"; "You are the son of great Kuntī, so act greatly — don't get caught in bodily misidentification; act according to the glory of your position as a soul" `30:00 – 32:00`.
- The principle: rather than rejecting people's material identity, use its emotion and pride to get them to act in responsible, respectable, spiritually elevating ways `32:00`.

#### Prabhupāda at the wrestlers' gymnasium [32:00 – 34:00]

- Prabhupāda visited a traditional bodybuilding place where wrestlers train. Instead of saying "you are not the body," he said, in a Hindi lecture: "Society needs _kṣatriyas_; you are training _kṣatriyas_ as we train _brāhmaṇas_ — when the two come together, society is well administered."
- Noticing their small image of Hanumān, he pointed out that Hanumān did superhuman feats of strength in Lord Rāma's service while always chanting Rāma's names — so chant "Rāma" while doing push-ups and exercises.
- He spiritualised their urge for a strong body rather than dismissing it.

#### The food-distribution example [34:00 – 36:00]

- To someone eager to feed the hungry, saying "the food will pass out as stool in six hours — what's the use?" is dismissive; a tender-hearted person may simply go away.
- Instead: _anna-dāna_ is highly glorified in the Vedic literature, and the best kind feeds not only the body but the mind and soul — when the food is offered to God and served as _prasāda_, it nourishes the body, purifies the mind and awakens the soul.
- A religiously minded person will readily agree, and the charity becomes spiritualised.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Neither massage nor pinch people's false ego — pat it onward: as Kṛṣṇa addressed Arjuna by his lineage to make him act greatly, we can use people's identities and good impulses to lead them toward God. [26:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Multi-level spirituality: from kindergarten to doctorate [36:00 – 40:00]

**Context.** People cannot jump from the kindergarten of religious life to the doctorate level, so the Vedic scriptures offer step-by-step spirituality `36:00`.

#### The levels [36:00]

- **Doctorate** — pure devotional service: "just practise devotion; there is nothing else to do."
- **Kindergarten** — there is a God, there is life after this one, we are accountable for our actions, and we should act so as to return to God.
- Telling someone who does not believe in God to give up everything for God will make him give up God instead.
- The first step: "You want material things — religion will provide them, and by that you will be elevated." At this level one may also warn: without religion, material life is doomed.

#### The zeros and the one [36:00 – 38:00]

- A hundred zeros in a row are still zero; only when a one is placed in front do they acquire value.
- Similarly, material life acquires value only when God is brought into it.
- Prabhupāda writes that there is no harm in being a family man, altruist, socialist, communist, nationalist or humanitarian, provided one acts in relation to the God-centred conception `38:00`.
- So one need not speak against household life to someone inclined to it — that only creates a negative conception of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. "No harm — become a family man, but keep God in the centre."

#### From temple to home to heart [38:00 – 40:00]

1. "My home is everything; God may exist, but I don't care."
2. "There is a temple of God; I should go there."
3. "God should be in my house too" — an altar comes, then God comes to the centre of the house.
4. "My house is also a temple."
5. "My heart should be a temple" — and the person becomes a surrendered devotee.

- So: first bring a person to the temple, then the temple to his home, then the temple to his heart `38:00 – 40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Vedic spirituality is multi-level: like adding a one before many zeros, bringing God into ordinary life gives it value, and from there a person progresses from temple, to home, to heart. [36:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Without God everything is in vain [40:00 – 46:00]

**Context.** The next paragraph of the purport: with God we flourish; without God we perish `40:00`.

#### No loss on the spiritual path [40:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes BG 2.40: on this path there is no loss or diminution, and even a little progress saves one from the greatest danger.
- Whatever little we do spiritually can give eternal benefit — the teacher cites the example of a man who named his son Nārāyaṇa, called that name at the moment of death, and was saved from the danger of going to hell when the Viṣṇudūtas intervened.

#### A humble God-centred life [40:00 – 42:00]

- Prabhupāda's memorable line: a humble God-centred life is more valuable than a colossal hoax of a life dedicated to godless altruism or socialism.
- A little-known person who chants and serves God attains an eternal destination; a big social worker who does not care for God may do some material good, but spiritually it will not help — he continues in the cycle of birth and death.
- Without God, whatever we do may be good, but it is not good enough to get us out of karmic bondage. (Social service will be discussed in detail in a future class.)

#### Living like a tree [42:00 – 46:00]

- Prabhupāda paraphrases a _Bhāgavatam_ verse: trees live for hundreds of years, but there is no point in living long like a tree. This correlates with the mantra — wanting to live for hundreds of years is pointless if one lives like a vegetable, making no spiritual advancement.
- **What makes a life valuable is not its length but its content.** The teacher notes, as Prabhupāda would point out, that some great figures lived short lives — around 48, 33 and 32 years — yet made unparalleled contributions; when good content is joined with long life (he cites a Vaiṣṇava who lived past 100 sharing pure God consciousness), that is even more glorious `44:00`.
- **The blacksmith's bellows** — air moves in and out, so they seem to breathe, but they are not conscious and make no spiritual progress `44:00`.
- **Animals** — eating and producing children are done by cats and dogs too; nothing glorious about it.
- Living like that deprives us of life's higher potential; it is discouraged and even castigated, and this "shock treatment" grows stronger in the next mantra `46:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Life's value lies in its content, not its length; without God even great altruism remains within karmic bondage, while a humble God-centred life reaches the eternal. [40:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Half-finished work is not lost — and the path to Nectar of Devotion [46:00 – 54:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda explains that even half-finished God-centred activities guarantee a human form in the next life, giving another chance on the path of liberation `46:00`.

#### Three steps in the purport [46:00]

- BG 6.37–45: a yogi who cannot complete the journey in this life continues in the next, born in a wealthy, brahminical or devotee family, and keeps advancing.
- Prabhupāda's three steps:
  1. Add God to whatever you do — it will spiritualise it.
  2. Without God, everything is a waste.
  3. With God, even partial practice brings benefit; you will not be lost.
- The focus of the mantra: act with the _īśāvāsya_ principle — live in harmony with God and _dharma_.

#### A preliminary argument that leads to bhakti [48:00 – 50:00]

- Prabhupāda says the details of God-centred activity are explained in a Sanskrit work he rendered into English as _The Nectar of Devotion_.
- That book says nothing about _karma-yoga_; it describes intimate, exalted and intricate details of _bhakti-yoga_.
- Here Prabhupāda presents only _karma-yoga_ — bring God into your work — a preliminary level, argued so that religiously inclined people find it intelligible. Once they want to know more, he points them to the Gītā and then to that book, where they get a complete understanding of _bhakti_.
- **Entering enemy territory:** an army does not attack the capital first; it penetrates a weak point and advances further. Likewise, to people who think their material life is their duty, Prabhupāda says, "Family, dynasty, country are important — bring God into them"; reading further, they learn that _karma_ and _jñāna_ are temporary and how to practise pure devotional service.
- Prabhupāda's purport is a masterpiece of elevating people gently and progressively, not dismissively `50:00`.

#### A note on the text's history [50:00]

- Prabhupāda wrote the Īśopaniṣad not as a book but as a series of purports in his original _Back to Godhead_ magazine, before he had written _The Nectar of Devotion_ (which is a general summary study of the Sanskrit work, not a word-for-word translation).
- So this paragraph — especially the sentence recommending _The Nectar of Devotion_ — was added later by the editors.

#### From God as facilitator to God as goal [52:00 – 54:00]

- In _karma-kāṇḍa_ the idea is: "God, come here and help me with the pastimes I want to perform in this world."
- In pure devotional service, Kṛṣṇa is performing pastimes in the spiritual world and says, "Come and join Me there."
- The ultimate goal of _dharma_ is to take us from the material world to the spiritual world; until then, bring God to the level where people are.

#### Summary of the purport [52:00 – 54:00]

1. Karmic bondage causes death — the eternal soul is bound by the desire to enjoy matter.
2. _Akarma_ leads to liberation — understanding a life beyond matter, one turns away from it.
3. The Vedas lead gradually to _akarma_, beginning with _karma-kāṇḍa_ — bringing God into a life of sense gratification.
4. God enters "through a crack" in our life, expands His place and finally takes over our heart — the ultimate success.
5. Without God, even social service and nationalism are of no ultimate use.

- **Closing principle:** bring God into your life, and God will bring you into His life — lifting us to His eternal abode of eternal love and happiness `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
No sincere God-centred effort is lost; Prabhupāda's purport begins at the preliminary level of karma-yoga to lead readers onward to pure devotion — bring God into your life, and He will bring you into His. [46:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
