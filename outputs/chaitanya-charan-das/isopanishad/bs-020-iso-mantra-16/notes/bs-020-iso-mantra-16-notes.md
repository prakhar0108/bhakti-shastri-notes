## Mantra-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Mantra 16: glorification and a plea [00:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/16 -->

**Context.** The mantra combines glorification of how great the Absolute Truth is with a request — kindly remove, kindly withdraw — so that the speaker can see the Lord's form `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pūṣan_ — maintainer; the word also appeared in Mantra 15 `00:00`.
- _Ṛṣi_ — philosopher, thinker; the Lord is the original thinker `00:00`.
- _Yama_ — as in _yama_ and _niyama_ at the start of the _yoga_ process: rules, regulative principles; Yamarāja is one who regulates, and ultimately the Lord is the supreme regulator `00:00`.
- _Sūrya_ — can mean the sun, but also the destination of the _suras_ (devotees) `00:00`.
- _Rūpa_ — form `02:00`.
- _Kalyāṇa_ — auspiciousness; the word used is the superlative — supremely auspicious, as in "good, better, best" `02:00`.

#### A real form [02:00]

- Some say the Upaniṣads speak only of impersonalism; they do speak of it, but there is personalism too.
- If there is any doubt whether "form" here is merely metaphorical — no: the sage says "I want to see that form." The form is real and supremely auspicious.
- "As the sun is effulgent, so am I" — the speaker compares himself to the effulgent Lord.

#### Honest reading of the Upaniṣads [02:00 – 04:00]

- The impersonalists take only "so am I" — "I am that" — to argue the Absolute is non-personal and that the self and the Absolute are ultimately the same.
- To represent the Upaniṣads honestly, we must take everything they say, not only the parts that agree with one's own philosophy.
- Calling the inconvenient parts merely relative (_vyāvahārika_) and one's own part ultimate (_pāramārthika_) "rips apart the _śāstra_" — it imposes one's philosophy on scripture rather than understanding it.
- The reconciliation: Caitanya Mahāprabhu's teaching of qualitative oneness and quantitative difference `04:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's translation and purport map [04:00 – 06:00]

- Prabhupāda's translation glorifies the Lord as primeval philosopher, maintainer of the universe, regulating principle, destination of the pure devotees and well-wisher of the progenitors of mankind, and asks Him to remove the effulgence so that the devotee can see His form of bliss; it makes clear that "like unto the sun, as am I" refers to qualitative oneness.
- The purport's points, taken one by one in the class:
  1. Beyond Brahman is Bhagavān.
  2. Bhagavān is the most auspicious realisation.
  3. As maintainer, the Lord specially maintains the devotees and in general everyone else.
  4. The living entity is one with yet different from the Lord.
  5. The Lord's favour sought by this prayer is fulfilled in pure devotional service.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Mantra 16 asks to see a real, supremely auspicious form; read honestly and as a whole, it shows the Upaniṣads moving toward the personal Lord, with "so am I" meaning qualitative oneness only. [00:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### Beyond Brahman is Bhagavān [06:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Both impersonalists and personalists consider their own conception the highest reality; the teacher compares the two `06:00`.

#### Two opposite conceptions [06:00 – 08:00]

- Impersonalists: Brahman is highest; when a form is imposed on or accepted by Brahman, Bhagavān appears — something illusory and temporary is added, so Bhagavān is lower.
- Vaiṣṇavas: Bhagavān is the highest truth; when the _cit_ and _ānanda_ aspects of the Absolute are not properly comprehended and only _sat_ is grasped, one sees Brahman.

#### What scripture shows [08:00 – 10:00]

- The Upaniṣads, the Purāṇas and even the original Vedas contain many prayers, which indicate a higher reality to whom one prays.
- The idea that one must ultimately go beyond that reality and merge is not substantiated by scripture; it has to be extrapolated.
- Kṛṣṇa says He is the foundation of Brahman (cited from Gītā chapter 14): Brahman rests on Him and is a partial manifestation of Bhagavān.

#### Intellectually appealing but incomplete [10:00]

- Impersonalism may seem intellectually appealing — no forms, no personality, just oneness — but it strips the Absolute of an attribute, form, so something is missing.
- It also loses all emotional appeal: a dry, abstract consciousness with no reciprocation or activity has little appeal for the heart.
- That is why many impersonalists still encourage _bhakti_ as a lower path for "less evolved" people — but scripture teaches that devotion continues to the end; the teacher cites Gītā 9.2, that it is imperishable.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Brahman is Bhagavān partially understood — only the sat aspect — and rests on Him; impersonalism leaves the Absolute incomplete intellectually and empty emotionally. [06:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Why Bhagavān is most auspicious [10:00 – 22:00]

**Context.** The teacher gives several reasons why the Lord is _kalyāṇa-tama_, the most auspicious `10:00`.

#### Highest and best destination [10:00 – 12:00]

- Highest — He is beyond Brahman and Paramātmā.
- Best — in Brahman there is no reciprocation and no real satisfaction for the heart; in Bhagavān the heart's longing for love is eternally and completely satisfied.
- Therefore Kṛṣṇa repeatedly assures that one who returns to His abode does not come back here.

#### The rope in the well [12:00 – 14:00]

- We have all fallen into a well.
- Climbing the wall by finding finger-holds and foot-holds = advancing by one's own concocted methods — possible but extremely difficult, with a large chance of falling back.
- The rope = the scriptural process.
- Two ways of using the rope:
  - _Bhaktas_ hold the rope and beg the person outside to pull them out — they practise devotion and pray to the Lord for help.
  - _Jñānīs_ and _yogīs_, for whom Bhagavān is not the ultimate reality, often do not pray to Him; they try to climb the rope themselves — possible, but more laborious.

#### Easy, yet rare [14:00 – 16:00]

- Gītā 8.14: the one verse where Kṛṣṇa says attaining Him is easy — for one who fixes the mind on Him and serves Him.
- Yet in 7.19 He says one who knows Him as everything and surrenders is a very rare _mahātmā_; in 9.13 He describes the _mahātmās_, clearly devotees.
- Reconciliation: _bhakti_ can raise anyone to the highest destination, but because of misconceptions many people minimise it as simplistic and do not follow it. The Lord personally helps His devotees, so they advance rapidly.

#### Knowledge, taste and mercy [16:00 – 22:00]

- **Knowledge** — "I am not the body, I am the soul"; material enjoyment is temporary and entangles. For many it strikes like a thunderbolt, shaking their old world-view and lighting new hope `16:00 – 18:00`.
- **Taste** — repeating "I am the soul" alone does not satisfy, just as repeating "I am a billionaire" does not make one happy; one must act on that platform. Serving Kṛṣṇa is the soul's eternal nature (the teacher cites Gītā 15.7), so devotional service gives taste `18:00 – 20:00`.
  - Prabhupāda writes that service to the senses is misleading (it promises happiness but does not deliver) and degrading (it drags us to lower modes and destinations).
  - Even mechanical chanting, done diligently, calms passion and ignorance and brings at least the happiness of goodness — and can lead beyond `20:00`.
- **Mercy** — sudden moments of realisation, illumination, ecstasy and deep fulfilment during devotional service are one aspect of the Lord's grace `20:00`.
- This triple impetus comes when we devote ourselves to Bhagavān — not to Paramātmā or Brahman `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhagavān is most auspicious because He is the highest destination, fully satisfies the heart, and personally lifts His devotees — like a rope pulled from outside — through knowledge, taste and mercy. [10:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### The meaning of "Bhagavān" [22:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda takes Jīva Gosvāmī's commentary and explains the word _bhagavān_ syllable by syllable `22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bha_ — one who fully maintains, and guardian `22:00`.
- _Ga_ — guide, leader or creator; one who takes to a destination `22:00`.
- _Vān_ — here not "possessor" but from the root meaning "to reside": every being lives in Him and He lives in every being `22:00 – 24:00`.
- The standard etymology, by contrast, is _bhaga_ (opulences) + _vān_ (possessor) `22:00`.

#### Everything in Him, He in everything [24:00]

- Everything resides in Him — all existence is sheltered within His energy.
- He resides in everything — He enters everything as the Supersoul.
- The teacher calls this play of words a "chiasmus," like his earlier example: "Nothing holds a devotee back; a devotee holds nothing back."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
In Jīva Gosvāmī's reading, Bhagavān is the maintainer and guide in whom everything resides and who resides in everything. [22:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### From so 'ham to dāso 'ham [24:00 – 28:00]

**Context.** "So am I" (_so 'ham_) is a favourite phrase of the _māyāvādīs_; the teacher explains how one must move from _so 'ham_ to _dāso 'ham_ — "I am the servant" `24:00`.

#### Oneness in quality, not quantity [24:00 – 26:00]

- Scripture does at times emphasise oneness in quality, but elsewhere it speaks of us as infinitesimal and the Lord as infinite.
- Denying the quantitative difference misrepresents scripture.

#### What blocks the move [26:00]

- As long as one is attached to false ego and wants to be great, spiritual life becomes a means to one's own agenda, and _dāso 'ham_ is very difficult.
- One who sincerely seeks the highest truth can rise to it.

#### The "eureka moment" [26:00]

- Archimedes, discovering his principle in a bathtub, jumped out shouting "Eureka!" — a sudden spark of insight that makes one forget everything else.
- The _māyāvādīs_ think their eureka moment will be saying "I am that" with realisation; it may be brilliant for them, but it is incomplete.
- The complete realisation: "Yes, I am like the Absolute — and I am His servant; let me serve Him by serving His servants."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
So 'ham is an incomplete realisation; it is completed in dāso 'ham — qualitatively like the Lord, yet His eternal servant. [24:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Jñāna unfavourable and favourable to bhakti [28:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** Based on Jīva Gosvāmī's writings and the _Durgama-saṅgamanī_ — his commentary on Rūpa Gosvāmī's _Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu_, which has been translated into English — the teacher contrasts _jñāna_ that is _pratikūla_ (unfavourable) and _anukūla_ (favourable) to _bhakti_ `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ṭīkā_ — a commentary, or sometimes a commentary on a commentary `28:00`.
- _Jñāna_ can refer to three things: knowledge of the soul, of the _īśvara_, and of their oneness. Devotees accept the first two and reject the third — thinking the two of us are one is, for Vaiṣṇavas, a disastrous misunderstanding `28:00`.

#### Unfavourable jñāna [28:00 – 32:00]

- **Constitution** — oneness denies the eternality of _bhakti_: if devotee and Bhagavān will become one, why pray, "Please allow me to serve You"? Hence devotees in the _Bhāgavatam_ pray that they would rather go to hell with the chance to chant the Lord's glories than to heaven without it; for them losing _bhakti_ is the greatest disaster `28:00 – 30:00`.
- **Application** — it hardens the heart: constant analysis makes one too objective, clinical and detached, and sentiments dry up. Telling a sick devotee who needs water, medicine or food, "Prabhu, you are not the body — that's _māyā_," is itself _māyā_. We may educate people to rise above bodily consciousness, but to practise _bhakti_ we must keep soft hearts, at least toward Kṛṣṇa's devotees `30:00 – 32:00`.
- **Destination** — merging, the end of _bhakti_. So a devotee gives up "the urge to merge" and prays for devotion birth after birth `32:00`.

#### Favourable jñāna [32:00 – 36:00]

- What makes _jñāna_ favourable: whether it enhances one's _bhakti_ and brings one closer to Kṛṣṇa `32:00`.
- **Constitution** — the soul's subordination is emphasised: I am a part and He is the whole, and without Him I cannot be happy — this inspires spiritual practice `34:00`.
- **Application** — a _jñānī_ may think, "Everyone is already liberated; this existence is illusory, so I need not help anyone." For a devotee this existence is used in Kṛṣṇa's service; intellectual understanding of devotional practices stabilises the restless mind and keeps one inspired `34:00`.
- **Destination** — enhanced appreciation of the Lord's glories, not only His greatness but His sweetness: "Kṛṣṇa is such a wonderful person — I want to love Him" `34:00 – 36:00`.

#### Harmonising intellectual attraction [36:00]

- Prabhupāda began preaching by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa and giving people ecstasy, but he also explained the bodily conception and how to rise beyond it.
- _Bhakti_ is so inclusive that we need not reject an attraction to philosophy or serious study; we can harmonise it and use it to come toward Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Jñāna that ends in oneness hardens the heart and ends bhakti; jñāna that stresses our subordination, stabilises practice and deepens appreciation of Kṛṣṇa's sweetness becomes a powerful support for bhakti. [28:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### The favour fulfilled in pure devotional service [36:00 – 46:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda concludes the purport by saying the Lord's favour sought in this prayer is fulfilled in pure devotional service `36:00`.

#### Who receives the favour [36:00 – 38:00]

- The Lord can be known only by those He favours; He is not partial — He favours whoever tries to serve and please Him.
- Special favours go to pure devotees because only they accept the Lord as the ultimate person; _jñānīs_ and _yogīs_ see Him as a transitional reality and so cannot beseech Him wholeheartedly, even when He appears.

#### "O sky, I love you" [38:00]

- The favour sought is beyond the _brahmajyoti_ — the _brahmajyoti_ cannot bless anyone.
- Saying "O sky, I love you" makes no sense; the sky is too vast to love. Beholding the sky may give peace, as contemplating Brahman may — but we want love, which comes through _bhakti_ or the grace of devotees.

#### Chanting: prayer and its answer [38:00 – 40:00]

- Chanting is a prayer — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura explains its mood as "please engage me in Your service," "please purify me," "please help me return to You."
- It is also the fulfilment — Kṛṣṇa manifests as the holy name; Prabhupāda says Kṛṣṇa dances on our tongue. By reciting the name respectfully, chanting clearly and hearing attentively, we already serve and worship Him.

#### The light in the head [40:00]

- A young girl in America told Prabhupāda she saw a light in her head when chanting; he said, "Keep chanting, the light will go away."
- She was shocked, but the goal is not a white light — it is to behold Kṛṣṇa's beautiful form, which requires purification.

#### Miracles and the circus [42:00 – 44:00]

- If miracles become the criteria, the circus becomes the testing ground — who dances most in _kīrtana_, who sheds most tears, who has paranormal visions or manifests a four-handed form.
- Such things may happen to a few, but a devoted, committed person advances even without them; devotional service is a serious arena for purification, not a circus.
- The Lord's mercy is engagement in devotional service: as with the person in the well, being given a rope to hold is itself the miracle.
- What we need is the simple faith that God exists, is our well-wisher, will guide us if we surrender, and that His will manifests through His devotees `44:00`.

#### Summary [44:00 – 46:00]

- Brahman is lower; Bhagavān manifests the same Absolute most fully.
- _Kalyāṇa-tama_ — the Lord's form is the most auspicious perception.
- _Bha-ga-vān_ — maintainer, guide, residing in all.
- Take the whole scripture — personal and impersonal — and reconcile them in a higher reality.
- When we ask Kṛṣṇa for mercy He gives devotional service, which keeps us so busy that we stay protected from _māyā_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The favour this prayer seeks is devotional service itself: chanting is both the prayer and its answer, and engagement in service — not miracles — is how the Lord lifts us out of the well. [36:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>
