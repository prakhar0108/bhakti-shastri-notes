### Even the demigods do not fully know the Absolute [06:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** The Absolute Truth, the Supreme Lord, is so great that He is not easy to understand `06:00`.

#### Arjuna's statement [06:00]

- The teacher cites Arjuna in the Gītā (given as 10.14): neither the gods nor the demons know the Lord's personality.
- The teacher paraphrases: "O Lord, You are a person — but what kind of person You are, nobody knows."

#### The demigods' limited concept [08:00]

- Even the gods, as related in the scriptures, do not easily understand the truth.
- They obviously go and pray to Viṣṇu when in danger, and know there is some higher being.
- But their concept of the Supreme is not proper: they may think that beyond Viṣṇu there is something else that is the ultimate truth.
- If even the demigods misunderstand, what to speak of others.

#### Ritualists miss the essence [08:00 – 10:00]

- Those following the Vedic literature through _karma-kāṇḍa_ — activity according to religious rituals — miss its essential meaning.
- Prabhupāda then turns, in the purport, to those pursuing _jñāna_ `10:00`.

#### Entering darkness [10:00]

- The mantra's phrase about entering darkness is explained: here worship of _asambhūti_ means demigod worshippers stay in the dark material world.
- They may get some elevation, but not substantial elevation.
- As long as they are without spiritual truth, they continue going round and round in the cycle.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Supreme Lord is beyond even the demigods' full understanding; worship and ritual that stop short of Him keep one circling in the material world, however elevated. [06:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### The good is the worst enemy of the best [12:00 – 14:00]

**Context.** The teacher explains why the purport says demigod worship is not encouraged: so that people can focus simply on the highest goal, putting everything else aside `12:00`.

#### Good versus best [12:00]

- "The good is often the worst enemy of the best": there is a best thing we can do, and the good thing is not bad — but often we do the good and miss the chance for the best.
- Demigod worship is not necessarily bad: it is good, it is religious — but it is often the worst enemy of the best.
- The same applies to the impersonal.
- Two different problems:
  1. Choosing the good instead of the best.
  2. Worse still — misunderstanding the good to be the best, or considering the best to be lower.

#### The logic of the Vedic system [12:00]

- Different people have different natures, and because of their nature they cannot be elevated directly.
- If they are unable to be attracted to the Supreme Lord, an alternative is provided in the form of intermediates — the demigods.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Demigod worship is good and religious, but the good becomes an enemy of the best when it replaces the best — and most of all when it is mistaken for the best. [12:00]
</Callout>

### The prince, the minister and the king [14:00 – 30:00]

**Context.** The teacher explains the Vedic system of multi-level worship through an extended analogy, starting from a story in the Bible `14:00`.

#### The prodigal son [14:00]

- A son wanted his share, left his father, squandered it, and finally decided to come back; the father welcomed him.
- It is a wonderful story about the selflessness of God's love: even when we turn away and squander what He has given, He welcomes us back when we return.
- The Vedic scriptures describe God's love as even greater: God not only welcomes us back when we turn to Him, but strives in many ways to get us back.

#### The king's government [14:00 – 16:00]

- A king rules through ministers and many government authorities, organised in major departments — an ascending sequence leading ultimately to the king.
- Likewise, the demigods are not God, but part of the system arranged by the Supreme Lord, by which people can be elevated and eventually liberated `16:00`.

#### The story [16:00 – 20:00]

1. The prince leaves his father's kingdom, goes to another land and serves another master, who exploits him and makes him work very hard.
2. The father knows that even if he invites the son, he will not come — so he sends his own minister.
3. The minister invites the prince back to the kingdom — not to reunite with his father, but for better material facilities, better pay and better care `18:00`.
4. The prince comes back, though still serving the minister rather than the king; even this is better than working for a foreign master `18:00`.
5. Gradually the prince sees that the king is really kind-hearted: "Why did I ever leave?" By serving, associating and receiving affection, his love awakens, and he asks, "Can I come back home?" — and is welcomed `20:00`.

#### Applying the analogy [20:00 – 22:00]

- The soul turns away from Kṛṣṇa and lives materialistically.
- The demigods are like ministers of Kṛṣṇa, allocated different departments in the universal "cabinet."
- They offer their worshippers material benefits, but within a religious context.
- People come to demigod worship not selflessly but for material ends; yet because the demigods represent Kṛṣṇa, people come closer to the Supreme Lord through this arrangement `22:00`.
- The demigods are servants of the Supreme Lord, capable of giving material results.

#### Kṛṣṇa's kindness and flexibility [24:00 – 26:00]

- An individual may not be ready to worship the Supreme Lord; Kṛṣṇa is so kind that He says, in effect: "You cannot worship Me because of your conditioning and nature — fine, worship some representative of Mine."
- The system of multi-level worship shows two things:
  - the **individuality of the worshipper**, who is met at his level;
  - the **flexibility of the worshipable**, who makes Himself accessible.
- Like the father: "If you don't want to serve me, I am available through my minister; if you come into the kingdom, I'll take care of you." Kṛṣṇa does not even mind that they worship His representative `26:00`.

#### The 60% analogy [26:00 – 30:00]

- Whether this system is good or bad depends on the level of the person.
- Is 60% good or bad? A student who used to get 80% and now gets 60% has fallen; one who used to get 40% and now gets 60% has progressed.
- **40% → 60%:** progress from godless materialism to religious materialism `28:00`.
- **80% → 60%:** for someone with the understanding and spiritual evolution to worship Kṛṣṇa directly, going to the devatās is a step down — like the prince telling the father, "I'll go to the minister; I don't need you."
- So demigod worship is good for those at a godless level, but undesirable for those at a higher level; the Upaniṣad is showing the highest Absolute Truth, so here it calls the lower option darkness — it keeps people in the cycle of birth and death `28:00 – 30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Like a king sending his minister to bring back a runaway prince, Kṛṣṇa provides demigod worship to draw materialistic souls closer to Him; it is progress for the godless, but a step down for anyone able to serve Him directly. [14:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### When the step becomes the destination [30:00 – 40:00]

**Context.** The teacher gives two stories in which demigods direct their worshippers to Kṛṣṇa, then shows the danger of stopping at the demigod `30:00`.

#### Keśava Kāśmīrī [30:00 – 32:00]

- Keśava Kāśmīrī was a great worshipper who had received the blessing that he would defeat all scholars.
- In a debate he was defeated, but not humiliated; he was told to go, think about it, study his books and come back the next day to continue.
- Back at his place, the deity he worshipped appeared and told him that the person he had met was not from this world; the highest blessing was that he had come to the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord — he should go and surrender to Him `32:00`.

#### Caṇḍīdāsa [32:00 – 36:00]

- Caṇḍīdāsa had received great prosperity by worshipping the goddess with flowers.
- Once he offered the flowers in his mind (_mānasa-pūjā_) `34:00`.
- The goddess then appeared to him for the first time: "You have worshipped me so long, but always asked 'give me this, give me that' — and I gave whatever you asked. Today, because you did extra service, I will give you the one ultimate thing" `34:00 – 36:00`.
- Thereafter he wrote beautiful songs glorifying Kṛṣṇa's intimate pastimes; the goddess acted like his guru, guiding him to Kṛṣṇa `36:00`.

#### Progression versus destination [36:00 – 38:00]

- Demigod worship is meant to be integrated as part of a step-by-step progression that brings people closer to Kṛṣṇa.
- When it is not seen as a progression to Kṛṣṇa, it becomes an alternative destination that keeps people away from Kṛṣṇa.
- This danger is highly probable, because many people think the devatā is supreme `38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Demigods who understand their role guide their worshippers to Kṛṣṇa, as with Keśava Kāśmīrī and Caṇḍīdāsa; the danger arises when a step meant to lead to Kṛṣṇa is treated as the final goal. [30:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gītā Chapter 7 on demigod worship [40:00 – 54:00]

**Context.** The teacher turns to what Kṛṣṇa says about demigod worship in the Gītā, reading it through the prince analogy `40:00`.

#### Intelligence stolen by desire [40:00]

- The prince left his father for self-centred enjoyment; similarly the soul comes here because of desire for self-enjoyment, and its intelligence is lost.
- Such souls go to other gods, just as the prince served another master — hoping to get something from nature instead of coming to Kṛṣṇa.

#### Between the two extremes [40:00 – 42:00]

- These verses come after Kṛṣṇa describes two extremes in Chapter 7:
  - **7.14** — _māyā_ is very difficult to overcome, but those who surrender can overcome it.
  - **7.15** — those who do not surrender.
  - **7.16 – 7.19** — the four categories of people who surrender — the positive extreme.
- In between are those who neither fully surrender to Kṛṣṇa nor reject Him: they worship others. Kṛṣṇa now describes this category and what happens to them `42:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa gives faith in the demigods [42:00 – 46:00]

- In the next verse, as the teacher explains, Kṛṣṇa speaks of whatever form a person wants to worship with faith; different devatās have different forms, natures and mentalities `42:00`.
- In the analogy: if the son has some negativity against the father, the father thinks, "He will like this minister — I'll send him into his life," and ensures the minister has the right power, prestige and position so that the prince develops faith in him `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Likewise Kṛṣṇa, as the Supersoul in the heart of all living beings, gives faith when they want to worship a particular devatā — turning them indirectly towards Himself through the devatās `44:00`.
- Even the faith people have in the devatās comes from Kṛṣṇa — like helping the 40% student rise to 60% `44:00`.
- The king pays all employees, including the minister; likewise Kṛṣṇa says He alone arranges the results of such worship `46:00`.
- Another way Kṛṣṇa gives faith is through the Purāṇas: different Purāṇas glorify different devatās — those connected with different modes, such as Purāṇas on Śiva and Durgā — making them attractive to their worshippers `46:00`.

#### Created by Him, yet not the same [48:00]

- Though the system is created by Kṛṣṇa, worshipping the devatās is not exactly the same as worshipping Him, because it leads to a different destination.
- Just by serving the minister, the prince will not come back to the king; he must raise his consciousness. Similarly, the soul must rise from worship of the devatās to worship of Kṛṣṇa.

#### Trapped in the cycle [50:00]

- Otherwise one is trapped in a cycle: worship the devatās, get temporary results, exhaust them, return to worship again.
- The teacher notes this is also discussed in Gītā 9.20–21: worshippers may go to the heavens and enjoy, but afterwards come back.
- Only Kṛṣṇa's devotees go back to Kṛṣṇa; others continue in birth and death — going up and coming back, and sometimes, misdirected by bad association, going down to lower species and lower planets.

#### Four levels — summary [50:00 – 54:00]

- The purpose of demigod worship is to bring souls back; it demonstrates Kṛṣṇa's unlimited compassion and selfless desire for the elevation of all living beings, like a father whose son has gone away `50:00 – 52:00`.
- The four levels `52:00`:
  1. **Prince serving another master** — godless materialism.
  2. **Prince serving the king's minister** — the soul serving a devatā who represents Kṛṣṇa, still for material motives: _karma-kāṇḍa_.
  3. **Prince serving the king, but for material motives** — the soul serving the Supreme Lord with material desires.
  4. **Prince returning home out of love for the father** — the soul coming back to Kṛṣṇa out of love.
- Rising from 40% to 60% is good, but if 60% is taken as the highest, the student never achieves his higher potential `54:00`.
- The difference between demigod worship and Kṛṣṇa worship is not just a matter of percentage — it is a huge difference: demigod worship cannot liberate a person; only worship of the Supreme Lord does `54:00`.
- That is why the mantra says those who worship the temporary will continue in the cycle of birth and death `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa Himself gives faith in the demigods and arranges their results, to lift souls step by step — but only devotion to Him liberates; treating the intermediate step as final keeps the soul in birth and death. [40:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
