### The paradox of Mantra 4 [02:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/4 -->

**Context.** The teacher explains the mantra's sense: it describes the Absolute Truth in apparently contradictory terms `02:00 – 04:00`.

#### The Lord's paradoxical attributes [02:00 – 04:00]

- The Lord is fixed in His abode, yet faster than everyone else — faster than the _devas_, faster even than the mind.
- He is so far ahead that nobody can approach Him: when people run to catch Him, He leaves them all far behind.
- He is the master of those who supply water and the necessities of this world — the controller of those who control great elements like rain and wind.
- From that one place, He controls those who control things everywhere `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Mantra 4 deliberately describes the Lord in paradoxes — fixed yet swifter than the mind, beyond reach yet controlling the controllers of nature — showing that He is inconceivable to ordinary understanding. [02:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### Revelation, not speculation [04:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** The purport's first statement: the Absolute (the word "that" in the mantra refers to the Absolute Truth) cannot be understood by speculation, only by revelation `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Speculation_ — guessing at the truth by our own limited means, without certainty `06:00`.
- _Revelation_ — the unlimited making Himself known to the limited `04:00 – 06:00`.
- _Abrahamic religions_ — Christianity, Islam and Judaism, so called because all three accept Abraham as a prominent prophet `06:00`.

#### Flow of the purport [04:00 – 06:00]

1. The Absolute cannot be understood by speculation.
2. It is understood through revelation, which helps us understand God and His energies.
3. The understanding received: the energies are different and non-different from Him — which also makes sense logically.
4. Being limited, we can understand the unlimited only through His revelation.
5. How do we receive revelation? Play your part, and you will be given understanding — harmonising with the Absolute Truth through devotional service `06:00`.

#### The noise outside the room [06:00]

- Sitting in this room, I hear a noise outside. Someone may have fallen; someone may be watching a movie in which something fell.
- All such guesses are speculation — there is no certainty, and we always remain unsure.

#### Two extremes [06:00 – 08:00]

- **"We can never know"** — some say there is no conclusive way to know the Absolute Truth, so whatever we claim to know is uncertain.
- **No positive description** — some religious traditions give no positive knowledge about God; the teacher cites the Abrahamic religions, where there is no positive description of what God looks like.
- Both rest on the idea that the finite cannot comprehend the infinite, so God can never be known `08:00`.

#### The infinite can reveal Himself [08:00]

- It is true that we are finite.
- But if God is truly infinite and omnipotent, He can do anything — and "anything" includes giving the finite the ability to understand the infinite.
- The finite cannot understand the infinite by its own effort, but the infinite can reveal Himself to the finite.

#### Like a sunrise [08:00 – 10:00]

- The sun rises gradually — slowly, slowly, rising, rising.
- A realisation such as "I believe in Kṛṣṇa, I love Kṛṣṇa, I want to offer my life to Kṛṣṇa" may be celebrated as one moment, but it was being created gradually in the background.
- Flashes of inspiration do not come abruptly or arbitrarily; there is a background preparing them.
- In that way revelation works — by His grace the finite gets the opportunity to understand the infinite `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Speculation leaves us forever unsure, and the finite cannot reach the infinite by its own effort; but the omnipotent Lord can reveal Himself, gradually like a sunrise, to those who play their part in His service. [04:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Analysis, synthesis and living demonstration [10:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** The purport's next point: God is not alone; He has energies. To prepare for this, the teacher distinguishes two modes of thinking `10:00 – 12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Analysis_ — breaking things down into their parts; it is for understanding `12:00`.
- _Synthesis_ — the parts coming together as an organic whole; it is for working `12:00`.

#### Three examples [12:00 – 16:00]

- **A computer** — analysed, there is a CPU, a keyboard, a monitor and a power cord; but to work, the keyboard cannot work in one place and the monitor in another — they must come together. Input goes from the keyboard and appears on the monitor; analysis helps us understand how things work, synthesis makes them work `12:00`, `16:00`.
- **A child learning language** — the child says "Mama, I want water" and learns language through using it; grammar comes later. Language is learned naturally as a working tool for communication `12:00 – 14:00`.
- **Kṛṣṇa consciousness** — people take to it not just because of theoretical propositions but because of its living demonstrations: devotees living as examples. Seeing their kindness, one learns why — as souls we are all parts of Kṛṣṇa and so related as one family. First we see the result; then we look into the philosophy `14:00 – 16:00`.

- So whether it is a machine, a language or relationships, the synthetic mode shows things working; the analytical mode later explains why they work as they do `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Analysis helps us understand, but synthesis makes things work — a computer, a language, or Kṛṣṇa consciousness seen alive in devotees' lives; Prabhupāda uses analysis in the purport to help us understand the Lord and His energies. [12:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### The Lord and His energies: one and different [16:00 – 22:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda's point: the Lord's energy is everywhere; the Supreme Lord is one unit, one Absolute Truth, and He has energies `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### Fire and burning [18:00 – 20:00]

- Where there is fire, there is burning — the two appear together and are naturally related.
- Are they entirely the same? If so, we would not need to say "fire burns"; "fire" would be enough. To a child we explain that fire is the source of the energy and burning is its action — the way it acts.
- Burning is the same as fire, in that there is no fire without burning; it is different, in that burning is a property of fire.
- Likewise the Absolute Truth is one, complete and perfect, with material and spiritual energies; the Lord and His energies are the same and also different.

#### "Be a part" [20:00 – 22:00]

- Again, being limited, we can understand the unlimited only through His revelation `20:00`.
- "Be a part" means: be a part of Kṛṣṇa's plan, a part of the whole that is the Supreme Kṛṣṇa; acting apart from it causes suffering. The teacher cites BG 15.7 `20:00`.
- When we act as parts of the whole, we gain the greatest happiness and also do the best service to others `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord and His energies are one and different, just as burning is inseparable from fire yet is its property; as parts of the whole, we find happiness and serve others best by playing our part in His plan. [18:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Using our talents: materialist, impersonalist and devotional views [22:00 – 28:00]

**Context.** Different people hold different ideas about this world; the teacher contrasts them through a practical example — the use of talent `22:00`.

#### Two mistaken views [22:00]

- **Materialists** — believe the material world is the only thing that exists and matters, and want to enjoy here; their outlook is summed up in a popular Hindi song line: live here, die here — where else is there to go?
- **Impersonalists** — hold that everything in this world is false and meaningless.

#### The singer's talent [22:00 – 26:00]

- Suppose someone has a talent for singing and sings only mundane songs about this world's attractions, such as between male and female.
- That person becomes captive to the attraction and drives others into it too; the sweeter the voice and the more expert the singer, the greater the illusion created.
- Using talents only for material purposes increases bondage, for the person and for the many who are trapped in the illusion `24:00`.
- Actually the ability in people comes from Kṛṣṇa. The question is: is that ability taking the person toward Kṛṣṇa, or away from Him? `24:00 – 26:00`

#### Talents for glorifying Kṛṣṇa [26:00]

- The teacher cites a verse describing that the ultimate perfection of every branch of knowledge is to glorify the Lord.
- Prabhupāda writes that people should use science to demonstrate the existence of God and to glorify Him.
- So the _bhakti_ philosophy says: use all our talents to glorify Kṛṣṇa — this brings the greatest good for ourselves and for all.
- The thought flow of the purport: we cannot know God by speculation; He reveals Himself when we serve. Then — how do we serve? `26:00`

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every talent comes from Kṛṣṇa; the same ability that binds the singer and his listeners in illusion becomes the greatest good for all when it is used to glorify Kṛṣṇa — neither enjoyed as the only reality nor rejected as meaningless. [22:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Service that is individual, voluntary and spontaneous [28:00 – 40:00]

**Context.** Each of us has a particular energy, and its purpose is to act according to the Lord's will. Prabhupāda says we must be very careful to play the part designated for us by the Lord — according to our nature `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Varṇāśrama_ — recognises people's individuality and engages them according to it `28:00`.
- Individual, voluntary and spontaneous — the three features of devotional service `30:00`.

#### The book distributors' proposal [28:00 – 30:00]

- At one time in the movement's history, book distribution was taking off in a big way, and expert book distributors were told how pleased the leadership was with them and asked to distribute more and to train others.
- They proposed going from temple to temple to teach set sentences and special expressions for convincing people to take books.
- The response was the principle that devotional service is individual, voluntary and spontaneous.

#### Individual [32:00]

- Each of us has a unique set of characteristics, and these give us a unique relationship with Kṛṣṇa.
- Individuality is inherent in our fabric — material or spiritual — and cannot be negated or rejected; each person must find out what is his or her best service.

#### Voluntary: the car that needs a push [32:00 – 36:00]

- Sometimes a car needs a push: people push from outside while the driver steers and turns the ignition key, giving it initial momentum.
- But if the driver inside is asleep while others push, what will happen? Eventually they will give up and look for someone more interested.
- We are like the driver inside the body; others can help, but if we show no interest, the effort fails.
- Service cannot be forced. Out of anxiety we may try to control others and make them do things they are not ready for; we must realise it is voluntary `34:00`.

#### Spontaneous [36:00]

- Service is something I perform based on my nature and my own inspiration.

#### Will I lose my individuality? [36:00 – 38:00]

- A common concern: if everybody chants the same mantra, reads the same books and eats the same _prasādam_, what happens to my individuality?
- Real individuality is not based on externals.
- **The hospital example:** patients take the same treatment, yet remain distinct individuals with their own personalities, likes and dislikes. Treatment cures the disease; it does not curb individuality.
- Likewise devotional practices are the treatment; once we are purified, our healthy individuality surfaces. _Bhakti_ does not reject our individuality; it supplements it `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Join the race from your place [38:00 – 40:00]

- Different people go at different paces; some practise at home, some become _brahmacārīs_ and more serious.
- Everyone who accepts Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Lord and tries to serve Him is advancing, and gradually all become more serious.
- Prabhupāda's emphasis: live under the Lord's shelter, do our part within His plan, be in harmony and not disharmony with Him — and join the race from our place, at our pace.
- Everybody can be accommodated in service `40:00`.

#### The patient and the doctor [40:00]

- This world is like a hospital; the patients' job is to cooperate with the doctor. When we cooperate with Kṛṣṇa, He cures us in the fastest possible way.
- A patient's cooperation is often passive — lie down, rest, take medicine.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Devotional service is individual, voluntary and spontaneous: it engages each person's unique nature, cannot be forced, and flows from one's own inspiration — like a shared treatment that cures the disease and lets a healthy individuality surface. [28:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Discovering ourselves and taking responsibility [40:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda would often emphasise the individuality of a person `40:00`.

#### Prabhupāda in London [40:00 – 42:00]

- London had become a beachhead — the starting point for expansion into Europe — and devotees from France, Germany and other parts of Europe had gathered there and were giving him reports.
- He asked each of them to understand what they wanted to do for Kṛṣṇa and focus on that; doing that work wholeheartedly, they would be doing right.
- He used devotees' individuality to discover their inspiration for serving, and encouraged them to continue wholeheartedly `42:00`.

#### Individuality within the principles [42:00 – 44:00]

- Expressing individuality does not mean doing something out of harmony with the principles of devotional service; it must be in harmony overall.
- A devotee takes responsibility for his own service and thinks: "How can I serve? This is what I like to do, this is my interest, and this is what is required — let me do this." He presents the proposal, receives blessings, and does it from the heart, out of inspiration and interest.

#### Varṇāśrama today [44:00]

- This is a practical way to apply _varṇāśrama_ today: with much intermarriage between castes, and with the system mostly reckoned by birth, we often do not know our nature.
- So we must take responsibility for finding out our nature and then serve accordingly.

#### My advancement is my responsibility [44:00 – 46:00]

- My spiritual advancement is not my spiritual master's responsibility; it is mine.
- This does not mean I can advance without the mercy of the spiritual master or of Kṛṣṇa; responsibility means I have an important part to play.
- **The business example:** to expand my business I may have a guide who advises me periodically, but expanding it is ultimately my responsibility. Likewise, the spiritual master corrects me and shows the way, but it is for me to move along it and discover what makes me more inspired `46:00`.

#### Two phases of self-discovery [46:00]

1. **Spiritual** — I understand that I am not the body.
2. **Psycho-physical** — I understand my talents, interests, strengths and inspirations, and use them in Kṛṣṇa's service.

- The second phase matters too, because it enables us to serve enthusiastically lifelong.

#### How do we know our part? [46:00 – 48:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is not going to appear in a dream and say, "Build a temple here" — that may happen for some exceptional souls.
- More important is an eminently practical understanding of Kṛṣṇa consciousness: discovering our nature and serving according to it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa has given each of us a particular energy to glorify Him; we must discover both our spiritual identity and our psycho-physical nature, and take personal responsibility — with the mercy of guru and Kṛṣṇa — for serving according to it. [40:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Devotion as dynamic, joyful reciprocation [48:00 – 54:00]

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa consciousness at our level must be practical, not merely hypothetical `48:00`.

#### Substance over sentiment [48:00]

- Dwelling only on very advanced topics of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, at our stage, would be mostly hypothetical and sentimental, not substantial.
- What is substantial is practical engagement — Kṛṣṇa's question to us is, in effect, "What are you doing for Me?"
- This is not only a matter of quantity — how many books distributed — though quantity is also one way of expressing devotion.
- The important point: we have a relationship with Kṛṣṇa, and it develops through:
  - **Action** — the practical services we do;
  - **Emotion** — the feelings we bring to them;
  - **Intention** — why we do them.
- So we focus on reciprocating with Kṛṣṇa and take responsibility for our future advancement and for developing our relationship with Him.

#### We offer, Kṛṣṇa reveals [50:00]

- As we offer ourselves, Kṛṣṇa reveals His glory, beauty and love in our heart, and through that revelation He attracts us.
- This is a blessing: when we are attracted to Kṛṣṇa, we are no longer attracted to the things of this world, and we are on the path of liberation and back to Kṛṣṇa.
- We offer, and Kṛṣṇa offers Himself.

#### Dynamic and ecstatic [52:00]

- **Dynamic** — because it is a constant reciprocation: we offer, and Kṛṣṇa reveals Himself more.
- **Ecstatic** — because the more He reveals Himself, the more joy there is in the heart; Kṛṣṇa is an ocean of happiness, possessing all qualities in unlimited quantity, the reservoir of all happiness.
- With a service attitude — "I want to serve Kṛṣṇa; He will show me how" — we serve accordingly, understand Him more and more, and fall in love with Him.

#### Summary [54:00]

1. The paradox of the mantra — the Lord in one place is faster than the mind and the great _devatās_, and from there controls the controllers.
2. The Absolute is understood not by speculation but through revelation.
3. The Lord and His energies are different and non-different; analysis and synthesis illustrated through a machine, a language and devotional culture.
4. Dovetailing: our abilities used only for ourselves bring bondage, but used in Kṛṣṇa's service they become joyful engagement that also benefits others.
5. _Bhakti_ enables us to use the material in the service of the spiritual, and we receive the supreme blessing to the extent we offer ourselves to Him through our individuality.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Devotion grows through action, emotion and intention offered to Kṛṣṇa; as we offer ourselves, He reveals His glory, beauty and love, making devotional service a dynamic and ever-increasing joy. [48:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
