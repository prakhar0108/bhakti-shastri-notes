### The mantra and its main theme [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/13 -->

**Context.** The teacher reads the mantra and Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation: one result is obtained by worshipping the Supreme cause of all causes and another by worshipping what is not supreme, and this is heard from undisturbed authorities who clearly explained it `00:00`. Śrīla Prabhupāda's purports are now longer — this one has sixteen paragraphs `00:00`.

#### Flow of the purport [02:00]

- The verse clearly says that different paths lead to different results — one path, one result; another path, another.
- It therefore exposes the folly of the idea that all paths lead to the same goal; analysing this is the main theme of the purport and of the class.
- Śāstra teaches that different paths lead to different goals, so we must choose the right path to reach the right goal.
- Two courses of action:
  - Accepting Kṛṣṇa as the Absolute Truth — as Arjuna did, who for Kṛṣṇa's satisfaction was ready to do anything, even fight his so-called relatives.
  - Not accepting Kṛṣṇa — then one chooses an entirely different course, such as "people are my God" (to be discussed elaborately) or "I am God," which is _māyāvāda_.
- Hearing from the authorities helps everyone attain perfection.

#### Three angles of analysis [02:00]

- The question "do all paths lead to the same goal?" is examined from:
  1. the logical point of view,
  2. the scriptural point of view,
  3. the practical, socio-cultural point of view.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Mantra 13 teaches by contrast: different kinds of worship lead to different results, which is why the purport's central task is to expose the folly of "all paths lead to the same goal." [00:00 – 02:00]

### The logical view: the trains example [02:00 – 10:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda's standard example: from Mumbai, one train goes to Chennai, another to Delhi, another to Kolkata — they cannot all reach the same destination `02:00`.

#### The impersonalist objection and its answer [04:00 – 06:00]

- Objection: the Absolute Truth is not a small, limited place like Delhi or Chennai; it is everywhere, so the example is false.
- Answer: the thrust of the example is not whether the destination is limited or unlimited, but whether one must evolve one's consciousness in a particular way to reach the Absolute Truth.
- The Absolute Truth is all-pervading, yet it is perceived only at a particular level of consciousness:
  - the modes of material nature — ignorance, passion and goodness;
  - beyond them, transcendence.
- Only if our consciousness moves towards transcendence can we pursue transcendental reality; otherwise it remains inaccessible even though it is everywhere.
- Different paths lead to different kinds of consciousness, and not all kinds are equal for perceiving, realising or relishing the Absolute.

#### "Why would God have to speak the Gītā?" [04:00]

- After a university programme in America, someone told Śrīla Prabhupāda he had heard that the Gītā says whatever we do, we ultimately go back to God.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda replied: if that were so, why would God have to speak the _Bhagavad-gītā_? We would do whatever we wanted and still go back to Him.
- Instead Kṛṣṇa says "if you do this, this will happen" — e.g. in BG 18.58: if you become conscious of Me you will cross all obstacles by My grace; if not, you will be lost — two paths, two consequences.

#### The Absolute Truth is a person [06:00 – 08:00]

- To come close to a person we must do certain things: to grow closer to a spouse, one learns his or her likes and dislikes and changes one's behaviour.
- People work hard to win the favour of ordinary persons; how much more must we strive for a relationship with the Supreme Person.
- The Lord is very merciful and accessible, yet relationships require commitment, and He must be approached as a person.
- Kṛṣṇa's likes and dislikes are not arbitrary: what pleases Him is also for our ultimate good — the more we do it, the more we are purified, elevated, happier, and ultimately liberated `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The trains example is not about the size of the destination but about consciousness: the all-pervading Absolute is perceived only when consciousness is raised to transcendence, and as a person He is approached by learning and doing what pleases Him. [04:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### The scriptural view: instruction by contrast [08:00 – 22:00]

**Context.** If scripture meant that all paths lead to the same goal, it would not repeatedly use instruction by contrast — "if you do this, this will happen; if you do that, that will happen" `08:00`.

#### Contrasting pairs in the Bhagavad-gītā [10:00 – 14:00]

- **BG 3.31–3.32** — one who has faith and follows Kṛṣṇa's instructions becomes free from sinful reactions; one who is envious and faithless loses whatever knowledge he acquires, because his consciousness is misdirected `10:00`.
- **BG 4.39–4.40** — faith leads quickly to supreme peace; without faith one is happy neither in this world nor the next `10:00`.
- **BG 16.23–16.24** — here the negative comes first: those who give up śāstra and act on selfish desires attain neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the supreme destination; therefore understand from śāstra what is to be done and act accordingly `10:00 – 12:00`.
- **BG 18.58** — already discussed `12:00`.
- **BG 9.25** — one verse giving four trajectories `12:00 – 14:00`:
  1. worshippers of the _devas_ (in goodness) go to the _devas_;
  2. worshippers of the ancestors (in passion) go to the ancestors;
  3. worshippers of ghostly beings (in ignorance) go to them;
  4. those who worship Kṛṣṇa come to Him.
- "What can be clearer, more unambiguous?" — different paths lead to different goals `14:00`.

#### Where the idea comes from [14:00]

- It comes from a desire to water down complexity and make things artificially simple.
- Things should not be made more complicated than they are, but neither can they be made simplistic: a kindergarten student cannot understand advanced calculus — the student has to evolve.
- The wish "you follow your path, I follow mine, we'll all be happy, there'll be no conflict" is a good desire — but it just does not work.

#### BG 4.11: all people, not all paths [16:00 – 22:00]

- Impersonalists often cite BG 4.11 for "all paths lead to the same goal."
- Śrīla Prabhupāda's emphasis in the first half is reciprocation: Kṛṣṇa rewards each according to how they surrender — eternal destination for pure devotion, whatever is desired for material motivation.
- The second half, in Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation, says everyone follows Kṛṣṇa's path in all respects. The word for "all" applies to the people, not to the paths — "all people are on My path," not "all paths come to Me" `18:00`.
- The profound meaning `18:00 – 20:00`:
  - Everyone is attracted to something — the materialistic to good-looking and good-feeling things, the religious to various _devas_, atheists to their ideas, drunkards to alcohol, sports lovers to sports, devotees to Kṛṣṇa.
  - Whatever attracts anyone is ultimately a spark of Kṛṣṇa's all-attractiveness (BG 10.41: all opulent, beautiful and glorious creations spring from a spark of His splendour).
  - A woman's beauty or a batsman's talent is theirs only temporarily — given by Kṛṣṇa (through past karma, along with present effort) and lost with age and sickness.
- So in one sense everyone is attracted to Kṛṣṇa — but attraction to the sparks does not take one to Him. Attraction to alcohol takes one away from Kṛṣṇa; attraction to Kṛṣṇa with _bhakti_ takes one to Him `20:00`.
- "All people are on My path" thus means all are attracted to Kṛṣṇa in His various manifestations, yet some are going towards Him and some away. Only when we see the sparks' connection with the whole do we stop being infatuated by the sparks and concentrate our attraction on the whole `20:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Scripture repeatedly teaches by contrast, and BG 4.11 says all people are on Kṛṣṇa's path — attracted to sparks of His splendour — not that every path brings them to Him; we must redirect attraction from the sparks to the whole. [08:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### The socio-cultural view: exclusivism, pluralism, inclusivism [22:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** If logic and scripture show that all paths do not lead to the same goal, why do people subscribe to the idea? The teacher looks at three broad approaches `22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Exclusivism_ — "my way is the only right way," like a channel claiming "exclusive coverage" of the World Cup; it reduces God to a monopolised commodity in the religious marketplace and alienates intelligent people `22:00`.
- _Pluralism_ — "all ways are right," another way of saying all paths lead to the same goal; some even say there are as many religions as human beings `24:00`.
- _Inclusivism_ — one way with various levels on it: people approach God at different levels, with different motivations and conceptions, but there is ultimately one way `30:00`.

#### Exclusivism: narrow-mindedness [22:00]

- If God is great and the father of all, why would He be restricted to one path?
- The teacher says most Abrahamic religions are based on this exclusivist idea, and it is one main reason people become alienated from religion.

#### Pluralism: zero-mindedness [24:00 – 28:00]

- A reaction to exclusivism, attractive in multicultural societies where calling all other religions wrong seems alienating and conflict-prone.
- We should be open-minded — but not so open-minded that our brains fall out; tolerance should not lead to impotence.
- The great theistic religions broadly agree: develop love for God, do not become infatuated with worldly things.
- Logical problem: if all paths are right, atheism is right too — and if there is no God, all religious paths become wrong. So all paths cannot be right `26:00`.
- Exclusivism marks narrow-mindedness; pluralism marks zero-mindedness — no intelligence used at all `26:00`.

#### The medicine analogy [26:00 – 28:00]

- A patient may choose Ayurveda, allopathy, homoeopathy or naturopathy — but not everything a patient does is treatment. Eating the very foods that cause the disease is not "my variety of treatment."
- The test is objective: energy returns, the face regains its lustre, the medical reports come out well — signs of health that hold across systems of medicine.
- Likewise, the objective criterion in spiritual life is whether a person is developing love for God `28:00`.

#### Śrīla Prabhupāda's broad vision [28:00 – 32:00]

- Asked by a Christian priest about Jesus Christ, Śrīla Prabhupāda said "He is our guru" — because Jesus had so much love for God that he laid down his life in God's service.
- Inclusivism applied to medicine: one road towards health or away from it, with different lanes (Ayurveda, homoeopathy, naturopathy) — but one should be becoming healthier `30:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda said our goal is not to convert a Christian or a Muslim into a Hindu, but to make people lovers of God; the only conversion is from materialism to spirituality `30:00 – 32:00`.
- Any path by which this happens is bona fide; if it is not happening, the path is not bona fide. Sentimental pluralism equates sickness with health `32:00`.

#### The television debate [32:00 – 36:00]

- On a television show, Śrīla Prabhupāda and a disciple appeared with a Christian priest and his student — a populist confrontation.
- The host asked the priest whether one who follows the Gītā would go back to God; the priest stayed silent.
- Asked whether one who follows the Bible would go back to God, Śrīla Prabhupāda said yes — if he follows God's message and develops love for God.
- The priest, asked about the Swami, said the path back to God is very narrow and one must follow the Bible — exposing narrow-mindedness `34:00`.
- Affiliation with an institution does not take us back to God; purification of the heart does. Certain paths and institutions can facilitate developing love for God `34:00`.
- To a nominal Christian, Śrīla Prabhupāda would recommend chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa — Caitanya Mahāprabhu's mercy, the _yuga-dharma_, a swift and effective treatment; a committed Christian he would inspire to follow more seriously, e.g. to take "Thou shalt not kill" to vegetarianism `34:00 – 36:00`.

#### The hospital analogy [36:00]

- We invite the sick lying on the street into our hospital; we do not break into another hospital, destroy it and drag its patients to ours.
- Exclusivist religions break other people's temples, persecute and even kill to monopolise the religious marketplace — that is not our understanding.
- Communal harmony will not come by whitewashing all paths as good; we must accept that different paths have different consequences.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Between narrow exclusivism and zero-minded pluralism lies inclusivism: one path with many levels, judged by an objective test — growth in love of God — just as treatments are judged by whether the patient becomes healthier. [22:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Impersonalism judged by its consequences [36:00 – 44:00]

**Context.** Applying the consequence criterion to impersonalism `36:00`.

#### When impersonalism helps and when it harms [38:00]

- If it does not lead to criticism of _bhakti_ or offence towards the Lord, impersonalism is also progress: many impersonalists respect Vedic culture, the Vedas, cows and deities, and come to some level of goodness.
- The problem arises when they offend the Supreme Lord — then they become more hostile to devotional culture.
- _Māyāvādīs_ hold that all paths lead to the same goal because they want to unite everyone: each worships his own deity, and all supposedly lead to the one supreme Brahman. This is not scriptural — one must have a particular conception of God as the Supreme Being and worship Him to make spiritual advancement.

#### Strong philosophy, cordial dealings [38:00 – 40:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda made very strong statements about impersonalism because of its harmful consequences, yet with people he was warm, cordial and gentlemanly.
- While staying with a Dr Mishra at his yoga studio in New York — philosophically at loggerheads, since Dr Mishra taught impersonalism based on Śaṅkara — Śrīla Prabhupāda not only talked with him on friendly terms but cooked for him and fed him.
- Philosophical differences should not make us arrogant or culturally insensitive in ways that hurt outreach.

#### Levels of authority in Śrīla Prabhupāda's words [40:00 – 42:00]

- Words like "fools and rascals" appear mainly in conversations with small groups of intimate disciples, to make right and wrong very clear; they appear very rarely in the purports.
- In a 1976 letter, replying to a disciple who wanted to transcribe all his lectures, Śrīla Prabhupāda said it was unnecessary: whatever he wanted to teach was in his purports.
- All his words are authoritative, but there is a hierarchy:
  1. **Purports** — most universal, broadest in appeal.
  2. **Lectures** — often context-specific (e.g. addressing a Mumbai-specific issue).
  3. **Conversations** — with particular people, in a particular context.
  4. **Letters** — the most contextual.

#### Purity and potency [42:00 – 44:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda could use strong words because he had the authority of age and, above all, of purity.
- In one lecture he describes a devotee praying: "Kṛṣṇa, all these fools and rascals are suffering; please give me the ability to help them come back to You" — used compassionately, not angrily.
- When we use such words, it is usually judgmental; two questions: do we have the purity of motivation, and do we have the potency of transformation?
- Our purpose is to attract people to Kṛṣṇa; driving them away is disservice. BG 3.26: do not disturb the minds of those attached; engage them and guide them to a higher consciousness `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Śrīla Prabhupāda's strong words came from purity and compassion and are found mainly in contextual settings; his purports are the primary authority, and what we must imitate is his result — bringing people to Kṛṣṇa — not merely his harshest vocabulary. [38:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Dealing with people under impersonalist influence [44:00 – 58:00]

**Context.** Devotees use "_māyāvādī_" as an open-ended label for anyone who thinks the Absolute is impersonal, but there is a huge variety within that group `44:00`.

#### Convinced māyāvādīs versus people under influence [44:00 – 46:00]

- **Hard-line _māyāvādīs_** — intellectually convinced, quoting (and misinterpreting) scripture; they are few.
- **People under _māyāvāda_ influence** — the large majority; they have given it no serious thought, having picked up "we have to merge" from movies, television or popular comics.
- Before Śrīla Prabhupāda, most prominent Indian spiritual teachers were impersonalists, and the national leaders praised in pre-independence history were mostly impersonalists, so people have emotional affection for such figures without knowing their teachings `46:00`.
- Telling such a person "your Swami is a fool and a rascal" shocks them — "how can one _sādhu_ call another _sādhu_ that?" — and they go away and never come back `46:00`.

#### How to respond instead [48:00]

- If someone mentions a spiritual teacher, do not pounce; appreciate their spiritual inclination — rare in this age — and invite them to read Śrīla Prabhupāda's books and come to programmes for a systematic understanding.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda taught: ask what that teacher's philosophy is; usually the person does not know. Then say the Gītā is accepted by all great teachers in Vedic culture — study it and understand its essential message.

#### The bridge [48:00 – 50:00, 54:00]

- The person stands on a small hill of present faith; Kṛṣṇa's message is a tall mountain. By our conduct and words we build a bridge so they develop faith in us, and through us faith in Kṛṣṇa.
- Two ways we can break the bridge:
  1. before they start to cross it;
  2. while they are on it — creating a confrontation in the middle, so they conclude "spiritualists all fight among themselves" and return to material life.

#### A college story [50:00 – 52:00]

- In college, the teacher invited a dynamic, spiritually minded hostel-mate who liked the programme, the devotees, the classes and the _prasāda_.
- At a crowded temple festival they missed each other; the boy asked another devotee about a spiritual teacher he admired, and was told that teacher would not even get a place in hell.
- Shocked, he spread the story; the hostel programme stopped for weeks and much "firefighting" was needed. He later became a _sannyāsī_ in an impersonalist mission — a person of vigour who could have become a devotee.

#### Emotional filters [52:00 – 56:00]

- Philosophy does not stir emotions, but names and persons do; criticising someone by name closes people's emotional filters, and then they are not open to rational discussion.
- First, we ourselves should be convinced that _māyāvāda_ is philosophically harmful; at the same time, culturally it is good that people under its influence have sympathy for the Vedas, deities and cows — not the best, but devotees should be able to see good in others.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda said his devotees should be perfect gentlemen.
- The test of strong preaching is not the strength of the words but the strength of the transformation in people's hearts — which comes through sensitive, respectful speech, careful explanation, cultured behaviour, love and care `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Śrīla Prabhupāda's varied strategies [56:00]

- In his full conversation with George Harrison (only part appears in _Chant and Be Happy_), Harrison mentions many spiritual teachers; Śrīla Prabhupāda makes no comment about them, simply keeps speaking Kṛṣṇa consciousness vigorously, and Harrison became a devotee.
- Like a warrior choosing weapons for the situation, Śrīla Prabhupāda used different strategies at different times. What we must follow is not one specific strategy but the same result: getting people to Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Most people today are under impersonalist influence, not convinced impersonalists; dealt with sensitively — appreciating their inclination and building a bridge of trust — they can be brought to Kṛṣṇa, whereas harsh "strong preaching" breaks the bridge. [44:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
