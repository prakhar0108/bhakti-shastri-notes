## Mantra-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Mantra 11: two kinds of knowledge side by side [02:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:iso/11 -->

**Context.** The teacher explains the mantra's key words and Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation, then gives an overview of the purport's structure `02:00 – 06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **"Both"** — the teacher highlights the mantra's word for "both": one must know both kinds of knowledge together `02:00`.
- _Avidyā_ (here) — knowledge for material maintenance, needed to navigate the journey of this life, which is bounded by birth and death `02:00`.
- _Vidyā_ (here) — knowledge for spiritual advancement, by which one attains eternal existence `02:00`.

#### The boat and the land [02:00 – 04:00]

- Crossing an ocean, I need to know two things:
  - how to ride the boat across the ocean;
  - where the land is, so I can reach it.
- Knowing where the land is without basic knowledge of the boat, I will not get there.
- Likewise, with only spiritual knowledge and no basic knowledge for bodily maintenance, one cannot live in this world — and one must live in order to reach the destination.
- Material knowledge here is not knowledge of how to become happy in this world, but of how to live fruitfully so as to fulfil life's purpose.
- We cannot solve life's problems at the material level, but by living intelligently we can raise our consciousness beyond them, ultimately back to the spiritual world `04:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's translation and the map of the purport [04:00 – 06:00]

- Prabhupāda's translation stresses learning the process of nescience and transcendental knowledge "side by side" to transcend repeated birth and death and enjoy the full blessings of immortality `04:00`.
- The purport is among the longest in the book — fourteen paragraphs — and the teacher outlines it `04:00 – 06:00`:
  1. Paragraphs 1–5 — immortality is impossible materially.
  2. The overarching theme — immortality is possible only by following Kṛṣṇa's teachings.
  3. Paragraphs 6–7 — Kṛṣṇa Himself comes to make us immortal.
  4. Next — sense gratification binds us to mortality, so restrict it.
  5. Two paragraphs — _apara-dharma_ teaches how to balance the material and spiritual sides.
  6. Last three paragraphs — _para-dharma_ grants immortality.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Mantra 11 asks for balance, not rejection: knowledge for maintaining the body is the boat, knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is the land — both are needed, but the boat exists only to reach the shore. [02:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### Why immortality is impossible materially [06:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda gives a scriptural example and a logical analysis for why material immortality is impossible; the teacher adds a parallel anecdote `06:00`.

#### Hiraṇyakaśipu's negative example [06:00]

- Hiraṇyakaśipu used his full intelligence and enormous austerity to gain power none of us could ever get, yet in one moment everything was destroyed by the power of time, _kāla_ — the Lord manifesting as time.
- The lesson the teacher draws from this episode: even opulence up to the level of Indra or Brahmā is of no lasting use; only the association of devotees, and through it devotional service, has real value.
- Hiraṇyakaśipu thus shows, by negative example, what will not grant immortality.

#### Rāvaṇa and Nārada [08:00 – 10:00]

- During his campaign to conquer the universe, Rāvaṇa saw Nārada, considered him an enemy because of his association with the sages, the gods and the Lord, and tried to kill him.
- Arrows that made the gods fall or flee simply disappeared into Nārada's body; even Rāvaṇa's most formidable weapons had no effect.
- Rāvaṇa then changed tactics — the demoniac strategy is "if you can't conquer someone, befriend him" — and asked the secret of Nārada's power.
- Nārada explained he has a spiritual body, eternal and indestructible, obtained by chanting the Lord's names and becoming His devotee; Rāvaṇa could do the same.
- Rāvaṇa refused.
- **The demoniac mentality:** wanting immortality at the material level, without the spiritual consciousness that actually leads to it `10:00`.

#### Mystic power and scientific power [10:00 – 12:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that mystic and scientific power are similar: both try to manipulate material nature.
  - **Mystic power** taps higher sources of energy among higher beings in the universe — as Rāvaṇa and Hiraṇyakaśipu gained powers through austerity.
  - **Scientific power** acknowledges no higher beings, but studies how nature's power works and tries to capture it for oneself.
- In _The Nectar of Devotion_ and _The Nectar of Instruction_, Prabhupāda compares _yogic siddhis_ to modern technology: yogīs fly, and science flies by airplane — both do with matter what we normally cannot.
- Today people mostly neither believe in nor access mystic power, so their efforts to control nature go into science and technology `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Whether through mystic austerity or modern technology, trying to gain immortality by manipulating matter is the demoniac mentality; immortality comes only by rising to spiritual consciousness, as Nārada showed. [06:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### The six changes and the limits of science [12:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda's simple reasoning: matter undergoes six changes that are unstoppable and even incomprehensible `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ensouled matter_ — the teacher's term for what scientists call "living matter"; matter in which the soul, consciousness, is present `12:00`.
- _Oxymoron_ — two words with contradictory meanings put together (e.g. "courageous coward"); "living matter" is an oxymoron in this philosophy, since matter is defined as having no consciousness `14:00`.

#### Ordinary matter versus ensouled matter [12:00 – 16:00]

- Ordinary matter goes through three phases — creation, deterioration, destruction (a house left alone decays under time and the elements).
- Ensouled matter undergoes three extra changes:
  - **Growth** — a laptop's screen does not grow in twenty years; a baby, a plant, even microbes grow `14:00`.
  - **Reproduction** — a carpenter may call fitting parts "male" and "female," but tools never produce more tools; living beings reproduce `14:00 – 16:00`.
  - **Preservation** — a cut watch strap stays cut; a cut hand bleeds, clots and heals itself `16:00`.
- These three are, in one sense, the physical symptoms of the soul's presence `16:00`.

#### "How" is not "why" [16:00 – 18:00]

- Science may describe mechanisms — how clotting, growth or reproduction happen — but mechanisms answer "how," not "why."
- Why does the matter of a laptop not grow, reproduce or preserve itself, while the matter of a living person does?
- These changes are largely incomprehensible; if we cannot even understand them, there is little real chance of stopping them.

#### The failed search for an "aging gene" [18:00 – 20:00]

- Cells are in constant flux; with age, fewer cells are produced than destroyed, and that is aging.
- Researchers hoped to find and deactivate one gene controlling this, but could not isolate any; it turned out to be a whole network of mechanisms, and disrupting it would disrupt the body.
- An international gerontological association announced that no technology, medicine, surgery or therapy can reverse, stop or slow aging `20:00`.
- What remains is only cosmetic — dyeing hair, removing wrinkles, replacing teeth — which does not improve the body's inner health.

#### Forced to bow to nature [20:00 – 22:00]

- Prabhupāda said material nature will quietly, helplessly make all of us old.
- On a morning walk a young man insisted no one could force him to bow down; Prabhupāda asked if he would not grow old. "That is simply biology." "So it is biological force — you do not want to grow old, yet you must."
- We may not believe in God, but we must submit to some higher principle that makes us do things against our will `22:00`.

#### Changing within nature, not the nature of nature [22:00]

- Shrinking a computer from large to small is manipulating nature within a particular zone, changing only how we process it.
- The nature of nature is mortality and destructibility; we can change how nature works within limits, but never its nature.
- So technological advancement should not deceive us: death is inevitable.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Matter's six changes — especially growth, reproduction and preservation in ensouled matter — are beyond our understanding and control; technology can rearrange nature but cannot change the nature of nature, which is mortality. [12:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### The longing for immortality points to the soul [22:00 – 30:00]

**Context.** If material immortality is impossible, where does our urge for it come from? `22:00`

#### Every fundamental need has its fulfilment [22:00 – 24:00]

- Whatever we desire at a universal, fundamental level is provided for in nature: thirst — water; hunger — food; cold — warmth.
- The longing for everlasting existence is deep-rooted, and not only in humans: a fly expertly evades our swats because it loves life `24:00`.

#### From inference to scripture [24:00 – 26:00]

- The urge comes from a part of us that is immortal — the _ātmā_.
- Logic lets us infer that something immortal is present within us through our longing for immortality, but not what it is; for its nature we must turn to scripture.
- Scripture says the _ātmā_ belongs to the spiritual world: the eternal soul seeks eternity here, where it is impossible, and will find it on returning there `26:00`.

#### Don't make a one-sided attempt [26:00 – 28:00]

- Prabhupāda: if even Hiraṇyakaśipu, the most powerful materialist, could not become deathless, what can the "tiny Hiraṇyakaśipus" of today accomplish, whose plans are thwarted moment to moment?
- So the Īśopaniṣad tells us not to make one-sided attempts to win the struggle for existence.
- Darwin spoke of the "survival of the fittest"; Prabhupāda's penetrating one-sentence reply: even the fittest do not survive `26:00`.
- One soldier against a million has a tiny chance; material immortality has zero chance — so why fight for a lost cause? Find another way `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa comes to show the way back [28:00]

- Immortality is possible by raising consciousness to the spiritual level; Kṛṣṇa descends from the spiritual world to tell us how to go back to Godhead.
- Prabhupāda notes the Lord has given the Vedic scriptures in India and other scriptures in other countries to remind forgetful humans that their home is not here.
- Prabhupāda's vision, as the teacher explains it: the world's great religions are arrangements by the Lord to gradually elevate the soul back to the supreme destination — specific differences exist, but the essential purpose is to take the soul from matter back to God.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The universal longing for immortality is itself evidence of the immortal soul; since that longing cannot be fulfilled here, Kṛṣṇa and the scriptures come to direct it back to the spiritual world. [22:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Miseries as indirect teachers [28:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** Why should the soul want to go back to God? Prabhupāda: the miseries of this world serve to indirectly remind us of our incompatibility with dead matter `28:00 – 30:00`.

#### Incompatibility and the right kind of divorce [30:00]

- I don't want to die, yet death is forced on me — something must be incompatible: the eternal soul is seeking enjoyment and existence in a temporary arena.
- Vedic culture does not encourage divorce in marriage, but it encourages another divorce — of spirit from matter, which are intrinsically and irresolvably incompatible.
- This disentangling is not a one-moment rupture; there is a process for it, but first we must recognise the incompatibility.

#### We are the aliens [32:00]

- Many are fascinated by aliens and flying saucers; the Vedic worldview accepts life on various planets, so there is nothing to get excited about.
- The teacher mentions one devotee who correlated alien sightings with the Bhāgavatam's description of Śālva's airplane.
- The real point: scripture tells us we ourselves are the aliens here; we do not belong to this world and must return to the spiritual world.

#### Education plus experience equals learning [32:00 – 36:00]

- **Direct teaching** comes through hearing: a doctor or parent says "don't overeat."
- **Indirect teaching** comes through experience: overeat, get a severe stomach upset, suffer.
- Learning happens only when experience is connected with education; otherwise the experience is wasted — "I was just unlucky," and tomorrow one overeats again `34:00`.
- Most people either lack the education or don't take it seriously, so they suffer without learning.
- Prabhupāda's point (as the teacher recalls it): millions since creation have died without exception, yet we hope to be the exception — and that hope itself is what we must "take exception to" `34:00`.
- Scriptures and devotees can share realizations and help, but we ourselves must make the connection `36:00`.

#### Suffering confirms faith [36:00]

- Some devotees are disturbed: "I'm practising, yet there are so many problems — why isn't Kṛṣṇa helping?"
- For a serious devotee, problems increase faith: scripture said this world is full of misery, and now I'm experiencing it — so scripture is true.
- This doesn't mean we must stay miserable; scripture gives a process for taking Kṛṣṇa's shelter and minimizing misery.
- Comparing ourselves with those comfortable now is short-term thinking; overall, the material journey is doomed, with misery increasing.

#### "Happily ever after" versus the Gītā [36:00 – 38:00]

- Movies end with "happily ever after"; Kṛṣṇa in the Gītā describes this world as a place of misery — the exact opposite.
- In reality nobody lives "ever after," and very few live happily.
- **Lollipop spirituality:** like a child happy with a lollipop, we think spirituality will get us what we want and solve our problem, and everything will be fine. With this idea we cannot be steady; scripture teaches that this world is a place of misery and we must raise our consciousness beyond it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The miseries of life are indirect teachers: when experience is connected with scriptural education, suffering confirms that we do not belong here and strengthens rather than weakens faith. [30:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### The body is a bad bargain — DIVE [38:00 – 46:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda later says the body is a bad bargain; the teacher explains it through the acronym _DIVE_ — understanding it, we will dive deep into Kṛṣṇa consciousness `38:00`.

#### D — Duration [38:00 – 40:00]

- Sensual enjoyment lasts moments or minutes, though people fantasize about it for much of their lives.
- Suffering lasts long: a toothache for days, an ulcer that hurts with every tongue movement, constant back pain, terminal illnesses with weeks of pain.
- And everyone must grow old, become diseased and die.

#### I — Intensity [40:00 – 42:00]

- The body is sensitised far more for pain than for pleasure.
- A man enjoying a luxurious massage on a soft bed — one pinprick, and all the pleasure is countered.
- Relishing sweet rice over every part of the tongue — bite the tongue once, and all the pleasure disappears.

#### V — Variety [42:00 – 44:00]

- Each part gives pleasure in few ways but pain in many.
- The eyes see beauty, but dust, germs or degeneration can harm them.
- Teeth let us bite and enjoy food, yet are so vulnerable that a whole branch of medicine — dentistry — exists for them.
- Skin enjoys a soft touch, but can be burned, cut, frozen or diseased.

#### E — Extent [44:00 – 46:00]

- Only the senses — the _karmendriyas_ and _jñānendriyas_ — can give pleasure.
- The liver, kidneys or medulla oblongata can never make us smile, but each can make us cry and scream, and each can get many diseases.
- **Conclusion:** rather than hoping for enjoyment here and there, dive deep into Kṛṣṇa consciousness and seek happiness at the spiritual level `46:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
In duration, intensity, variety and extent, the body gives far more pain than pleasure — so the intelligent response is to stop expecting happiness from it and dive deep into Kṛṣṇa consciousness. [38:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Apara-dharma, adharma and para-dharma [46:00 – 58:00]

**Context.** How do we make the best use of a bad bargain? Through the Vedic system of _dharma_, _artha_, _kāma_ and _mokṣa_ — called _apara-dharma_ `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Apara-dharma_ — "material religion," not in a negative sense: living harmoniously in this world while fulfilling material needs within dharmic morality `46:00`, `50:00`.
- _Para-dharma_ — the supreme religion, pure devotional service, by which one develops pure love for the transcendental Lord `50:00 – 52:00`.
- _Adharma_ — making the worst use of the bad bargain: pursuing only _artha_ and _kāma_ without _dharma_ `52:00`.
- _Kaitava-dharma_ — "cheating religion"; the Bhāgavatam's phrase for religion that does not lead to devotion to God `58:00`.

#### Dharma brings social and cosmic harmony [46:00 – 50:00]

- **Social level:** _dharma_ gives moral principles, so _artha_ is managed equitably ("don't take others' property"); _kāma_ is regulated through marriage, with a culture of responsibility and commitment. Without _dharma_, chaos and disruption follow.
- **Cosmic level:** without _yajña_ the rains do not come; even someone who doubts this must accept that we depend on nature. Human beings have disrupted ecology — desertification, deforestation, pollution — and "climate change" is a euphemism for likely environmental super-disasters `48:00`.
- Seeing _dharma_ work, one develops faith, inquires into scripture, learns of _mokṣa_ and begins striving for liberation — a gradual progression that keeps humanity on a positive track `50:00`.

#### A gradual way up [50:00]

- I cannot simply wish the body away; even knowing I am not the body, I cannot suddenly get rid of it. There is a process of purification through harmonious living by which detachment develops.
- _Apara-dharma_ may not take us immediately back to Kṛṣṇa; it takes us up to _svarga_ and back again — but by it one learns that scripture works, and then turns to scripture's higher teaching: practise _dharma_ to develop love for God and go back to Him.
- If there is a conflict between the two, _para-dharma_ should be chosen; in general they can be harmonised `52:00`.

#### Adharma: the worst use of the bad bargain [52:00 – 54:00]

- Obsession with _artha_ and _kāma_ alone leads to:
  - **spiritual degradation** — it drags consciousness down and leads to more immoral acts;
  - **material devastation** — uncontrolled lust makes people violate others; uncontrolled greed makes them exploit others, even to starvation.
- _Dharma_ is the check that directs the drive for _artha_ and _kāma_; without it, that drive "goes mad" and riots `54:00`.

#### The sick man's feast [54:00]

- Prabhupāda compares our condition to a sick person: if he tries to enjoy, the sickness both worsens and prolongs.
- Eating a feast with an upset stomach makes it worse and slower to heal; enjoying in our diseased material condition creates more karmic bondage — more suffering, and longer to get free.
- This doesn't mean giving up all material enjoyment, but restricting it within dharmic limits — the program given by the great sages, applied by saints according to time, place and circumstance, as Prabhupāda did for modern society.

#### Apara-dharma as a safety net for devotees [54:00 – 58:00]

- Prabhupāda wanted _varṇāśrama_ — the social and spiritual orders — established, because it creates a social arrangement conducive to spiritual advancement.
- _Apara-dharma_ is like a safety net beneath the tightrope walk of _para-dharma_ `56:00`.
- Most devotees will be householders, who must act as responsible family members and citizens; renunciants contribute differently, by sharing spiritual knowledge — everyone has their _dharma_.
- Neglecting material responsibilities makes life so complex it distracts from spiritual life: a student who doesn't study and fails faces such anxiety that spiritual practice becomes very hard. This is the _avidyā_ the mantra refers to `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Only para-dharma satisfies the heart [58:00]

- Taking care of bodily needs does not satisfy the heart; anything short of pure devotional service satisfies neither Kṛṣṇa nor the soul.
- The "full blessings of immortality" in Prabhupāda's translation come only through _para-dharma_.
- One may say "I have so many family, national and professional duties — no time for spiritual life." These duties matter, but caught only in them, one gets material results, continues in birth and death, and misses the real happiness of the soul connecting in love with Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Apara-dharma — responsible, moral, dharmic living — is the best use of the bad bargain and a safety net for spiritual life; but only para-dharma, pure devotional service, satisfies the heart and grants immortality. [46:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>

### Bhakti: the steady state of the pendulum [58:00 – 64:00]

**Context.** The class concludes with the glory of _bhakti_, which balances between _karma_ and _jñāna_ `58:00`.

#### The pendulum [58:00 – 60:00]

- **_Karma_ — one extreme:** with only _avidyā_ and no spiritual knowledge, we abuse the senses for material purposes without regulation; acting on sensual desire is natural, but binding, and even the body suffers.
- **_Jñāna_ — the other extreme:** the senses cause suffering, so I reject them — "sense objects are false, everything is false, I want to merge into Brahman." Non-binding, but unnatural; almost impossible for most, and very laborious even for those who can, as the Gītā says in 12.5 `60:00`.
- **_Bhakti_ — the steady state:** using the material senses for a spiritual purpose — serving Kṛṣṇa — so the senses that were a cause of bondage become a cause of liberation.

#### Natural and liberating [60:00]

- Being in a physical body, activity is natural for us, so _bhakti-yoga_ is natural because it involves action.
- Because that action is divinely selfless, meant for Kṛṣṇa, it is liberating.
- Thus by _apara-dharma_ one negotiates the journey through this world, and by devotional service one attains eternity `60:00`.

#### Summary [62:00 – 64:00]

- Immortality is impossible materially, by mystic or scientific power; the three extra changes of ensouled matter are unstoppable and incomprehensible.
- The longing for immortality shows the soul belongs to another world; connecting education with experience lets misery teach us.
- The body is a bad bargain (DIVE); _dharma_, _artha_, _kāma_ and _mokṣa_ make the best use of it, a slow way up.
- _Para-dharma_ liberates and returns us to Kṛṣṇa; _bhakti_ integrates the naturalness of _karma_ with the non-binding nature of _jñāna_, removing both their deficiencies `64:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti joins what karma and jñāna each get right — the naturalness of activity and freedom from bondage — by engaging the material senses in Kṛṣṇa's service. [58:00 – 64:00]
</Callout>
