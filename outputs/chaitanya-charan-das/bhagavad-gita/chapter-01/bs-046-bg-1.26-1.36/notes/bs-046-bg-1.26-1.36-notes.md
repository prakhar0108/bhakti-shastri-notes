## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 1.26 [00:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.26 -->

**Context.** From between the armies Arjuna sees, on both sides, the members of his own family — several generations of them — assembled not for a celebration but for mutual destruction `02:00`.

#### A family get-together for destruction [00:00 – 02:00]

- A family get-together is normally a joyful occasion.
- Today, with both husband and wife working, even husband, wife and children getting together can be difficult; when children, parents and grandparents come together — three generations — it is a rare occasion.
- Here several generations have come together, but not for a celebration: for mutual destruction `02:00`.

#### The generations Arjuna sees [02:00 – 04:00]

- **Father's generation** — for example Śalya, a relative of Mādrī, the mother of Nakula and Sahadeva (a step-mother to Arjuna); others of that generation are also present `02:00`.
- **Grandfather's generation** — Bhīṣma Pitāmaha `02:00`.
- **Teachers (_ācāryas_), brothers, sons and grandsons** `02:00 – 04:00`.
- Even Arjuna's son Abhimanyu was already married by the time of the war, and his wife was already pregnant — so the coming generation too was represented `04:00`.
- Arjuna sees relatives not only on the opposite side but on his own side as well `04:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
What Arjuna sees is not an enemy army but his own family, several generations deep, gathered on both sides for mutual destruction — the scene that sets off his crisis. [02:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.28 [08:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.28 -->

**Context.** These are Arjuna's first words after seeing his relatives; his whole perception of the opposing army has changed `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kṛṣṇa_ — this verse contains the first use of the name "Kṛṣṇa" in the Gītā; earlier Kṛṣṇa is called by other names such as Hṛṣīkeśa. In the _Mahābhārata_ the name "Kṛṣṇa" does occur but not very frequently; it is a very intimate, affectionate name `08:00`.
- _Pratyakṣa-pramāṇa_ — knowledge by direct sense perception `10:00`.
- _Śabda-pramāṇa_ — knowledge from scripture `10:00 – 12:00`.

#### From "those who want to fight" to "my own people" [08:00 – 10:00]

- Earlier Arjuna wanted to see those who had come to fight; now he speaks of them as "my own people."
- He sees: "These are my people, yet they are fighting against me — and what is happening to me by seeing this?"
- The teacher describes this as the start of Arjuna's emotional breakdown `10:00`.

#### Pratyakṣa put him in illusion; śabda takes him out [10:00 – 14:00]

- Arjuna came to the battlefield ready to fight; what put him into illusion was _pratyakṣa_ — what he saw.
- _Pratyakṣa_ is not to be dismissed: it is also a valid source of knowledge, and practical transactions in the world are not possible without it `10:00 – 12:00`.
- But when one is overpowered by emotions, the emotions disconnect one from one's scriptural roots — they uproot a person from the ground of scriptural knowledge in which he had been rooted `12:00`.
- Part of the point of study is to connect the specific situation we are experiencing with the universal instructions of scripture, and then learn from it `12:00`.
- Arjuna is in illusion because of _pratyakṣa_, and _śabda_ — Kṛṣṇa's instruction — will take him out of it `12:00`.
- We live in the world and use sense perception, but sometimes it misleads; at such times we have to consult scripture to get a proper understanding `14:00`.

#### Soft-heartedness, not weakness [14:00]

- An interesting point from the purport: Arjuna's symptoms are not due to weakness but to soft-heartedness, a characteristic of a pure devotee.
- Feeling emotions is not wrong; compassion for others' suffering is a very noble emotion, and devotees do have such good qualities `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sense perception is valid but can mislead; when strong emotion uproots us from scriptural understanding — as it did Arjuna — the way back is through śabda, scripture applied to our specific situation. [10:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.29 [16:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.29 -->

**Context.** Arjuna describes the physical effects of his emotional state, including his bow slipping from his hand `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### Emotions have physical effects [16:00 – 18:00]

- Arjuna is afraid not just for himself but for the bloodbath that is going to spread over the whole of Kurukṣetra and the death of so many of his relatives `16:00`.
- Example: a lady is bringing a plate of food when a phone call says her husband has had a heart attack — the plate falls from her hands. Strong emotions take control of the body.
- The teacher notes that emotions generally have physical effects more quickly on females; for a warrior to have his bow slipping from his hand is extraordinary — a sign of a deeply troubling emotional experience `16:00 – 18:00`.
- The weakness moves from the physical to the mental: it is not that Arjuna chooses to let go — his bow is slipping `18:00`.

#### Two purports, two perspectives [18:00 – 22:00]

- The purport of the previous verse calls Arjuna a pure devotee and his compassion the sign of a pure devotee; the next purports say these symptoms are due to the material conception of life `18:00`.
- How do we understand both? When scripture narrates the Lord's pastimes, the narration is not only about deciding what is right and wrong; it is about learning what we can learn `20:00`.
- **First perspective:** everything in the Lord's pastimes, especially with His associates, is orchestrated by the Lord; His associates exhibit exalted qualities. From this angle Arjuna displays concern and compassion — devotional qualities `20:00`.
- **Second perspective:** Arjuna acts like a conditioned soul, and we look at the philosophical point we can learn from his actions — the bodily conception of life `20:00`.
- This is not contradictory; it depends on the perspective taken, and we can learn through both. We should not see the Lord's associates as ordinary, yet we can learn from their moments of apparent illusion `20:00`.
- When one is in the bodily concept one is bewildered; rising to a higher level of consciousness happens when one gets spiritual knowledge `22:00`.
- The first chapter shows how situations create perplexity — and at the same time shows Arjuna as thoughtful and compassionate `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's grief can be read two ways without contradiction — as a pure devotee's compassion orchestrated by the Lord, and as a conditioned soul's bodily conception from which we learn; both perspectives teach. [18:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.30 [22:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.30 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says he cannot stand, his mind is reeling, and he sees only signs of disaster `22:00 – 24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nimitta_ — can mean causes, and can also mean symbols and omens `24:00`.

#### Omens and an interconnected universe [24:00 – 26:00]

- Arjuna's involuntary reactions can themselves be seen as omens of what is going to happen.
- Example: a pain in a certain part of the body may be a sign of an impending heart attack; one with knowledge connects a symptom with something more serious that requires attention `24:00`.
- The idea is that the universe is a coherent whole: cosmological, ecological, topographical and physiological factors all contribute to our karmic reactions, and so they can point to what is coming `24:00 – 26:00`.
- The teacher gives a further example of signs in nature before a calamity, when everyone — from the old to babies — runs out `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Nimitta means both causes and omens: in a coherent universe, even Arjuna's own involuntary reactions signal the tragedy ahead. [24:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.31 [26:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.31 -->

**Context.** Arjuna sees no good in killing his own people and says he desires neither victory, nor kingdom, nor happiness `26:00 – 34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śreyas_ — long-term good, contrasted with immediate pleasure: watching a cricket match gives pleasure, but a student who studies more and more is thinking of a long-term career `30:00 – 32:00`.

#### "What is in it for me?" [26:00 – 30:00]

- People are generally self-centred; they think in terms of what is in it for them.
- Arjuna finds himself in a lose-lose situation: whatever he does, he loses — "if I do this there is a problem, if I do that there is a problem; what should I do?" `28:00`.
- The question is to be considered not from our perspective but from God's perspective `28:00`.
- When we are self-centred we think only in terms of our own goals; when we find no good for ourselves, the situation seems senseless `28:00`.

#### The incomprehensible is not insensible [28:00 – 30:00]

- Something incomprehensible to me is not therefore without sense; just because it does not make sense to me does not mean it has no sense.
- Expecting everything to be comprehensible can be devastating.
- Illumination comes into our heart if we turn to Kṛṣṇa `30:00`.
- When things don't make sense it does not mean there is no sense in them — only that we are unable to make sense of them; Kṛṣṇa has a plan in everything that happens, and we simply have to do the best we can `30:00`.
- From the purport: everyone is interested in himself and his own welfare; no one is interested in the Supreme Self `30:00`.

#### Arjuna thinks long-term [32:00 – 36:00]

- Arjuna is thinking from a long-term point of view: even if he wins the war and gets the kingdom, what pleasure will be left if his teacher and grandfather are dead? "How can I kill them?" `32:00`.
- Bhīṣma dominated the battle for the first nine days; he was unstoppable `32:00`.
- Arjuna remembers how as a child he climbed onto his grandfather's lap and soiled him, and Bhīṣma stroked his hair and told him: "I am not your father; I am your father's father" `32:00 – 34:00`.
- Such sentiments are good, but the path of duty must be remembered: Arjuna's obligation is to the whole world — to ensure that righteousness prevails `34:00`.
- Arjuna thinks that if all are killed, the kingdom will be like an empty trophy — whom will he enjoy it with? Hence: "I don't desire victory, I don't desire the kingdom" `34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna, seeing only loss for himself, finds the situation senseless; the teacher's answer is that the incomprehensible is not insensible — Kṛṣṇa has a plan, and duty must be judged from His perspective, not ours. [26:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.32 – 1.35 [36:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** Arjuna asks Govinda what use a kingdom or enjoyment would be when those for whom one desires them are standing on the battlefield; he lists teachers, fathers, sons, grandfathers and other relatives, and says he would not kill them even for the three worlds, let alone this earth `36:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Govinda_ — the one who gives pleasure to the senses; Kṛṣṇa is addressed by this name in the first line of this section `38:00 – 40:00`.
- _Janārdana_ — the protector (maintainer) of the people `46:00`.

#### The repeated list and the World Cup [36:00 – 38:00]

- Earlier Sañjaya listed the warriors; now it is Arjuna repeating the list of relatives — a parallel, with the question "how can I kill them?" `36:00`.
- Returning to the World Cup example: imagine winning the World Cup but everyone in your country has been killed — with whom would you celebrate? `36:00`.
- The teacher clarifies that Arjuna speaks here not from objective appropriateness but from subjective emotion: we cannot enjoy anything material when the loved ones with whom we would celebrate are gone `36:00`.
- Arjuna says he would not kill them even for the three worlds — what then to speak of this earth; what would he gain? `38:00`.

#### The many names of Kṛṣṇa [38:00 – 42:00]

- Throughout these verses Arjuna refers to Kṛṣṇa by various names — a repeated phenomenon `40:00`.
- Our name is important to us; we generally remember a person's name, and a person feels valued when his name is used `40:00`.
- The name one uses also indicates mood and the state of the relationship; so the various names indicate Arjuna's thinking `40:00`.

#### Govinda — pleasure for the senses [40:00 – 46:00]

- When we engage our senses with Kṛṣṇa, the senses get the supreme beauty, and the pleasure the senses get is far greater than what they get by looking at ordinary good-looking objects or by eating `40:00 – 42:00`.
- The teacher cites Vaiṣṇava writings on the glories of the holy name, including a verse of Kulaśekhara Mahārāja about the sweet names of Kṛṣṇa `42:00`.
- When we chant Kṛṣṇa's holy names, Kṛṣṇa is present; the experience begins with the senses but goes beyond them — it is "transsensory": it enters the mind and is so fulfilling that bodily discomfort is hardly felt. Even in a crowded, sweaty place, without proper seating or sleep, one does not feel physical misery `44:00`.
- The holy name is the greatest delight to our senses; yet Arjuna says, "my senses are drying up" `46:00`.

#### Janārdana — protector of the people [46:00]

- Arjuna's implication: "You are the protector of the people; why are You engaging me in something that will destroy the people?"
- The teacher explains that destruction can also be protection: Kṛṣṇa has come to relieve the world of the burden of demoniac elements; though the world has its own equilibrium, it is sometimes disturbed by demons, and their destruction gives at least a temporary check on the disruption of social order they cause `46:00`.
- So Arjuna asks: "What pleasure will we have from killing the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Janārdana?" `46:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's names for Kṛṣṇa reveal his mood: he calls on Govinda, the giver of sensual delight, while his senses dry up, and on Janārdana, the protector, while asking why the people must be destroyed — not seeing that destroying the demoniac protects the world. [36:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.36 [48:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.36 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says sin will overcome them if they kill these aggressors, and asks Mādhava how they could be happy by killing their own kinsmen `48:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pāpa_ — sinful reaction; Arjuna fears that sin "will take shelter" of them `48:00`.
- _Ātatāyī_ — an aggressor: one who comes with weapons, with the intention to destroy and kill; the purport describes six kinds of aggressors `50:00`.
- _Mādhava_ — husband of the goddess of fortune; Arjuna's implication is "why are You bringing misfortune upon me?" `50:00`.

#### A note on verse numbering [48:00]

- Some verses in Chapter 1 are combined rather than standing as one verse each, which is why the chapter's total comes to forty-six; the teacher points this out while reading the verse, noting that the arrangement is based on continuity of thought `48:00`.

#### God never desires harm [50:00 – 52:00]

- From the purport: God never desires anything harmful for anyone; whatever Kṛṣṇa brings is ultimately good `50:00 – 52:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's actions can be like surgery — painful, but for the ultimate good `52:00`.
- Arjuna, in the bodily conception, feels that cutting off his relatives would be harmful `52:00`.

#### The Kauravas as aggressors [52:00 – 56:00]

- Duryodhana tried to poison Bhīma: on an outing he gave him poison, and after Bhīma fell asleep had him tied up and thrown into the river. By Kṛṣṇa's arrangement there were snakes there; their poison counteracted the poison, and Bhīma was given nectar and came back stronger `52:00 – 54:00`.
- There were several other such attempts before the war `54:00`.
- **The gambling match:** by the code of gambling, those who play should stake their own wealth; it is against the code for one person to play while another's wealth is staked. Śakuni played on behalf of Duryodhana, which went against the code `56:00`.
- **Draupadī:** while she was in her period and wearing a single cloth, Duḥśāsana dragged her out of the women's chambers into the public assembly. The teacher says this was equal to kidnapping someone's wife `56:00`.
- So the Kauravas had already committed several of the six kinds of aggression; they deserved capital punishment, and killing them would bring no sinful reaction `56:00`.

#### Aggressors who are also relatives [58:00 – 60:00]

- Arjuna fears he will incur sinful reaction; but the aggressors deserve to be killed `58:00`.
- The problem: they are aggressors and at the same time relatives, and it is _dharma_ to protect one's relatives. So there is a conflict in Arjuna's mind: should he act as a _kṣatriya_? `58:00`.
- The teacher then turns to how the Pāṇḍavas' kingdom was taken away by conspiracy, and begins to explain that it is normally the king who decides the successor — usually the eldest — as the captions end `58:00 – 60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
By the six kinds of aggression the Kauravas had earned death, and killing them would bring no sin; Arjuna's fear comes from their being relatives, and from not yet seeing that whatever Kṛṣṇa arranges, like surgery, is for the ultimate good. [50:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
