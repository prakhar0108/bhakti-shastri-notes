## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 1.8 [00:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.8 -->

**Context.** Standing before Droṇācārya, Duryodhana names the leading warriors of his army `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bhavān_ — a respectful second-person address; _tvam_ is the ordinary second-person "you." Duryodhana, a _kṣatriya_, uses _bhavān_ for Droṇa, a _brāhmaṇa_ `00:00`.
- _Samitiṁ-jaya_ — "always victorious," the epithet for Kṛpa `34:00`.

#### Whose name comes first? [02:00 – 04:00]

- Bhīṣma is the commander and senior-most, greater than Droṇa in seniority and rank; normally his name would come first.
- But Duryodhana is standing in front of Droṇa, and glorifying someone else in front of a person can be taken as a snub. He has already slightly irritated Droṇa by mentioning Drupada.
- Bhīṣma, who is close by and listening, will not mind if Droṇa is named first: Droṇa was born a _brāhmaṇa_ and has mostly lived as one, and _brāhmaṇas_ are to be respected more than _kṣatriyas_.
- So Duryodhana says "you" first — he wants to agitate Droṇa to fight wholeheartedly, but not agitate him against himself.

#### Karṇa — absent, yet foremost in Duryodhana's mind [04:00]

- At this time Karṇa was not on the battlefield, but he was very prominent in Duryodhana's mind: Duryodhana was sure of Karṇa's full commitment, though not of Bhīṣma's and Droṇa's.
- Before the war, Duryodhana asked Bhīṣma for a comparative assessment of both forces. Bhīṣma ranked Karṇa very low; Karṇa, infuriated, boasted that he alone could defeat the whole Pāṇḍava army.
- Bhīṣma scornfully replied: when have your boasts become actions? Every time you have confronted Arjuna, you have been bested.

#### Was Karṇa stronger than Arjuna? [04:00 – 18:00]

Many think Karṇa was more powerful and that Kṛṣṇa used deviousness to defeat him. The teacher lists the encounters showing Arjuna always got the better of him:

1. **The martial exhibition** `06:00 – 08:00` — After the princes' education, Droṇa arranged an exhibition outside Hastināpura. Arjuna excelled; then Karṇa entered, matched his feats and challenged him to a duel. Kṛpācārya said duels are only among equals and asked whose son he was. Duryodhana argued from scripture that _varṇa_ is decided by quality, not birth — "can a cat give birth to a lion?" — and made Karṇa king of Aṅga on the spot. Then an elderly charioteer, Adhiratha, came running, embraced Karṇa and called him his son. Duryodhana's kindness was not real benefaction; he wanted to use Karṇa for his own political purpose.
2. **Capturing Drupada** `10:00` — For _dakṣiṇā_ Droṇa asked his students to arrest Drupada. The hundred Kaurava brothers went first, with Karṇa, and were all defeated; then the five Pāṇḍavas overcame Drupada and brought him as a prisoner. Humiliated but admiring Arjuna's expertise, Drupada desired a daughter who could become Arjuna's wife; his _yajña_ produced Draupadī as well as the son meant to take revenge on Droṇa. Karṇa was defeated by Drupada, and Drupada by Arjuna.
3. **Draupadī's svayaṁvara** `10:00 – 12:00` — The Pāṇḍavas were disguised as _brāhmaṇas_. Karṇa was not allowed to compete; Arjuna pierced the fish's eye and won Draupadī. The angry kings charged at Drupada; Yudhiṣṭhira told Arjuna and Bhīma that protecting their father-in-law was now their duty. Arjuna and Karṇa fought; Arjuna expertly countered all Karṇa's weapons. Karṇa asked whether he was a god; Arjuna answered that he was a faithful servant of his guru. Karṇa put down his bow.
4. **The Gandharvas** `12:00 – 14:00` — During the forest exile Duryodhana came with Karṇa to flaunt his wealth before the Pāṇḍavas. Gandharvas sent by Indra said he was trespassing; in the fight Karṇa was wounded and fled, and Duryodhana was arrested along with the ladies of the family. When the soldiers begged the Pāṇḍavas for help, Bhīma felt destiny had become favourable, but Yudhiṣṭhira said that before the world they were one family and asked what greater joy there was than showing mercy to an enemy. Arjuna defeated the Gandharvas — who had defeated Karṇa.
5. **At Virāṭa** `14:00 – 16:00` — At the end of the year incognito, Arjuna, living as a dance teacher, came out with Virāṭa's son and single-handedly defeated the whole Kaurava army — Duryodhana, Karṇa, Śakuni, Droṇa, Bhīṣma, Duḥśāsana, Kṛpa and the others.
6. **The final battle** `16:00 – 18:00` — Commanders: Bhīṣma for 10 days, Droṇa for five, Karṇa for two, Śalya on the last day, and Aśvatthāmā, self-appointed, for the last night, in which he slaughtered those sleeping. As commander, Karṇa defeated all the Pāṇḍavas except Arjuna. In their fierce head-on battle the next day, Karṇa's chariot wheel began sinking into the ground; he asked Arjuna to wait by the codes of morality. Kṛṣṇa asked where that code had been when Draupadī was dragged into the assembly, when Karṇa took part in the cheating gambling match, when the plot to burn the Pāṇḍavas alive was made, and when the 16-year-old Abhimanyu was killed by many great warriors together. Then Arjuna shot, and Karṇa was killed.

#### Śaṭhe śāṭhyam — morality that is lofty and practical [18:00 – 22:00]

- Even in a fair fight Arjuna could have defeated Karṇa; Kṛṣṇa's point was that with the cunning one must be cunning when required — one should not, in the name of a small morality, sacrifice a bigger morality `18:00`.
- The teacher says later Indian history forgot this lesson, at great cost, giving examples from the captions `18:00 – 20:00`:
  - Invading armies placed herds of cows in front of themselves; the Hindu warriors would not shoot at the cows and so did not shoot back.
  - Cow carcasses were thrown into a river; the Hindu army would not drink that water, had no other water, and became weak.
- In Vedic culture there is room for practical adjustments — for example, _kṣatriyas_ were allowed to eat meat, though it is not normally recommended `20:00`.
- The _Mahābhārata_ says that what a person does under the spell of four activities, including intoxication, gambling and hunting, should not be taken seriously. In the Hastināpura assembly, Vikarṇa used this to argue that Draupadī should be spared and Yudhiṣṭhira's kingdom returned, since gambling maddens a person `20:00`.
- The _Mahābhārata_ teaches a morality that is lofty and at the same time practical. Kṛṣṇa tried His very best to avoid the war as a peace messenger; when nothing worked, He did what was required. He did not encourage Arjuna to be immoral — it was "paying back in the same coin" `22:00`.

#### Karṇa's armour and his walk-out [22:00 – 26:00]

- Bhīṣma also told Karṇa that he had lost his _kavaca_ and _kuṇḍala_ — he was born with impenetrable armour and earrings, and with them he was almost undefeatable. Indra came as a _brāhmaṇa_, took them in charity, and gave Karṇa the _śakti_ weapon `22:00 – 24:00`.
- Insulted, Karṇa insisted he could win alone. But in war the glory goes to the commander — just as when India won the World Cup, people remember under whose captaincy, even if someone else was man of the match. So he declared he would not fight while Bhīṣma was commander, and walked off `24:00`.
- Duryodhana had to choose. Karṇa was committed and would come back later; Bhīṣma — older, his grandfather, born a _kṣatriya_ — would feel snubbed and not return. So he kept Bhīṣma, but his mind was always on Karṇa `24:00 – 26:00`.
- After Bhīṣma fell on the 10th day, Karṇa came to offer respects. Bhīṣma explained that he had spoken harshly because he was still trying to stop the war: Duryodhana had too much confidence in Karṇa, and Bhīṣma wanted to reduce it. He asked Karṇa to end the hostilities; Karṇa said he knew he could not win, but Duryodhana had trusted him — how could he give that up? `26:00`

#### A heroic and tragic figure [26:00 – 34:00]

- Karṇa was virtuous in many ways — very charitable, a powerful warrior, an adherent of _dharma_, a worshipper of Sūrya — yet he is an example of a good person spoiled by bad association `26:00 – 28:00`.
- We should help those who helped us in need, but that does not mean joining their immorality. Karṇa himself told Bhīṣma he regretted suggesting that Draupadī be brought into the assembly; he had said it only to please Duryodhana `28:00`.
- Application: in bad association we sometimes crack vulgar jokes or say things we would not otherwise say, because we want to feel respected and accepted there `28:00`.
- **Kṛṣṇa's offer** `28:00 – 30:00`: after His peace mission was rejected, Kṛṣṇa met Karṇa and told him he was the eldest of the Pāṇḍavas and could become king — the Pāṇḍavas would follow him as the _devatās_ follow Indra. He would gain worldly glory and the credit of _dharma_; on the side of _adharma_ he would surely be killed.
- **Karṇa's dream** `28:00 – 30:00`: Karṇa replied that he knew. He had dreamt of Yudhiṣṭhira dressed in white on a chariot rising toward the heavens, and Duryodhana with shaven head, dressed in black, dragged on a cart drawn by donkeys toward the lower regions. The dice of the gambling match would come back as Arjuna's arrows; Duryodhana would be killed by Bhīma and he by Arjuna. Still, how could he be unfaithful to a friend who had helped him in need?
- **Where his thinking fell short** `30:00 – 32:00`:
  - He thought he was following _dharma_, but not deeply: the kingdom of Aṅga that Duryodhana gave him really belonged to the Pāṇḍavas, obtained by jugglery while they were sent away.
  - More deeply, Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Lord and everything belongs to Him.
- Kṛṣṇa was not superficially trying to win Karṇa over; He was the well-wisher of everyone, even Karṇa. A virtuous person should be on the side of morality, and Karṇa's presence made Duryodhana bolder for war. But at the time of destruction the intelligence becomes perverse; Karṇa remains a tragic figure `32:00`.

#### Kṛpa, the Yādavas and the others [34:00 – 38:00]

- **Kṛpa, "always victorious"** `34:00 – 36:00`: before the fighting began, Yudhiṣṭhira went across to the enemy side to seek blessings:
  - Bhīṣma, asked how he could be defeated, said to come back another time.
  - Droṇa said that when he heard disagreeable news from a trustworthy source he would lay down his arms; then he could be overcome.
  - Kṛpa said he was undefeatable — "knowing this, go and attain victory."
  - "Undefeatable" meant he could not be killed: he had been granted a very long life. He was formidable in one-on-one combat but did not know mystic weapons, so he was not a great threat. He was one of the survivors of the carnage.
- **The Yādavas** `36:00 – 38:00`: Duryodhana and Arjuna both went to Dvārakā for support; Arjuna chose Kṛṣṇa, and the Yādava army went to Duryodhana under Kṛtavarmā. Balarāma refused to fight because both sides were equal to Him, and the other Yādavas followed. The only other Yādava who fought was Sātyaki — a disciple of Droṇa who received special training from Arjuna — and he was a survivor on the Pāṇḍava side.
- **Aśvatthāmā and Vikarṇa** `38:00`: Vikarṇa was not a formidable warrior, not in Aśvatthāmā's class. The teacher notes that Vikarṇa was the only one who opposed what was done to Draupadī, and suggests Duryodhana may have had doubts about his commitment.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Karṇa's story shows that virtue without deep understanding of dharma is not enough: loyalty to a benefactor who stands for adharma, and the bad association that comes with it, can ruin even a great, generous soul. [26:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.9 [38:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.9 -->

**Context.** Duryodhana says there are many other heroes ready to lay down their lives for him `38:00 – 40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tyakta-jīvitāḥ_ — literally, their lives given up `40:00`.
- Many kinds of weapons, and all experienced in battle — the qualities Duryodhana attributes to his warriors `40:00`.

#### A double meaning [40:00]

- Duryodhana means that they are ready to sacrifice their lives for him.
- But the teacher explains that Sarasvatī-devī speaks through him: by taking the side of _adharma_, they have in effect already died for him — they have made their own pact with death and chosen the side that will be defeated.
- Duryodhana himself does not think of it this way.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Duryodhana boasts of warriors ready to die for him, but his words unknowingly speak the truth: by choosing adharma they have already given up their lives. [40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.10 [40:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.10 -->

**Context.** Duryodhana gives a summary comparison of the two forces: his own, protected by Bhīṣma, and the Pāṇḍavas', protected by Bhīma `40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paryāpta_ — sufficient, measurable; with the prefix _a_, _aparyāpta_ can mean either "immeasurable" or "insufficient" `40:00 – 44:00`.

#### How a prefix can intensify or negate [42:00]

- "Valuable" with "in" becomes "invaluable" — so valuable it cannot be measured: the prefix intensifies.
- "Substantial" with "in" becomes "insubstantial" — having no substance: the prefix negates.
- So the _a_ in _aparyāpta_ can be read either way.

#### Two readings of the ācāryas [42:00 – 46:00]

- **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa — intensifier** `42:00 – 44:00`: our force under Bhīṣma is immeasurable; theirs under Bhīma is measurable. This is what Duryodhana intended to say, and Śrīla Prabhupāda takes this perspective: externally, Bhīṣma is far more expert, experienced and learned than Bhīma, who is insignificant before him.
- **Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura — negator** `44:00 – 46:00`: the Pāṇḍava force under Bhīma is adequate, ours is inadequate. Again the words are spoken through him by Sarasvatī-devī — what he intends and what he ends up saying.
  - Internally, a warrior's strength is determined by his will to fight; if the heart is not there, the expertise may not come out.
  - Bhīṣma's heart is not in the war — he would much rather it did not take place.
  - Bhīma has been "breathing fire" since the attempt to disrobe Draupadī; the whole exile was a period of intense fury, waiting for this battle.
  - Viśvanātha also reads Duryodhana as addressing all his warriors: Bhīṣma alone is not enough, all of you are important — a reading supported by the next verse `46:00`.

#### Not a contradiction [46:00]

- Baladeva is a disciple in the line of Viśvanātha, yet gives a seemingly different meaning. There is no essential difference — only a different focus.
- Baladeva generally speaks in terms of _tattva_ (facts): Bhīṣma is far more capable than Bhīma.
- Viśvanātha speaks in terms of _rasa_ (inner feeling): Bhīma's hunger for the war is far greater than Bhīṣma's.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
One word, two readings: by tattva Bhīṣma's force is immeasurable, by rasa it is inadequate — because a warrior's strength depends finally on his heart, and Bhīṣma's heart is not in this war while Bhīma's is fully in it. [42:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.11 [48:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.11 -->

**Context.** Having compared the forces, Duryodhana tells all his warriors to support Bhīṣma `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Strategic points — each warrior is stationed at his own position in the formation, to defend it and attack from it `48:00`.

#### Everyone protects Bhīṣma [48:00]

- Bhīṣma is the commander, but he may get caught up fighting at one point and not pay attention to the others; so everyone must play an important role.
- Duryodhana tries to get everyone involved — politically, an expert speech.

#### Judge the motive, not the eloquence [48:00 – 52:00]

- His speech is thoughtful and impressive, but a person's calibre is determined not by expertise of speech but by purity of motive — and his motives are filled with greed and envy.
- With good intention, expert speaking is best; with evil intention, it is like a serpent decorated with a jewel — the jewel is attractive, but the serpent is dangerous. Instead of being cautious at seeing the serpent, we are drawn by the jewel.
- People with demoniac propensities may have material skill and expertise; impressed by it, we may lower our guard and start admiring their ideas.
- The teacher distinguishes abilities — speaking in a way that captivates and commands others — from intrinsic qualities such as humility and tolerance; abilities alone are not a sign of spiritual advancement.
- One characteristic of Kali-yuga: expertise in word jugglery will be considered a sign of scholarship `52:00`.
- Duryodhana is doing a balancing act, like walking a tightrope `52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Expert speech with an evil intention is a serpent decorated with a jewel; we must judge people by the purity of their motives, not by their eloquence. [48:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.12 [52:00 – 64:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.12 -->

**Context.** Seeing Duryodhana's strained balancing act, Bhīṣma feels pity and decides it is time to fight; he blows his conch, increasing Duryodhana's joy `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kuru-vṛddha_ — the elder of the Kuru dynasty `52:00`.
- _Pitāmaha_ — grandsire, grandfather; Bhīṣma is of the generation of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's father, two generations above Duryodhana `52:00`.
- _Pratāpavān_ — courageous and powerful `52:00`.
- His conch sounded like the roar of a lion `52:00`.

#### Bhīṣma's extraordinary sacrifice [54:00 – 56:00]

- Living amid royal luxury, with so many pleasures available in a _kṣatriya_ palace, to maintain celibacy is nearly impossible; normally one goes to a secluded place for that. Bhīṣma did it in the palace of the capital.
- Śantanu became attracted to Satyavatī. Her father, a fisherman, insisted that his daughter's son must be the next king. Śantanu was torn between his attraction and his commitment to his son.
- His son Devavrata found out the reason, went to the fisherman and renounced the kingdom.
- The father asked, "What about your sons?" He then vowed to have no sons at all. The _devatās_ called this vow fierce, and from then on he was known as Bhīṣma.
- He also vowed that as long as he lived he would protect the ruling throne of the Kuru dynasty of Hastināpura `60:00`.

#### A higher plan behind the events [56:00 – 60:00]

- When the Lord's pastimes are about to happen, the characters are moved in many ways; we should understand there is a higher plan, not just ordinary material events. These are precursors to the Lord's pastimes.
- **On polygamy** — the teacher explains why, in Vedic culture, it was accepted for _kṣatriyas_ to have many wives:
  - Every woman was considered to need marriage.
  - A small but significant number of men took the renounced order as _brahmacārīs_ or _sannyāsīs_.
  - Begetting a son requires more _puṇya_, so when the population was less pious, more women than men would be born.
  - It was allowed only for those who could maintain the wives properly — especially kings and _kṣatriyas_ — and all wives had to be cared for.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda, asked about this, said that by today's law polygamy is illegal and we should not have it; it is not practical today. In Kali-yuga even maintaining one household is difficult, so our goal is not to complicate life more `58:00`.

#### Why Bhīṣma fought for Duryodhana [60:00 – 62:00]

- He could not have conceived that a spineless, weak-hearted ruler like Dhṛtarāṣṭra and a Duryodhana would sit on the throne he had vowed to protect — so he had to fight on Duryodhana's side.
- He also says in the _Mahābhārata_ that he was bound by their wealth.
- Ultimately the Lord wanted him to fight on the Kaurava side to teach a lesson: however powerful a person is, one who goes against the Lord's plan will not succeed.

#### How powerful Bhīṣma was [62:00]

- He defeated the _kṣatriyas_ of the world at a _svayaṁvara_ and won Ambikā and Ambālikā as brides for his brother Vicitravīrya.
- He fought Paraśurāma — who had defeated the _kṣatriyas_ of the world 21 times, and who had been his martial teacher — for many days and practically defeated him; sages came and told him not to kill a _brāhmaṇa_, so he refrained.
- He took up the thankless task of fighting against the Pāṇḍavas — a task for which he could be misunderstood for all time to come — so that he could be an instrument for demonstrating the Lord's teaching.

#### "Let's get on with it" [62:00 – 64:00]

- Enough politics and talking: Bhīṣma knew the Kauravas were ultimately not going to win and Duryodhana would die, but he wanted to get on with it — "I want to see how Kṛṣṇa protects."
- Duryodhana, by contrast, took the conch as a sign that his speech had been successful, and his joy increased.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhīṣma, the most powerful warrior, bound by his vow to the throne, fights on the wrong side as an instrument for the Lord's lesson: however powerful one is, no one can succeed against the Lord's plan. [60:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.13 [64:00 – 66:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.13 -->

**Context.** Bhīṣma's conch is the commander's signal; then all the warriors and their supporting staff sound their instruments `64:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śaṅkha_ — conch `64:00`.
- _Bherī_ — large drum `64:00`.
- _Paṇava_ and _ānaka_ — small drums and kettledrums `66:00`.
- _Go-mukha_ — horns `66:00`.
- _Sahasā_ — suddenly; everyone had been waiting for the signal `66:00`.
- The combined sound was tumultuous `66:00`.

#### The spirit of the battle [64:00]

- Just as a conch is sounded at the start of _ārati_, the commander's conch signals the beginning.
- The war was fought in a sporting spirit — "you have your skills, I have mine; let us fight."
- There was bloodshed and there could be death, but the mood was heroic: if we die fighting heroically, we will attain heaven.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
At the commander's signal the whole Kaurava side erupts in sound — a heroic, sporting mood of warriors ready to die in battle, though they stand on the side of adharma. [64:00 – 66:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.14 [66:00 – 70:19]

<!-- vedabase:1.14 -->

**Context.** The scene shifts to the other side, and here Kṛṣṇa enters the Bhagavad-gītā `66:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mādhava_ — the Lord of the goddess of fortune `68:00`.
- _Divya_ — transcendental, said of the conches of Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna `68:00`.

#### Absent from Duryodhana's calculations [66:00]

- Duryodhana, listing warriors on both sides, never mentioned Kṛṣṇa. This shows his calculating mentality: Kṛṣṇa, as a non-fighting charioteer, had no role in his calculations.
- But in the Lord's plan, Kṛṣṇa has the decisive role.

#### A hero's entry [66:00 – 68:00]

- In a movie the hero does not just walk in: a fast car arrives, the wheels and screeching brakes are shown, the door opens, the shoes are shown, and the camera moves up to the hero's face.
- Likewise the "camera" moves to the Pāṇḍava side: first the beautiful white horses, then the magnificent chariot, then Mādhava.

#### The charioteer named before the warrior [68:00 – 70:00]

- Normally nobody mentions the charioteer before the king; Duryodhana did not mention charioteers at all — they were of no consequence.
- Here the charioteer is of supreme consequence, and His name, Mādhava, comes before the Pāṇḍava, Arjuna.
- The conches are transcendental because Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Lord and Arjuna is His pure devotee — even Arjuna's intention is transcendental, and so even his conch is considered transcendental.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The one Duryodhana left out of his calculations makes the grandest entry: Kṛṣṇa, the charioteer named before the warrior, whose presence makes even His devotee's conch transcendental. [66:00 – 70:00]
</Callout>
