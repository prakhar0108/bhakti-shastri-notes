## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 1.15 [04:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.15 -->

**Context.** The conches of the Pāṇḍava side are described, beginning with Kṛṣṇa as Hṛṣīkeśa and including the mighty Bhīma `04:00`, `12:00 – 14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Hṛṣīkeśa_ — the controller of the senses; Kṛṣṇa is objectively the controller `12:00`.
- Pāñcajanya — Kṛṣṇa's conch, with "a whole story" behind it `14:00`.

#### Bhīma's strength and appetite [04:00 – 08:00]

- Bhīma was an extremely powerful person, and such a powerful person needs a lot of power from eating `04:00`.
- The teacher recounts how, in the episode around the marriage, Kuntī's instruction to share led to the food being divided into parts, with a large share for Bhīma `04:00 – 06:00`.
- When the Kauravas conspired to kill the Pāṇḍavas in the house of lac, they escaped at night through a tunnel before the house burned. Making their way through the wilderness with their mother, the others became exhausted; Bhīma had them sit on his shoulders and ran, and whatever branches were in his way simply broke apart `06:00`.
- On eating, the teacher notes that different people need different amounts depending on their nature and body type; what matters is not so much the quantity as the consistency — regularity rather than alternating too little and too much `06:00 – 08:00`.

#### Hṛṣīkeśa — the objective controller [12:00 – 14:00]

- "I think I am speaking and I have the power to speak" — but objectively our senses are not under our control; we have limited powers, and those powers can be taken away at any time.
- Kṛṣṇa is objectively the controller; according to our _karma_ He gives us a certain level of control over the body and senses.
- We recognise this objective reality and then subjectively, voluntarily accept Kṛṣṇa as the controller and agree to be controlled by Him.

#### Pāñcajanya [14:00]

- The conch is Kṛṣṇa's eternal associate, always with Him spiritually.
- When He appears in this world, there is also a pastime by which it becomes associated with Him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is named Hṛṣīkeśa because He is the real controller of the senses: our power over body and senses is limited and given according to karma, so wisdom lies in recognising this and voluntarily accepting Him as controller. [12:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.19 [18:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.19 -->

**Context.** The sound of the Pāṇḍava conches spreads from the sky to the earth and shatters the hearts of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Courage — not just the absence of fear `20:00`.
- Fearlessness — that which comes from knowledge and from awareness of God's presence `22:00`.

#### The confidence behind the sound [18:00]

- The sound extends from the sky to the earth. When the Lord blows the conch, it signals the confidence of the side He is with: the Lord's side is undefeatable.

#### What fear is, and what courage is not [20:00 – 22:00]

- We fear many things — the future, terrible diseases, and the miseries (the teacher names _ādhyātmika_ and _ādi-bhautika_ among them) `20:00`.
- Courage is not just the absence of fear `20:00`.
- The fearlessness we want is the one that comes from knowledge: we are not the body, Kṛṣṇa exists and Kṛṣṇa is our master `22:00`.

#### Awareness of God's presence [22:00 – 24:00]

- Fearlessness comes from awareness of God's presence. God is there, and in front of God, whatever causes fear — whatever problems there are — becomes insignificant.
- To make fear small, we have to increase our Kṛṣṇa consciousness and become aware of Him; then fear goes away, and we move forward in life.
- The Pāṇḍavas were not afraid because Kṛṣṇa was with them.
- The Kauravas' hearts were shattered because they had no awareness of a personal presence for them; having chosen the side opposite the Supreme Lord, their destruction was guaranteed.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Real fearlessness is not ignorance of danger but awareness of Kṛṣṇa's presence: before Him every cause of fear becomes small — which is why the same sound that emboldens the Pāṇḍavas shatters those who chose to oppose the Lord. [20:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.20 [24:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.20 -->

**Context.** Sañjaya describes Arjuna, whose flag bears Hanumān, preparing to speak to Hṛṣīkeśa `24:00`, `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Standard (flag) — every warrior had a distinctive flag on a staff by which others recognised him; Arjuna's bore a monkey `24:00`.

#### Hanumān on the flag [24:00 – 26:00]

- At Kurukṣetra, Hanumān agreed, on Bhīma's request, to be present on Arjuna's flag.
- Because Hanumān was present on the side of Lord Rāma's forces, that side was victorious; so he was requested to be present in this war too, and he agreed.
- His presence on Arjuna's standard is another sign of the Pāṇḍavas' victory.

#### How verses are counted [28:00]

- Here Sañjaya says that Arjuna then spoke these words to Hṛṣīkeśa.
- Verses are sometimes split differently depending on the continuity of thought; Śrīla Prabhupāda puts some verses together because of that continuity, and elsewhere some are counted as one — yet the overall numbering of the verses remains 700.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Hanumān, whose presence brought victory to Lord Rāma's side, stands on Arjuna's flag — one more sign, before a single arrow is shot, of where victory lies. [24:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

**Context.** Arjuna asks Kṛṣṇa to place his chariot between the two armies so that he can see those who have come to fight `34:00 – 36:00`, `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Nested dialogue — the _Mahābhārata_ has conversations at multiple levels, one inside another `38:00`.

#### Why does Arjuna want to see? [30:00 – 32:00]

- The teacher raises an interesting question: Arjuna already knew who was on the opposite side — before the war the alliances were formed, and both sides had spies, so even the strategy was known. Why then does he want to see?
- In his answer the teacher touches on how the Pāṇḍavas tried everything possible to avoid the war, and how, as the war approached, they were ready to fight — not as instigators, but not as cowards either.

#### Kṛṣṇa, the non-fighting charioteer [34:00 – 36:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is part of the non-fighting contingent in the battle, serving as Arjuna's charioteer.
- Arjuna asks Him to take the chariot in between the armies before the shooting starts.

#### Dialogues within dialogues [38:00]

- In Śrīla Prabhupāda's edition, verses 21 and 22 are one.
- The _Mahābhārata_ is a nested dialogue: the conversation between Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna is within Sañjaya's narration to Dhṛtarāṣṭra, and the _Mahābhārata_ itself is a conversation involving Janamejaya.

#### The Lord's extraordinary love [40:00 – 42:00]

- In this exchange, where the Lord drives the chariot at His devotee's request, the teacher points to the extraordinary love of the Lord.
- Arjuna repeats the request he made in verse 21: place the chariot between the two armies so he can see who has come, ready to fight.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Supreme Lord, who takes no weapon in this war, agrees to act as His devotee's charioteer and drive where Arjuna asks — a display of the Lord's extraordinary love. [34:00 – 42:00]

### Bhagavad-gita 1.24 – 1.25 [42:00 – 47:03]

<!-- vedabase:1.24 -->

<!-- vedabase:1.25 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa places the chariot between the armies, and Arjuna is about to look upon those assembled — and the teacher reflects on what comes through the eyes and what comes through the ears `42:00 – 46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pratyakṣa_ — direct perception, which can be subject to illusion `46:00`.
- _Śabda_ — knowledge received by hearing `46:00`.

#### Māyā through the eyes, Kṛṣṇa through the ears [44:00]

- Generally speaking, in this world _māyā_ comes through the eyes and Kṛṣṇa comes through the ears.
- This is a very general statement: Kṛṣṇa can also come through the eyes, as in _darśana_.
- When Kṛṣṇa is far away from our consciousness, material pleasures appear real and available, and Kṛṣṇa seems remote and irrelevant.
- So we must be very careful and walk in this world with protection.

#### On the verge of confusion [46:00]

- Arjuna is about to become confused about his _kṣatriya_ duty to fight — doubtful whether he should be doing it at all.
- What he perceives with his eyes will become the source of his illusion; the next class will show what happened after this.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
What we see can pull us into illusion, while what we hear about Kṛṣṇa protects us: as Arjuna turns his eyes on the assembled armies, he is about to become confused about his duty. [44:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

- **Socrates and the immortality of the soul** `02:00`: In response to a question, the teacher notes that Socrates spoke to his friends, giving reasons for the immortality of the soul — reasons practically identical with the Gītā's second chapter. He suggests that after the Kurukṣetra war people left the Indian mainland and settled in various parts; over the centuries, as the power of the Kuru kings waned, their connection with the parent culture weakened, leaving local cultural derivatives of an expatriate Vedic civilisation.
- **Vyāsadeva and the Pāṇḍavas** `08:00 – 10:00`: Vyāsa's union with Ambikā and Ambālikā was a contingent arrangement in an emergency, to continue the dynasty; they were not considered unchaste. Though Vyāsadeva could be called the Pāṇḍavas' grandfather, they are not shown referring to him that way; their relationship with him was primarily one of great respect.
- **Why Arjuna wants to see the armies** `30:00`: The teacher raises this as an interesting question, since the opposing alliances were already known (see BG 1.21 – 1.23).
