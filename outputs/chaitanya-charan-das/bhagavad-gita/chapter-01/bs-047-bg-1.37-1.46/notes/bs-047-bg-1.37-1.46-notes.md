### Bhagavad-gita 1.37 – 1.38 [02:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.37 -->

<!-- vedabase:1.38 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says that even if the Kauravas, their hearts overtaken by greed, cannot see the fault in destroying a dynasty or in quarrelling with friends, why should "we," who can see it, engage in such acts? `04:00 – 12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Lobha_ — greed; the Kauravas' consciousness has been taken away by it `04:00`.
- _Mitra-droha_ — quarrelling with, or betraying, friends `12:00`.
- _Pāpa_ — sinful reaction `12:00`.

#### Repeated lines in the Gītā [02:00 – 04:00]

- Lines are sometimes repeated in the Gītā; the most famous example is 9.34 and 18.65 `02:00`.
- Here the same line — destroying one's dynasty is a fault — is used twice because Arjuna is contrasting two kinds of thinking: two different parties perceive the same event differently `04:00`.

#### Anticipating an objection [04:00]

- Arjuna anticipates an objection to his argument: "If we don't kill them, they will kill us — that is what they have been trying to do."
- His answer: they are blinded; greed has taken their consciousness away `04:00`.

#### How Duryodhana became blind [06:00 – 12:00]

- Pāṇḍu had conquered the world but then went to the forest, toward the Himālayas, to live an ascetic way of life; his wives Kuntī and Mādrī went with him `06:00`.
- Dhṛtarāṣṭra was the acting king, never officially king but receiving the honour of the position `06:00`.
- In the forest Pāṇḍu realised he had a responsibility to continue the dynasty; the Pāṇḍavas were born there, while Duryodhana and his ninety-nine brothers were born in the palace `06:00`.
- Since Pāṇḍu had left and there was no sign he would return, everyone in the palace thought Duryodhana would become the next king. Treated as the future king, he was pampered and spoiled, and became very proud, arrogant and demanding `08:00`.
- When Kuntī and the Pāṇḍavas came back, their pleasant behaviour contrasted with his. Duryodhana was no longer the centre of attention, and his claim to the kingdom was now in question `08:00 – 10:00`.
- From then on envy and greed entered his heart; he felt no affection for the Pāṇḍavas, only anger, and their humiliation and destruction became the goal of his life — conspiracy after conspiracy, up to the final confrontation `10:00`.
- His brothers were similarly blinded `10:00`.

#### "They cannot see, but we can" [12:00 – 14:00]

- The teacher illustrates _mitra-droha_ with an example of childhood friends who became enemies `12:00`.
- The contrast between the two verses: "they are not able to see — but we can see." Because we can see the sinful reaction, we must not engage in such activity `12:00`.
- Arjuna takes the moral high ground: even if they do wrong, we should not do wrong; because we can see, we would incur the reaction even more `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna contrasts two perceptions of the same act: the Kauravas, blinded by greed, cannot see the sin, but "we can see it" — so, he argues, we should not do it. [04:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.39 [14:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.39 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says that when the dynasty is destroyed, its eternal family traditions are destroyed and irreligion overtakes the family `14:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kula-dharma_ — every dynasty has its own duty or _dharma_ `14:00`.
- _Sanātana_ — eternal `14:00`.

#### Arjuna's fourfold thought [14:00]

1. **Compassion** — why should my relatives die? (the present)
2. **Enjoyment** — how will I enjoy? (this lifetime, the near future)
3. **Sinful reaction** — the reactions that will come in the next life.
4. **Legacy** — what will happen to the dynasty itself after this generation, and to the successive generations.

#### How can the eternal be destroyed? [14:00 – 16:00]

- The teacher notes an antithesis in the verse: the family traditions are called eternal (_sanātana_), yet they are said to be destroyed — how can something eternal be destroyed? `14:00`.
- Arjuna is overcome by illusion. The _ātmā_'s _dharma_ is _bhakti_, service — the supreme religion `16:00`.
- Other _dharmas_ are also important: spouses to be faithful to each other, parents to take care of children, children to care for elderly parents, people to care for relatives. Some kind of obligation has to be there in life and in society `16:00`.

#### Dharma and adharma always in conflict [18:00]

- In the world, _dharma_ and _adharma_ are always in conflict — not only in this world, but throughout the universe `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's concern now reaches beyond this life to the dynasty's legacy; family dharmas — faithfulness, care for children and elders — truly matter, even though the soul's eternal dharma is bhakti. [14:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.40 [18:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.40 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says that when irreligion prevails the women of the family become polluted, and from that comes unwanted progeny `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Varṇa-saṅkara_ — unwanted progeny `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### Why good progeny mattered [18:00 – 20:00]

- Continuing the dynasty with good progeny was considered a very important responsibility, and for that a good marriage `18:00`.
- If the men are killed in the fight, the women are left unprotected and may be exploited, resulting in unwanted children `20:00`.

#### Consciousness at conception [20:00 – 24:00]

- How a child is conceived is not just a material process; the teacher says the topic here is the consciousness of the mother and father during their union `20:00 – 22:00`.
- If they are completely in the throes of passion, a passionate soul is attracted `22:00`.
- The teacher quotes a European movie star who said most people are born of a bottle of whiskey on a Saturday night `22:00`.
- Such children may become criminals — and nobody wants criminals in their house or city `22:00`.
- Good children can help their parents and elders spiritually, and can do good in the world; with children of low consciousness this becomes very difficult `24:00`.

#### Understanding the purport's statement on women [24:00 – 30:00]

- The teacher addresses the purport's statement about women being not very intelligent, explaining that "intelligence" is used in different senses in different contexts `24:00 – 26:00`:
  - **Intelligence as capacity** — to manipulate, control and organise matter, or to organise concepts.
  - **Intelligence in this context** — understanding the fundamental truths of life: that I am not the body.
- In the female body, the sense of self-identity generally comes more strongly from the body; bodily misidentification is stronger `26:00`.
- Along with that there is a greater level of sentimentality than rationality `26:00`.
- That does not make it all bad: women can become devotees more easily; they have a tendency to accept God and can practise devotional life more easily. But sentimentality can also misguide the spiritual search `26:00 – 28:00`.
- So the context is these two things — stronger bodily misidentification and greater sentimentality; that is why such statements are made `28:00`.

#### Principle versus context [28:00 – 32:00]

- An argument sometimes used is that the female brain is smaller and therefore less intelligent; this is not necessarily true `28:00`:
  - Brain size is not necessarily an indicator of intelligence.
  - The whole body is bigger too; in proportion, women can have bigger brains than men `28:00 – 30:00`.
- Scripture speaks through _śabda_ (and we use _anumāna_, inference), but not everything quoted in a purport is itself scriptural authority. Prabhupāda once cited a statement by a college professor; it reflected the scientific belief of that time. We can learn from science, but it is not always reliable and is not part of our authority `30:00`.
- We must differentiate between the principle that comes from scripture and the examples that come from the context `32:00`.
- The teacher relates an anecdote in which Prabhupāda, speaking with a journalist on this topic, made the point that one becomes intelligent when one starts understanding "I am not the body" `32:00`.

#### Protection is also a social and religious arrangement [32:00 – 34:00]

- The general point is a social arrangement in which both male and female contribute `32:00`.
- Protection does not mean only physical provision and protection; it also means a religious arrangement — and this applies not just to women but to everyone `32:00`.
- If we have a lot of unstructured time, we drift toward unhealthy activities; when there is a proper social arrangement with the facility to practise _dharma_, it becomes easier to practise. Temptations are always there `32:00 – 34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Unwanted progeny arise when family dharma breaks down; scripture's statements must be read by separating eternal principle from contextual example, and protection means a social and religious arrangement that helps everyone practise dharma. [18:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.41 [34:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.41 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says the increase of unwanted population brings hellish life both to the family and to those who destroy it, and the ancestors fall because the offerings to them stop `34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pitṛs_ — the ancestors `34:00 – 36:00`.

#### An intergenerational connection [36:00]

- The ancestors are expected to have an intergenerational connection with us; when we do _puṇya_ (pious acts) on their behalf, it contributes to their continued existence there `36:00`.
- Generally the earth is where we can do pious acts; elsewhere one is exhausting one's _puṇya_ `36:00`.
- If the descendants on earth keep doing pious offerings, the ancestors can stay in the higher realm; if they stop, the ancestors fall `36:00`.
- Such rituals have to be performed by those who are not devotees — those who are not in devotional service are required to perform them `36:00`.

#### Gratitude: everything comes from Kṛṣṇa [38:00]

- A mother gives a child breast milk; ultimately it is Kṛṣṇa who arranges this, even sending milk into the mother's breast before the child is born `38:00`.
- Similarly, whoever does whatever for us, ultimately it is Kṛṣṇa doing it for us. This means we appreciate and value such people, because they are instruments through which Kṛṣṇa's kindness and love come to us `38:00`.
- Normally, whatever we get from someone, we have to give back to that person `38:00`.

#### The debts we owe [38:00 – 42:00]

- The teacher cites _Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_ 11.5.41: we have debts to the _devas_, the _ṛṣis_, the ancestors and others; the third chapter of the Gītā will discuss this further `38:00`.
- **Devas** — repaid by fire sacrifices (_agnihotra_) and other sacrifices `40:00`.
- **Ṛṣis** — repaid by learning what they have given `40:00`.
- **Pitṛs** — repaid by continuing the line, maintaining its prestige and progeny `40:00`.
- **Other living beings** — the teacher recalls a family cloth shop where food items were kept so that rats coming in would take the food rather than the cloth — not just to avoid damage, but so that the living beings there got some food `40:00`.
- We also have duties toward other human beings, especially those with whom we have a relationship `40:00`.
- But if we serve Kṛṣṇa directly — understanding that all of them are giving Kṛṣṇa's love to me — then I am no longer obligated to them separately `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Everyone who helps us is an instrument of Kṛṣṇa's kindness, so we owe debts to devas, sages, ancestors and other beings; those debts are fully met by serving Kṛṣṇa directly. [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.42 [42:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.42 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says that by the evil deeds of those who destroy the family tradition, community projects and family welfare activities are devastated `42:00`.

#### Responsible for the chain reaction [42:00 – 44:00]

- Different levels of people have their duties; all these will be disrupted, many people will be upset, and many generations will be disrupted `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Arjuna's thinking: I am responsible not only for killing the aggressors; if my actions lead to a chain reaction, I am responsible for that too `44:00`.
- Example: a person intends to shoot only one man in a room, but because of what follows, a hundred people in the building are killed — and that person will be held responsible for killing them all `44:00`.
- Likewise Arjuna thinks: "I will not only kill my elders; I will be responsible for the whole destruction that follows" `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna holds himself responsible not only for the killing but for every consequence that follows from it — the disruption of community and family duties for generations. [42:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.43 [44:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.43 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says he has heard by disciplic succession that those who destroy family traditions dwell always in hell `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Janārdana_ — protector of the people; used repeatedly in this section: "You are the protector of people — why are You letting this destruction happen?" `44:00`.

#### Arjuna appeals to authority [44:00 – 46:00]

- Arjuna is not just reasoning; he is saying, "I have heard this from authorities" `44:00 – 46:00`.
- Scripture has multiple levels of teaching, and Arjuna's reasoning is not at the highest level of scriptural understanding `46:00`.

#### Believing in a next life is not yet spirituality [46:00 – 50:00]

- Someone can hold the idea that there is a next life, but just knowing about a next life does not purify one's material conception of life `46:00`.
- The teacher's example: a particular misinterpretation of the Qur'an circulating among militants tells young people that if they lay down their lives fighting for Allah, they will go to paradise and enjoy many virgins. He calls this a distortion of what the Qur'an teaches; and the attraction in it is not love of Allah but love of enjoyment `46:00 – 48:00`.
- So there can be this-worldly materialism and other-worldly materialism `48:00`.
- The teacher also mentions the _Narnia_ books, in which heaven is pictured as a continuation of this world without its problems `48:00`.
- What makes one spiritual is understanding the difference between the body and the soul and rising to the spiritual level of consciousness `48:00`.
- Arjuna has awareness of the next life — that one who does this will suffer in hell for a very long time — but his frame of thinking is still the bodily conception `48:00 – 50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Belief in a next life is not by itself spirituality — it can be other-worldly materialism; real spirituality is understanding the difference between body and soul and rising to spiritual consciousness. [46:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.44 [50:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.44 -->

**Context.** Arjuna laments that they were preparing to commit greatly sinful acts, driven by the desire for royal happiness `50:00`.

#### A changed frame of thinking [50:00 – 52:00]

- Arjuna's frame of thinking has changed: earlier he had come to fight; now he sees the fight itself as a great sin `50:00`.
- The cause of the change is attachment — the bodily conception `52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna now calls the very fight he came for a great sin; the teacher traces this reversal to attachment rooted in the bodily conception. [50:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.45 [52:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.45 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says it would be better for him if the Kauravas killed him unarmed and unresisting `52:00`.

#### Holy war and unholy war [52:00 – 54:00]

- Arjuna says: "I will not respond to their attacks; not only will I not respond, I will put down my weapons" `52:00`.
- In the normal _kṣatriya_ code there is holy war and unholy war `52:00`:
  - **Holy war** — war meant for protecting religious principles.
  - **Unholy war** — war for personal aggrandisement.
- Terrorism is completely antithetical to the idea of holy war `52:00`.
- In the _kṣatriya_ code the fight is between equals: a chariot warrior should not fight a foot soldier; both should be equally equipped and both should be alert; one does not attack from behind `54:00`.
- Terrorism is the exact opposite: the parties are not equal, not equipped and not alert `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The kṣatriya code allowed only a holy war — fought to protect religious principles, between equals who are armed and alert; Arjuna, rather than fight, would let himself be killed unarmed. [52:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 1.46 [54:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:1.46 -->

**Context.** The concluding verse of the chapter: the scene shifts back to Sañjaya's report — having spoken, Arjuna puts aside his bow and arrows and sits down on the chariot, overwhelmed by grief `54:00`.

#### From speech to action [54:00]

- Arjuna stops speaking; Sañjaya now reports what Arjuna does — his action follows his words `54:00`.

#### The symbolic chariot [56:00]

- A symbolic reading: Arjuna has given the reins of his chariot to Kṛṣṇa — like the body as a chariot with Kṛṣṇa as its driver `56:00`.
- Symbolic meaning is not wrong if it does not replace the literal meaning; it can supplement the literal meaning with further illumination, and that is perfectly okay `56:00`.

#### Looking ahead [58:00]

- From Kṛṣṇa's instructions Arjuna will get strength and determination, and his resolve will be restored `58:00`.
- He will come to understand that serving Kṛṣṇa is in his own best interest `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The chapter ends with Arjuna's words turning into action — he puts down his bow and sits; symbolic readings, like the body as a chariot driven by Kṛṣṇa, may enrich the literal meaning but never replace it. [54:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
