## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 9.3 [00:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains what happens to those who lack faith in this devotional service. The teacher focuses on why faith is essential and how to protect and increase it.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Faith_ — here, faith in the process of devotional service; the teacher recalls that there are multiple levels of faith, such as faith that God exists and faith that God is a person `02:00`.

#### Faith in the medicine [00:00 – 02:00]

- If you do not have faith in a medicine, you will not take it — and then the disease continues `00:00`.
- If one practises sincerely, one naturally experiences purification `02:00`.

#### Watering the root, feeding the stomach [04:00]

- Watering the root of a tree satisfies all its branches; similarly, feeding the stomach satisfies all the senses of the body.
- This is a spiritual mystery: how is it possible that by satisfying Kṛṣṇa, everyone is satisfied?
- When we see a limited person, we think how little he can give to anyone; but when we understand, internalise and realise the principle that by satisfying Kṛṣṇa everything is satisfied, our faith becomes firm `04:00`.

#### Why faith is essential [06:00]

- Devotional service is not just a matter of external engagement but of inner involvement.
- Activities like _kīrtana_ are good and essential, but if someone is not working on their doubts and on their faith — if they are engaged in service for some motive other than faith — they will not continue for very long.

#### How to keep and increase faith [06:00 – 08:00]

- Find out what increases faith and do those things:
  - study;
  - association;
  - the activities that actually help our purification.
- Certain issues can be put in a state of suspended judgement if they trouble us too much; meanwhile, keep moving forward and keep doing the things that help faith develop `06:00 – 08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Faith is to devotion what faith in a medicine is to the patient: without it we stop taking the cure. We protect it by doing what increases it — study, association and purifying practice — and by suspending judgement on doubts that would otherwise stop our progress. [00:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.4 – 9.5 [08:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.4 -->

<!-- vedabase:9.5 -->

**Context.** With BG 9.4 Kṛṣṇa begins the second section of the chapter, describing His inconceivable relationship with the material world. He says all beings are in Him but He is not in them — and then that they are not in Him either — and calls this His mystic opulence `08:00 – 12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yoga_ (from Kṛṣṇa's side) — usually yoga means the process by which we connect with the Lord; here Kṛṣṇa speaks of yoga from His side, His self-revelation that enables us to connect with Him `20:00 – 22:00`.
- _Spiritual revelation_ — Kṛṣṇa cannot be understood by perception; He is understood by spiritual revelation `12:00`.

#### All beings in Him, He not in them [08:00 – 12:00]

- "All living beings are situated in Me" can refer to Kṛṣṇa's Supersoul aspect, which sustains everything `10:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa is the maintainer of all living beings, yet He is not situated in the cosmic manifestation `12:00`.

#### Is Kṛṣṇa contradicting Himself? [14:00 – 20:00]

- When we give classes we may make mistakes — is Kṛṣṇa making a mistake here? `14:00`
- The teacher gives an example of a genuinely contradictory statement: "a person did not eat food throughout the day, and his weight increased" — not eating cannot increase weight, so one of the two statements must be wrong `14:00`.
- If we treat Kṛṣṇa's statements only conceptually and intellectually, the intellect slips; to avoid that, we have to understand carefully how to move forward `18:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport reconciles the statements: if all beings are situated in the Lord, is the Lord burdened? Prabhupāda contrasts this with the Greek and Roman concept of someone carrying the world as a burden for a long time — that concept should not be entertained about Kṛṣṇa, because Kṛṣṇa is not burdened `18:00`.
- We struggle, strain and sweat to get things done; Kṛṣṇa does not `20:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa celebrates the contradiction [20:00 – 22:00]

- Far from apologising for the apparent contradiction, Kṛṣṇa celebrates it — He is not minimising, hushing up or covering it up.
- By calling it His mystic opulence, He speaks of yoga from His side: Kṛṣṇa reveals Himself so that we can connect with Him.
- For the connection between the Lord and the soul to happen, the soul approaches through various paths; the revelation from the Lord can also be called yoga because it too helps us connect `22:00`.
- Understanding this increases our appreciation for the Lord. The teacher points to the simple, faithful appreciation in the story of the sage and the cobbler, without retelling it `22:00`.

#### God means God can do anything [24:00 – 26:00]

- Some say the accounts of Kṛṣṇa are exaggerations: the original Kṛṣṇa was a very nice, intelligent person and a great diplomat, and later He was made supernatural for political or other purposes.
- Such views arise because people do not understand that God means God can do anything.
- If God can do anything, then the more amazing the things He is reported to have done, the more evidence it is that He is God `26:00`.

#### The maintainer who is not in what He maintains [26:00 – 30:00]

- Normally, someone who maintains something is part of it: the head of a family maintains the family and is also in the family `26:00`.
- The living entities do things in this existence, and He allows it because He gives them freedom `28:00`.
- **Rāhu and the nectar** — during the churning for nectar, Mohinī Mūrti seated the gods and demons and promised to serve both, but gave the nectar fully to the gods. Rāhu understood what was happening, sat among the gods and began asking for nectar. When he was pointed out, Mohinī's disc cut off his head — but the nectar had gone as far as his head, so Rāhu did not die, and he now exists as nothing but a head `28:00 – 30:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa states in BG 7.7 that there is no truth higher than Him; He is the Supreme Person, to be understood wholeheartedly `30:00`.

#### Reading in the flow of the Gītā [32:00]

- Statements like these must be understood in the flow of the Gītā; otherwise we take one verse out of context and confusion results.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
All beings rest on Kṛṣṇa, yet He is neither contained in them nor burdened by them; rather than apologising for this apparent contradiction, Kṛṣṇa celebrates it as His mystic opulence — a self-revelation meant to help us connect with Him. [08:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.6 [32:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.6 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains how all beings rest in Him through the example of the wind that blows everywhere yet remains within the sky `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ākāśa_ — translated differently: sky, space or ether. Technically it refers to ether, the substratum in which everything is present and within which all movement happens; here it is used in the conversational sense of "sky" `34:00`, `38:00`.

#### Terms are verbal, concepts are mental [34:00]

- When a concept is new, the term for it is also new.
- When conveying a new concept, we may use familiar English "handles" even though they are not entirely accurate, because English words carry different meanings; with that handle people start understanding, and then they can move forward.

#### Freedom within limits [36:00 – 40:00]

- The wind moves everywhere, but its area of movement is limited by the sky — like the soul in this world `36:00`.
- **The tethered horse** — a horse tied to a post can move right or left, but only within the reach of its tether `38:00`.
- The living entity has freedom within a limited framework: it can move in all directions but stays contained, as wind stays within the sky `38:00`.
- "All living beings are situated in Me" means they are given freedom by Kṛṣṇa, and within the area of freedom He gives, they act `38:00`.
- **This class** — while sitting in this class, you could go to sleep or go anywhere; we have freedom, but within limitations. That limitation is Kṛṣṇa's potency `38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Like wind that blows anywhere yet never leaves the sky, living beings act freely, but only within the bounds of freedom Kṛṣṇa provides; that boundary itself is His potency. [36:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.7 – 9.8 [42:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.7 -->

<!-- vedabase:9.8 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the cyclic nature of material existence: at the end of a cycle all beings enter His nature, and at the beginning He creates them again and again, by His nature, while they are helpless under its control `42:00 – 48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Māmikām_ — "My" nature: everything is situated in Kṛṣṇa and created by Him `44:00`.
- _Punaḥ punaḥ_ — again and again `48:00`.
- _Avaśa_ — no control; the living entities have no control over the process `48:00`.

#### Same subject, different focus from the eighth chapter [42:00 – 46:00]

- The cyclic nature of material existence was already described in the eighth chapter; there it was discussed as being under the influence of material nature `42:00 – 44:00`.
- **Eighth chapter** — the primary focus was not on who controls material nature but that material nature is temporary and must be transcended: beyond creation is Kṛṣṇa's abode, and one should aspire to go there `44:00`.
- **Ninth chapter** — the focus is on who is in charge of this huge creation and destruction; the thought develops to describe the creation, with all its magnificence, being created and destroyed under the will of the Lord `46:00`.
- All living beings are created at different stages in the unfolding of creation `46:00`.

#### Who really holds the power [46:00 – 48:00]

- Material nature has control over the living entity `46:00 – 48:00`.
- From our perspective there is no control: when a cosmic devastation happens, everything will be destroyed and nobody will survive `48:00`.
- But the power of nature is itself under Kṛṣṇa; material nature is only under Him `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Where the eighth chapter stressed that the cycle of creation and annihilation must be transcended, the ninth reveals who controls it: material nature controls us, but it works only under Kṛṣṇa. [42:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.9 [48:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.9 -->

**Context.** After describing in BG 9.4–9.8 how He oversees creation and destruction, Kṛṣṇa says that He is not entangled by these activities `48:00`.

#### The scale of His responsibility [50:00]

- A person may be responsible for one house; others for a city, a country, a continent, a planet or a planetary system — but Kṛṣṇa is responsible for the entire universe.

#### The king and the prison [52:00]

- The king is not held responsible for prisoners, because he did not force them into jail. The jail is part of the state's system, but the king did not personally force anyone in.
- The king has no personal vendetta against criminals; according to the law they are sent to a correction centre.
- Similarly, souls who want to enjoy separately from the Lord come into this world; Kṛṣṇa is not implicated, because He is not emotionally driven against anyone.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Though responsible for the whole universe, Kṛṣṇa is not entangled by its creation and destruction — like a just king who has no vendetta against prisoners, He only upholds an arrangement that souls enter by their own desire to enjoy apart from Him. [48:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.10 [54:00 – 59:25]

<!-- vedabase:9.10 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes this section by stating that material nature works under His supervision `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Adhyakṣa_ — supervisor, overseer; one who sees from above `54:00`.

#### Overseeing from above and from within [54:00]

- All the creation and destruction described in BG 9.7–9.9 happens under Kṛṣṇa's supervision.
- He sees from above, but He is also present inside as the Supersoul; even while seeing from inside He is not attached, and though present within matter He is not caught up in material consciousness.

#### We are never alone [56:00 – 58:00]

- Sometimes we feel trapped and are fighting forces both inside and outside us; we wonder how we can keep fighting, lonely and destitute.
- We should know that we are not alone: everything is ultimately under Kṛṣṇa's supervision.
- He is involved and concerned — especially hands-on for His devotees, and at the same time present for everyone else as the one in charge of all existence.
- A king is not directly hands-on in the jail environment, but the purpose of the jail is that prisoners can come out as good citizens. Similarly this world, with its destruction and misery, is ultimately part of an arrangement for reform `56:00 – 58:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa cares, so that we can return to Him; He is there to help `58:00`.

#### Compassion even toward the karmīs [58:00]

- His care for the devotees is not partial attachment, and allowing others to remain in this world is also part of His compassion: they want to be in this world to play out their fantasies of independence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Material nature works under Kṛṣṇa's supervision, from above and from within; so even when we feel trapped and alone, we are not — this world is an arrangement for our reform, overseen by a Lord who wants us to return. [54:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
