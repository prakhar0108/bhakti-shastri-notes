## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 9.11 [00:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.11 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says that fools deride Him when He descends in a human-like form, not knowing His transcendental nature as the great controller of all that exists `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paraṁ bhāvam_ — Kṛṣṇa's transcendental nature, which such people do not understand `02:00`.
- _Bhūta_ — everything that exists `02:00`.
- _Maheśvara_ — sometimes used to refer to Lord Śiva, but actually a generic name: _īśa_ is controller, _maheśa_ is the great controller; Kṛṣṇa is the great controller of all existence `02:00`.
- _Jānanti_, _ajānanti_, _avajānanti_ — three ways of approaching: to know, to not know, and to know the opposite of what is right (to misunderstand) `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Mānuṣīṁ tanum āśritam_ — the misconception that the Absolute has taken shelter of a mortal, material human body `06:00`, `14:00`.

#### Same theme as the previous verse [02:00]

- In BG 9.10 Kṛṣṇa said that material nature works under His supervision; here He continues the same theme: He is the controller of all existence.

#### Ignorance versus misunderstanding [04:00 – 06:00]

- When Śrīla Prabhupāda shared Kṛṣṇa consciousness in India, most people knew of Kṛṣṇa but held misconceptions, for example seeing Him only as a cultural or historical figure.
- In the West people had not heard of Kṛṣṇa — yet they took up Kṛṣṇa consciousness much more easily.
- Both ignorance and misunderstanding are obstacles, but misunderstanding can be the greater obstacle because of the conceptual barrier it creates: "I think I already know," so I must first erase the mental slate and then write afresh — which requires more effort.
- **India** — conceptually there was misunderstanding, but culturally there was agreement: people knew about Kṛṣṇa, Deity worship and His pastimes, so dedicating themselves to Kṛṣṇa was relatively easier.
- **The West** — with no preconception or misconception, people accepted the philosophy; but culturally it was difficult, because the culture was radically _tāmasic_ at that time.

#### The cost of misunderstanding [06:00]

- Misunderstanding leads to continued material existence and increasing bondage, because with misunderstanding we can commit offences, and offences lead to suffering.
- Kṛṣṇa uses both words, _ajānantaḥ_ and _avajānanti_: they misunderstand Him and do not know His transcendental nature.

#### Form without limitation [06:00 – 08:00]

- God can be a person: the Absolute Truth can have a form and still be unlimited, by His inconceivable power.
- He can have a form that fits in the lap of mother Yaśodā, yet when He opens His mouth the whole universe is seen.
- The impersonalists extrapolate from limited material experience:
  - matter means limitation, imperfection and entanglement — true for everything material;
  - but they equate the material with form, concluding that form causes entanglement and that everything with form must be limited.

#### Prabhupāda's evidence for Kṛṣṇa's eternal form [08:00 – 10:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes the _Brahma-saṁhitā_, which calls Kṛṣṇa the supreme controller and describes His form as _sac-cid-ānanda_ (eternal, full of knowledge and bliss).
- He also quotes the _Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad_, one of the Upaniṣads that establishes Gopāla's divinity and supremacy; it uses the explicit word for form (_rūpa_), leaving no doubt.

#### Why Prabhupāda calls Kṛṣṇa's body a "symbol" [08:00 – 12:00]

- Prabhupāda says people do not know that Kṛṣṇa's body is the symbol of complete knowledge and bliss; the word "symbol" seems a little disturbing to some, since Kṛṣṇa's body is the ultimate reality.
- What it means: Kṛṣṇa's body is _sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha_, non-different from Him. Impersonalists conceive of knowledge and bliss as impersonal, but they are manifest in Kṛṣṇa's personal form.
- **Cream on a cake** — cream is normally diffused, spread all over the top of a cake. But it can be taken and shaped into a figure — a mouse, a rabbit, a dog, an elephant for a child's birthday — and that figure is entirely cream. Similarly, the Absolute Truth is diffused everywhere as Brahman, but is concentrated in a form that is entirely _sac-cid-ānanda_ `10:00`.

#### The Lord who appears human [12:00 – 14:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes the _Bhāgavatam_, from the inquiry of Śaunaka Ṛṣi: Keśava, along with Balarāma, performed many pastimes; He is Bhagavān, situated beyond the mortal, concealing Himself while appearing human `12:00`.
- Another _Bhāgavatam_ reference: the Lord first appeared as four-armed Viṣṇu in the prison of Devakī and Vasudeva, and then became like an ordinary child `12:00`.
- In the eleventh chapter of the Gītā, Kṛṣṇa shows Arjuna His four-armed form and the universal form, and then returns to His human-like form `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### What "taking shelter of a human body" implies [14:00]

- Taking shelter implies that the giver of shelter is different from the one who takes shelter:
  - if it is raining, I take shelter of a tree;
  - if I am poverty-stricken, I may take shelter of a wealthy person.
- The impersonalist notion is that the impersonal Absolute has taken shelter of a material, human-like form.
- But the Gītā says that this human-like form is His eternal form; all the verses Prabhupāda quotes point this out.

#### Lord in the heart and in the Deity [16:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes a verse that the Lord is always situated in all beings; the teacher cites the explanation of Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura and Jīva Gosvāmī: the Lord is present with the living entity as the Supersoul, and the Supersoul present internally manifests externally as the _arcā-vigraha_ (Deity).
- Prabhupāda explains the derision in two ways:
  1. deriding the personal form of the Lord;
  2. deriding the Deity form, which is the object of worship.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's human-like form is not a material shelter taken by an impersonal Absolute but His eternal form of knowledge and bliss — like a figure made entirely of cream; deriding it is a misunderstanding more obstructive than ignorance, because the mind must first be emptied of what it thinks it knows. [04:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.12 [18:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.12 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the result for those deluded in this way: their hopes, fruitive work and knowledge are all defeated, and they take shelter of a deluding, demonic and atheistic nature `18:00 – 22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mogha_ — waste, useless, failure; Kṛṣṇa used the word earlier in the third chapter. Prabhupāda's translation renders it "baffled" — frustrated, deluded, defeated `18:00`.
- _Āśā_ — their hope `18:00`.
- _Rākṣasī_, _āsurī_, _mohinī prakṛti_ — a demonic, atheistic and deluding nature `20:00 – 22:00`.

#### Hope, work and knowledge all baffled [18:00]

- Three things become _mogha_: hope, work (_karma_) and knowledge (_jñāna_).
- Some follow the path of _karma_ hoping to make advancement; others first follow the path of _jñāna_ hoping for advancement.
- In both cases they fail, because their vision is misdirected — away from Kṛṣṇa, toward some conception of a higher truth that is not Kṛṣṇa.

#### Sinking deeper into illusion [20:00 – 22:00]

- Kṛṣṇa strongly condemns this mentality.
- Having a misconception, whatever such people practise takes them deeper into illusion and into worse suffering.
- The nature they take shelter of is anti-theistic, anti-devotional and anti-spiritual `22:00`.

#### This verse refers to the Māyāvādīs [22:00]

- In the twelfth chapter, answering Arjuna's question, Kṛṣṇa says that those who worship the impersonal also attain Him, but their path is more troublesome.
- Here, however, it is not just attraction to the impersonal; it is minimisation of and offence to the personal, which leads to terrible results and deeper suffering.
- Prabhupāda quotes a strongly worded verse in the purport: one who thinks Kṛṣṇa's body is material should be excluded from all scriptural activities, and even seeing such a person's face calls for purificatory bathing `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### Why such strong words: the quarantine analogy [24:00 – 28:00]

- The material world is like a diseased place, a hospital, where different people have different infections. Someone may look very healthy externally but have a deadly infection internally `24:00`.
- A person with an infection is quarantined — told to live in a separate room, and given food and other things very carefully — not because the person is hated or ridiculed, but so that the disease does not spread to others `26:00`.
- Some diseases are invisible to our eyes but seen with the eyes of knowledge; those who see them warn us to protect ourselves and may even impose things that look extreme — a TB patient, for example, is told to wear a mask while talking `26:00`.
- The Māyāvādī misconception kills _bhakti_: if the devotee and Bhagavān are one, there is no meaning to _bhakti_; _bhakti_ is choked and killed. That is the greatest disaster for the soul `26:00`.
- Materialistic association can also adversely affect _bhakti_ to some extent, but Māyāvāda is largely like an infection that is not visible on the surface, so we have to be careful `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### How devotees relate to impersonalists [28:00 – 30:00]

- Most people have some personal conception of God, but some may be under Māyāvādī influence `28:00`.
- If an old friend comes, we do not drive him away; the principle is simply to protect ourselves from infection `28:00`.
- At the cultural level we can be polite: when a Māyāvādī _sannyāsī_ came to meet Prabhupāda in America, Prabhupāda received him politely `28:00 – 30:00`.
- We have philosophical differences, but that does not mean we abandon our culture. Scriptural statements show how grave the misconception and how serious the disease can be, so we learn to be alert and protect ourselves; we should not appear uncultured `30:00`.
- Ultimately even impersonalist souls need the shelter of Kṛṣṇa. But if we do not have the qualification (_adhikāra_) to give Kṛṣṇa to them, they will give their conception to us — so we need not have full discussions; be culturally polite, but do not become intimate `30:00`.

#### The full spectrum, compared with the seventh chapter [30:00 – 34:00]

- Kṛṣṇa now gives two extremes: BG 9.11–9.12 the most dangerous misunderstanding, and next the highest understanding `30:00`.
- He did something similar in the seventh chapter: 7.14 that He controls the insurmountable _māyā_, 7.15 four kinds of people who do not surrender, and 7.16 four kinds who do `30:00`.
- **Seventh chapter** — the focus was on the people and their own conceptions and desires `32:00`:
  - those who work hard like asses for a worldly standard of success;
  - those caught up in sensual, worldly cravings who do not think of anything higher;
  - those so proud of their knowledge that they have forgotten its ultimate purpose;
  - those with a demonic, anti-religious, anti-God mentality — but not specifically anti-Kṛṣṇa.
- **Ninth chapter** — the focus comes more and more onto Kṛṣṇa — what people's conception of Him is — so that the glory of _bhakti_ to Him can be revealed `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Those who think Kṛṣṇa's form is material find their hopes, work and knowledge baffled and sink deeper into illusion; because this misconception kills bhakti like an invisible infection, devotees stay culturally polite but protect themselves rather than become intimate with it. [18:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.13 [32:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.13 -->

**Context.** In contrast, the great souls take shelter of the divine nature, are devoted with minds fixed on nothing else, and know Kṛṣṇa as the original and inexhaustible source of everything `32:00 – 36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mahātmā_ — a great soul: the tiny soul (_aṇu-ātmā_) that becomes great by taking shelter of the all-pervading soul (_vibhu-ātmā_) `38:00 – 40:00`.
- _Daivī prakṛti_ — divine nature; the Lord's internal potency, referring to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī `40:00 – 44:00`.
- _Āśritāḥ_ — having taken shelter; the same word was used for the demonic in the previous verse `40:00`.
- _Ananya-manasaḥ_ — with the mind on nothing else; mind fixed only on Kṛṣṇa `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Bhūtādim avyayam_ — Kṛṣṇa as the beginning of all existence and eternal `34:00`.
- _Aṇu_ and _vibhu_ — words that contrast the position of the _jīva_ (minute) and Bhagavān (all-pervading) `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### The opposite of the misconception [34:00 – 36:00]

- Those in BG 9.11 take Kṛṣṇa's form to be human and do not know His transcendental nature; the _mahātmās_ know Him as the beginning of all existence, and eternal — utterly opposite.
- Their minds are fixed only on Kṛṣṇa: they do not think of Brahman or of demigods as the supreme reality `36:00`.

#### Three uses of the word mahātmā [36:00]

- **BG 7.19** — for one who surrenders to Kṛṣṇa after many lifetimes.
- **BG 8.15** — for those who come to Kṛṣṇa, return to the spiritual world, and do not return to this material world.
- **BG 9.13** — the third use.

#### The word mahātmā today [36:00 – 38:00]

- Today _mahātmā_ is a social, cultural and political designation, for example for a freedom fighter. Prabhupāda objected, because the word has a specific meaning and using it that way is inappropriate.
- That does not mean we must campaign against it everywhere: if, at an Independence Day programme, someone calls a leader the father of the nation, that may not be the forum to object. In an appropriate forum, though, we can clarify the word's particular meaning `36:00 – 38:00`.
- What matters is the central "carry-home" message we want people to remember from a programme: chant Hare Kṛṣṇa; we are not the body but the soul; the soul can be truly happy when it serves Kṛṣṇa. As people appreciate the coherence and depth of this message, they become more open — and then such points can also be brought in `38:00`.

#### How a tiny soul becomes great [38:00 – 40:00]

- The soul is very tiny; it becomes a great soul by linking with the greatest soul, Kṛṣṇa.
- The soul does not become greater in size, but becomes great in power and influence — not by becoming independently powerful, but by connecting with the person who is supremely powerful.
- _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ uses similar contrasting terminology: the Lord is the master of _māyā_, the _jīva_ is subject to _māyā_ `40:00`.

#### Two kinds of shelter [40:00]

- The same word for taking shelter is used for both groups: the deluded take shelter of the demonic, deluding nature; the _mahātmās_ take shelter of the divine nature. Both are taking shelter, but of radically different things.

#### Daivī in the seventh chapter versus here [40:00 – 44:00]

- The word _daivī_ has to be understood by context `40:00`.
- **BG 7.14** — the illusory energy made of the three modes is called divine and very difficult to overcome; there the word emphasises that this energy is not independent but is Kṛṣṇa's energy acting under His will `42:00`.
  - Temptations in this world take us away from Kṛṣṇa, no doubt; but since that potency is also Kṛṣṇa's, we can come back to Him only by resisting it — and to resist it we must take shelter of Kṛṣṇa, not rely on our own effort.
- **BG 9.13** — the context is the exalted _mahātmās_; there is no concept of the illusory energy here, so _daivī prakṛti_ means the divine nature, the internal potency of the Lord `44:00`.

#### Yogamāyā, mahāmāyā and the pleasure potency [44:00 – 46:00]

- When we render devotional service we come out of the illusion of _mahāmāyā_ and come under _yogamāyā_:
  - _yogamāyā_ connects us with Kṛṣṇa;
  - _mahāmāyā_ disconnects us from Kṛṣṇa.
- Jīva Gosvāmī, Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura and Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura explain this divine nature as Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, the pleasure potency (_hlādinī-śakti_), the internal (_antaraṅga_) potency `44:00`.
- Because we are parts of Kṛṣṇa, we also have a pleasure potency, but ours is minute and very limited; Kṛṣṇa's is unlimited. Happiness that comes by serving Kṛṣṇa through His pleasure potency is unlimited `44:00`.
- When the _jīva_ tries to enjoy separately from Kṛṣṇa, it gets entangled in _karma_; it is meant to connect with Kṛṣṇa, and by connecting with Kṛṣṇa's potency it becomes divine in nature `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### Inner world of the mahātmā [46:00]

- The _mahātmā_ does not divert attention anywhere else.
- We know others by their actions; we cannot see their consciousness directly, though how they talk and live tells us about it. But to know ourselves, we look at our inner world — our thoughts.
- This verse describes the _mahātmā_'s inner world: the mind fixed on Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A tiny soul becomes a mahātmā not by growing independently powerful but by taking shelter of the all-pervading Lord through His divine, internal potency; the mahātmā's inner world is fixed on Kṛṣṇa alone, knowing Him as the eternal origin of everything. [34:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.14 [46:00 – 57:41]

<!-- vedabase:9.14 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the external activities of these great souls: always glorifying Him, endeavouring with great determination, offering obeisances, and worshipping Him with devotion, constantly engaged `46:00 – 48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Devotional service_ — Prabhupāda's English translation of _bhakti_: devotion is internal, service is external, and both together are _bhakti_ `46:00`.
- _Kīrtana_ — glorification of the Lord in its broad sense: speaking _kṛṣṇa-kathā_, chanting and dancing in _kīrtana_, chanting _japa_ `48:00`.
- _Satatam_ — always; a very strong adjective, similar to Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's instruction that the Lord is to be glorified always (_sadā_) `50:00`.
- _Dṛḍha-vrata_ — great determination `52:00 – 54:00`.
- _Namasyantaḥ_ — offering obeisances `56:00`.
- _Nitya-yuktāḥ_ — constantly engaged `56:00`.

#### Internal and external together [46:00]

- BG 9.13 describes the internal aspect (devotion); BG 9.14 describes the service aspect, the external aspect.
- Internally the mind is fixed on Kṛṣṇa; externally one is constantly engaged `56:00`.

#### Why the Gītā does not detail the path [48:00]

- The Gītā does not go into the details of the spiritual path, because for Arjuna the point is not how to follow a path but which path to follow. He is cultured and knows the different paths, but is undecided about which one to follow.
- Here these verses make an explicit reference to the path of _bhakti_ and its chanting; the Gītā also has Kṛṣṇa say that among sacrifices He is the sacrifice of _japa_ — so chanting is very powerful `48:00`.

#### Kīrtana in many forms [50:00 – 52:00]

- _Satatam_ does not mean we must chant the mantra mechanically all the time, even in the middle of a conversation; that can be very irritating, especially with new people `50:00`.
- If we are discussing something serious with a devotee about Kṛṣṇa's service — such as the management of various services — that is also _kīrtana_ `50:00`.
- **Prabhupāda's final days** — when Prabhupāda was very weak and many devotees came to meet and discuss with him, a devotee suggested having _kīrtana_. Prabhupāda asked what they thought they were doing now — their discussion was also _kīrtana_ `50:00 – 52:00`.
- Prabhupāda wanted to drive home emphatically that discussion related to Kṛṣṇa's service is also _kīrtana_ `52:00`.
- Caution: if we are always only discussing practical things — the festival, the programme, the distribution — we may not stay conscious that this is _kīrtana_ of Kṛṣṇa. Any form of glorification, and the arrangements needed so that Kṛṣṇa can be glorified, can be part of _kīrtana_ `52:00`.

#### Why determination, even for great souls [52:00 – 56:00]

- For us, determination is needed because the mind goes here and there and we do not feel like serving; we must bring it back and fix it on Kṛṣṇa `52:00`.
- But why do elevated devotees need determination? Because determination is the way love is expressed `52:00 – 54:00`:
  - love means we want to do wonderful things for the beloved, including challenging things that will please the beloved;
  - a lover does not go around searching for challenges; the lover focuses on the beloved, and for the beloved's pleasure accepts whatever challenges come.
- Prabhupāda's example: his achievements may blind us to how much he struggled. One of the secrets of his success was that he was a tireless, hard worker who slept just a few hours at night, earnestly sharing Kṛṣṇa consciousness `54:00`.
- If we want to do anything wonderful for Kṛṣṇa in this material world, it will not be a bed of roses `54:00`.

#### Higher taste and obeisances [56:00]

- Through _kīrtana_ one gets a higher taste and experiences the reality and beauty of Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- Kṛṣṇa describes different forms of devotional service — _kīrtana_ and offering obeisances — which also indicate the other forms.
- Prabhupāda describes how a devotee is constantly engaged in activities from morning to night, and in this way advances steadily.

#### What comes next [56:00]

- BG 9.11–9.14 describe the two extremes: those who misunderstand Kṛṣṇa and take shelter of the demonic nature, and those who take shelter of the divine nature, internally focused only on Kṛṣṇa and externally constantly engaged in His service.
- Many people lie between these two extremes; Kṛṣṇa will speak about them in the next verse, to be continued in the next class.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti is devotion within and service without: the great souls glorify Kṛṣṇa constantly — in chanting, in kṛṣṇa-kathā and even in service-related discussion — and their determination is not grim effort but the natural expression of love that accepts any challenge to please the beloved. [46:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

- No separate audience Q&A appears in the captions. The teacher raises and answers questions within the class:
