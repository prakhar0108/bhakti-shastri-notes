## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 9.15 [00:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.15 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the different conceptions by which people who want to be God-conscious approach the Absolute Truth `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bahudhā_ — the Absolute manifest in many forms; worship through the various controllers of the universe `00:00`, `08:00`.
- _Viśvato-mukham_ — refers to the idea of the _viśva-rūpa_, the universal form `02:00`, `08:00`.
- _Pratīka-upāsanā_ — worship through a symbol or representation `08:00`.
- _Ahaṅgraha-upāsanā_ — worshipping oneself as God; described as the lowest conception `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Where the chapter is heading [02:00 – 04:00]

- The universal form is mentioned briefly in the following verses of this chapter and elaborated later in Chapter 11.
- Worship of Kṛṣṇa Himself is discussed toward BG 9.29 – 9.34, and continues into Chapter 10, after which Arjuna asks about Kṛṣṇa's opulences.
- So this verse is an overview that is central to understanding the rest of the discussion.

#### The universe as God: pantheism [04:00 – 06:00]

- Greatness is naturally associated with God: God is the greatest, so He must be the biggest — and the biggest thing we see is the universe.
- So one may think the universe itself is divine and start worshipping it — a form of pantheism, the idea that everything is God.
- One may also visualise different aspects of the universe as parts of the Absolute Truth.

#### The controllers of nature: polytheism [06:00 – 08:00]

- But the universe is ultimately temporary, so one goes further and thinks there is some reality beyond it.
- Inspiring features of nature — such as the sun — seem to require controllers; so one thinks there are many beings controlling various aspects of the universe.
- One may not be clear how these controllers are connected to the Absolute; this leads to polytheism.
- From pantheism (everything is God) one comes to polytheism (there are many gods).

#### From material toward spiritual [08:00]

- These conceptions show a progression: the understanding moves from the material toward the spiritual — first the universe, then its conscious controllers, and then the spiritual One.
- The aspects of the universal form act as material tools through which people begin to conceive of the Absolute Truth.

#### The offensive conception [10:00 – 12:00]

- Some think Kṛṣṇa's form is false and that one must go beyond it to a higher reality; from the devotional perspective this is an offensive conception.
- The _Māyāvādīs_ have understood to some extent that this material world cannot provide happiness, so at the level of philosophical insight they may seem greater.
- But one who blasphemes Kṛṣṇa is offensive toward Kṛṣṇa, and that offence makes devotional advancement very difficult; in this sense even those absorbed in material activity may be better placed, because they are not offensive.

#### Worshipping oneself [12:00 – 16:00]

- Viśvanātha Cakravartī explains that the lowest category is of those who cannot conceive of the Absolute except in terms of their own existence.
- This is most prominent in _ahaṅgraha-upāsanā_ — and it is a contradiction in terms: if I say "I am God" and worship myself, what does worship even mean?
- Such a conception is not devotional and not emotionally evolved; it is the greatest obstruction to devotion.
- Properly understood, however, such people can at least come to see that they are not the material body but the spiritual soul — and in that sense it too is a type of God-worship `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
People approach God through progressively deeper conceptions — the universe, its controllers, the One beyond — but denying Kṛṣṇa's form is offensive, and making oneself God is the greatest obstruction to devotion. [04:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.16 [16:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.16 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa identifies Himself with the various elements of sacrifice `16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kratu_ — the Vedic ritual `16:00`.
- _Yajña_ — here, the sacrifice described in the _Purāṇas_ `16:00`.

#### Sacrifice is not merely material [16:00 – 20:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is helping us see that the performance of sacrifice is not just something material.
- The teacher traces an evolution of thought in the Gītā: in BG 4.24 the elements of sacrifice were identified with _Brahman_ — at the seeker's level at that time the Absolute was not yet clearly specified.
- Now, from the devotional perspective, it becomes clear that all the elements of the sacrifice are ultimately manifestations of Kṛṣṇa Himself.
- The same concepts are revealed again in a more Kṛṣṇa-centred way.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
What BG 4.24 called Brahman, Kṛṣṇa now reveals as Himself: every element of sacrifice is His manifestation, so sacrifice is never merely a material act. [16:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.17 [20:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.17 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says He is the father, mother, support and grandfather of the world, the object of knowledge, the purifier and the syllable _oṁ_ `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pavitra_ — the purifier `20:00`, `24:00`.
- _Oṁkāra_ — the sacred sound syllable `20:00`.

#### In what sense is Kṛṣṇa our father and mother? [20:00 – 24:00]

- In this world we have different supports, inspirations and shelters; Kṛṣṇa says, "I am all of them."
- This is not to be taken literally — sometimes people misconceive it to mean "mother is God, father is God."
- We should be grateful to our parents, but the point is that Kṛṣṇa is the one benefiting us through them: He gives shelter and strength through the mother, the father and the grandfather.
- Likewise it is He who gives us knowledge and He who purifies; ultimately everything, and every potency, comes from Him.
- This is repeated in Chapter 10 in terms of Kṛṣṇa's opulences — a spiritual vision of seeing Kṛṣṇa everywhere `24:00`.

#### The part and the whole [24:00]

- The philosophical principle: the part is not different from the whole, because the part is qualitatively similar to the whole.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Parents, teachers and every other support are channels through which Kṛṣṇa gives shelter, strength and knowledge; we honour them while recognising Him as the real source. [20:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.18 [24:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.18 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes Himself as the goal, sustainer, master, witness, abode, refuge and dearmost friend — a description of the Absolute Truth in terms of His relationship with us and the things around us `24:00 – 28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Gati_ — the ultimate goal `24:00`.
- _Suhṛt_ — the greatest friend `26:00`.

#### Defining God through relationship [26:00 – 28:00]

- God can be defined as the creator, maintainer and destroyer, the source of everything, the Absolute Truth.
- Here, however, Kṛṣṇa describes Himself in terms of His relationship with us — a very beautiful verse to meditate on.

#### Seeing Kṛṣṇa's arrangement [28:00]

- The teacher recounts an observation from the early days: in a shop where most sellers were non-vegetarian, there was still an abundant supply of vegetables and grains.
- This was understood as Kṛṣṇa arranging things properly; a devotee does not see only material things, but sees through them to Kṛṣṇa's arrangement.

#### The doctor and the real life-giver [30:00]

- When we go to a doctor for an operation, the doctor strives and helps to save our life, and we are grateful to the doctor.
- But the ultimate life-giver is not the doctor — it is Kṛṣṇa.
- This is a multi-level understanding of how things are acting.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is not only the cause of the cosmos but our goal, shelter and dearest friend; a devotee sees through doctors, shops and supplies to the Lord who is really sustaining us. [26:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.19 [30:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa continues to explain how the various universal manifestations are connected with Him — He gives heat, sends and withholds rain, and is immortality and death, spirit and matter `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Amṛtam_ and _mṛtyu_ — immortality and death `36:00`.
- _Sat_ and _asat_ — the teacher does not go into a technical analysis; he simply translates them as spirit and matter `36:00`.

#### Seeing the positive and the negative [32:00 – 34:00]

- From the point of view of material science, the sun exists and gives heat and light; without heat we would not survive. Kṛṣṇa says it is He who gives the heat — He is manifesting as that energy.
- Similarly we need water.
- The previous verse spoke more of Kṛṣṇa as the positive maintainer; here we see both the positive and the negative as manifestations of the power of the Absolute Truth.
- God is not a judge with an emotional agenda against anyone.
- Rain can come heavily and cause huge floods: we can discharge water through hoses, even the big hoses of a fire brigade — but who can shower as much water as the rains?

#### Kolkata under bombing [34:00 – 36:00]

- During the war the Japanese were bombing Kolkata, wanting to attack the East and conquer India; bomb shelters were made and people sheltered in them.
- At that time Śrīla Prabhupāda could see even that destructive power — the power of the most powerful — as a manifestation of Kṛṣṇa.

#### Immortality and death [36:00]

- Kṛṣṇa comes and gives His message so that we can attain immortality; He exists at the level of immortality.
- Matter and spirit are both energies of Kṛṣṇa, as discussed in BG 7.4 – 7.5; and in BG 7.6 whatever exists is a combination of both, and both come from Him `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Helping the worshippers of the universal form [38:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is revealing something important: those who worship through the universal form do not know that it is Kṛṣṇa whom they are worshipping.
- The _Bhāgavatam_ speaks of the universal form as the first step in God-realisation; later steps will come.
- Here Kṛṣṇa helps such worshippers understand that all this is connected with Him and will ultimately lead to Him — He is the ultimate goal.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
With spiritual vision both the pleasant and the destructive forces of nature are seen as Kṛṣṇa's power; and those who worship the universe are told that it is ultimately He whom they are approaching. [32:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.20 [40:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.20 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes those who perform the ritualistic activities of _karma-kāṇḍa_, drink _soma_, and attain the heavenly planets to enjoy celestial pleasures `40:00 – 46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma-kāṇḍa_ — ritualistic fruitive activities `40:00`, `46:00`.
- _Soma_ — a drink that is part of the sacrifice `40:00`.
- _Divya_ — its meaning depends on the context: in BG 4.9 it means transcendental; here it means celestial `44:00`.

#### Soma and intoxication [40:00 – 42:00]

- In this world people take intoxication. First of all, a person who thinks some toxic substance will make him happy is foolish — and the drink makes him more foolish still.
- Getting drunk is like a temporary spell of madness: the person speaks incoherently, walks unsteadily, and becomes a different person.
- People compare _soma_ to such intoxicants, but it is entirely different: normal alcohol takes our consciousness down, whereas what Kṛṣṇa speaks of here is connected with going to _svarga_ `42:00`.

#### Three levels of existence [44:00 – 46:00]

- Terrestrial — this earth.
- Celestial — the higher, heavenly levels of material existence.
- Transcendental — beyond the material altogether.
- In this world bodies are attractive only for a very short time; in the heavens people are far more beautiful and stay good-looking for a very long time.
- So here _divya_ refers to higher material happiness — enjoyment in the heavens — not spiritual happiness.

#### Not a criticism here [46:00]

- In BG 2.42 – 2.43 Kṛṣṇa spoke of such people as lacking discrimination.
- Here Kṛṣṇa is not trying to criticise: they too worship Him, but indirectly, through His manifestations as the demigods.
- The problem is that they do not come to Him directly; their aspiration is to go to the heavens.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Ritual worship can lift one to celestial pleasures far beyond earthly ones — but celestial is not transcendental; such worship reaches Kṛṣṇa only indirectly and aims at heaven, not at Him. [40:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.21 [48:00 – 57:45]

<!-- vedabase:9.21 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes what happens to such people after they have enjoyed the heavens: when their pious credit is exhausted they return to this world `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dharma, artha, kāma_ — three of the _puruṣārthas_; such people are caught in these three and do not aspire for _mokṣa_ `48:00`.
- _Gatāgatam_ — coming and going: birth and death `48:00`.
- _Kāma_ — desire, specifically the desire for enjoyment `50:00`.

#### A temporary system [50:00 – 52:00]

- The whole system gives a temporary benefit in terms of enjoyment.
- For an intelligent person, anything that gives a result that is not eternal is less intelligent — one should go for the best.
- The soul has the capacity to go for the best; the coming verses will reveal how, by actually worshipping Kṛṣṇa, one attains eternity — which is not so difficult, though relatively rare in this world `52:00`.

#### Back to the struggle [54:00]

- The path of material _dharma_ and _puṇya_ remains within birth and death.
- Whatever we get will come back to normal life — the stress, the strain, the struggle for existence and the cut-throat competition return.
- It is just a temporary escape and not lasting; an intelligent person aspires for something that lasts.

#### The melting iceberg [56:00]

- Temperatures are rising and icebergs are melting. Imagine a person sitting very comfortably on an iceberg — when it melts, he falls into the ocean.
- Likewise, good _karma_ gives a comfortable situation, but when it is exhausted one falls back into the material system.
- So rather than trying for a comfortable material situation, we should aim for the spiritual destination — which Kṛṣṇa will talk about next.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Heavenly enjoyment is like sitting comfortably on a melting iceberg: when pious credit runs out one falls back into the struggle of birth and death, so the intelligent aim is the spiritual destination. [48:00 – 57:45]
</Callout>
