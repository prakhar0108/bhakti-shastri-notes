## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 9.31 [00:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.31 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa assures that the devotee will soon become righteous and attain lasting peace, and asks Arjuna to declare that His devotee never perishes `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Declare_ — not just to tell, but to proclaim and announce publicly, like a royal announcer who goes to the public square with drums and bugles to make an authoritative, authorised announcement `00:00 – 02:00`.
- _Never perishes_ — not the same as the soul's indestructibility taught in the second chapter; here it means the devotee's devotional advancement never perishes. It may be covered temporarily, but it is recovered and manifested again by the Lord's merciful arrangement `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### The inner conversation between Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna [02:00 – 04:00]

- The teacher presents the verses as a set of inner exchanges following the flow of their thoughts:
  - Kṛṣṇa: My devotee should be considered saintly.
  - Arjuna's implied doubt: how can one doing terribly bad activities be considered saintly?
  - Kṛṣṇa: the devotee will soon become religious, the agitations of the mind will go away, and lasting peace will come.
  - Arjuna's further doubt: what if the devotee does not become religious?
  - Kṛṣṇa: even then, My devotee will never perish.
- This is "an endearing revelation of Kṛṣṇa's heart" `02:00`.

#### Real peace is internal [02:00]

- Some peaceful external situation is needed at some level, but lasting peace is internal.
- It comes when our heart is in harmony with God — when we are not resenting and rejecting His guidance and love.

#### A fall grieves the community — and the Lord [04:00 – 06:00]

- The whole context is the devotee's spiritual advancement and protection: the devotee is eager to serve Kṛṣṇa and Kṛṣṇa is eager to protect the devotee — a natural reciprocation of love `04:00`.
- When a devotee falls, it is heartbreaking for mentors and carers, saddens the whole devotee community — especially if the devotee is well known — and also causes distress to the Lord.
- This does not mean the Lord falls into a "bad mood": God can do multiple things at once — He reciprocates fully with the _gopīs_ in the spiritual world, while as the Supersoul in every heart He is available to the falling _sādhaka_ if they just turn to Him `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### Slips, falls — but no fatalities [06:00]

- "My devotee will never perish" does not mean a devotee cannot fall; Kṛṣṇa has already acknowledged that even a grievous fall can happen.
- The emphasis is that the fall is temporary, not eternal.
- **Three degrees of accident:**
  1. A _slip_ — losing footing while walking.
  2. A _fall_ — actually hitting the ground.
  3. A _fatality_ — dying.
- On the spiritual path there can be slips and falls, but no fatalities: the soul is eternal, and whatever spiritual credits one has acquired stay with the person forever.

#### Free will and how far one falls [06:00 – 08:00]

- The soul always has free will — to serve Kṛṣṇa or to go away from Him — throughout the journey, even in the spiritual world.
- There, having thoroughly experienced both the misery of defying Kṛṣṇa and the joy of loving Him, we will not make the wrong choice; in the material world we remain vulnerable.
- The mind internally and the world externally tempt us away, so we must be cautious; one who is not cautious can be seduced and deluded by _māyā_.
- How far one falls depends on how attached one is to sense gratification and how much attachment to Kṛṣṇa one has developed.

#### Revival is rapid [08:00]

- Attachment to Kṛṣṇa never goes away; it can only be covered temporarily.
- Reviving it is not like ordinary _sādhana_, which uncovers what has been obscured for many lifetimes: for one who has fallen, the Kṛṣṇa-conscious impressions are in recent memory, so with the right impetus the devotee comes back rapidly to the previous level.
- Kṛṣṇa never forsakes a devotee, especially an advanced one; He arranges every possible impetus and circumstance to help the devotee make the right choice and come back.

#### Falling down versus falling away [10:00 – 12:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's love says, "Even if you fall away, I will take care"; our love for Kṛṣṇa says, "I will not fall away."
- **Falling down** — moving forward towards Kṛṣṇa, but some lapse happens and we do something wrong.
- **Falling away** — going away from Kṛṣṇa altogether.
- Kṛṣṇa is everywhere, so even when we fall we should fall into His shelter: "Kṛṣṇa, I'm sorry this happened; please forgive me; please rectify me."
- Even with lapses, we should not give up the practice of Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- After a serious moral lapse, a devotee may not be in the best position to serve as a teacher, guide or leader, and such readjustment can and should be made when required — but leaving a leadership position need not mean leaving Kṛṣṇa. It can even purify one's motive: "I serve not for respect, but because loving and serving Kṛṣṇa is the best thing for me" `12:00`.

#### Assurance should inspire, not make us complacent [12:00 – 16:00]

- The verse should not make us think, "I will never perish no matter what I do."
- It should make us feel: if Kṛṣṇa goes so much out of His way and gives such a guarantee of His love, why should I ever leave His feet?
- **Ajāmila example** `12:00 – 14:00`: Ajāmila committed grievous sins yet was delivered by the Viṣṇudūtas after chanting the Lord's name even unconsciously. A devotee should not conclude, "I too will chant unconsciously and demand to be rescued."
- Extraordinary demonstrations of mercy, in scripture statements or incidents, should reassure us of His love and inspire us to commit to loving Him — not make us demand the same for ourselves. The mood of love is to please the Beloved, not to demand His love `14:00`.
- Even when displeased, the Lord does not stop loving; but if we love Him we will avoid what displeases Him.
- If, by some conditioning or situation, we still do something displeasing, that too should not make us give up: "Kṛṣṇa, only You can purify me; please help me." `16:00`

#### Coming back, in this life or the next [16:00]

- **Child-and-ground example:** a child who falls gets up by taking support of the same ground; similarly, when we fall we turn to Kṛṣṇa, whose instructions we could not follow, and beg Him for strength to follow them.
- The teacher mentions devotees, including some who fell away, who in their final days dramatically came back, gave up all attachments and left their bodies in extraordinary ways.
- Even if that does not happen for everyone, Kṛṣṇa is with them in their heart; He wants them back as early as possible in this life, and if not, He is with them in the next body too, trying to bring them back to His lotus feet.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A devotee may slip or fall, but never perishes: Kṛṣṇa never stops loving, and devotional credit is never lost. This assurance should not make us complacent but should inspire us never to fall away — and, if we fall down, to fall into Kṛṣṇa's shelter. [06:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.32 [18:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.32 -->

**Context.** After incidental faults, Kṛṣṇa says that even those with inherent faults — by conventional reckoning — can attain the supreme destination by taking shelter of Him `18:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Incidental faults_ — faults arising from some incident: a person does a wrong act and that contaminates them, though they are not inherently deficient (the subject of 9.30–9.31) `18:00`.
- _Inherent faults_ — from the conventional _varṇāśrama_ perspective, an unfavourable starting point due to birth `18:00`.
- The three examples Kṛṣṇa gives — women, _vaiśyas_ (the mercantile community) and _śūdras_ (labourers) — even they can attain the supreme destination `20:00`.

#### Bhakti does not recognise such discrimination [18:00]

- Within _varṇāśrama_ culture, the higher _varṇas_ are considered more suitable for the practice of _dharma_ and the lower _varṇas_ less suitable.
- Kṛṣṇa acknowledges this point but then says that _bhakti_ does not recognise such discrimination.

#### Does the Gītā call women sinful? [20:00 – 22:00]

- To many, this verse seems discriminatory; the teacher says it must be understood in its cultural context.
- Any soul who gets a human body — male or female — is extremely fortunate: considering the many species and their vast numbers, souls in human form are very rare, and both forms have the capacity for spiritual realisation.

#### Varṇa is by quality, not by birth [22:00 – 24:00]

- Within the human form there are gradations of suitability for spiritual realisation.
- _Brāhmaṇas_ are ready to live lives focused on Brahman — simplicity, austerity, purity, sense control and balanced behaviour; these qualities are more suitable for spiritual realisation, so _brāhmaṇa_ is by quality, not by birth.
- Those not ready to regulate themselves much are considered _śūdras_ — again not by birth, but by their unwillingness or inability to harmonise with spiritual principles; they too are given arrangements to be gradually elevated, though it may take time.
- This is true for _karma_ and _jñāna_; it is not the case for _bhakti_. _Bhakti_ is based on a relationship of love; the soul is the same in a _śūdra_ body or a _brāhmaṇa_ body, and the heart can offer love from whichever body one is in `24:00`.

#### Different starting points — bhakti makes them not matter [24:00 – 28:00]

- Generally the male body is considered more suitable for self-realisation: males are more analytical and philosophical, while bodily identification is generally greater in the female body — with exceptions. (The teacher refers back to the first chapter's discussion around 1.37–1.38.) `24:00`
- Different bodies come according to one's _puṇya_ and attachments; we all have different starting points, and denying that is "making ourselves blind." Acknowledging this is acknowledging reality, not discrimination `26:00`.
- The process of _bhakti_ is so powerful, potent and fast that once we start, the starting points don't matter — Kṛṣṇa's emphasis is not on supporting discrimination but on teaching the process that ends it `26:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda also says elsewhere that women are more devotionally minded and faithful and take to worship more easily. _Bhakti_ uses every emotion in Kṛṣṇa's service, so directing their emotions to Kṛṣṇa can make advancement easier. He notes in a purport to Queen Kuntī's prayers that women generally come to religious programmes more than men `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We all begin from different starting points, and acknowledging that is not discrimination; but bhakti — a relationship of love that any soul in any body can offer — is so powerful that once we start, the starting point no longer matters. [24:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.33 [28:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.33 -->

**Context.** If even those with unfavourable starting points can be elevated, how much more those with favourable ones — the devotees and saintly kings. Kṛṣṇa therefore urges Arjuna to worship Him, having come to this temporary, miserable world `28:00 – 32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _How much more_ (the force of the verse's opening) — how much more likely it is for those with favourable starting points `28:00`.
- _Rājarṣi_ — saintly king; Kṛṣṇa indirectly but emphatically tells Arjuna, "You belong to this category — practise _bhakti_." `30:00`

#### The coaching-class analogy [30:00]

- In the Indian system, a coaching class that takes a student with very poor 10th-standard marks and makes them a top ranker in 12th standard shows its power.
- Then a student who is already a top ranker in 10th has even brighter prospects and can rise more easily and rapidly.
- Likewise, if even those born in unfavourable circumstances can be elevated, those with favourable starting points can surely be elevated.

#### A passing reference, as in 8.15 [30:00 – 32:00]

- The second half of the verse — that this world is temporary and miserable — repeats a point made in 8.15.
- As in 8.15, it is an incidental, passing mention; the stress is not on misery but on the call: since we are here, get out of this world and return to Kṛṣṇa.

#### "This world is miserable" does not mean be insensitive [32:00 – 34:00]

- It does not mean we must let ourselves always be miserable; a mother who lets her crying child cry, saying "this world is miserable," would be monstrously insensitive.
- It means we should not let all our energies be consumed in trying to make this world comfortable.
- We need a basic level of comfort, and scripture arranges for it: the mother cares for the children, the husband for the wife and child, the king for the citizens. In the fourth canto of the _Bhāgavatam_, when citizens come to the king saying hunger is burning their stomachs, he takes care of them `32:00`.

#### The hospital analogy [34:00]

- In a hospital the misery is there, and there is a process to become free from it; patients who follow the process are gradually cured.
- Kṛṣṇa similarly gives a process; to the extent we follow it we become free from misery — spiritually, and even materially, since living in harmony with Kṛṣṇa lessens misery.
- A patient who doubts recovery is told: those with far greater disease were cured; yours is much less — so just take the medicine. "Therefore worship Me."

#### Don't over-emphasise material classifications [36:00 – 38:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that ultimately nobody is happy in this world, so we should not over-emphasise its classifications.
- If our vision stays at the material level we compare endlessly — wealth, memory, looks — and forget that the body is temporary and will grow old and die.
- **Train analogy:**
  - Passengers travel in first, second and third class; there is a real difference in comfort.
  - Wanting first class is not wrong, but if someone gets off the train to get into first class, the train leaves without them.
  - The most important point is that the train moves towards the destination; the journey is temporary, so comfort is secondary as long as we keep moving forward.

#### Birth shapes nature, but should not become a label [38:00 – 42:00]

- From the spiritual point of view bodily attributes are not inherent, but from the bodily point of view they are: we each have a psychophysical nature (_svabhāva_) that comes from birth.
- One's _varṇa_ is largely determined by birth in this sense — by birth we get a body with certain inclinations — but it is not necessary that we belong to the _varṇa_ of the family we are born in.
- It is for us to acknowledge our nature and act in harmony with it materially, while practising spiritually to transcend it. It is not for society to label people by family; that labelling leads to discrimination `40:00`.
- A person with a business propensity made to teach in a college will be miserable; allowed to do business, they will be comfortable internally and good at it `40:00 – 42:00`.
- Whatever our starting point, this world is miserable for everyone — so let us practise devotional service, become purified and become liberated `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
If even those with unfavourable starting points can be elevated, those with favourable ones surely can; since this world is a temporary, miserable train journey, the point is not first-class comfort but keeping the train moving toward Kṛṣṇa. [30:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.34 [42:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.34 -->

**Context.** The last verse of the chapter tells how to become a devotee — fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa, become His devotee, worship Him, offer obeisances — and promises that one so absorbed will come to Him `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Fix your mind on Me_ — not cold, clinical thinking, but warm, transformational thinking `42:00 – 44:00`.
- _Become My devotee_ — fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa in such a way that He can enter our heart `44:00`.
- _Ātmā_ — the consciousness acting at the physical and mental levels; it becomes fully devoted to Kṛṣṇa `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Cold, clinical thinking versus transformational thinking [42:00 – 44:00]

- One can fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa as an object of theoretical study — e.g. a PhD on Kṛṣṇa's position in the _Mahābhārata_ — without any devotion or transformation.
- **Laboratory example:** a scientist studies an amoeba with full concentration but develops no affection for it. Kṛṣṇa does not want that kind of thinking.
- Kṛṣṇa is such an attractive conception of God that just by thinking of Him open-heartedly He enters our heart `44:00`.
- Activities like offering obeisances and _ārati_ foster the feeling of devotion `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### Three levels offered to Kṛṣṇa [46:00]

- **Subtle level** — the mind: "think of Me."
- **Spiritual level** — the heart, the seat of emotions, which ultimately come from the soul.
- **Physical level** — the body: offering obeisances with the hands and engaging the body in His service.
- Offering all three levels, one becomes not just engaged but completely absorbed in Kṛṣṇa; the _ātmā_ itself becomes devoted, and by this devotion one returns to Him `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### The essence of the essence of the essence of the essence [48:00 – 50:00]

- The _ācāryas_ call this verse the essence of the essence of the essence of the essence:
  1. The Gītā is the essence of all Vedic literature (the Upaniṣads are the cow and the Gītā the milk).
  2. The middle six chapters (7–12) are the essence of the Gītā, because they focus on _bhakti_.
  3. Within them, Kṛṣṇa Himself calls the ninth chapter the king of knowledge, the most confidential knowledge.
  4. Within the ninth chapter, this last verse calls for pure devotion and guarantees the supreme liberation.
- **Sandwich analogy:** in a sandwich, the bread above and below is assumed; what names and flavours it — cheese or vegetable — is the middle. The first and last six chapters are the "bread" of the Gītā, and the middle six give it its taste `48:00`.
- Prabhupāda quoted this verse frequently `50:00`.

#### Imposing impersonalism here is violence to the Gītā [50:00 – 54:00]

- The whole flow of this chapter is personal and devotional; the only mention of the impersonal conception (9.11–9.12) is Kṛṣṇa condemning it `50:00`.
- A famous _māyāvādī_ commentator says that on this verse we are to think not of Kṛṣṇa but of "the Unborn within Kṛṣṇa." Seeing the Gītā's flow, we understand why Prabhupāda was so upset about it.
- Others may hold their philosophy if they wish, but imposing it on the Gītā — right in the heart of the _bhakti_ section — is "brutal violence" to its mood and message. Opposing it is not intolerance but fidelity and common sense `52:00`.
- Faithful students and teachers need not become physical, but must be vigorous in ensuring people understand the Gītā correctly.

#### Why Western and Indian scholars read it differently [54:00]

- Before Prabhupāda went to America, many teachers presented the Gītā there from an impersonalist perspective.
- Western scholars have generally studied the Gītā as a devotional book, while many Indian scholars have taken it as impersonalist — not because Westerners lean personalist, but because they are less biased by the impersonalism prevalent in modern India and by the respected tradition of Śaṅkarācārya's impersonal commentary.
- The _ācāryas_ have been both **vigorous** (travelling, writing, speaking, debating) and **rigorous** (systematic analysis of scripture and context, not sentiment) in defending the personalist message. We internalise it and share it with others.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The ninth chapter's last verse — the essence of the essence of Vedic wisdom — asks us to offer mind, heart and body to Kṛṣṇa in warm, transformational thinking, not cold study; so absorbed, the soul becomes fully devoted and returns to Him. [42:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Overview of the ninth chapter [56:00 – 59:20]

- **9.1–9.3** — this knowledge is most confidential; it removes one from all misery; those without faith continue in material existence. The knowledge is about Kṛṣṇa and _bhakti_, meant to inspire _bhakti_.
- **9.4–9.10** — who Bhagavān is: His inconceivable relationship with the material world; Creator, Maintainer and Destroyer, yet detached.
- **9.11–9.19** — how different people understand Kṛṣṇa: the impersonalists who deride Him (9.11–9.12), the great souls, three kinds of worshippers (9.15), and further description.
- **9.20–9.28** — beautiful, inviting verses emphasising devotion (9.22, 9.26): _bhakti_ gives eternal results while demigod worship gives temporary ones; _bhakti_ is easy, demigod worship difficult and only indirect worship of Kṛṣṇa.
- **9.29–9.34** — _bhakti_ elevates even the most fallen, whether disqualified by incidental actions or inherent dispositions.
- The eighth chapter ended with "therefore practise _yoga_"; this chapter conclusively establishes which _yoga_ — _bhakti-yoga_ — and its last verse is the most direct call to _bhakti_ so far. In the next chapter Kṛṣṇa, in His own ecstasy, describes Himself and _bhakti_ further `58:00`.
