**Context.** Before moving to BG 9.29, the teacher goes deeper into demigod worship: how the scriptures and _ācāryas_ regard it, and what conceptions underlie it `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Demigod_ — Śrīla Prabhupāda's word for _devatā_; like "semi" in "semicircle," _demi_ means partial: beings who have part of God's power, more powerful than us but not as powerful as the Supreme Being `10:00`.
- _Multi-level monotheism_ — one Absolute Truth manifesting at different levels and in different ways, allowing people to worship accordingly `08:00`.

#### Different attitudes in the scriptures [02:00 – 08:00]

- In general Śrīla Prabhupāda did not encourage demigod worship; when a devotee asked whether to do Gaṇeśa worship, his response showed he did not want that kind of contamination to enter `02:00`.
- **The _Mahābhārata_** — the Pāṇḍavas worship Śiva on many occasions; Arjuna worships demigods to get weapons from them, and does austerity to please Śiva; just before the Kurukṣetra war Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to take the blessings of Devī, the goddess in charge of material nature `02:00 – 04:00`.
  - When Arjuna prays, Devī tells him his victory is guaranteed because Keśava is with him, and wherever He is there is victory, because He is the Lord of the goddess of fortune.
  - Demigod worship appears here because every book has a particular focus: the _Mahābhārata_'s focus is largely _karma-kāṇḍa_, though it also contains pure devotion.
- **Rāmānujācārya** (around a thousand years ago) considered worship of the _devatās_ unchastity: as a wife serves her husband and not other men, a worshipper should serve only the Supreme Lord `04:00 – 06:00`.
  - The teacher tells a story from one of his biographies: in Purī, Rāmānuja pointed out to the priests that the starch had not been removed from Lord Jagannātha's garments; the Lord did not like His servants being criticised, and in his sleep Rāmānuja was carried elsewhere, waking in a temple he thought was a Śiva temple.
- **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu** said there is nothing wrong in worshipping the _devatās_ if one understands that they are devotees `06:00`.
  - On His way to Purī He visited the Bhuvaneśvara temple and danced before Lord Śiva; an _aṣṭakam_ He composed glorifies Lord Śiva as among the greatest Vaiṣṇavas.
  - But where people's understanding and worship were improper, He strongly disapproved: one person placed black-magic paraphernalia of goddess worship at Śrīvāsa Ṭhākura's door to frame him, and as a consequence of that offence contracted leprosy; he was forgiven when he begged forgiveness, but still had to suffer the consequences `06:00 – 08:00`.
- So Mahāprabhu did not reject Vedic culture; He integrated it into a proper devotional understanding. The demigods are not competitors of the Supreme Lord `08:00`.

#### Multi-level monotheism [08:00 – 12:00]

- The Judeo-Christian and Islamic traditions consider themselves monotheistic and Hindus polytheistic; but Vedic culture is actually monotheistic — a multi-level monotheism.
- In those traditions, conquerors would destroy the idols of a conquered place — as when Muhammad conquered Mecca — because other gods were considered false: imaginary, temptations, manifestations of the devil meant to distract people from the true God `08:00 – 10:00`.
- Vedic culture speaks not of false gods and a true God but of God and demigods `10:00`.
- Today people's philosophical understanding is unclear ("all gods are the same, all manifest the same Brahman"), and few understand this complex, accommodating system.
- To avoid confusion, serious devotees within the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement should not worship other _devatās_ but focus on Kṛṣṇa — which is what the Gītā and the _Bhāgavatam_ ultimately teach; by worshipping Kṛṣṇa everyone else is worshipped `10:00 – 12:00`.
- If someone is very attached to worshipping a demigod, one may do so understanding the demigod as the Lord's devotee `12:00`.

#### Proper noun and common noun [12:00 – 14:00]

- _Śiva_ is both a proper noun (the person Lord Śiva) and a common noun (the quality of auspiciousness); in the _Viṣṇu-sahasra-nāma_, Śiva is also used as a name of Viṣṇu.
- If one chants "Śiva" as a proper noun, thinking of the specific person who holds a trident and wears a snake, one goes to that person's abode — depending on whom we remember, that is what we attain.
- If, while primarily chanting the names of Viṣṇu or Kṛṣṇa, one chants "Śiva" as a common noun referring to Viṣṇu's qualities, it is part of _bhakti-yoga_ and takes one back to Godhead.

#### Name and conception [14:00 – 18:00]

1. **Right name, right conception** — chanting Kṛṣṇa's names with the right understanding.
2. **Right name, wrong conception** — e.g. chanting Lord Rāma's names while thinking "one day I will become Rāma"; such chanting does not give the desired result. The chanting of the _Māyāvādīs_ is compared to seeing the sunrise in a reflection — one cannot get much warmth from it `16:00`.
3. **Wrong name, right conception** — non-Vedic names in other languages, such as "Allah," which may not appear in a list like the _Viṣṇu-sahasra-nāma_, still purify, because chanting God's names is meant to help us remember God — either through His personal pastimes or through His generic characteristics as the Supreme Being. The conception of the one Supreme Being in the Bible shares these broad characteristics; that is why Prabhupāda would say that chanting any bona fide name of God purifies `16:00 – 18:00`.
4. **Wrong name, wrong conception** — the many self-proclaimed gods worshipped by thousands or millions of followers: that person is not God and his name is not God's name, so this leads ultimately to futility; the teacher connects this with BG 9.12 `18:00`.

#### Conceptions of demigod worship [18:00 – 22:00]

- **The demigod is the Absolute Truth, no different from the Supreme Lord** — a _Purāṇic_ verse rejects as completely wrong the conception that a demigod is equal to Lord Viṣṇu.
- **One among many gods, none supreme** — the popular _karma-kāṇḍa_ idea today; also false, because there has to be one Supreme — the gods are not eternally fighting.
- **A symbol of an impersonal Brahman** — the _Māyāvāda_ idea that forms are a convenient fiction, a meditational tool to be transcended; this becomes offensive to the Supreme Lord.
- **The demigod as Kṛṣṇa's representative and devotee** — this is included within devotional service itself `20:00`.

#### The gopīs' worship of Kātyāyanī [20:00 – 24:00]

- The _gopīs_ knew no one but Kṛṣṇa, yet they worshipped Kātyāyanī for her blessings to get Kṛṣṇa as their husband.
- When a devotee is intensely desirous of the Lord's association, the devotee adopts any and every possible means; as women in their society, the _gopīs_ could not simply approach Kṛṣṇa directly, so within their culture they expressed their intense desire through this vow.
- Toward the end of the vow Kṛṣṇa fulfilled their desire, taking away their garments and returning them.
- So their worship is not a _dūṣaṇa_ (contamination) but a _bhūṣaṇa_ (ornament): it shows the intensity, desperation and single-mindedness of their desire for Kṛṣṇa `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### All worship reaches Kṛṣṇa — not all worshippers [24:00 – 26:00]

- Does all worship go to Kṛṣṇa? Yes — but all worshippers do not go to Kṛṣṇa.
- Water offered into a river goes to the ocean, but the person who offers it does not.
- A well-known saying compares the worship of any _devatā_ reaching Keśava to rain falling from the sky reaching the ocean; it is not inaccurate, but it is not completely accurate, and it is often interpreted to mean all worship is on the same level.
- Worshippers go where their conceptions and aspirations drive them; one who worships Durgā or Indra does not go to Viṣṇu — as BG 9.25 clearly brings out `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Vedic culture is multi-level monotheism: demigods are the Lord's devotees, not rivals; all worship finally reaches Kṛṣṇa, but each worshipper reaches only what his conception and aspiration aim at — so serious devotees worship Kṛṣṇa alone. [08:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.27 [26:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.27 -->

**Context.** Having completed the discussion of demigod worship, the teacher returns to BG 9.27 to look at levels of devotional service: what level of _bhakti_ is "whatever you do, offer it to Me"? `26:00`

#### What this level is not [28:00 – 30:00]

A commentary classifies it with a technical name for a mixed level of _bhakti_, giving reasons:

- **Not _sakāma_** — the verse speaks of no material desires to be fulfilled.
- **Not _karma-yoga_** — all activities are offered, not just the scripturally prescribed ones; _karma-yoga_ focuses on offering prescribed activities.
- **Not _jñāna-yoga_** — appreciation of the Lord's personal features is present, whereas _jñāna-yoga_ focuses more on the impersonal conception.
- **Not _kevala-bhakti_** — the actions are offered after performance, not before.

#### Before or after? [30:00]

- "First" and "after" is not merely a matter of timing but of attachment.
- One attached to the Lord first thinks what will please Him and then does that.
- One not so attached to the Lord does what he wants to do and then offers it to Him — this is one level lower than _kevala-bhakti_.
- Prabhupāda quotes SB 7.5.24 here — especially its second part: the nine processes of _bhakti_ are characterised as _bhakti_ when they are directly connected with the Lord and done for His pleasure `30:00`.
- In such a mixed level _bhakti_ predominates, while _karma_ and _jñāna_ remain as components `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Offering what we have already chosen to do is good, but pure devotion first asks what will please Kṛṣṇa and then acts — the difference is not timing but attachment. [28:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.29 [32:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.29 -->

**Context.** The teacher places the coming verses within the chapter's glorification of _bhakti_, then explains Kṛṣṇa's declaration that He is envious of no one and partial to no one — yet those who worship Him with devotion are in Him and He is in them `32:00 – 34:00`.

#### Five glories of bhakti in this chapter [32:00 – 34:00]

1. **Destination** — _bhakti_ alone gives the highest, eternal destination; other worship does not.
2. **Possession** — it does not need fancy material assets.
3. **Devotional qualification** — it can be performed even by those who are not yet pure-hearted.
4. **Deviation** — it can redeem even those who commit occasional or incidental faults.
5. **Material qualification** — it can uplift those not qualified even for _karma-mārga_, removing inherent faults; the coming verses say even the low-born can be elevated.

Three were discussed already; the last two come now.

#### "They are in Me and I am in them" [34:00]

- A very endearing declaration of an intimate connection; in the Gītā the Lord does not often speak in such endearing terms, so when He does it is significant.

#### Impartially partial [36:00 – 38:00]

- The statement seems contradictory but is not; it depends on which aspect of the Lord is being described.
- As a judge, the Lord is impartial: each person gets what he is entitled to by his actions.
- In His _Bhagavān_ aspect — intimate and exalted — He focuses on pure devotional service, and wherever He sees it He embraces that person.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda's example: a charitable person may be charitable to all, but is naturally more affectionate to his own children, and that is not considered a fault `36:00 – 38:00`.
- All living beings are His children, but some are obedient and loving, others defiant and even hateful; the Lord cares for everyone, but intimacy is possible only with those who try to reconnect with Him `38:00`.
- So Kṛṣṇa is "impartially partial": impartial in opening the door to everyone to receive and benefit from His partiality `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### Gold and diamond [38:00 – 40:00]

- When the living entity becomes inclined to the Lord's service, the devotee is like gold and the Lord like a diamond; together, as in a necklace, they look very beautiful.
- Without the principle of reciprocation, no personal philosophy is possible.

#### More than a desire tree [40:00 – 42:00]

- The Lord is often compared to a desire tree that supplies whatever one asks — a good example of His reciprocal nature.
- But the Lord is not just a desire-fulfiller; He is a relationship-builder:
  - a desire tree does not become attached to the person asking;
  - it does not strive on its own to protect him;
  - it does not become the enemy of his enemy.
- The Lord does all this: the Pāṇḍavas were attached to Him and He became attached to them; when they were in danger He became their ally — even their subordinate, their charioteer.
- He gives the ultimate blessing by creating circumstances that help us develop love for Him `42:00`.
- Prabhupāda concludes that devotional service is not an activity of this material world; it belongs to the spiritual world, and to the extent we situate ourselves in it, we too become situated in the spiritual world `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is impartially partial: He judges everyone fairly and opens the door to all, but He embraces those who love Him — not as a desire tree but as an ally who protects, serves and draws them closer. [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.30 – 9.31 [42:00 – 1:00:56]

<!-- vedabase:9.30 -->

<!-- vedabase:9.31 -->

**Context.** An extraordinary verse: even if someone commits extremely bad actions, if he continues to worship Kṛṣṇa determinedly, he is to be considered a _sādhu_ — and, the next verse adds, such a person soon becomes righteous `42:00 – 44:00`, `56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sādhu_ — "good"; one whose behaviour is very good `44:00`.
- _Su-durācāra_ — one whose conduct is terrible, extremely unsaintly `44:00`.
- _Ananya-bhāk_ — undeviating worship `44:00 – 46:00`.
- _Dharmātmā_ — a righteous person, elevated by the practice of _dharma_ `56:00`, `60:00`.

#### A mysterious verse [44:00 – 46:00]

- Normally we would say a _sādhu_ is never a _durācārī_; one who behaves terribly is not a _sādhu_ at all — yet Kṛṣṇa says he is to be considered a _sādhu_.
- And if a person is battling with conditionings and succumbing to them, how can he do undeviating devotion?
- _Ananya_ here does not mean the person practises devotional service unbreakably, twenty-four hours a day; it means he does not deviate from the path of _bhakti_ and does not doubt that Kṛṣṇa is the Absolute Truth — he just cannot maintain steady worship and the required standards.
- Such a person stays fixed in the worship of Kṛṣṇa (or of Viṣṇu, Rāma and other such forms), and Kṛṣṇa says he should be considered a _sādhu_ `46:00`.

#### A hypothetical glorification [46:00 – 50:00]

- There is a range of explanations, from conservative to liberal and accommodating.
- One explanation: this is a rhetorical or hypothetical statement, made to highlight a particular glory — not to say that falling should happen.
- The devotee does not go to another deity, nor to another process: he does not say, "I fell from my principles, so _bhakti_ doesn't work — I'll practise some other yoga."
- The essential point: the process of devotion is so great that even if a person commits a grievous wrong, as long as he stays fixed in worshipping the Lord, devotion can still redeem him `48:00`.

#### Not antinomianism [50:00 – 54:00]

- _Antinomianism_ — held by certain Christian branches: since Jesus has died for our sins and paid for them with his blood, we have no obligation to follow any moral principles.
- Such a philosophy cannot be accepted on the devotional path. Devotion can ultimately free one — when one surrenders everything else and centres one's life on Kṛṣṇa and love for all His children; then Kṛṣṇa frees us from sinful reactions. It does not mean He lets us continue sinful actions for all time.
- A serious devotee has to practise the regulative principles and thereby give up sinful activities; Prabhupāda explains in the purport that one who cannot do so is not considered a very advanced devotee `50:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa consciousness does not justify wrongdoing just because a devotee does it: devotees must also follow traffic rules and the laws of the state `52:00`.
- Asked how his disciples would be known, Prabhupāda did not say "by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa" or "by wearing _tilaka_" — he said they are perfect gentlemen, of exemplary character `52:00`.
- Someone casually breaking moral codes or legal rules because he is a devotee is not a serious devotee at all — such misuse is very unfortunate `52:00`.

#### Fish in one hand [52:00 – 54:00]

- In Bengal at one time many claiming to be Vaiṣṇavas held this antinomian idea: the holy name is so powerful that whatever wrong we do doesn't matter.
- They would come from the market with fish in one hand, arguing that as coast-dwellers they had to eat fish — an attitude not at all compatible with aspiring for pure devotional service.
- The response focused on compassion: how can we kill other children of God and claim to love God?
- So devotion does not free one from moral responsibility `54:00`.

#### A sweet explanation: the gopīs [54:00 – 1:00:56]

- Read with the next verse, which says such a person soon becomes righteous, the statement can seem not to make sense; one commentary gives a very sweet explanation `56:00`.
- The verse refers to the _gopīs_ of Vṛndāvana: when they heard Kṛṣṇa's flute, they left their homes and families in the middle of the night to go to Him.
- From the worldly point of view, a woman leaving her family at night to see another man is terrible conduct `58:00`.
- But it represents the supremacy of their surrender: for God we give up not only the bad but even the good of this world.
- For a woman, chastity is considered as precious as life — the scriptures show this: when the Kauravas tried to violate Draupadī's modesty, the whole Kurukṣetra war followed and the Kuru dynasty was destroyed.
- For Kṛṣṇa the _gopīs_ were ready to give even that up — an exalted level of devotion; and because Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme, they were not actually unchaste but supremely chaste, though not in the world's eyes `58:00 – 60:00`.
- Those who understand this and consider the _gopīs_ to be _sādhus_ soon become righteous, elevated by the practice of _dharma_ `60:00`.
- The next part of the verse is discussed in the following class, which concludes the ninth chapter.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Devotion is so powerful that one who stays fixed in worshipping Kṛṣṇa is a sādhu even while still struggling with faults — but this glorifies bhakti, it never licenses sin: Kṛṣṇa's devotees must be perfect gentlemen. [44:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
