## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 9.22 [00:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.22 -->

**Context.** A famous verse: for those who approach Him personally and devotionally, Kṛṣṇa promises to protect what they have and provide what they lack `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ananya-cintā_ — exclusive, undeviating thought of Kṛṣṇa; not an obligation but the demonstration of love `04:00`.
- _Yoga_ — as discussed with BG 9.5, essentially "connection"; here the provision of whatever is required `10:00`.
- _Kṣema_ — protection `10:00`.

#### Reciprocation, not business [02:00 – 06:00]

- The principle of BG 4.11 — Kṛṣṇa reciprocates with everyone according to how they approach Him — applies here; but here He says He takes personal responsibility for those who approach Him in devotion.
- There are pastimes in which the Lord manifested extraordinary phenomena to protect His devotees; this verse and later BG 9.30 – 9.31 are inspiring in this way, but the teacher focuses on the principles rather than the stories `02:00 – 04:00`.
- This is not a business arrangement — not like hiring a security agent or bodyguard and paying him; it is a bond of love, and _ananya-cintā_ is the demonstration of that love.
- _Ananya-cintā_ is not a restriction we must follow to get a result; it comes naturally when the mind is fixed on the Supreme Lord.

#### From practice to spontaneity [06:00]

- Thinking of Kṛṣṇa constantly may seem very difficult; it is a discipline and a practice.
- The more we think of Him, the more we are purified; His beauty attracts us and we fall in love with Him.
- Then thinking of Him becomes natural, perpetual and habitual.
- At first it is a consciously chosen cultivation — _sādhana_; later it becomes spontaneous.
- The Lord is located in the love of the devotee — the verse shows the intimate loving relationship between them.

#### The real life insurance [06:00 – 08:00]

- The Life Insurance Corporation has appropriated part of this verse.
- But material life insurance pays someone else after we die — nobody can insure life at the material level, because death is certain.
- Real life insurance is the assurance that we will attain eternal life — and that insurance is given by Kṛṣṇa.

#### Caring for the complete person [08:00]

- When we care for someone, we care for the whole person, not just the body.
- Kṛṣṇa likewise cares for His devotee as a complete person: naturally more for the soul, which is the real person, but He also takes care of material needs.

#### Two fears, two assurances [08:00 – 10:00]

- Fear that what we have will be taken away — like a thief stealing our internet-banking security code and password and emptying our account.
- Fear that what we need will not be available — such as not having health insurance and wondering how we will live.
- Kṛṣṇa answers both: provision (_yoga_) and protection (_kṣema_) are His two assurances.

#### The why and the how [10:00 – 14:00]

- When we take care of the "why" of life — living for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure — He takes care of the "how" of life.
- This does not mean we become impractical: if we start something new we arrange basic financial support; that is the basic thing we do.
- Beyond that, we do not think only in practical terms, because ultimately our faith is in Kṛṣṇa.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda came to America with just forty rupees, which could not even be converted to dollars; asked on the way from the airport how he would maintain himself, he expressed complete confidence that whatever he needed would be provided by Kṛṣṇa `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Contrast with the previous verses [14:00 – 18:00]

- In BG 9.20 – 9.21 the ritualists accumulated a lot of pious credit, but that stockpile cannot last, and nobody provides for them — they fall back.
- Here Kṛṣṇa reveals His personal intervention; the Gītā is progressive: in Chapter 2 Kṛṣṇa said that on the spiritual path there is no loss, and here He shows that it is He personally who sees to it `14:00 – 16:00`.
- Prabhupāda struggled to live in India, practically homeless after taking _sannyāsa_ and even before, moving from house to house; yet he was never consumed, exhausted or defeated by these difficulties, because behind them was Kṛṣṇa's hand and protection `16:00`.
- In devotional terms, the devotee's only desire is to attain the Lord: _yoga_ is the provision of purity and mercy needed to connect with Him, and _kṣema_ is His protection so that we never fall back `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
For one who thinks of Him exclusively, Kṛṣṇa personally provides what is needed and protects what is gained — not as a business deal but as love's reciprocation; when we take care of the why of life, He takes care of the how. [04:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.23 [18:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.23 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says that the devotees of other gods who worship with faith are also worshipping Him, but not in the proper way `18:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Anya-devatā-bhaktāḥ_ — devotees of other gods `18:00`.
- _Vidhi_ — scriptural rules and regulations; _avidhi-pūrvakam_ — worship done in the wrong way `20:00`.
- _Śraddhā_ — faith; Kṛṣṇa discussed it in BG 7.21 – 7.22 `20:00`.

#### The prince who left home [20:00]

- A prince has left home, and his father the king wants him back, but the prince is not ready to come.
- So the king gets the prince back into the kingdom by getting him to come with a minister.
- The teacher applies this to Kṛṣṇa's dealings with those not yet ready to come to Him directly.

#### Half a verse is a misrepresentation [22:00]

- Many Gītā commentators and popular Hindu websites translate this verse but drop the last part, _avidhi-pūrvakam_, so that it says simply: those who worship other gods also worship Me.
- This is a misrepresentation by omission: what they say is correct but incomplete, and the essential point of the verse is lost — that such worship is not proper.
- Kṛṣṇa already said in BG 7.23 that such people are less intelligent: they reach temporary destinations, not the eternal one `24:00`.

#### Is demigod worship like bribery? [24:00 – 26:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda gives the example of bribing an officer, which is illegal.
- The teacher asks: is demigod worship illegal? Demigod worship given in the Vedas is part of Vedic culture — a multi-level culture — and is definitely legitimate.
- At the lower levels there are corresponding forms of worship to attract people and gradually elevate them; the ultimate goal is to take a person to the transcendental level, where one serves out of pure love with no other desire.
- Prabhupāda explains _avidhi-pūrvakam_ from the perspective of that ultimate goal, as in BG 18.66: the various _dharmas_ are part of Vedic culture, but finally one should give them up and surrender to Kṛṣṇa alone `26:00`.

#### Cheating religion [26:00]

- The danger is that people continue in materialism while thinking they are doing it in God's name.
- From the transcendental perspective, anything that calls itself religion but is not service to Kṛṣṇa is _kaitava-dharma_ — cheating religion.
- Still, whatever we want we can also get by worshipping Kṛṣṇa, because He is the ultimate Lord of all the demigods.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Demigod worship is a legitimate rung of Vedic culture, yet Kṛṣṇa calls it improper because it does not lead to Him; quoting only half the verse hides exactly this point. [20:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.24 [28:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.24 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains why demigod worshippers fall down: He is the enjoyer and master of all sacrifices, but they do not know His transcendental position `28:00`.

#### Not knowing Kṛṣṇa [28:00 – 30:00]

- Those who worship other gods are in one sense worshipping Him — but they do not know His transcendental position.
- If we do not know Kṛṣṇa's position and do not love Him, we cannot return to Him, because that is not what we are seeking; ultimately we get what we desire.
- This is a general principle: whoever does not understand His position does not get the result available to those who worship Him.

#### Ignorance of His position [30:00]

- The point Kṛṣṇa makes is ignorance of His position: He is not some generic being but the master of all the demigods.
- By knowing Him in truth, as in BG 4.9, one can return to Him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
All sacrifices finally reach Kṛṣṇa, but the worshipper reaches only what he knows and desires; ignorance of Kṛṣṇa's position is why demigod worshippers fall back. [28:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.25 [32:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.25 -->

**Context.** Answering the question "then what is the use of worship, and what is the difference?", Kṛṣṇa states that worshippers reach the destination of whomever they worship — and His worshippers come to Him `32:00 – 34:00`.

#### Different worship, different destinations [32:00 – 34:00]

- BG 9.21 described those who are elevated to heaven and fall back when their pious credit is exhausted.
- Here Kṛṣṇa explains where each kind of worship leads, and that those who worship Him reach Him.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda explains that this clear knowledge is not easily available; the movement is meant to distribute this information so that people can turn back to Kṛṣṇa for eternal life `34:00`.
- After this, Kṛṣṇa contrasts devotional service with demigod worship in terms of how simple devotional worship is `34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Each worshipper reaches the object of his worship; because this clear knowledge is rare, sharing it is itself a great service. [32:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.26 [36:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.26 -->

**Context.** Pleasing the demigods requires elaborate sacrifices done properly, but worshipping Kṛṣṇa is extremely simple: a leaf, a flower, fruit or water offered with love, He accepts `36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bhaktyā_ — with devotion; the verse is about the consciousness of the offering `36:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa at Vidura's house [38:00 – 42:00]

- Kṛṣṇa did not accept the opulent feast at Duryodhana's house; instead He chose to go to the house of Vidura.
- Vidura was a brother of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, who was officially the king; though connected to the royal family, Vidura was living simply.
- Kṛṣṇa came unexpectedly, and Vidura's wife was so excited that, while offering bananas, she peeled them, threw away the fruit and offered Kṛṣṇa the peels — and Kṛṣṇa accepted them.
- A banana peel is very difficult to eat — one can hardly taste even a fraction of it — yet the Lord accepted it because of her love.
- So the Lord does not need an opulent feast; He accepts even simple fruit — or a _tulasī_ leaf or flower — offered with love `42:00`.

#### Devotion is the point — and devotion offers the best [44:00]

- What pleases the Lord is the devotion of the heart; even if its expression is very simple, what He primarily looks at is the devotion.
- This does not mean that if we have a million dollars we should offer Kṛṣṇa only a leaf: when there is devotion in the heart, we want to offer the best we can.
- That is the meaning of devotion — whatever we have, we offer the best of it to the person we love.
- Kṛṣṇa mentions only such simple items; whatever benefits are available through other kinds of worship are also available through this simple worship `46:00`.

#### The box of sweets [48:00 – 50:00]

- A mother puts some nice sweets in a box, and her child takes them to an uncle's house for a sleepover and gives them to the uncle and cousins.
- The next morning, as the child leaves, the aunt does not let the box go back empty; she fills it with sweets she has made.
- From an external view nothing happened: a box of sweets went in and a box of sweets came out.
- Actually, love was offered through the sweets and love was returned through the sweets.
- Similarly, when we offer food to Kṛṣṇa we are offering our _bhakti_ to Him; externally the food remains the same, but it returns as _prasāda_ — the medium through which Kṛṣṇa reciprocates with His mercy.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa accepts even a banana peel offered with love, because He looks at the heart; and the devotion that pleases Him naturally wants to give Him the best — returned to us as prasāda, His love in edible form. [38:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.27 [50:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:9.27 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa considers the person who does not yet have that devotion: whatever you do, eat, offer, give or perform as austerity, do it as an offering to Him `50:00`.

#### Offering without the word bhakti [50:00 – 52:00]

- Here Kṛṣṇa does not use the word _bhakti_; even the bare principle of offering to Him is special.
- Unlike the previous verse, He does not specifically say here that He will accept it.
- Does that mean Kṛṣṇa would reject it? No — there is a difference in the quality of devotion, but that does not mean anyone is left out of the world of devotion.
- The broad principle: whatever we do, we offer it to the Supreme Lord; by acting this way we make rapid spiritual advancement `52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even where devotion is not yet deep, the simple principle of offering all one's activities to Kṛṣṇa keeps one within the world of devotion and brings rapid spiritual advancement. [50:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 9.28 [52:00 – 57:40]

<!-- vedabase:9.28 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the result of offering everything to Him: one becomes free from the bondage of auspicious and inauspicious results and comes to Him `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sannyāsa_ — disconnection from matter `52:00`.
- _Yoga_ — connection with Kṛṣṇa, integrating everything toward Him `52:00`.
- _Puṇya_ and _pāpa_ — pious and sinful activity; _puṇya_ is definitely better, but both are material and both keep us in karmic bondage `54:00`.

#### Free from both kinds of bondage [54:00]

- If we want liberation from karmic bondage, we must become free from both pious and sinful reactions.
- By doing our work for Kṛṣṇa — offering it to Him — we become free from both.
- The ultimate result is the highest liberation: "you will return to Me." This is a gradual progression.
- Whatever we have that can be used in Kṛṣṇa's service should be used for Him.

#### Bound only by love [56:00]

- The advanced devotee is not bound by the goodness or badness of this world; he is bound simply by love for Kṛṣṇa.
- Because of that love, whatever the devotee does is ultimately good for everyone — but the devotee is not focused on "doing good."
- The teacher cites a devotee's words: "I cannot do my _sandhyā-vandana_, I cannot do my _tarpaṇa_, I cannot do the various rituals; I offer my respects to you, but I simply go to remember Kṛṣṇa."
- In this way one becomes free from all material complications, whether positive or negative — the ultimate perfection of worship is devotional service.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Pious and sinful reactions both bind; offering all work to Kṛṣṇa frees one from both, until one is bound only by love for Him — the perfection of worship. [52:00 – 57:40]
</Callout>
