## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 14.8 [00:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.8 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the mode of ignorance: it is born of ignorance, deludes all living beings, and binds through madness, laziness and sleep `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tamas_ — generally translated as ignorance; the mode of ignorance `00:00 – 02:00`.
- _Pramāda_ — madness `00:00`, `06:00`.
- _Ālasya_ — laziness: we have to do something but refuse to do it and keep putting it off `00:00`, `06:00`.
- _Nidrā_ — sleep `00:00`.
- _Prakāśa_ — the illumination that enables one to see the right thing; the characteristic of goodness, which is the opposite of ignorance `06:00`.

#### Ignorance born of ignorance: a vicious cycle [02:00]

- The phrase "ignorance born of ignorance" points to a self-perpetuating vicious cycle:
  - First there is basic ignorance.
  - Acting on it aggravates the mode of ignorance.
  - That pushes one into deeper ignorance, which complicates life further.
- Example — alcohol:
  - The idea "drinking will free me from my troubles" is itself born of ignorance; the troubles come back in a far more troubling form.
  - One peg becomes two, then five; then money runs out, the house is mortgaged, one gambles, takes loans, robs, and may even end up killing.
  - There is no limit to how low blinding addiction — characteristic of ignorance — can drag a person.

#### Why "deludes all living beings" [04:00]

- For goodness and passion Kṛṣṇa did not use such a descriptor; here He says ignorance deludes _all_ living beings. The teacher gives levels of understanding:
  - The bodily conception of life is itself ignorance, and all living beings are in it.
  - Everyone has to sleep and feels tired before sleep; sleep is a basic bodily function, so in that sense ignorance affects everyone. Pure devotees who sleep for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure and dream of Him are exceptions.
- The key distinction:
  - _Necessary_ contact with the mode of ignorance — e.g. sleep.
  - _Unnecessary aggravation_ of it — e.g. drinking or taking drugs, which only makes the situation worse.

#### The opposite of goodness [06:00]

- The common factor is delusion, but some people become especially lethargic, intoxicated and infatuated, particularly by destructive substances.
- Prabhupāda notes that the mode of ignorance is a peculiar qualification of the embodied soul, just the opposite of goodness: goodness is characterised by illumination, ignorance by darkness — unawareness of what is true.
- In 14.6–14.8 Kṛṣṇa has given a brief introduction to each mode with its primary characteristics; the whole point of the discussion is that the modes bind `06:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Ignorance feeds on itself: acting on a deluded idea deepens the delusion. Some contact with ignorance, like sleep, is unavoidable, but aggravating it through laziness and intoxication drags a person ever lower. [02:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.9 [08:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.9 -->

**Context.** After the one-verse introductions to each mode, Kṛṣṇa gives a one-verse summary of how the three modes bind — in one word each `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sukha_ — happiness; goodness binds through it `08:00`.
- _Karma_ — action; passion binds through it `08:00`.
- _Rāga_ and _tṛṣṇā_ — the characteristics of passion discussed earlier: _rāga_ is attachment to what one has, _tṛṣṇā_ is hankering for what one wants; both bind one to _karma_ `08:00`.
- _Pramāda_ — madness; ignorance binds through it once knowledge is covered `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### Each mode's binding force [08:00]

- **Goodness** — one feels relatively happy and satisfied: "I know more than others, I look pure and better than others, I'm happy living this way."
  - Good in one sense — one is not living sinfully.
  - But it also prevents one from elevating oneself further and transcending the whole gamut of material existence.
- **Passion** — one wants more and more, and so gets caught in _karma_.
- **Ignorance** — once knowledge is covered, one becomes bound to madness.

#### What "madness" means here [10:00 – 12:00]

- Not necessarily someone admitted to a mental asylum.
- It means acting in self-destructive, distressing ways — causing distress to oneself and to others — whether or not one is aware of it, and generally without intending to cause pain.
- Example — "cutters":
  - Some people develop the perverse idea of cutting their own skin, often in places others cannot see.
  - The intensity of the pain makes them forget everything else, and that gives them a kind of "kick"; this can become an addiction needing psychiatric attention.
  - It is like those who go to horror movies — not for entertainment, but to be horrified and thus forget their own anxieties.
- It seems absurd and irrational, yet that is how people act in ignorance.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Each mode binds in its own way: goodness through a comfortable sense of happiness, passion through endless action for more, and ignorance through self-destructive madness. Even goodness binds, because its contentment can stop us from rising beyond matter. [08:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.10 [12:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.10 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the competition among the modes: sometimes goodness triumphs, overcoming passion and ignorance; sometimes passion triumphs; sometimes ignorance triumphs `12:00`.

#### Neither discrete nor static [12:00 – 14:00]

- A person is like a puppet pulled by three strings at once; the teacher mentions a picture of a figure pulled by three puppeteers representing the three modes.
- The modes are discrete in their existence — there is goodness, passion and ignorance — but their influence on us is neither discrete nor static.
- Bathing in the morning and feeling in goodness does not mean we stay in goodness for the rest of the day.

#### Watching the competition in a class [14:00 – 18:00]

- **Goodness:** "Hearing the class is important; I must understand the philosophy" — we start interested.
- **Ignorance:** "I need seven hours of sleep and slept only 6 hours 55 minutes — maybe a five-minute nap."
- **Passion:** "There are so many other things to do — when will this get over?" This creates restlessness.
- Restlessness is either given in to (switching off an online class, using a phone call as an excuse to leave) or fought against. Genuine emergencies do happen and must be dealt with; the point is to observe how the modes compete within our own psychology.
- Ignorance also speaks as weariness: "I thought I had finished studying after college; I came to spiritual life to be free from tension, and these exams mean more tension."

#### Purification, not deactivation [16:00 – 18:00]

- Seeing spiritual life as a place to relax from everything is not a proper understanding.
- Spiritual life does give relaxation, but through _purification_, not _deactivation_.
  - Deactivation — simply not doing anything — is succumbing to ignorance and brings no advancement.
  - The soul is truly happy when it is fixed in loving service to Kṛṣṇa, doing various services for His pleasure.

#### Indecisiveness [18:00 – 20:00]

- The conflict of the modes underlies most other conflicts; one of the biggest is indecisiveness — a general inability to act decisively, as distinct from indecision in one particular situation.
- If we don't take decisions, life takes decisions for us.
- In a cricket match with ten runs needed in the last over, the captain _must_ decide which bowler to use; in life — career, job, marriage — we can keep dragging our feet.
- Dragging one's feet is characteristic of ignorance. It is not the same as thoughtfulness, where data is processed, choices and consequences are considered, and time is given for mature reasoning and for understanding one's deepest feelings.

#### Three modes, two dimensions [20:00]

- Goodness — contemplative _and_ active.
- Passion — active but not very contemplative.
- Ignorance — neither active nor contemplative.
- Externally goodness and ignorance can look alike: two people sit in a class, one hearing attentively, the other calmly sleeping; a third who walks out to do many other things may be in passion. Externally similar, internally radically opposite.

#### Moving up and down quickly [20:00 – 22:00]

- We think of the modes vertically — ignorance, passion, goodness, then transcendence — and that is true, but in practice one can move between them very rapidly.
- Because goodness and ignorance look similar, there is a "shortcut" from goodness to ignorance: chanting in the morning, one feels tired and simply falls asleep.
- Sometimes passion protects us from ignorance: if sleepy while chanting, stand up and walk, wash one's face. Walking keeps us awake and at least uttering the holy name; some awareness is better than being half or fully asleep.
- The principle: overcome the lower modes with the higher mode.

#### A fork at every moment [22:00 – 24:00]

- Imagine a road that forks three ways — and then forks again at every moment.
- Our situation is like that: continuously we choose whether to act in goodness, passion or ignorance, and our life moves accordingly.
- This explains much of our confusion — why our feelings change so rapidly. Rather than being carried away, we can recognise "this is goodness, this is passion, this is ignorance," choose goodness, and as we choose it, goodness increases within us `24:00`.

#### Static and dynamic descriptions [24:00 – 26:00]

- 14.6–14.8: the static characteristics of how each mode binds.
- 14.10: the conflict among the modes.
- 14.11–14.13: the dynamic picture — the psychological condition when a particular mode triumphs; 14.11 parallels 14.6 (goodness), 14.12 parallels 14.7 (passion) and 14.13 parallels 14.8 (ignorance), with some differences.
- Prabhupāda connects the modes with actions, explored much more in Chapter 17: by looking at externals we can get a glimpse — not entirely, but some understanding — of the internal `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The modes are not fixed labels but competing forces pulling us at every moment; recognising which one is speaking — and choosing goodness, even using a little passion to fight off ignorance — is how we steer through this constant fork in the road. [12:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.11 [26:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.11 -->

**Context.** When goodness becomes prominent, all the "doors" of the body are illuminated by knowledge `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- The doors of the body — the nine gates discussed in 5.13; the senses are the doors through which consciousness travels `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Prakāśa_ — illumination `26:00`.
- _Jñāna_ — knowledge, which brings illumination `28:00`.

#### Light at the doors [26:00 – 28:00]

- Leaving a dark room, I cannot make out the door from the wall, or what lies beyond — steps, a slope, a road, an elevator.
- With illumination I can see things clearly.
- Example — a cough: to me it is just a cough, but a doctor's diagnosis may reveal a serious disease. Medical knowledge illuminates the symptoms; similarly knowledge illuminates the consequences of our choices.
- Consciousness can go many ways through the senses, and each choice brings consequences. Perceiving those consequences means there is light; without it there is delusion, confusion and stupefaction, and one acts foolishly and self-destructively.

#### The "right position" [30:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that in goodness one can see, hear and taste things in the right position — a curious word, the teacher notes.
- "Position" means how an object interacts with us, how we choose to deal with it, and what effect it will have on us.
- Example — food (discussed more specifically in Chapter 17):
  - In ignorance one may eat something harmful.
  - In passion one may eat only for taste, without thinking of health.
  - In goodness one understands "this will affect me in this way."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Goodness lights up the senses: we see where each sense choice leads and how each object will affect us, so we can act rightly instead of stumbling in the dark. [26:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.12 [30:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.12 -->

**Context.** When passion increases, there is greed, intense endeavour, fruitive activity and uncontrollable desire `30:00 – 34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Lobha_ — greed; a prominent characteristic of passion `30:00`, `34:00`.
- _Pravṛtti_ — action according to one's inclination `30:00`.
- _Ārambha_ — in Hindi, Marathi and other vernaculars it often means "beginning"; in Sanskrit it can mean that, but here it means endeavour — as in 4.19, where every endeavour of the _karma-yogī_ is purified by the fire of knowledge; the word also comes in Chapter 12 `30:00 – 32:00`.
- _Karma_ — fruitive activity `32:00`.
- _Aśama_ — desire that cannot be calmed; contrasted with _śama_ (calming the mind), which with _dama_ is a characteristic of a _brāhmaṇa_ `32:00`.
- _Vivṛddhe_ — when a mode becomes strong or predominant `34:00`.

#### More, more, more [34:00]

- The primary feature of passion is "more, more, more" in terms of matter.
- Directed at sexually attractive objects it manifests as lust; directed at things it becomes greed.

#### Disposition or imposition? [34:00 – 38:00]

- Question: do the modes create these feelings — is a person greedy by disposition (innate inclination) or by imposition (external pressure)?
- Answer: both — though "imposition" is not an irresistible, fatalistic force.
  - By past _karma_ some people are by nature greedier than others; such diversity is always present in society.
  - Beyond that, people place themselves in, or choose, circumstances that provoke greed — e.g. constantly watching advertisements or television showing huge material prosperity.
- Lust likewise: some level is natural and biological, and sex drive varies, but whatever it is, a person can increase or decrease it. Today's social and cultural environment is designed to increase it, leaving people in artificial stimulation and perpetual dissatisfaction.

#### Forced to act as we choose to desire [38:00 – 40:00]

- "We are forced to act as we choose to desire": we have the choice of desiring, but once we choose, the modes take over and it becomes very difficult to resist.
- The river with three currents (a subtler example than the three-way fork):
  - One current is calmer, one more turbulent, one swirling round and round.
  - The boatman can choose which current to enter, but once in it, the waves carry him in that direction.

#### Can we choose our desires? [40:00 – 42:00]

- The modes do not determine our specific desires; they influence which desires are presented to us. They are contributive, not decisive factors — ultimately we have free will.
- Example — meat eating:
  - Someone raised in a family and culture where everyone eats meat may never consider vegetarianism; technically there is a choice, but it doesn't arise within the framework of thought.
  - Input from elsewhere — meeting a vegetarian, reading about it, seeing the brutality of animal slaughter — can lead one to choose a better option.
  - Even within an addiction there is a range of choice (different kinds of meat).
- We choose desires based on intelligence, knowledge, culture and free will — an indicative, not exhaustive, list.
- Once a choice is made, free will almost disappears — not entirely, since change is always possible — but some actions have massive consequences, so major life decisions need much more caution `42:00`.

#### Passion colours perception [42:00 – 46:00]

- Choosing the calm current seems obvious when the currents are outside us; but the currents are inside us and have already shaped our perception.
- To a person in passion — texting, answering emails and calls — a calm, serene beach with no network is "boring" and causes agitation.
- The irony: what agitated them (endless messages) is gone, and its absence now agitates them.
- So the cause of agitation is not the phone but being in passion; in goodness, wherever we are, we will not be agitated.
- Still, our disposition cannot survive every position: some places are strongly in passion, so devotees try to avoid external stimulations that trigger internal emotions and then actions `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### Workaholism [46:00 – 50:00]

- A form of addiction in today's passionate society: work, work, work — no time for spiritual life, family or other aspects of life; a unidimensional existence harmful even at the material level.
- Unlike alcoholism, it is glamorised: "he hasn't taken leave in seven years," "works fourteen hours a day" — rewarded with awards, perks and raises.
- On alcohol, the teacher notes the advice "drink but don't get drunk" is as impractical as advice can be: when drinking is an escape route, in real trouble one escapes along it much further than intended.
- Deadlines and emergencies may require choosing passion at times; there is no mental "button panel" for the modes, but if passion becomes the normal way of functioning, it takes a toll psychologically, emotionally, and on relationships and balance.

#### Busy, not passionate [50:00 – 52:00]

- In devotional service we are told to stay busy so as not to fall into _māyā_ — but Prabhupāda was busy, never passionate; we are often passionate but not busy.
- The fan example: a fan moves a lot but gets nowhere; that energy could have moved a car a long way. Our mind similarly goes round and round, and our actions become uncoordinated and incompletely executed.
- Doing a little of many things is fine with a plan; acting on whatever we feel like at each moment destroys balance.
- There is no end to sense gratification in passion — no matter how much one has, one wants more; this is the meaning of _aśama_ `52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Passion is "more, more, more" — endless endeavour driven by desire that never calms. The modes don't fix our desires, but once we choose a desire we are carried by its current, so we must choose carefully and cultivate a goodness in which we can stay busy without being restless. [34:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.13 [52:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.13 -->

**Context.** When ignorance increases, there is darkness, inertia, madness and illusion. Kṛṣṇa now uses a word indicating neither the illumination of goodness nor the action of passion — simply immobilisation `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pramāda_ — madness; addictions are an example `54:00`, `56:00`.
- _Moha_ — illusion `54:00`, `56:00`.

#### Addressing Arjuna [52:00 – 54:00]

- Kṛṣṇa uses various names for Arjuna in this section, reminding him that he is illustrious and well connected — yet the modes are formidable and can overpower anyone, even him.

#### Why sleep is not mentioned here [54:00]

- In 14.8 _nidrā_ was mentioned; here it is not, because the topic is not the necessary bodily function of sleep but the unnecessary, undesirable aggravation of ignorance when it dominates in the competition among the modes.
- When one mode dominates, it shakes up the person and makes them act in undesirable ways. In goodness the action may be relatively better and desirable in some ways, but it still keeps one in bondage; in passion and ignorance it is much worse.

#### Perversion of pleasure [54:00 – 56:00]

- Sadism — causing pain to others and getting pleasure from it.
- Masochism — causing pain to oneself and getting pleasure from it, such as the cutting described earlier or self-flagellation.
- Male–female attraction is normally in passion, but when passion combines with ignorance, sexuality gets perverted into various unnatural and distorted forms; these are largely in ignorance.

#### Illusion about one's material capacity [56:00]

- All of us are in _moha_ about our spiritual identity — we have forgotten that we are the soul, not the body.
- In ignorance there is additionally illusion about one's _material_ capacities — e.g. a poverty-stricken weakling convinced he will be the next world wrestling champion and can beat anyone.

#### Looking ahead [56:00 – 58:00]

- 14.6–14.8 described the character of the modes; 14.11–14.13 how people are influenced when one mode dominates within the competition.
- Next, Kṛṣṇa will describe what happens at death in each mode — a pressing topic on a battlefield where death is probable, and one already discussed in Chapter 8 — to be taken up in the next class.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Dominant ignorance means neither light nor purposeful action but immobilisation — madness that seeks pleasure in pain and illusion even about one's own material strength. [52:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
