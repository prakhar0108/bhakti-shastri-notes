## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 14.20 [00:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.20 -->

**Context.** When the embodied soul goes beyond the three modes, it is freed from the miseries born with the body — birth, old age and death — and attains immortality `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Amṛta_ — nectar; often used for something delicious that also prolongs life, but here primarily immortality — a state beyond death and destruction `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### Immortality across scripture [02:00 – 04:00]

- Kṛṣṇa uses this or similar words several times in the Gītā:
  - 2.15 — one who stays equipoised in happiness and distress becomes eligible for immortality.
  - 13.13 — Kṛṣṇa will explain the knowable, by knowing which one attains immortality.
- The same idea appears in the Śrī Īśopaniṣad (Mantras 9–11) on _vidyā_ and _avidyā_: by knowledge of _avidyā_ one goes beyond material existence, and by knowledge of _vidyā_ one attains immortality.

#### Not the heavenly nectar [04:00 – 06:00]

- Many products carry the word _amṛta_, but none can make one immortal.
- The heavenly nectar obtained from churning the milk ocean does not actually grant immortality; it only prolongs life — the very long lifetime of the demigods, which, as discussed earlier, is not the same as eternity.
- Here Kṛṣṇa means real immortality, attained by realising one's eternal nature — by transcending the modes, which are what keep us bound to material existence `06:00`.

#### Two, three or four miseries [06:00 – 08:00]

- Kṛṣṇa refers to the miseries of material existence in several places, sometimes naming two, three or four of them:
  - 13.8–13.12 — among the items of knowledge.
  - 7.29 — those seeking liberation from old age and death.
- The basic point is the same: these are the miseries of material existence, from which transcendence frees us.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Transcending the modes is the real nectar of immortality — not a prolonged life like the demigods', but freedom from the birth, old age and death that come with the body. [02:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.21 [08:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.21 -->

**Context.** Having analysed the modes and described the state beyond them, Kṛṣṇa is now asked by Arjuna how one can transcend them `10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Liṅga_ — characteristic; it can also mean gender, which is itself a bodily characteristic by which a newborn's gender is known. Here it means internal characteristics `10:00 – 12:00`.
- _Ācāra_ — external characteristics: behaviour `12:00`.

#### Three questions, three answers [12:00]

1. What are the characteristics (_liṅga_) of one who has transcended the modes? — answered in 14.22–14.23.
2. What is such a person's behaviour (_ācāra_)? — answered in 14.24–14.25.
3. How does one transcend the modes? — answered in 14.26: by the practice of devotion.

#### A natural, appropriate question [12:00 – 14:00]

- When a child hears that someone topped an exam and won many laurels, the natural question is "How did he do it?" — the answer is regular, intelligent, disciplined study.
- Hearing of heroes and models of excellence, we naturally think, "Maybe I can become like that."
- Implied in Arjuna's question: can he, on the battlefield, transcend the modes?
- The question shows Arjuna is seriously contemplating what Kṛṣṇa says; Prabhupāda calls his questions "very appropriate" — relevant, intelligent and important.
- Prabhupāda combines 14.22–14.25 and gives one purport for all four `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's three questions — symptoms, behaviour, method — show a serious student turning what he hears into a personal aspiration: "How can I, here and now, go beyond the modes?" [10:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.22 – 14.23 [14:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.22 -->

<!-- vedabase:14.23 -->

**Context.** The internal characteristics of one beyond the modes: when illumination, attachment to action, or illusion arises, he does not resent it, and when it ceases he does not long for it; he remains situated as if detached, knowing that only the modes are acting `16:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prakāśa_ — illumination; represents goodness, as used in 14.11 `16:00`.
- _Pravṛtti_ — passionate action according to one's inclinations; represents passion, as used in 14.12 `16:00`.
- _Moha_ — illusion; represents ignorance `16:00`.
- _Kāma-kāmī_ — one who is a desirer of desires `22:00`.

#### One word for each mode [16:00 – 18:00]

- Kṛṣṇa uses one word to represent each mode: feeling illuminated, feeling hyperactive ("do this, do this"), or feeling deluded ("why bother about all this").
- When any of these comes, the transcendentalist does not resent it; when it goes, he does not crave it.
- Kṛṣṇa uses almost the same phrase for Himself in 9.9 — situated as if detached; as the Supersoul is situated detached, so the soul can become situated detached `18:00`.

#### Become observers of our own thoughts [18:00 – 22:00]

- We naturally observe the outer world — if a door opens, we see who comes in or goes out. Kṛṣṇa asks us to observe the inner world as well.
- The inner world is not all spiritual: within us there is the mental and there is the spiritual; whatever is internal is not necessarily spiritual.
- When lust, greed or anger come, they are within us but not necessarily ours — just as not everyone in our house is family: there may be strangers, trespassers, thieves.
- Understanding "I am a soul, different from the body" enables dispassionate observation: "this thought came in, this thought went out."
- Lust gains control only when we get excited about it. Temptations may come, but we don't have to succumb; when we say no, they cannot stay long, and over time they stop visiting `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### How to observe: cultivate goodness [24:00]

- In passion we are too caught up in the external world, and even there we observe only what promises enjoyment.
- In goodness we start observing the external world more fully and learning from it.
- Just as we observe externally, we can train ourselves to observe internally — identifying which thoughts are intruders to be neglected and which are our own, to be cultivated and nourished.

#### Journaling and retrospection [24:00 – 30:00]

- One practical way: keep a journal. After getting angry or succumbing to an _anartha_, ask: "What was my thought process?"
- Not to beat ourselves down — "I'm so fallen" serves no purpose.
- The wings analogy:
  - The principles of _bhakti_ are meant to give us wings to fly, not to weigh us down with guilt.
  - A person suddenly given wings might feel them as extra weight; but once he learns to use them, they let him fly — without wings the whole body is the burden.
  - The teacher mentions a figure who fell when his wings were cut.
- Analyse the fall-down sequence: "First this thought came, then this temptation; at this point I could have chosen a different reaction — this is where I can change."
- Merely knowing "I shouldn't have done it" is of little use; we must catch how the mind deceives us. The mind normally acts as our enemy and can become our friend when purified, but friend or enemy, it is there and must be dealt with `28:00 – 30:00`.
- If not during action, then in retrospection; and gradually, during action too `30:00`.

#### Conscious of our consciousness [30:00 – 32:00]

- While speaking, the teacher is conscious of what he says and, at a subconscious level, of what comes next in the flow of the chapter.
- We are simultaneously aware of the fan overhead, the comfort of the chair, a pain in the back — we can be conscious of many things at once.
- So while acting we can be conscious of our thoughts — "becoming conscious of our consciousness": "Now I'm feeling sleepy; this is the mode of ignorance trying to catch me — I won't let myself be caught."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The one beyond the modes watches them come and go without craving or resenting them. We can begin by becoming observers of our own thoughts — in retrospection through journaling, and gradually in the moment — treating intruding impulses as visitors, not as ourselves. [16:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.24 – 14.25 [32:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.24 -->

<!-- vedabase:14.25 -->

**Context.** The behaviour by which one can infer who has transcended the modes: equal in happiness and distress, situated in the self, seeing a stone and gold alike, equal toward the favourable and unfavourable, praise and criticism, honour and dishonour, friend and enemy `32:00 – 36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sva-stha_ — situated in one's own nature `32:00`.
- _Nindā_ — criticism; _stuti_ — praise; these concern what others do `32:00 – 34:00`.
- _Māna_ — honour; _apamāna_ — dishonour; these concern how we perceive it `34:00`.

#### Praise is not the same as honour [34:00]

- Generally praise brings a feeling of honour and criticism a feeling of dishonour, but they are not the same.
- If we expect more praise than we get, even praise feels like an insult — e.g. praising a PhD holder for passing school exams; in a place where people are not even educated, that might be real praise.
- The transcendentalist is equal in both pairs — in what others say and in how he perceives it.

#### Equal to all [34:00 – 36:00]

- Kṛṣṇa spoke earlier of equality toward friends and enemies, especially in 6.9, where He describes being equal toward all kinds of people; here too one is equal toward all parties.
- The common denominator of all these characteristics: being equipoised and calm — not getting excited by material things, knowing that beyond matter is spirit, and that spiritual reality is ultimate while material reality is temporary.

#### Beyond material dualities [36:00 – 38:00]

- All material varieties — good and bad, attractive and unattractive — are dualities created by material nature, so they no longer attract one who is transcendental; one who wants to become transcendental should minimise his attraction to them.
- Kṛṣṇa does not describe transcendental variety here, because the topic is the three modes, which create material varieties.
- Prabhupāda writes that such a person neither desires to enjoy the body nor desires to get out of it; social and political issues do not affect him, because he knows they are temporary upheavals and disturbances `38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Outwardly, the one beyond the modes is equipoised — unmoved by praise or blame, gain or loss, friend or foe — because he sees all material dualities as temporary products of nature and spirit as the ultimate reality. [32:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.26 [38:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.26 -->

**Context.** Answering how one transcends the modes: one who engages in unfailing devotional service transcends the modes and comes to the Brahman platform `38:00 – 40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vyabhicāra_ — often used for illicit relationships outside marriage (in the context of the regulative principles); here it means distraction — temptations that pull one from the right path toward the wrong path `38:00`.
- _Avyabhicāriṇī_ — unfailing; undistracted `40:00`.
- _Ahaitukī_ — unmotivated `40:00`.
- _Bhakti-yoga_ — the teacher notes this is the only verse in the Gītā where Kṛṣṇa uses _bhakti_ and _yoga_ together as one word, though each is used many times separately `40:00`.
- Object and objective — the object is what we meditate on; the objective is the goal of that meditation `44:00`.

#### Unfailing is not the same as unmotivated [40:00]

- _Ahaitukī_ concerns intention; _avyabhicāriṇī_ concerns execution.
- One can be unfailing in devotion without being unmotivated.

#### The jñānī practising bhakti [40:00 – 44:00]

- A _jñānī_ seeking to transcend the modes recognises he must take shelter of the Supreme Being who is beyond them, and so practises _bhakti-yoga_.
- Mixed devotion was discussed earlier in proportions:
  - Pure devotion — 100% _bhakti_.
  - One kind — the other processes are more than 50%.
  - Another kind — _bhakti_ is more than 50%.
- This chapter is in the _jñāna_ section of the Gītā; most verses describe a _jñānī_ seriously practising _jñāna-yoga_ — philosophical discrimination — who understands that _bhakti_ is required to go beyond the modes.
- Kṛṣṇa said the same among the items of knowledge in 13.8–13.12 — practising devotional service without fail — using the same word `42:00`.
- To the extent the _jñānī_ practises devotional service seriously, he advances spiritually.
- For the devotee Kṛṣṇa is both the object and the objective of meditation; for the _jñānī_ Kṛṣṇa may be the object and _bhakti_ a tool, while the goal may be impersonal Brahman — yet even he must practise _bhakti_, possibly uninterruptedly `44:00`.

#### Isn't this self-defeating? [44:00 – 46:00]

- The modes interrupt our _bhakti_, yet to go beyond the modes we must practise _bhakti_ without interruption — where does it begin?
- Like a disease caused by germs, cured by taking medicine: we begin by practising _bhakti_ to whatever capacity we can, and rise upward until we practise purely and seriously — gradually, subtly but surely returning to Kṛṣṇa `46:00`.

#### Use the freedom we have [46:00 – 50:00]

- The modes don't shackle us completely; they leave a little freedom, and we use and expand it.
- The alarm example: we cannot control whether we feel fresh on waking, but if we have had adequate rest we can push ourselves up, sprinkle water on our face, and soon feel fresher.
- The fractured hand: a champion boxer who once knocked people out now cannot lift his hand. The doctor says, "Raise it this much; now a little more; move it fully, but don't punch yet." Thinking "it's all lost" keeps it lost; using whatever capacity one has brings it back.
- Likewise, we may not yet practise unbreakable devotional service, but we can practise with as few breaks and as much consistency as possible, and eventually come to pure, uninterrupted, unmotivated devotion `48:00`.
- _Jñānīs_ who practise devotional service without offending Kṛṣṇa attain Brahman, as the next verse says `48:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's purport [48:00 – 50:00]

- All forms of Kṛṣṇa are fully transcendental, blissful, full of knowledge and eternal; one can meditate on and serve any of them.
- The living entities are part and parcel of the Supreme as gold particles are part of a gold mine — the same in quality, different in quantity.
- To be the personal attendant of a king one must acquire the qualifications; to serve the transcendental Lord one must become transcendental `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Unfailing devotional service is the way beyond the modes. It begins wherever we are: using whatever freedom the modes leave us, practising with as much consistency as we can, we gradually rise to pure, uninterrupted devotion. [40:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 14.27 [50:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:14.27 -->

**Context.** The last verse of the chapter: Kṛṣṇa declares that He is the foundation of Brahman, which is immortal, imperishable, eternal and the platform of ultimate happiness `50:00 – 52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pratiṣṭhā_ — often means honour or prestige, but here it means foundation: Kṛṣṇa is the foundation of Brahman `52:00`.
- Brahman — non-differentiated; an emanation from the Supreme Lord `52:00 – 54:00`.
- _Brahmānanda_ — the happiness of Brahman `54:00`.

#### Bhagavān is the source [52:00 – 54:00]

- Brahman is supported by Bhagavān — it emanates from Him; Brahman is not the source, Bhagavān is.
- The teacher cites a scriptural description of the Lord's effulgence from which countless universes come — the undivided, transcendental Brahman.
- Kṛṣṇa puts "Brahman" and "I" together in one verse and gives their relationship. Arjuna earlier called Him the Supreme Brahman.
- The Māyāvāda idea is that Brahman is the higher reality and the personal Lord the lower; Kṛṣṇa says the opposite — "I am the higher" `54:00`.

#### A hierarchy of happiness [54:00 – 56:00]

- An Upaniṣad describes levels of happiness — earthly, heavenly, of still higher planets, and ultimately transcendental happiness.
- A later devotional work, based on scripture, extends this: far greater than _brahmānanda_ is the happiness of devotional service.
- Why greater? It fulfils the heart's longing for love in the transcendental realm and gives an arena for our emotions. Brahman happiness is a happiness of negation — disentanglement from agitating material emotions — and is not the greatest happiness.

#### The billionaire with amnesia [56:00]

- A billionaire enjoys being one by acting at that level — the car, the house, the power.
- If he gets amnesia and forgets he is a billionaire, it is as if he is not one at all.
- Realising "I am Brahman" is good — it is the platform of happiness — but on that platform one must act, and action on the spiritual platform is serving Kṛṣṇa.

#### The world's greatest stage [56:00 – 58:00]

- If I get a stage where the world's top performers have performed — Broadway in America, or a famous Indian stage — I don't stand mutely on it; I perform.
- Likewise, coming to the spiritual platform is good, but there we must act, and that action is devotional service; the spiritual platform is the arena on which we perform devotional service.

#### Prabhupāda's purport [58:00 – 60:00]

- Attainment of the Brahman conception is the first stage of self-realisation; realisation of eternity and imperishability is already included in devotional service.
- Following devotional service, one becomes detached and situated in Brahman, equal to the Supreme Personality of Godhead in quality.
- We already have qualitative oneness, but our awareness of it does not stay with us — though we are spirit now, we think of ourselves as matter. When purified, we realise we are spirit `60:00`.

#### Summary of Chapter 14 [60:00]

- In response to Arjuna's question, Kṛṣṇa describes devotional service as the process, and indicates that it should not be practised merely as a tool, since beyond the Brahman platform is Kṛṣṇa — eternal, the support of Brahman.
- By analysing how the living entity is entangled in the three modes and why disentanglement is necessary, one takes shelter of devotional service as the best way out — and goes not just to Brahman but to its support, Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is the foundation of Brahman, so Brahman realisation is only the first stage: the spiritual platform is a stage on which to act, and the soul's real happiness is in performing devotional service there. [52:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
