**Context.** Arjuna continues and clarifies his question: what happens to a person trying to progress on the transcendental path who becomes deviated in mind and is unsuccessful in yoga? `04:00`

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Deviated / unsuccessful** — the person whose mind deviates and who does not reach success on the path of yoga `04:00`.
- **Without foundation** — without any base or position to stand on `06:00`.

#### The first point and the next result [04:00]

- The first, most important point is that the person's mind has deviated and he has been unsuccessful on the path of yoga.
- Arjuna's question is: what is the next result of that deviation?

#### The cloud blown apart [06:00 – 08:00]

- The teacher illustrates Arjuna's image: one cloud breaks from its formation, wanting to move across to another cloud formation, but is blown away before it can reach from the first to the second.
- Similarly a group of people who leave one path (such as the path of _karma_) without reaching the other are left with no shelter anywhere — lost completely `06:00 – 08:00`.

#### Not an academic question [08:00 – 12:00]

- Arjuna asks intensely; it is a serious question because it concerns our future and our future practice `08:00`.
- This sequence of verses is in question format, and the teacher notes it is among the longest questions Arjuna asks `10:00`.
- The teacher connects its intensity to 2.7 – 2.8, where Arjuna surrenders and asks Kṛṣṇa for instruction: apart from Kṛṣṇa he sees no one else who can remove his problem; likewise here only Kṛṣṇa can dispel the doubt `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's fear for the unsuccessful yogī — a cloud torn from one formation and blown away before reaching another — is the practitioner's own question about his future, and he asks it of the only one who can answer it, Kṛṣṇa. [04:00 – 10:00]

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa answers that such a person meets no destruction, neither in this world nor in the next: one who does what is auspicious never comes to a bad end `12:00`.

### Bhagavad-gita 6.41 [20:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.41 -->

**Context.** The unsuccessful yogī, after enjoying in the heavenly planets, takes birth in a family of the pious and the prosperous `20:00 – 26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Gṛha_ — "in the house" `20:00`.
- _Śrī_ — the goddess of fortune, who takes care of the family; hence a prosperous family `20:00`.

#### Why such a birth helps [20:00 – 24:00]

- In this world people get distracted from spiritual life for many reasons. Among the primary ones:
  - **Upbringing / circumstances** — an environment that is very unconducive, with pressure against spiritual practice `20:00`.
  - **Struggle for maintenance** — people who would otherwise be spiritually inclined are so caught up in struggling to maintain the body that practice becomes difficult `22:00`.
- So the obstacles are either internal (mental obstacles of the soul) or external (problems in maintaining the body) `22:00`.
- Born in such a family, the person gets material prosperity that does not distract him and he can continue his practice `24:00`.

#### Who goes to heaven first [24:00 – 28:00]

- The teacher says this refers to those who have made a little advancement but also have desires to enjoy heavenly pleasures `24:00`.
- It is not mere self-indulgence; it is enjoyment along with the practice of _dharma_ — but attraction to that lower taste makes the person give up the path `24:00`.
- So the person is given that facility: taken to heaven, he enjoys there and then comes back, born in an environment favourable for practice `26:00`.
- The universe's planets are primarily meant for exhausting _karma_ acquired here on earth — in higher planets or lower planets — and in this way one moves on `26:00 – 28:00`.
- He then continues spiritual practice until completely purified, to go back to the spiritual world `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The unsuccessful yogī who still desired enjoyment gets heavenly pleasures and then a pious, prosperous birth — removing both the inner and the outer obstacles of an unconducive upbringing and the struggle for maintenance — so that he can resume practice. [20:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.42 [28:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.42 -->

**Context.** Alternatively, the person takes birth in a family of transcendentalists (yogīs) — a birth the teacher describes as especially auspicious `28:00 – 30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Devotional family** — a family whose atmosphere itself supports spiritual advancement `30:00`, `40:00`.
- **Yoga** — connection; spiritual advancement is the revival of that connection `40:00`.

#### The auspiciousness of a devotional family [30:00]

- In such a family the auspiciousness begins even before birth: what nourishes the embryo in the womb is _prasāda_, and the milk the infant receives is also _prasāda_.
- This is a very special facility for the soul inside to practise spiritual life — given by the grace of the Lord.

#### Don't speculate about past-life yogīs [32:00 – 40:00]

- The teacher cautions against claims that a particular person "was a yogī in a previous life who could not complete his practice" `38:00`.
- Example of such propaganda: a child plays at being in a Kṛṣṇa temple, and people immediately say he must have been a yogī `38:00`.
- Advancement is not shown by such speculation — or by supernatural claims and visions — but by commitment to practice, depth of understanding and purity of heart `34:00 – 36:00`.
- Inheritance example: a child may inherit millions that came to the father by his own hard work; what is simply given is different from what is earned and accumulated `34:00`.
- Being born in a devotional family is certainly an advantage for spiritual advancement, but beyond that we need not speculate; we can see a person's commitment and advancement `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Birth in a devotional family — where even the embryo is nourished by prasāda — is a special facility given by the Lord's grace; yet a person's advancement should be judged by commitment, understanding and purity of heart, not by speculation about past lives. [30:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.43 [42:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.43 -->

**Context.** In the new birth the person's previous spiritual attraction is reactivated and he again strives for advancement `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Subtle body** — what carries one's spiritual impressions from one life to another `42:00 – 44:00`.
- **Revival** — the reawakening of previous spiritual practice `44:00`.

#### How the previous practice carries over [42:00 – 44:00]

- Reactivated spiritual attractions continue from one life to the next.
- The subtle body becomes more and more purified with spiritual advancement, and it stays with the soul from one life to another.
- These impressions are not stored in physical form or connected to the physical body; they are stored within the subtle body, which is why they can be revived.

#### Revival as Kṛṣṇa's opportunity [44:00]

- Once revived, one starts striving once again.
- The important point is that this revival is an opportunity Kṛṣṇa gives.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Spiritual progress is never lost because it is carried in the subtle body, not the physical one; in the next life Kṛṣṇa gives the opportunity for that practice to be revived. [42:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.44 [46:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.44 -->

**Context.** By virtue of previous practice, such a person is automatically — even helplessly — attracted to the spiritual path `46:00 – 48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Previous practice** — the force that draws the person, the theme of this verse `46:00`.
- **Helplessly attracted** — drawn without deliberate effort `48:00`.

#### Why the attraction comes [48:00 – 50:00]

- It happens because one is not satisfied with the material; we want something higher, something greater.
- In some cases, like Gajendra, the attraction to the Lord comes in a moment of crisis `48:00 – 50:00`.
- In some cases specific knowledge comes; in other cases it may not, but the attraction comes otherwise `50:00`.
- The Supersoul directs one toward Kṛṣṇa, and one advances in Kṛṣṇa consciousness `50:00`.

#### Chanting the holy names [52:00 – 54:00]

- The teacher refers to a verse about those who have the holy name on the tongue, explaining that such persons have come to the level of chanting the holy names of Kṛṣṇa through previous spiritual activity `52:00`.
- The practice of _bhakti-yoga_ is a superset that includes everything `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Previous practice creates a helpless attraction to the spiritual — born of dissatisfaction with matter, sometimes awakened by crisis as with Gajendra — and the Supersoul guides such a soul onward to Kṛṣṇa and the chanting of His names. [46:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
