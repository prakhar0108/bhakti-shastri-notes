## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 6.1 [02:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.1 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa opens the chapter by saying that one who does his prescribed duty without taking shelter of its fruit is both a renunciate and a yogī — not one who simply stops working `02:00 – 08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Not taking shelter of the fruit of work — not making the fruit one's motivation `04:00`.
- Prescribed duty — "this is my obligatory duty; I'll do it" `06:00`.
- _Sannyāsī_ and _yogī_ — the renunciate and the yogī; here used together for one person `06:00`.
- Not lighting the fire / not doing activity — the two terms Kṛṣṇa uses for one who gives up work; such a person is not the real yogī or _sannyāsī_ `08:00`.

#### Taking shelter of the fruit [04:00]

- People need motivation for work, and the usual motivation is the fruit.
- **The World Cup captain** — asked what motivated him, a winning cricket captain says, "Since childhood I imagined myself holding up the World Cup." That is taking shelter of the fruit.
- The Gītā is not against achievements, but if outer achievement alone is our motivation, we make no inner, spiritual development and do not necessarily come closer to Kṛṣṇa.
- A pure devotee may visualise things to be offered in Kṛṣṇa's service — that is different. The _karma-yogī_ focuses on detaching from worldly things; as discussed in 5.11, the yogī works primarily for purification, not for the fruit `04:00`.

#### Renunciate and yogī in one person [06:00 – 08:00]

- Kṛṣṇa suddenly uses _sannyāsa_ and _yoga_ together for one person.
- Earlier the two were presented differently:
  - **Chapter 3** — almost as opposite paths.
  - **Chapter 5** — as different paths leading to the same destination, contrasting one who tries renunciation alone, without yoga, with one who practises yoga and attains perfection soon.
- Now Kṛṣṇa says the two are essentially the same: _sannyāsa_ is disconnection from the material world, and _yoga_ is connection with the Absolute Truth; one way to achieve both is by doing one's work `08:00`.

#### Don't give up lighting the fire [08:00]

- Among prescribed duties, a prominent one for a householder living in the world is lighting the sacrificial fire so that _yajña_ can be performed.
- Kṛṣṇa discussed doing one's duty at the opening of the third chapter; right at the start of the sixth He says one should not give up such duties in the name of renunciation.

#### Prabhupāda's devotional explanation [10:00 – 12:00]

- **Limbs and body** — the parts of the body work for the satisfaction of the whole body, not for self-satisfaction. Likewise, the living entity who acts for the satisfaction of the Supreme Whole, not for personal satisfaction, is the perfect yogī.
  - A _yogī_ — because he is connected with the complete whole.
  - A _sannyāsī_ — because he is free from selfish desire.
- Prabhupāda then quotes the prayer of the one he calls the highest perfectional symbol of renunciation `10:00 – 12:00`:
  - "I do not want" what people generally define as success — wealth, followers, the enjoyable association of the opposite sex, and fame or learning. This first part is _sannyāsa_.
  - "I want unmotivated devotion" — devotion not conditional on what the Lord does for me; selfless and pure. This is _yoga_.
- This prayer exemplifies being disconnected from the world and connected to the Absolute Truth `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The real renunciate and the real yogī are the same person — one who keeps doing his duty but takes no shelter of its fruit, acting, like a limb for the body, for the satisfaction of the Supreme Whole. [04:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.2 [12:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.2 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa states that what is called renunciation is the same as yoga, because no one can be a yogī without giving up the desire for sense enjoyment `12:00 – 14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Saṅkalpa_ — desire, here the desire for worldly enjoyment; one who has not given it up cannot become a yogī `14:00`.
- _Sannyāsa_ — disconnection from the world; _yoga_ — connection with the Lord `16:00`.

#### Similar in content, not just destination [14:00]

- In the fifth chapter Kṛṣṇa spoke of a similarity in _destination_; now He speaks of a similarity in _content_.
- There cannot be authentic yoga — genuine connection to the Lord — unless there is disconnection from the enjoyments that take us away from Him.
- **Fire and darkness** — we cannot remove darkness unless we light a fire, and when we light the fire, darkness automatically goes. Where there is yoga, _sannyāsa_ will be there; without _sannyāsa_, not much real yoga is happening.

#### Chanting and detachment [14:00 – 16:00]

- If a person chants Hare Kṛṣṇa but is still engaged in all kinds of sense gratification — especially immoral, sinful gratification — that person's devotion is only external.
- Chanting is good and will help; if one chants sincerely, it should lead to detachment. The time taken differs from person to person, but there will be progress in that direction.

#### Combining sannyāsa and yoga [16:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that we simply act according to our constitutional position: the real aim is to give up all selfish satisfaction and be prepared to satisfy the Supreme.
- We work not for our pleasure but for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure — and in that way _sannyāsa_ and _yoga_ become combined.

#### Person and path: 6.1 and 6.2 compared [18:00 – 20:00]

- **6.1 focuses on the person** — an affirmative assertion: what the person _does_ (acts without taking shelter of the fruits).
- **6.2 focuses on the path** — a negative assertion: what both _sannyāsa_ and _yoga_ require in common, the giving up of desire for enjoyment.
- By a specific example and then a generic path, Kṛṣṇa makes clear that _sannyāsa_ and _yoga_ are identical.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Renunciation and yoga are one: just as lighting a fire automatically removes darkness, genuine connection with the Lord requires — and brings — disconnection from selfish enjoyment. [14:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.3 [16:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.3 -->

**Context.** Before elaborating, Kṛṣṇa introduces technical terminology: for the beginner work is the means, and for the advanced yogī the cessation of activity is the means `16:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- The beginning stage — one who is just starting to climb the yoga ladder; here work itself leads to advancement `18:00`.
- The advanced stage — one situated in yoga; here the cessation of activity leads to advancement `18:00`.
- Cessation — inactivity, pacification, calmness, cessation of activities `16:00`.
- _Pratyāhāra_ — the opposite of _āhāra_ (food): starving the senses `22:00`.

#### The structure of advancement [18:00]

- Yes, there is renunciation of activity — but that is the advanced stage.
- First we regulate our physical activities; this can be practised while living and working in the world.
- When one goes to the level of shutting out the sense objects, one cannot stay in the world; one renounces it and turns inward.

#### The eight stages of aṣṭāṅga-yoga [20:00 – 22:00]

- **_Yama_ and _niyama_** — rules for individual and social discipline, sometimes said to be do's and don'ts; either way, we regulate ourselves.
- **_Āsana_** — sitting in a particular posture that helps consciousness move inward.
- **_Prāṇāyāma_** — regulating the breath: when we are agitated the breath becomes fast; when it slows, the mind becomes calm.
- **_Pratyāhāra_** — the senses each have their own "food": the eyes want good-looking objects, the tongue delicious tastes, the ears melodious sounds. _Pratyāhāra_ is the opposite of feeding — starving the senses — by going to a secluded place, such as a forest, where no sense gratification is available.
- The later stages — moving progressively inward, to be discussed in later classes.
- The initial stages can be practised while doing _karma-yoga_ — the beginning stage; the next four involve rising to the advanced stage `18:00`.

#### What "giving up work" means [22:00 – 24:00]

- Even a yogī in the jungle must do basic activity — eating, bathing.
- Giving up work here means not doing duties within the _varṇāśrama_ system; the focus is on renouncing the world in order to connect with the Absolute Truth.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Yoga has a structure: in the beginning, regulated work in the world purifies; only when one rises to shutting out the senses does cessation of work become the means of advancement. [16:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.4 [24:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.4 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains what situates a person at the advanced stage of yoga `24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Not engaged in sense objects — not engaging in direct sense gratification `24:00`.
- Not engaged in fruitive activities — which would later provide sense gratification `24:00`.
- Renouncing all desires — giving up internally even the desire for sense gratification `24:00`.

#### Three levels of renunciation [24:00 – 26:00]

1. **Direct sense gratification** — not engaging in it.
2. **Fruitive activity that leads to sense gratification** — not pursuing it.
3. **The desire for sense gratification** — not even desiring it; this is a serious stage of renunciation.
- One who doesn't engage, doesn't pursue and doesn't desire is situated in yoga `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The advanced yogī renounces at three levels — enjoying, working to enjoy, and even desiring to enjoy; only the last makes renunciation complete. [24:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.5 [26:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.5 -->

**Context.** Having described the advanced stage, Kṛṣṇa names its main obstacle — the mind. The whole process of yoga is largely centred on controlling the mind, and this and the next verses show how to reach the advanced stage by bringing it under control `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ātmā_ — used several times in the verse: sometimes for the soul, sometimes for the mind `26:00`.
- Elevate — to raise up, deliver, liberate `26:00`.
- Degrade — to push down `26:00`.
- _Bandhu_ — friend; the mind is the friend of the soul, and also its enemy `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Body, mind and soul [28:00 – 30:00]

- In the conditioned stage, the mind is closely connected to the body: where we are physically largely determines where we are mentally. If we sit doing nothing physically, the mind becomes idle.
- Physical exposure to sense objects excites the mind; physical removal reduces the excitement — though if the mind is very captivated, it may stay excited.
- There are two connections — mind with body, and mind with soul. This verse is about the mind's connection with the soul.

#### The mind as a door [30:00 – 32:00]

- **The door in a dark room** — if the door opens, light streams in and I can see outside. Suppose there are three doors, each showing a different pathway, and only one can be open at a time; I cannot absolutely determine which, but I have some control.
- The door of consciousness is the mind: whatever it opens to is the reality we perceive, and we become blind to the rest.
- **Materialists in the temple** — some people visit the temple and see only expensive marble, chandeliers and good-looking people; they practically don't even notice the Deities, because their mind is open only to material things `30:00 – 32:00`.
- When the soul becomes conscious of material things, desires are aroused and bind the soul to the body and, by extension, to the world.
- The door can open toward Kṛṣṇa — devotees, the Gītā, the holy name — or toward sense objects; we have a choice, and the results follow accordingly `32:00`.

#### Bondage and liberation by the mind [32:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes a verse: the mind attached to sense objects causes bondage; the mind free from obsession with them leads to liberation `32:00 – 34:00`.
- Freeing the mind requires effort, and ultimately the mind must be fixed on Kṛṣṇa; He will say so repeatedly in later chapters, and in 6.47 the topmost yogī is one who fixes his mind on Him `34:00`.
- At this point in the Gītā's progression, the positive spiritual reality has not yet been revealed; Kṛṣṇa mainly says material reality is the source of bondage, so don't let the mind fix on sense objects. Once Kṛṣṇa is known as the positive reality, the mind freed from sense objects is fixed on Him `34:00 – 36:00`.

#### Conscious, consistent cultivation [36:00 – 40:00]

- Do we have control over what the mind fixes on? Not absolute control, but significant control.
- **The driver** — a car by default drifts off track; the trained driver keeps bringing it back. We may not stop the mind from going off track, but we can bring it back through _conscious consistent cultivation_ `36:00`.
  - **Conscious** — if we drive lost in our thoughts, we crash, become truly unconscious, and wake up asking "Why did I do that? What happened to me?" `36:00 – 38:00`.
  - **Consistent** — not alert today and asleep tomorrow; in war the enemy launches its most ferocious attack when the other side lowers its guard, and two soldiers facing each other — one moment of looking away can spell destruction `38:00 – 40:00`.
  - **Cultivation** — of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, of fixing the mind on Kṛṣṇa `40:00`.
- **The greatest threat to individual freedom is the individual** — people dread dictators and tyrants who violate liberty, but we ourselves act in ways that hurt us and cost us our freedom `38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The mind is the door of consciousness: we cannot stop it from drifting, but by conscious, consistent cultivation we can keep turning it back toward Kṛṣṇa — and so use it to elevate rather than degrade the soul. [28:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.6 [40:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.6 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains how the mind can be both friend and enemy: it depends on who is in control `40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Conquered mind — when the soul controls the mind, the mind is the friend `40:00`.
- Unconquered mind — when the mind is in control, it acts as the enemy `40:00`.

#### Two readings of ātmā [42:00 – 44:00]

- The verse makes best sense when _ātmā_ refers to the mind.
- If _ātmā_ is taken as the self throughout — "the self is the friend of the self, and the self is the enemy of the self" — some commentators speak of a lower self and a higher self:
  - **Higher self** — what we essentially are and can become: the pure soul, part of Kṛṣṇa, meant to act in selfless love.
  - **Lower self** — what we presently misidentify with: our body, attachments, misconceptions and prejudices.
  - There is a conflict between the two; if the higher self wins we are elevated, if the lower, degraded.
- Both readings can be used; Prabhupāda keeps things simple for us — the mind is the enemy, the mind is the friend `44:00`.

#### Horses and chariot [44:00]

- If the horses are in control, they go here and there; if they are controlled, they carry the chariot far faster than one could run on foot.
- Living in the material world we need the mind and senses to act; we cannot wish them away or reject them — we must regulate them.

#### Serving Kṛṣṇa or serving māyā [44:00 – 48:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport focuses on following a superior dictation: the constitutional position of the living entity is to carry out the order of the superior `44:00`.
- As long as the mind is unconquered, one must serve the dictations of lust, anger, avarice (greed) and illusion; when the mind is conquered, one voluntarily abides by the dictation of the Lord in the heart as _Paramātmā_ `44:00 – 46:00`.
- We are always servants — of Kṛṣṇa or of _māyā_; there is no third choice. Otherwise our senses become "almighty": the tongue drags us to eat, the mind drags us to think `46:00`.
- "I won't follow sense gratification but I won't surrender to Kṛṣṇa either" may last a little while, but it is a metastable state; one generally falls `46:00`.
- **Gravity of consciousness** — as gravity drags objects down, by default our consciousness goes down toward sense gratification; unless we consciously pull ourselves up, we are pulled down. _Bhakti-yoga_ is the easiest way to move upward `46:00`.
- Most of the time we act as very surrendered servants of the mind — "watch this TV show" — and leave more responsible, fruitful and joyful activities. We trust the mind too much; we should learn to trust Kṛṣṇa, our well-wisher, whose instructions are in our best interest `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We are always servants — either of a conquered mind that obeys the Lord within, or of an unconquered mind that dictates lust, anger, greed and illusion; to stop obeying the mind, we must obey Kṛṣṇa. [44:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.7 [48:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.7 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the symptoms of one who has conquered the mind — when the mind becomes the friend `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Deeply peaceful — not just peaceful, but deeply so `48:00`.
- _Paramātmā_ — the transcendental Soul, the Supersoul; such a person approaches Him `48:00 – 50:00`.
- Dualities — at three levels, which such a person transcends `52:00`.

#### The first mention of Paramātmā [50:00]

- This is the first verse in the Gītā where the word _Paramātmā_ is used; the Absolute Truth was referred to before but not by this name.
- Later Kṛṣṇa will say who this _Paramātmā_ is — for example in **13.23**: the transcendental Person in this body, the overseer and permitter, the enjoyer and maintainer.

#### Turning toward the Supersoul [50:00 – 52:00]

- The Supersoul is right next to us, but the soul is turned away, looking outward at the material world, wanting to enjoy it.
- What drags the soul away is the mind, filled with fantasies of enjoyment.
- When the mind is controlled, that distracting force subsides, and we can turn toward Kṛṣṇa.
- The uncontrolled mind constantly agitates — "this is wrong, that is wrong, that's not proper"; controlled, it becomes peaceful, no longer falsely excited by hopes of worldly pleasure, and we turn from the outer world to the inner world and approach the Supersoul.

#### Dualities at three levels [52:00]

- **Body** — heat and cold.
- **Mind** — the mental dualities.
- **Ego** — honour and dishonour.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The uncontrolled mind keeps the soul facing outward; once it is conquered, its agitation subsides, the yogī becomes deeply peaceful, rises above bodily, mental and ego-based dualities, and turns toward the Supersoul beside him. [48:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.8 [52:00 – 63:41]

<!-- vedabase:6.8 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the vision of one who is situated in self-realisation: satisfied by knowledge and realisation, he sees pebbles, stones and gold equally `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jñāna_ and _vijñāna_ — theoretical knowledge and realised knowledge `52:00`, `54:00`.
- Satisfied — only one with both knowledge and realisation becomes satisfied `54:00`.
- Equal vision — toward pebbles, stones and gold `52:00`.
- _Vimukha_ and _unmukha_ — facing away from Kṛṣṇa, and facing toward Him `56:00`.

#### Equal vision toward gold and stone [54:00]

- Materially, gold is precious and stone practically valueless — so how can one see them equally?
- One understands that neither gold nor stone can make me, the soul, happy; both are material, and a spiritual being cannot be satisfied by anything material. So he becomes detached.

#### Book knowledge versus realisation [54:00 – 58:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that book knowledge without realisation of the Absolute Truth is useless — it brings no satisfaction, transformation or purification `54:00`.
- He quotes a famous verse from the _Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu_ `54:00 – 56:00`:
  - We are presently _vimukha_ — Kṛṣṇa is here, but our face is turned away.
  - By rendering service, we turn 180 degrees and become _unmukha_ — facing Him.
  - When we turn toward Kṛṣṇa, He by His own free will reveals Himself.
- **Swimming and the wave** — by service, chanting and conscious remembrance (_smaraṇam_, a limb of _sādhana-bhakti_) we are like a swimmer moving toward a destination by our own effort; when Kṛṣṇa is pleased, He sends a wave of devotional thought that propels us toward Him far more rapidly than our own effort could `56:00`.
- Without _vijñāna_, things stay theoretical — "I'm not the body," "spiritual happiness is greater" — but we don't feel it; then we are dragged toward sense gratification, and not only desires but also doubts come up `58:00`.
- Prabhupāda: by realised knowledge one remains steady in conviction; by mere academic knowledge one is easily deluded and confused by apparent contradictions `58:00`.
- We turn inward, toward Kṛṣṇa, by the practice of devotional service, and by that we move forward `58:00`.

#### Scholarship as gold [60:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that mundane scholarship and mental speculation, which may be as good as gold to others, are of no greater value to the devotee than pebbles or stones.
- PhDs require years of diligence and hard work and can be valued to that extent, but academic scholarship does not equate with spiritual advancement; mistaking it for knowledge misleads us.
- A devotee sees that one may have much knowledge yet no attraction to or love for Kṛṣṇa — and the purpose of knowledge is to learn to love Kṛṣṇa.

#### Three perspectives on gold and stone [60:00 – 62:00]

- **Materialist** — gold is precious; stone is not valuable at all.
- **Detached _jñānī_** — neither stone nor gold can make me happy, so I don't care for either.
- **Devotee** — whatever can be used in Kṛṣṇa's service is valuable.
- **Cement bags in Vṛndāvana** — when Prabhupāda's disciples were building the Vṛndāvana temple, cement was very hard to obtain. When the trucks finally came, Prabhupāda himself counted the sacks and supervised their careful transport and locking; the devotees felt he was treating cement bags like bags of gold — because the cement was essential for building Kṛṣṇa's temple `60:00 – 62:00`.
- For a devotee, neither gold nor stone matters for his own sake; he values their devotional utility and potential — the transcendental, devotional perspective `62:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Only realised knowledge — not mere book learning — satisfies the heart and steadies conviction; such a person sees gold and stone equally for himself, yet, as a devotee, values anything by its use in Kṛṣṇa's service. [52:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
