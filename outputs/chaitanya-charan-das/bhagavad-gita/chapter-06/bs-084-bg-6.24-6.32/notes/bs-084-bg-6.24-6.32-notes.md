## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 6.24 [00:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.24 -->

**Context.** Starting again from practice, Kṛṣṇa speaks of the determination needed to keep the mind and senses on track `02:00 – 08:00`.

#### Getting back on track [02:00]

- The nature of the mind and the nature of the world are such that they will take us off track; we bring ourselves back on track.
- That is the essential process of _bhakti-yoga_ as well.

#### The crack in the dam [04:00]

- A dam may be made of extremely strong stone, concrete and material, but if one crack appears and is not fixed, water slowly comes through, the crack widens, and eventually the whole dam can collapse.
- A little crack, left unfixed, can lead to a big crash.

#### Where temptation is triggered [06:00 – 08:00]

- The senses can take the mind in the wrong direction, and the mind can take the senses in the wrong direction; it depends on where the trigger of temptation comes from.
- **Outside to inside** — a sense object agitates the mind.
- **Inside to outside** — we remember something enjoyed before, imagine it internally, then the mind drives the senses towards the object.
- For a yogi in the forest the triggers are primarily internal, so one controls at the level of the mind and does not let the senses become active.
- We cannot avoid contact with sense objects in our environment; we deal with them by internal strength. Kṛṣṇa speaks of this downward fall in 2.62 as well `08:00`.

#### The sparrow and the ocean [10:00]

- Prabhupāda gives the story of a sparrow whose eggs were taken by the ocean. The ocean did not care, so the sparrow resolved to dry up the ocean.
- It was impossible, but the sparrow's determination attracted help — and the impossible became possible.

#### "God helps those who help themselves" [12:00 – 16:00]

- Freeing ourselves from conditioning and being reinstated in our original position may seem very difficult, but if we follow the principles the Lord surely helps.
- Prabhupāda quotes the common saying; but people who are materialistic usually emphasise "help yourself" — "don't just sit and pray" — so God's help becomes a sideshow.
- In principle it is God's help that decides the result, and it cannot be neglected.
- How do we help ourselves? By taking God's help — His help is already there, and we simply have to stop obstructing it.
- **Doctor and patient:** a doctor wants to treat the patient, but the patient must cooperate `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The mind and world will keep pulling us off track, and an unfixed crack can bring down a dam; but determined practice attracts the Lord's help — and the best way to help ourselves is to stop obstructing the help He is already giving. [02:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.25 [16:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.25 -->

**Context.** The teacher stresses that the progress Kṛṣṇa describes is gradual, and that it needs intelligence sustained by conviction `16:00`, `22:00`.

#### Intelligence sustained by conviction [16:00]

- We need conviction; without it the cure does not come.
- The curative potential is there, but if we don't practise consistently we don't get the cure — so the intelligence has to be sustained by conviction.

#### When practice feels insipid [18:00 – 20:00]

- Material reality is deceptive: it promises pleasure but gives misery.
- In practice we will sometimes find it insipid; still, "I will keep practising" — practise again and again, and give it time.

#### The fifth stage [22:00]

- Gradually withdrawing the senses by proper conviction is called _pratyāhāra_, the fifth stage of _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_, followed by concentration, meditation and _samādhi_ — the inner progression.
- Kṛṣṇa says to do this slowly.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Withdrawal of the senses happens gradually, sustained by conviction; even when practice feels insipid, we keep practising and give it time, for material pleasure promises much but gives misery. [16:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.26 [22:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.26 -->

**Context.** Whenever and wherever the restless, unsteady mind wanders, one must bring it back under the control of the self `22:00`.

#### The mind's nature [22:00]

- Sitting in the temple thinking of Kṛṣṇa, the mind may wander to sinful thoughts; the same applies whenever the mind goes after sense objects.
- Restlessness and unsteadiness are the nature of the mind; we cannot change its nature — but we can bring it back.

#### Gosvāmī and godāsa [24:00 – 26:00]

- Prabhupāda speaks of the difference between a _godāsa_ and a _gosvāmī_.
  - _Gosvāmī_ — one who controls the mind and senses; master of the senses.
  - _Godāsa_ — servant of the senses.
- Both face the battle; the difference is that one wins it and the other loses.
- This is not a permanent, unchangeable designation: we may be _godāsas_ now, but by practice we can become _gosvāmīs_ — the whole idea is to change our response.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We cannot change the mind's restless nature, but we can keep bringing it back; whether we are servants or masters of the senses is not fixed — it depends on how we respond in the battle. [22:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

<!-- vedabase:6.27 -->

**Context.** The yogi whose mind is fixed on Kṛṣṇa becomes peaceful and attains the highest happiness `28:00`.

#### Peace when passion is pacified [28:00 – 30:00]

- One becomes peaceful when passion is pacified; then consciousness no longer moves in that direction.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">

### Bhagavad-gita 6.28 [32:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.28 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa speaks of the yogi attaining ultimate happiness through contact with the spiritual reality `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Brahma-saṁsparśa_ — contact with the spiritual reality; Prabhupāda explains it as transcendental contact with the Supreme `32:00 – 34:00`.

#### Contact with the spiritual reality [32:00]

- It is not physical contact — e.g. going to the temple and touching the Deity's feet is not primarily what is meant; it means contact of consciousness with the spiritual level of reality.
- Kṛṣṇa is describing a different kind of enjoyment from the one that comes by contact of the senses with sense objects `34:00`.

#### The superlative happiness [32:00]

- _Ātyantikam sukham_, ultimate happiness, appears in the Gītā in this section — in 6.21 and here; it is the superlative, the highest happiness.

#### Brahman, Paramātmā, Bhagavān [32:00 – 34:00]

- In 6.28, 6.29 and 6.30 one can see a progressive level: Brahman realisation, Paramātmā realisation and then Bhagavān realisation `32:00 – 34:00`, `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Brahma-saṁsparśa is contact of consciousness with the spiritual reality, giving the superlative happiness that sense contact cannot — and it opens a progression from Brahman to Paramātmā to Bhagavān. [32:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.29 [36:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.29 -->

**Context.** The true yogi sees the Lord in all beings and all beings in the Lord, seeing everywhere equally `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Everything inside, and inside everything [36:00 – 38:00]

- At one level, how can something be inside everything and everything be inside it?
- From the Lord, Brahmā is born, and then the universe; and the Lord is present in each living being's heart.
- So the Lord is present inside everything, and everything is inside Him.

#### The basis of equal vision [38:00]

- Seeing everything equally is based on connection with the Lord: everything is ultimately a manifestation, at different levels, of the Supreme Lord's control and energy.
- Every living being is seen equally.

#### Paramātmā realisation [40:00 – 42:00]

- Everybody is present within the Supreme; this verse is Paramātmā realisation.
- The teacher cautions that this is not importing something absent from the Gītā — it is in the Gītā, coming a little later, so we understand both and see the levels of perfection.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord is in everything and everything is in the Lord; seeing all beings as manifestations of His energy gives the yogi equal vision — the vision of Paramātmā realisation. [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.30 [42:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.30 -->

**Context.** For one who sees Kṛṣṇa everywhere and everything in Him, Kṛṣṇa is never lost, nor is that person ever lost to Kṛṣṇa; 6.28 is Brahman realisation, 6.29 Paramātmā, and 6.30 makes Bhagavān clear `42:00`.

#### Never lost [42:00]

- "He is never lost to Me, and I am not lost to him" — there is no fall from the relationship.

#### Seeing Kṛṣṇa in the world [44:00 – 46:00]

- What about what the devotee sees in this world? Everything can be understood at multiple levels.
- Seeing the sky, the mountains and the rivers, one can think: this is Bhūmi, this is Kṛṣṇa's energy — seeing Kṛṣṇa even where He is not visibly present.

#### Eyes for Kṛṣṇa [48:00]

- Currently our eyes are diseased by materialism, so we see only material things.
- _Bhakti_ is a stage-wise progression; in the final stage — love, _prema_ — that vision comes, and one sees Kṛṣṇa everywhere.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The devotee who sees Kṛṣṇa everywhere and everything in Him is never lost to Kṛṣṇa, nor Kṛṣṇa to him; this vision of Bhagavān matures through the stages of bhakti up to prema. [42:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.31 [50:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.31 -->

**Context.** There may be confusion after 6.29: the Lord is in every living being — so is there a difference between seeing Him that way and worshipping Kṛṣṇa? Kṛṣṇa clarifies in the next verse `50:00`.

#### The personal is higher [50:00 – 52:00]

- The reality perceived in 6.29 — present in everyone — is the impersonal; the reality here is personal.
- One who becomes situated "in Me" personally understands that there is Bhagavān, higher than Brahman — Kṛṣṇa says this very clearly.
- One who is situated in Brahman attains Brahman realisation; one who worships Kṛṣṇa attains the higher.

#### Form and formless [52:00 – 54:00]

- The formless is a lower level; it is not necessary that something be formless to be absolute.
- The Absolute can have form, and can manifest in multiple — even unlimited — forms.

#### Fixing the mind on the Absolute in His form [56:00]

- The best way to reach the Absolute is to fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa — on the Absolute Truth in the form of Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Being situated in Kṛṣṇa personally is higher than realising His all-pervading aspect; the Absolute is not obliged to be formless — He can have unlimited forms, and fixing the mind on Kṛṣṇa's form is the best way to reach Him. [50:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.32 [56:00 – 61:30]

<!-- vedabase:6.32 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes this section by saying whom He considers the highest yogi — the teacher connects this with seeing everything everywhere in comparison with one's own self `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Comparison with one's own self [58:00]

- Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the way to happiness — not just for me, but for all.
- In the inner meditation one finds inner fulfilment, and then one wants to make others happy too.

#### Compassion: reaching out [58:00]

- Such a yogi shares, making a call of compassion; we reach out to others because we understand what actually helps them.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The highest yogi, having found inner fulfilment in Kṛṣṇa, does not keep it to himself; understanding that Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the way to happiness for all, he reaches out with compassion so others too can be happy. [56:00 – 61:30]
</Callout>
