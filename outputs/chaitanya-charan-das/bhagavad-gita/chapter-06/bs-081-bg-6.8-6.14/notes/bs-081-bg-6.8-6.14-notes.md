## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 6.8 [00:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.8 -->

**Context.** Continuing from the last class, the teacher explains how knowledge (_jñāna_) moves toward realisation (_vijñāna_), so that one becomes truly satisfied `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śravaṇa_ — hearing; knowledge enters the ears `00:00 – 02:00`.
- _Manana_ — contemplating; knowledge enters the head and becomes _jñāna_ `02:00`.
- _Nididhyāsana_ — applying and internalising; knowledge enters the heart and becomes _vijñāna_ `02:00`.
- _Tṛpta_ — satisfied; one with both knowledge and realisation becomes satisfied `10:00`.

#### From the ears to the head to the heart [02:00 – 04:00]

- These three stages are described in the Upaniṣads.
- We hear many things all day — casual talk on the road, news on TV — and most of it does not register, or soon fades.
- **Hearing like a hog** — Prabhupāda says one should not hear like a hog: it may have big ears, but it cannot understand philosophy. Hearing like a hog means staying in animal consciousness, not letting hearing raise, illumine, purify and transform us.

#### Contemplation confronts preconceptions [04:00 – 06:00]

- Knowledge connects the unknown with the known. What we "know" from childhood, movies and advertisements is that sense gratification leads to pleasure; scripture says it leads to misery.
- If we dismiss scripture — "religious people are spoilsports and killjoys" — serious consequences follow: the body grows old and diseased, and bad karma returns as sinful reactions. A wise person does not neglect scripture.
- When what we hear contradicts our preconceptions, we cannot wish them away; we must challenge them by conscious contemplation: are those who enjoy the senses happy? Most harm themselves — smoking, drinking, intoxication — and harm others, as when drunk drivers hurt people. Pleasure is temporary and followed by trouble `06:00`.
- Then knowledge does not go in one ear and out the other; it settles in the head `06:00`.

#### Intellectual versus emotional internalisation [06:00 – 10:00]

- Even after intellectual acceptance, feelings may still run toward sense gratification — "given the opportunity, I would pounce on it." That requires purification and greater realisation `06:00 – 08:00`.
- **The person who has never drunk** — having seen drunkards topple in the street, throw up, suffer hangovers, make fools of themselves and waste money, he has no illusion that drinking gives pleasure. There is no inner battle between thoughts and feelings — no temptation at all `08:00`.
- We may feel that way about alcohol but not, say, about sexual pleasure. As we advance, more and more material pleasures are exposed as sources of delusion and distress `08:00`.
- Realisation means accepting as reality what is reality: sense gratification leads to misery, so I won't indulge — not because I've been told not to, but because it is a path to misery, and the best way to live is in service to Kṛṣṇa `10:00`.
- With only _jñāna_, one hears "it leads to misery" but feels "it leads to happiness — why am I not allowed?" — so there is no satisfaction, only irritation and frustration `10:00`.

#### Application moves knowledge to the heart [10:00 – 14:00]

- From head to heart, knowledge moves when we apply it. Which comes first — application or conviction? Both ways: with whatever little conviction we have, we apply. An alcoholic may still feel the urge, but from enough experience decides, "nothing doing, I'm going to be sober" — determination first, and then one goes beyond `12:00`.
- **Four examples** — each gives knowledge that benefits only when applied `12:00 – 14:00`:
  - **A map** — tells how to go from Mumbai to Pune, but I must make the journey.
  - **A prescription** — tells which medicine cures my cough, but I must take it.
  - **A recipe** — tells how to make _rasgullā_, but I must cook it.
  - **A manual** — tells how to operate a machine, but I must press the buttons.
- Scriptural application is largely internal, so others may think we are applying it when we are not — and we may be satisfied with that. That should not happen `14:00`.

#### Desires and doubts: the two obstacles [14:00 – 18:00]

- What stops _jñāna_ from becoming _vijñāna_ `14:00`:
  - **Desires** — arise from the mind and entrap the _karmīs_: "there's so much enjoyment here, why go toward Kṛṣṇa?"
  - **Doubts** — arise from the intelligence and entrap the _jñānīs_: "is Kṛṣṇa for real? Is this spiritual business for real?"
- Kṛṣṇa often says, "offer your mind and intelligence to Me," because these comprise the subtle body, and wherever the subtle body goes, we eventually go `14:00`.
- We have the worst of both — some _karmī_ tendency and some _jñānī_ tendency `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### Science is mostly karma, not jñāna [16:00 – 18:00]

- Many think science is _jñāna_ because scientists are intellectuals with complex formulas. A few scientists do ponder the nature of reality, but most work on technology — manipulating matter to bend the world to our will — and manipulating matter is _karma_.
- **The rain sacrifice** — in the path of _karma_, a _yajña_ is performed to bring rain. Whether people today believe it or not, the principle is using a method to manipulate matter for a material result and material enjoyment; the process may be high-tech, but its essence is material control.
- So much scientific technology is used to promote sense gratification — the entertainment industry, and especially pornography, uses advanced technology to bring out the lowest desires `18:00`.
- Scientific education, mistaken for intellectual knowledge, brings doubts (does God exist? does the soul? is reincarnation real?) while its products fuel desires `18:00`.

#### Why doubts are more dangerous [18:00 – 20:00]

- Both are dangerous, but desires make us feel lowly ("I'm so impure"), while doubts make us feel superior ("these people are so sentimental; I'm intelligent"). Feeling superior, we find it hard to seek shelter and ask for help humbly `18:00`.
- Prabhupāda considered philosophical deviation far more dangerous than sensual deviation: a disciple who fell sensually he would still encourage, but he strongly cautioned about one who deviated philosophically `18:00 – 20:00`.
- Dealing with them `20:00`:
  - **Desires** — by chanting and purification.
  - **Doubts** — by chanting and purification too, plus systematic study, inquiry and patience; some doubts can be kept in suspension, judged neither true nor false.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge satisfies only when it travels from the ears (hearing) to the head (contemplation) to the heart (application and realisation); like a map or a prescription, scripture benefits only when applied, and desires and doubts — especially doubts — are what block that journey. [00:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.9 [20:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.9 -->

**Context.** Having described equal vision toward things in 6.8, Kṛṣṇa now describes a higher level: equal vision toward all kinds of people `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Suhṛt_ — a very close well-wisher; the word used for Kṛṣṇa in 5.29 `20:00`.
- _Mitra_ — friend; _ari_ — enemy `20:00`.
- _Udāsīna_ — the neutral, who doesn't take sides `20:00`.
- Mediator — one who tries to resolve conflict by acting as a channel between opposing factions `20:00 – 22:00`.
- _Bandhu_ — one who is a friend `22:00`.
- _Sādhu_ — saintly person; _pāpī_ — sinful person `22:00`.

#### Why equal vision toward people is higher [22:00]

- Seeing things equally is one level — this table, computer, door, floor and roof are all matter and none can make me happy.
- But things stay in one place, while people act: some are kind and smiling, some grim and frowning, some negligent, some loving and caring. Being equal toward all of them is far harder.

#### Connection with 5.18 [22:00 – 24:00]

- In 5.18 Kṛṣṇa described the yogī seeing all living beings equally, across a spectrum from the _brāhmaṇa_ in the mode of goodness at the top to those in ignorance at the bottom, including animals.
- Here Kṛṣṇa focuses within that spectrum on relationships — the variety of relationships we generally see among humans, though it can apply to other beings too: some animals are hostile, some friendly, some negligent.

#### Seeing people by their sense-gratification potential [24:00 – 26:00]

- At the material level of consciousness, we evaluate people by their potential to provide sense gratification:
  - A very good-looking person attracts everyone's glances — pleasure for the eyes — while an old, haggard person goes unnoticed.
  - A wealthy-looking visitor to an office gets everyone's attention — "maybe he'll give me some money or a bribe" — while someone in torn clothes or disabled is kept waiting in the queue.
- In 6.3–6.4 the advanced yogī gave up the desire for sense gratification, knowing it cannot make one happy; then one no longer evaluates people by that potential, and so can have equal vision `24:00 – 26:00`.
- Those with such vision can move forward and take to the renounced order — going out of the world, as the next verse describes `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We usually value people by how much sense gratification they can give us; one who has truly given up that desire can see friend and enemy, neutral and mediator, saint and sinner equally — a higher vision than seeing gold and stone alike. [20:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.10 [26:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.10 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa begins describing the yogī who goes to the forest: always engaging the whole self in yoga, in a secluded place, alone, controlled, free from desires and possessions `26:00 – 28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Oneself — here body, mind, soul, everything `26:00`.
- Secluded place — externally one is secluded; sometimes even a secluded place has five or six people chatting, so one also sits alone `26:00`.
- Controlled and concentrated — internally `26:00`.
- Without possessions — giving up material possessions `28:00`.

#### Indirectly Kṛṣṇa conscious [28:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport makes a significant statement: the impersonalists and the meditators are also indirectly Kṛṣṇa conscious — the impersonalist meditating on _Brahman_, the meditator on _Paramātmā_.
- If they become directly Kṛṣṇa conscious, the process is easier and more fruitful, giving greater joy and swifter advancement.
- Prabhupāda explains how a devotee is the perfect yogī: he possesses nothing for himself but uses possessions in Kṛṣṇa's service.

#### Two aspects of aṣṭāṅga-yoga [28:00 – 30:00]

- **_Haṭha-yoga_ — the physical aspect:** _yama_, _niyama_, _āsana_, _prāṇāyāma_.
- **_Dhyāna-yoga_ — the psychological aspect:** turning inward.
- This is not a watertight division — the mind is involved at the physical level, and the body at the psychological level — but it shows the focus `30:00`.

#### Yoga is harmonisable with, but not essential to, bhakti [30:00 – 36:00]

- An _ācārya_ the teacher quotes writes that yoga is harmonisable with but not essential to _bhakti_: a devotee may practise yoga — for health or to help sense control — with _bhakti_ as the main process, but not every devotee has to `30:00`.
- Prabhupāda repeatedly says authentic yoga means giving up sense gratification and going to the forest; living in a city indulging the senses is not real yoga practice. He was critical of yoga taught and practised purely for health `30:00`.
- **Headstand competitions** — like weightlifters competing over who lifts more, people compete over tougher _āsanas_ or standing on the head for two, five, ten, fifteen minutes — far from yoga's purpose. Many authentic yoga teachers are also displeased with this; they see yoga as a means of transcendence, though it is not the recommended means in this age `32:00`.
- **Prabhupāda in South America** — as described in the seventh, miscellaneous volume of Prabhupāda's biography, devotees told him many people were interested in Indian culture through yoga. He said: encourage them, and tell them yoga is not just about the body but about the mind and heart, and the best way to practise yoga is to chant Hare Kṛṣṇa `32:00 – 34:00`.
- So we need not disapprove of yoga's popularity, but elevate it. Some devotees are yoga teachers who also teach _bhakti_. If yoga opens a door — "you're not interested in religion, but you're interested in yoga? The Bhagavad-gītā is a manual of yoga, and _bhakti_ is yoga" — then why not `34:00`.
- Devotees may do yoga for health like jogging, cycling or swimming — that's fine — but that time is for the body, not _sādhana_. The physical should not be mistaken for the spiritual, and no devotee need be told "you have to do yoga" `34:00 – 36:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The real yogī renounces possessions and company to concentrate within; physical yoga can supplement bhakti and even open doors to Kṛṣṇa, but it is harmonisable with, not essential to, devotion — the physical must never be mistaken for the spiritual. [26:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.11 – 6.12 [36:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.11 -->

<!-- vedabase:6.12 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the yogī's seat and how he fixes the mind there for purification; 6.11 treats externals and 6.12 internals `36:00 – 40:00`, `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Sacred place — a pure, sanctified place `36:00`.
- Neither too high nor too low `36:00`.
- _Kuśa_ grass and an animal skin — specific recommended materials with specific purposes `38:00`.
- Controlling the mind, senses and actions `38:00`.
- For purification — the purpose of the practice `40:00`.

#### The seat [36:00 – 38:00]

- **Too high** — sitting on a mountaintop, one does more sightseeing than inward meditation.
- **Too low** — in a ditch, something may fall in or attack; it is not secure.
- **The tiger skin** — recommended because it keeps snakes away. The yogī does not kill a tiger for it — since a yogī practises _ahiṁsā_ — rather, the skin of a tiger that dies naturally is used. Deerskin is also used at different times.
- In the same spirit, Prabhupāda would say that if people want to eat meat, let the animals die naturally; killing animals and eating meat is very detrimental to spiritual practice `38:00`.

#### Preparing consciousness for japa [40:00]

- The last line can be used when sitting down for morning chanting: "There are other things to do in life, but this is not the time; this is the time for fixing the mind on Kṛṣṇa." The mind and senses want to go here and there; bring them under control — for the sake of purification.

#### The sixth chapter as a sādhana manual [40:00 – 42:00]

- The chapter describes _sādhana_ for _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_, but the principles apply to all paths of yoga.
- Among all eighteen chapters, the sixth offers the most direct guidelines for the practice of _sādhana_:
  - **Chapter 9** — _rāja-vidyā_, more about the glory of _bhakti_ and of _Bhagavān_.
  - **Chapter 12** — the glories and qualities of the devotee.
- With slight modification, these instructions apply to _sādhana-bhakti_ `42:00`.

#### Applying it to chanting [42:00]

- We may not go to the forest or sit on a deerskin; we may sit on a simple mat, or a chair if we have back problems — the point is to sit in one place.
- Some may stand or walk while chanting. If combining walking for exercise with chanting, walk back and forth over a small area rather than going for a walk: new scenery means new distractions, while a familiar small space gives the mind nothing new, so it turns to hearing Kṛṣṇa.
- Prabhupāda constantly says in the purports that the best yoga for this age is chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The yogī's seat — sanctified, neither too high nor too low — and his control of mind and senses all serve one purpose, purification; for us, the same principles apply when we sit (or pace a small space) to chant Hare Kṛṣṇa. [36:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.13 – 6.14 [44:00 – 56:30]

<!-- vedabase:6.13 -->

<!-- vedabase:6.14 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes posture and gaze (6.13, externals) and then the inner state — peaceful, fearless, under a vow of _brahmacarya_, with the mind fixed on Him (6.14, internals) `44:00 – 46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Body, head and neck in a straight line `44:00`.
- Looking at the tip of the nose `44:00`.
- Not looking around — not being on a sightseeing mission `46:00`.
- Peaceful mind; free from fear `46:00`.
- _Brahmacārī_ / _brahmacarya_ — technical and generic meanings `48:00 – 50:00`.
- Fix the mind on Me `54:00`.

#### Sitting straight [44:00]

- Most people sit slumped, which also creates back problems.
- When told "sit straight," everyone does — for a while; sitting straight continuously for a long time is not easy. The yogī must.

#### Tip of the nose or between the eyebrows? [44:00 – 46:00]

- In 5.27–5.28 Kṛṣṇa said to look between the eyebrows; here, at the tip of the nose. Which is it?
- The point is to have a focus of concentration that is _not_ very interesting: an interesting object stimulates imagination and the mind wanders. Nothing is special about the tip of the nose or the space between the eyebrows, so the mind can be drawn inward.
- Concentration is aided by an object for vision; closing the eyes, one is likely to fall asleep `46:00`.
- Yogīs are in a more troublesome situation than _bhaktas_: _bhaktas_ can walk and chant, but yogīs must do everything sitting `46:00`.
- **Structure:** 6.11 externals → 6.12 internals; 6.13 externals → 6.14 internals `46:00`.

#### Fearlessness — and the difficulty in Kali-yuga [46:00 – 48:00]

- A yogī cannot meditate in the jungle with a gun beside him, ready for a tiger, while wondering if someone will steal the gun.
- In Kali-yuga there are very few forests left, very few people who want to go there, and few peaceful places; even without dangerous animals, there are far more dangerous humans — pirates, terrorists, lawbreakers of different kinds.
- So even the physical aspect of this process is hard in Kali-yuga, what to speak of the internal aspect `48:00`.

#### The first explicit mention of brahmacarya [48:00 – 50:00]

- This is the first explicit reference to _brahmacarya_ in the Gītā. In **4.26** there was an indirect reference — explained as referring to the _naiṣṭhika brahmacārīs_ and regulated householders.
- Sense control has been discussed since Chapter 2 (the person of steady wisdom), and detachment throughout, but _brahmacarya_ is named here.
- Women can also do yoga, but there is no mixing of the sexes; there has to be _brahmacarya_ `48:00`.

#### Technical and generic meanings [50:00 – 52:00]

- **Technical** — a particular _āśrama_: males who do not marry and fix their life in the pursuit of _Brahman_. Prabhupāda explains that bachelors are not automatically _brahmacārīs_.
- **Generic** — anyone trying to live in and move toward _Brahman_, spiritual reality; in that sense householders, married men and women can all be _brahmacārīs_.
- Here Kṛṣṇa uses the technical sense: giving up sexual indulgence and pursuing spiritual reality.
- The path of yoga in Vedic culture was generally followed by men. When Dhṛtarāṣṭra went to the forest, Gāndhārī went with him; while meditating he brought out a fire from within his body, and Gāndhārī entered the fire. Women can also perform austerities and be purified, but the focus was generally on men `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### What brahmacarya is [52:00 – 54:00]

- A definition the teacher cites: giving up sex life in one's actions, mind and words, in all places, all circumstances and all times.
- Prabhupāda explains in the purport that one can actually practise _brahmacarya_ by getting the higher taste; otherwise it is very difficult. No one can perform correct yoga practice through sex indulgence.
- Sex is a physical activity, but it also drags consciousness down to the physical level and keeps it there for a long time — very few things do so as much. To raise consciousness toward spiritual reality, this is a vital rule `52:00`.

#### "Fix your mind on Me" [54:00 – 56:30]

- After regulating the mind, fix it — on Kṛṣṇa. This is nearly identical to **2.61**: one who regulates the senses and fixes the mind on Me conquers the senses and is well situated.
- This is the first time in the sixth chapter that Kṛṣṇa refers to Himself. Until now He spoke of detachment, realisation, equal vision and seclusion.
- He reveals that the purpose of yoga is to fix the mind on Him and attain Him: the yogīs fix the mind on the Supersoul, and the Supersoul is His expansion.
- At this stage yogīs may not realise that this Supersoul is the Ultimate Reality — that depends on their path and conception of the Absolute Truth — but they must fix the mind on some centre. They begin by fixing the eyes on the tip of the nose, but the mind needs an object of meditation, and that object is Kṛṣṇa `54:00 – 56:00`.
- Further technicalities of _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ will be discussed in the next class `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Posture, gaze, fearlessness and a strict vow of brahmacarya all lead to one point Kṛṣṇa now reveals for the first time in this chapter: the mind must be fixed on Him, for the Supersoul the yogī seeks is His own expansion. [44:00 – 56:30]
</Callout>
