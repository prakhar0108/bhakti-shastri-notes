## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Prelude: Levels of samādhi in yoga and in bhakti [00:00 – 26:00]

**Context.** Before the verses, the teacher contrasts how yoga and _bhakti_ understand _samādhi_ `00:00 – 08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Samprajñāta-samādhi_ — the mind, which normally wanders over dozens of things, is focused on one thing `00:00`.
- The higher stage — the mind's activity at the material level stops altogether `00:00 – 02:00`.
- _Citta-vṛtti-nirodha_ — the principle of yoga: the movements of consciousness come to a complete stop `08:00`.

#### The television example [02:00]

- A person watching television has emotions that go up and down with what happens on the screen; our emotions in the material world are like that.
- Focusing on one thing is like staying absorbed in one programme; the higher level is like simply switching the TV off — one is no longer connected with those emotions.

#### Objects of concentration [04:00 – 08:00]

- **Physical object** — e.g. fixing the mind on the tip of the nose, as mentioned in this chapter.
- **Mental object** — a thought or concept, e.g. thinking of an image of Kṛṣṇa.
- **Directly spiritual** — no physical or mental object is needed; this higher level is considered the perfection of the yoga path.

#### Bhakti: perceiving and participating [08:00 – 14:00]

- In _bhakti_ the essential point is not the cessation of activity at the physical and mental level, but the **stimulation** of activity at the devotional level.
- The devotee starts perceiving higher reality — and not just perceiving but **participating**, recognising "I have a role in that."
- The teacher gives pastimes of the Gosvāmīs to illustrate:
  - A Gosvāmī meditating at night on a pastime of Kṛṣṇa and Rādhārāṇī in the forest was heard laughing aloud by a passing Vaiṣṇava, who asked why; the laughter came from what he was witnessing in meditation `10:00 – 12:00`.
  - In another pastime a Gosvāmī, while preparing and offering _khīr_ in meditation, was afterwards found physically affected — what happened at the spiritual level manifested at the material level `12:00 – 14:00`.
- So devotional _samādhi_ is not passive perception but active participation `14:00`.

#### Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's trances [14:00 – 20:00]

- Mahāprabhu's transcendental states can be seen at different levels of perception and participation.
- Walking on the road in Jagannātha Purī, He would suddenly see higher reality — not imagination, but direct perception `16:00`.
- **The fisherman:** once a fisherman caught Mahāprabhu in his net, thinking he had a giant fish; on touching Him he began acting like a madman, chanting Kṛṣṇa's name and thinking he was haunted by a ghost. The devotees, frantically searching everywhere, found him and so found Mahāprabhu `18:00`.
- In that state Mahāprabhu's body itself changed — His joints became dislocated and His limbs elongated; only when the holy names were chanted did He come back to external consciousness `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### Sahaja-samādhi and the gopīs [20:00 – 26:00]

- _Sahaja-samādhi_ — by purification, natural spiritual cognition comes `20:00`.
- The _gopīs_ of Vṛndāvana exhibit the highest _samādhi_, and Rādhārāṇī most of all; its climax is seen in the Tenth Canto, chapters 46 and 47 `20:00`.
- When Uddhava came from Mathurā, Rādhārāṇī, seeing a bumblebee, addressed it as Kṛṣṇa's messenger: "Don't touch My feet, you cannot flatter Me, go away" — speaking of her feelings for Kṛṣṇa in a way words can hardly capture `22:00`.
- Seeing this, Uddhava longed to be a blade of grass in Vṛndāvana so that the _gopīs_' feet would fall on his head and he would be blessed `24:00`.
- This is the ultimate goal — the kind of _samādhi_ we come to by study, practice and service `24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Yoga's samādhi stops the mind's material movements, but bhakti's samādhi is active participation in Kṛṣṇa's pastimes — seen in the Gosvāmīs, in Mahāprabhu and supremely in the gopīs. [08:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.17 – 6.18 [26:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.17 -->

<!-- vedabase:6.18 -->

**Context.** The teacher returns to 6.17's eating, recreation and work, then shows how 6.18 uses the same idea in a second sense `26:00 – 34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yukta_ — can mean "regulated," and can also mean "connected" `26:00`.

#### Yukta as connection [26:00 – 30:00]

- If, while doing all one's activities, one stays connected with the Absolute Truth, then yoga is not a part-time activity but a full-time one.
- The bodily requirements still have to be done, but whatever we do, we remember Kṛṣṇa — and so we stay purified and are not contaminated.
- Eating and sleeping continue, but consciousness is controlled and directed towards the Absolute Truth; we engage in the same activities with a higher purpose.
- When the senses are educated, sensory desires can actually elevate us `28:00`.

#### Two aspects of being well situated [32:00 – 34:00]

- In 6.17 _yukta_ refers to the **regulation** aspect; in 6.18 to the **connection** aspect.
- Both are needed: connection with the Absolute Truth, and regulation of our interaction with worldly objects.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
To be yukta is both to regulate one's dealings with matter and to stay connected with Kṛṣṇa throughout them, turning yoga from a part-time practice into a full-time life. [26:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.19 [34:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa gives an analogy: as a lamp in a windless place does not flicker, so the yogi's mind fixed in meditation stays steady `34:00`.

#### The flame and the wind [34:00 – 36:00]

- The flame refers to consciousness; the unflickering flame is consciousness fixed on the self and the Supreme.
- The wind is material desire — what makes our consciousness flicker is the things we desire and are attached to.

#### Busy but unproductive [38:00]

- We are always busy, always running here and there, yet the actual amount of work done is little — because the mind is restless and productivity suffers.
- When we purify ourselves in the morning _sādhana_ and then come to work focused, we do things much more productively, with intensity and focus.

#### The purpose of sādhana [40:00]

- _Sādhana_ makes our consciousness free from the wind of material desires so that we become fixed — and when the mind is fixed, we can do so much more.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Material desire is the wind that makes consciousness flicker; sādhana shelters the flame, and a steady consciousness accomplishes far more than a restless, busy one. [34:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.20 [40:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.20 -->

**Context.** The teacher introduces 6.20–6.23 as a very beautiful set of verses — the most thrilling description of _samādhi_ in the Bhagavad-gītā — describing the state in which mental activity, rooted in matter, ceases `40:00`.

#### The movie theatre [42:00 – 44:00]

- Consciousness is presently rooted in material energy.
- In a theatre, before the film, one is aware of the surroundings — other people, the chair, the floor.
- Once the movie starts, one's whole experience becomes centred on it; similarly the soul, which has nothing to do with sensual enjoyment, has its consciousness rooted and caught in matter.
- In _samādhi_ that consciousness in matter stops — like a person who stops watching the movie.
- The teacher notes that the seventh chapter speaks of the reasons why people come to Kṛṣṇa `44:00`.

#### Turning away and turning towards [46:00]

- The mind, too, turns away from matter and focuses on God.
- As our connection with illusion decreases, our connection with reality increases.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Like a viewer who stops watching a film, the yogi in samādhi withdraws consciousness from matter, and as the connection with illusion decreases the connection with reality grows. [42:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.21 [46:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** In that state one experiences ultimate happiness `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sukham ātyantikam_ — ultimate happiness `46:00`.
- _Atīndriyam_ — not involving the senses `46:00`.

#### Happiness without the senses [46:00 – 48:00]

- This happiness is beyond the senses — so rich, so deep, so fulfilling — whereas whatever we get through the senses, we soon want something more.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">

### Bhagavad-gita 6.22 [48:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.22 -->

**Context.** Though this chapter later describes the mind as restless, here Kṛṣṇa speaks of the greatest achievement in life — one that takes us beyond hankering and lamentation `48:00`.

#### The love that never ends [48:00 – 50:00]

- That achievement is love — infinite, and never ending.
- Our usual definitions of success — a job in a good company, a salary, a child becoming an engineer, a house — never give perennial fulfilment; after the dream house we want a bigger one `50:00`.

#### Loss is certain, love is not [52:00 – 56:00]

- Without a spiritual conception of life, enjoying this world ends in great loss when we lose everything.
- In material life lamentation is not a possibility but a certainty — not a question of "if" but of "when."
- Love for Kṛṣṇa, once achieved in the heart, will never ever be taken away; it stays forever.
- So whatever other conceptions of achievement we have, Kṛṣṇa gives the highest standard here `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every material success leaves us wanting more and will eventually be lost, but love for Kṛṣṇa, once achieved, is the greatest gain and can never be taken away. [48:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.23 [54:00 – 62:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.23 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa defines this yoga as freedom from contact with misery `54:00 – 56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Duḥkha-saṁyoga-viyogam_ — freedom from contact with misery `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Free from misery within the world? [56:00]

- Trying to be free from misery in the material world is like trying to be in the ocean while avoiding getting wet; however much we try, misery will come.
- What gives us misery is our material emotions — so the question is how one gives them up `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Yoga's negation and bhakti's positive path [58:00]

- In Patañjali's _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ there is not much description of positive spiritual emotion; perfection is _citta-vṛtti-nirodha_ — checking the movements of consciousness towards matter, i.e. eliminating material emotions.
- In _bhakti-yoga_ the process is different: we kindle spiritual emotions, and by them material emotions go away. The heart is filled with love for Kṛṣṇa and the desire to serve and glorify Him, and then material desires find no room.

#### Śrīla Prabhupāda's example [58:00 – 62:00]

- Prabhupāda went to America, considered a land of enjoyment, but he had no room for material desire there; he simply saw people forgetful of Kṛṣṇa and thought, "Let me help them remember."
- In _bhakti_ the focus is not just on giving up desires but on taking up spiritual desires `60:00`.
- What defined Prabhupāda was not merely the absence of material desires but the presence of spiritual desire — to glorify Kṛṣṇa, love Kṛṣṇa, and share Kṛṣṇa's message with the whole world `60:00 – 62:00`.
- That desire animated him even when his body was old and sick, constantly preaching; that is the perfection of life `62:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We cannot escape misery by negating emotions within the material world; bhakti frees us by filling the heart with spiritual desire — as in Prabhupāda, whose love to share Kṛṣṇa left no room for material desire. [56:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
