#### Terms and concepts [00:00 – 06:00]

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 6.33 [12:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.33 -->

**Context.** Arjuna responds to the system of yoga Kṛṣṇa has just described: he cannot see things that way because his mind is restless `12:00 – 14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Submission** — "I will do whatever you say"; "I am not saying what you say is wrong" `14:00 – 16:00`.
- **Reservation** — "I cannot do what you say; it is not practical for me" `14:00`.

#### Arjuna's echo of Kṛṣṇa's words [14:00]

- Kṛṣṇa has said that one should have equal vision everywhere, and three verses earlier (6.30) spoke of one who sees Him everywhere; Arjuna uses the same word to say "I don't see it."
- In effect: "I cannot see everyone equally — I cannot see Karṇa and Bhīma equally, or the people on my side and the other side; my mind is not steady, it is constantly restless."

#### Submissive reservation [14:00 – 18:00]

- Saying that what someone says is wrong can be offensive; Arjuna does not do that. He accepts the teaching but expresses that it is not possible for him.
- The same combination appears when Nārada gives two levels of instruction to the five-year-old Dhruva Mahārāja `16:00`:
  - Level one: quarrels between parents and children are not to be taken seriously — children who say "I won't talk to you" are playing happily together a few days later.
  - Level two: if you are truly grown up, spiritually too, honour and dishonour are the same.
  - Dhruva's reply: "Yes, but I have a _kṣatriya_'s blood within me; I cannot tolerate this insult." He wanted not just his father's lap but an exclusive throne over a kingdom bigger than his father's or his forefathers' — only that would resolve the burning sense of dishonour `18:00`.
- Application: it is a good attitude to aspire to simply follow instructions, but we need not always put our intelligence aside; we may express realistic reservations. "Everyone should chant 64 rounds every day" is a good aspiration, but may not be practical for all `18:00`.

#### Stars and stairs [18:00 – 20:00]

- In spiritual life there are **stars** — what we aspire to (to be pure devotees, constantly absorbed in Kṛṣṇa) — and **stairs** — the steps that take us there.
- Looking only at the stars, we never move ("it's such a high level"); looking only at the stairs, we lose inspiration. We need both: one step at a time, we can eventually get there.

#### Did Arjuna practise aṣṭāṅga-yoga? [20:00 – 22:00]

- Prabhupāda writes in the purport that there is no record of Arjuna ever practising this system, and that it was difficult even for him.
- Some point to the _Mahābhārata_, where Arjuna sits in a yogic posture in the Himalayas performing austerities to please Lord Śiva (and later Indra) for weapons.
- The teacher's answer: sitting in a yogic posture does not make one an _aṣṭāṅga-yogī_. Arjuna's goal was not _samādhi_ or Brahman realisation; he was acquiring what was needed for Kṛṣṇa's service — as part of his _bhakti-yoga_ `22:00`.
- The _Mahābhārata_ shows that a devotee is not deprived of benedictions and can worship other _devatās_ and receive their blessings, but what ultimately empowers the devotee is Kṛṣṇa's mercy `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna models "submissive reservation": he accepts Kṛṣṇa's teaching without calling it wrong, yet honestly says it is not practical for him — a realistic stance that keeps both the stars (the ideal) and the stairs (the next step) in view. [14:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.34 [22:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.34 -->

**Context.** Arjuna gives a graphic description of why the restless mind makes this yoga impossible for him `22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Cañcala_ — restless, like a child `24:00`.
- **Maddened** — agitated to the point of being mad `26:00`.
- **Powerful** — strong like a bully `24:00 – 28:00`.
- **Obstinate** — the word Arjuna uses can mean determination, but here it means obstinacy; the same word is used positively in 9.14 for the determined devotee `32:00 – 34:00`.

#### Like a restless child [24:00]

- Told by its mother to sit quietly, a child soon wanders — "let me play with this toy, let me go here, let me go there."
- Such restlessness is mostly innocent curiosity, sometimes mischief, and occasionally danger (a child can fall off the bed).
- But what makes the mind dangerous is that it is not only like a curious child.

#### Like a madman [26:00 – 28:00]

- Some people are so disconnected from reality that they live in an illusory world ("I am the President of India") and may act dangerously — imagining a battlefield and shooting "the enemy."
- We too go mad at times: anger is temporary insanity, making us say and do things we would never dream of otherwise `28:00`.

#### Like a bully [28:00 – 32:00]

- An ordinary madman can need two or three asylum staff to restrain him; imagine a madman who is also a boxing champion with huge muscles.
- In the eleventh canto, the Avantī _brāhmaṇa_'s prayers speak of the mind's "god-like" power: when it takes control it becomes almost omnipotent, and its command is almost impossible to disobey `28:00`.
- In the tenth chapter Kṛṣṇa says that among the senses He is the mind `30:00`.

#### The mind is Māyā for practical purposes [30:00 – 32:00]

- It is simplistic for men to think of women as Māyā; the goddess of learning is also female, and for a woman a man can equally be Māyā.
- Māyā is whatever takes us away from Kṛṣṇa, and more important than external things is the internal thing that does so — the mind.
- Ontologically, Māyā is a real being — a goddess, Māyā-devī, who functions as Durgā-devī, the consort of Lord Śiva — distinct from the mind; but functionally, the mind is Māyā: it makes us act restlessly, irrationally, self-destructively.
- Like a jail with its surveillance system, the material world is an arena for our free will; Māyā is given vigilance by Kṛṣṇa and knows our weaknesses. Her goal is not to trap us but to promote us — through testing `32:00`.

#### Obstinate, unlike determined [32:00 – 38:00]

- Determination is laudable: persevering despite repeated failures on a good path.
- Obstinacy is persisting on an undesirable path despite lessons, warnings and indicators. The difference is primarily whether the track is right or wrong `36:00`.
- Rāvaṇa's obstinacy in not giving up Sītā `34:00 – 36:00`:
  - He knew he had no protection from humans and had heard of Rāma's power from many sources.
  - Akampana, the only general to survive Rāma's single-handed slaughter, came trembling to his court and warned him he could not face Rāma.
  - Mārīca also warned him; spies reported that Rāma's army could not be faced.
  - Hanumān told him to surrender and showed his prowess by destroying nearly half his kingdom; Aṅgada warned him, and his generals could not even move Aṅgada's foot.
  - Despite so many warnings, he heeded none.
- Similarly the repeated failed attempts to push down the Pāṇḍavas did not stop those who kept trying `36:00`.
- Once the mind is hooked on an idea of enjoyment — "I want this, come what may" — it listens to nothing else `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Four features together [38:00 – 40:00]

- Restlessness, madness, power and obstinacy together make the mind extremely dangerous: our worst enemy is inside us.
- Any external enemy, however formidable, can be handled with extraordinary weapons; Arjuna has celestial weapons, yet none can control the mind `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### Like the wind [38:00 – 42:00]

- When the wind blows, we cannot stop it — we often cannot even hold on to things it blows away, like loose papers flying off one by one.
- Kṛṣṇa used a wind metaphor in this very chapter (6.19): the yogī's mind is steady like a lamp in a windless place. Arjuna is saying, in effect, "what to speak of a windless place — my mind is a hurricane, a tornado, a cyclone" `40:00`.
- The whole process of _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ centres on controlling the mind; the word _manas_ and its cognates come many times in this chapter `40:00`.
- In the purport Prabhupāda quotes a famous verse about the chariot of the body: the senses are the horses, the sense objects are where they want to run, the mind is the reins; the soul, equipped with the mind and senses, tries to enjoy and sometimes suffers `40:00 – 42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's diagnosis is precise: the mind is restless like a child, mad, powerful like a bully and obstinate — four features that together make it our worst enemy, uncontrollable even by celestial weapons, like the wind. [24:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.35 [42:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.35 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa first empathises — "undoubtedly, the mind is restless" — then says it is possible to control it and how `42:00 – 46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Abhyāsa_ — practice `46:00 – 48:00`.
- **Detachment** — the second principle Kṛṣṇa names alongside practice `46:00`.

#### Empathy before instruction [42:00 – 46:00]

- When a disciple expresses doubt, the response must be encouraging, and to be encouraging it must be empathetic — understanding the person's position.
- Not: "You've chanted for 10 years and still get angry? You're fallen." Such responses dishearten.
- Talking with a small child, one kneels or sits down to their level rather than making them look up; similarly, our philosophy is lofty, but we bring it to people's level and help them grow — as they grow, their level rises `44:00`.

#### Generic principles for all paths [46:00 – 48:00]

- Kṛṣṇa does not use technical, yoga-specific terms here; Arjuna's question is general, and so is the answer: practice and detachment apply on every path.
- Medical example: for a fracture, the therapy has prescriptions (walk a little, exercise the leg this way) and proscriptions (don't exert too much; avoid what damages the ligament).
- In _bhakti-yoga_: we keep practising, bringing the mind back whenever it wanders; we follow the regulative principles and avoid activities that agitate the mind disproportionately `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### "But it is the mind that stops me practising" [48:00]

- One may object: "Because of the mind I can't practise or detach — so how can the cure be the very thing I can't do?"
- Answer — progression: a person with a fracture is told to walk a few steps, not till it hurts too much; by walking a little, strength gradually returns.
- Likewise: "It's because of my uncontrolled mind that I can't chant" — keep chanting. We may not have full control, but we can have some, and it keeps increasing; meanwhile we avoid activities that simply entangle us.

#### Taste, strength and freedom from hankering [48:00 – 50:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport explains this from the _bhakti_ perspective with the example of eating: one who is weak eats, gains taste and strength, and the hunger disappears.
- Remembering Kṛṣṇa does the same: our hankering for worldly objects goes away, remembering Him becomes sweet, and we feel strong enough to face whatever temptations come.
- The more we engage in devotional service, the greater the satisfaction `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa first empathises, then prescribes: the mind is controlled by practice and detachment, applied progressively — like walking a few steps to heal a fracture — until remembering Kṛṣṇa gives the taste and strength that dissolve hankering. [42:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.36 [50:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.36 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes His answer: for one whose mind is uncontrolled, the perfection of yoga is very difficult to attain; one who controls the mind by proper endeavour will succeed `50:00 – 52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Uncontrolled** — describing the mind that has not been brought under control `50:00`.
- **Endeavour** — the right process, applied, which makes success possible `52:00`.

#### Encouraging without compromising [52:00]

- **Uncompromising:** if you cannot control the mind, you will not attain perfection — it is a requirement that has to be met.
- **Encouraging:** there is a right process; follow it and you will be able to control the mind.

#### The easy way is still to come [52:00]

- What the process is will be discussed later; the teacher notes that _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ and _bhakti-yoga_ are contrasted most directly in the eighth chapter.
- In 8.14 Kṛṣṇa says the attainment is easy — the only verse where He calls spiritual realisation easy — for one who remembers Him and uses that remembrance to control the mind.

#### Two verses of question, two of answer [54:00]

- In two verses Arjuna expresses apprehension; in two verses Kṛṣṇa gives clarification and encouragement: "Yes, it is difficult, no doubt — but by the right process it is possible."
- **Darkness and light:** darkness cannot be removed by punching, cursing or kicking it — only by bringing light.
- **Willpower and medicine:** willpower alone cannot cure a cough or loose motions; willpower is required, but it must be coupled with the curative process.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is encouraging without being compromising: an uncontrolled mind makes yoga's perfection impossible, but willpower coupled with the right process — like light that removes darkness — makes success certain. [50:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
