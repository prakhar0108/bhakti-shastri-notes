## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 6.14 [00:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.14 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa assumes the transcendentalist already knows about yoga; specific books such as Patañjali's _Yoga-sūtra_ give the technical knowledge. So the teacher explains the _yama_ and _niyama_ elements that 6.10 and 6.14 point to `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ — yoga of eight stages, beginning with _yama_, _niyama_, _āsana_ and _prāṇāyāma_ `00:00`.
- _Yama_ and _niyama_ — each has five elements; Patañjali describes each element and the result of following it `00:00`, `06:00`.
- _Īśvara-praṇidhāna_ — surrender to the Lord; the last _niyama_ `20:00`.
- _Samādhi_ — the eighth stage, the ultimate goal of yoga `20:00`.

#### Why the Gītā does not detail any one path [02:00 – 04:00]

- The Gītā gives a general outline of yoga, not a technical analysis — just as it does not list the 64 elements of _bhakti_ found in _The Nectar of Devotion_.
- It is an "emergency book," spoken on a battlefield, not at leisure in a _gurukula_, and spoken according to the audience's level.
- Arjuna already knows the various paths; his question is not "what is a path?" but "which path should I follow?"
- **Career-counselling analogy:** a student finishing 10th standard choosing arts, commerce or science is told the pluses and minuses of each, not the full syllabus. The Gītā is like a "yoga counselling centre" — life counselling on which yoga to follow.
- Kṛṣṇa will focus more on the paths of action than of renunciation, and among them recommend _bhakti-yoga_ `04:00`.

#### The five yamas and their fruits [04:00 – 12:00]

1. _Ahiṁsā_ (non-violence) — some violence is inevitable in the world (even eating kills microscopic organisms), and a _kṣatriya_ has to kill; but one practising yoga and renunciation should follow _ahiṁsā_. **Fruit:** others give up their enmity towards us, since people turn violent when they feel threatened `04:00 – 06:00`.
2. _Satya_ (truthfulness) — recognised as a virtue in all religions; habitual liars eventually lose others' trust ("you can't fool all the people all the time"). **Fruit:** one's words become endowed with power and come true; such careful speech earns respect, and as representatives of Kṛṣṇa, the ultimate truth, we need to speak truthfully `06:00 – 08:00`.
3. _Asteya_ (non-stealing) — remembered via the English word "steal." **Fruit:** one acquires true wealth, ultimately spiritual wealth `08:00`.
4. _Brahmacarya_ — at one level non-indulgence in sex, at another living spiritually. **Fruit:** power. Beyond the social, emotional and medical complications of indiscriminate sexuality, a subtle power is dissipated in sex; conserved, it manifests as creativity, concentration and clarity — one becomes intellectually radiant and spiritually potent. Prabhupāda said one reason Gandhi succeeded without weapons was that, though a householder, he practised _brahmacarya_ `08:00 – 10:00`.
5. _Aparigraha_ (non-possessiveness) — **Fruit:** one sees things truly — not just as events, but in their karmic connection. When Haridāsa Ṭhākura departed, Mahāprabhu said that Kṛṣṇa, being merciful, gave Him Haridāsa's association and, being independent, took it away `10:00`.

#### The five niyamas and their fruits [12:00 – 20:00]

1. _Śauca_ (cleanliness) — Prabhupāda links external cleanliness (bathing) with internal cleanliness (chanting). Cleaning the body regularly makes us aware how inherently unclean it is — unlike people who hide this with perfumes — and so brings detachment from the body `12:00`.
2. _Santoṣa_ (satisfaction):
   - There will always be people with more and with less; comparison — two-wheeler vs four-wheeler, 2BHK vs 3BHK, simple phone vs smartphone — is "the cancer of the mind" `12:00 – 14:00`.
   - Satisfaction is not just a condition or emotion; it is a **choice and a decision**, because the quest for material improvement is endless `14:00`.
   - It does not mean no ambition; choose a realistic standard (not a 35-year loan beyond capacity) and be clear about priorities `16:00`.
   - A devotee can have spiritual ambition — to advance, study, share the message; nothing satisfies the heart like seeing one's life make a difference in others' lives through Kṛṣṇa consciousness `16:00`.
   - The pursuit of material things is agitating and their achievement an anticlimax — "it never lives up to the hype" `18:00`.
3. _Tapas_ (austerity), e.g. fasting — removes impurities and makes body and senses strong in stamina and stability, not for sense gratification `18:00`.
4. _Svādhyāya_ (study of scripture) — **Fruit:** connection with God. Reading the newspaper for an hour is entertainment, not _svādhyāya_; by proper study we develop _śāstra-cakṣu_, the eyes of scripture, and see the world as Kṛṣṇa's energy `18:00 – 20:00`.
5. _Īśvara-praṇidhāna_ — **Fruit:** _samādhi_, the eighth stage; the primary purpose of spiritual practice is to attain love for the Lord, and surrender leads there `20:00`.

#### Yoga is not inherently non-devotional [20:00 – 24:00]

- Yoga is not one monolithic body of knowledge; there have been many thinkers on yoga, as there have been many _bhakti_ and _māyāvāda_ thinkers.
- The Vaiṣṇava commentaries refute yoga when it is taken as an independent system leading to the monistic conclusion of merging.
- From indications in his statements, Patañjali was a Vaiṣṇava; his _Yoga-sūtra_ is primarily a manual, so he simply says "surrender to _īśvara_," assuming readers know who _īśvara_ is.
- Some yogis take the devotional element as transitional (meditate on the Absolute until realising "I am the Absolute"); others understand the Lord as the eternal object of meditation. Such personalist yogis can attain _śānta_ perfection in the spiritual world — like trees meditating on the Lord's pastimes — or, with devotees' association, rise further through active devotion `22:00 – 24:00`.
- The last part of 6.14 refers to this limb of _īśvara-praṇidhāna_ — progressing to perfection by meditating on and surrendering to the Lord `24:00`.

#### The eight limbs in a cricket match [24:00 – 28:00]

- **_Yama_ and _niyama_** — dos and don'ts. Two ways of seeing them:
  - _Niyamas_ are positive "dos" and _yamas_ mostly "don'ts" (don't harm, don't steal, don't possess, don't lie, don't indulge).
  - _Niyamas_ are rules of individual discipline; _yamas_ are rules of group discipline — how one interacts with others.
  - A batsman must wear a helmet and carry a bat — and may not swing the bat at the wicket-keeper's head.
- **_Āsana_** — each batsman has his stance.
- **_Prāṇāyāma_** — breath control, whose purpose is channelling energy; a restless breath dissipates concentration.
- **_Pratyāhāra_ to _samādhi_** — last ball, six needed to win, fans screaming: the batsman shuts out everything, sees only the ball, hits a six and "attains _samādhi_" — a temporary pleasure.
- These eight are generic principles; the teacher notes an essay applying them even to Deity worship, and they can be applied to our _sādhana_ `28:00`.

#### Five states of the mind [28:00 – 34:00]

Yoga literature describes the mind rising through five states; Kṛṣṇa has already used _ekāgra_ in this chapter and will use _nirodha_ around 6.19–6.20 `28:00 – 30:00`.

1. _Mūḍha_ — foolish, deluded: sleep, laziness, dullness; lethargic, "lost somewhere" `30:00`.
2. _Kṣipta_ — agitated, hyperactive: stress or mania; like a fan going round and round yet getting nowhere `30:00 – 32:00`.
3. _Vikṣipta_ — distracted: wanting to concentrate but unable, like listening to a lecture in a noisy place `32:00`.
4. _Ekāgra_ — concentrated: a student two hours before an exam, completely absorbed `32:00`.
5. _Nirodha_ — controlled: other thoughts may come ("your favourite movie releases this week — forget it"), but now there is no attraction to them at all; the mind stops even fantasising about sense gratification `32:00 – 34:00`.

- This is moving up from the negative axis to zero; for a devotee there are many positive spiritual emotions beyond that — a whole different level `34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The yamas and niyamas each bear a concrete fruit, culminating in surrender to the Lord that leads to samādhi; yoga rightly understood is not non-devotional, and its eight limbs are universal principles that also apply to our sādhana. [04:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.15 [34:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.15 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the result of practising yoga this way: by constantly controlling the body, mind and activities, the yogi attains peace, the cessation of material existence, and abides in Kṛṣṇa's abode `34:00 – 36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ātmā_ — Prabhupāda uses it here to refer to body, mind and soul `34:00`.
- _Śānti_ — peace `36:00`.
- _Nirvāṇa_ — cessation of material existence `36:00`.
- _Mat-saṁsthām_ — situated with Kṛṣṇa, in the spiritual sky; the word does not allow a simplistic reduction to impersonalism `36:00`.

#### The goal of yoga is the end of material existence [36:00]

- The Ultimate Reality the yogi attains involves Kṛṣṇa Himself; Prabhupāda shows that Kṛṣṇa is the truth to be attained.
- One who seeks better health or material perfection is no yogi according to the Gītā, whatever today's definition.

#### No void anywhere [36:00 – 40:00]

- Cessation of material existence does not mean entering a void, which is a myth; it enables entry into the spiritual sky.
- Prabhupāda would say there is no void anywhere in creation "except in the head of the voidist."
- Material nature has a beautiful side (mountains, lakes, forests) and a dreadful side (landslides, earthquakes, floods, tsunamis, dangerous animals); a void cannot explain its beauty, variety and glory.
- Voidism is only negation of the negative, not comprehension of the positive; a patient needs both removal of disease and understanding of health `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### The cataract story [40:00 – 42:00]

The teacher recounts a story told by a Ṭhākura of the tradition, about a child with cataracts, in pain and unable to see:

- **First uncle:** pluck out the eyes — the impersonalist, who says forms, personalities, relationships and activities cause pain, so reject them all.
- **Second uncle:** kill the child — the voidist, who says existence is suffering, so end existence.
- **Third uncle:** remove the cataract and heal the eye — the devotee, who sees that the problem is self-centred, godless living; connected with Kṛṣṇa, our forms, relationships and activities can become sources of liberation.

- So yes, there is _nirvāṇa_, but after it comes real life — activity and relationship with Kṛṣṇa `42:00`.

#### Not gymnastic feats [42:00 – 44:00]

- Prabhupāda calls yoga used for magic feats to fool innocent people childish — like the child who can hit a six becoming a hero among children.
- Twisting the body may be good exercise, but it is a physical activity, not a spiritual one.

#### Kṛṣṇa's "doses" of yoga [44:00]

- In this chapter Kṛṣṇa goes up and down: from _sādhana_ at 6.10 up to perfection, then again from the basics up to 6.23, then again from 6.24 to perfection at 6.32 — giving the process in doses while pointing to higher and higher levels of perfection.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Yoga's perfection is not health, feats or a void but the end of material existence and entry into Kṛṣṇa's abode — like removing a cataract to heal the eye rather than plucking it out or killing the patient. [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.16 [44:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.16 -->

**Context.** Starting again from the basics, Kṛṣṇa says one should neither eat too much nor too little, neither sleep too much nor stay awake too long — He recommends balance, speaking from the negative side ("don't do this") `44:00 – 48:00`.

#### Too much and too little [46:00]

- Prabhupāda: sleep too much and you become lazy; sleep too little and you become crazy — unable to think clearly.
- Eat too little and you lack energy to serve, and the mind keeps thinking of food; eat too much and you cannot do things properly or offer your best.

#### Miserly with the body's time [48:00]

- Serious spiritualists are "miserly" about giving time to the body, asking: is this really required, and how much?
- **Car analogy:** a car needs fuel, air and servicing, but its purpose is to reach a destination, not to stand admiring it. Likewise the body is a vehicle meant for spiritual advancement — this is care with a purpose, not denial.
- Worldly professions too can be spiritualised by making Kṛṣṇa the purpose of life.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The body is a vehicle to be maintained, not admired: neither indulging nor starving it, the devotee is frugal with the time given to it so that energy goes to Kṛṣṇa. [46:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 6.17 [50:00 – 62:00]

<!-- vedabase:6.17 -->

**Context.** Now Kṛṣṇa speaks positively: one regulated in eating, recreation, work and sleep can mitigate all material pains by practising yoga `50:00 – 54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yukta_ — from the same root as _yoga_ and _yogī_, essentially "connected"; earlier (6.8) "well situated"; here Prabhupāda renders it "regulated" `50:00`.
- _Āhāra_ — eating `50:00`.
- _Vihāra_ — recreation, entertainment `50:00`.
- Work — whatever work (_karma_) or endeavour one does should be regulated `52:00`.
- _Duḥkha-hā_ — destroying misery `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### Even a yogi takes breaks [50:00]

- Sitting in _āsana_ in meditation is hard work; the yogi may walk and take in the beauty of nature and be refreshed — that is regulated.
- When Bharata Mahārāja saw a deer drowning, wanting to care for it was not in itself wrong.

#### Work in parallel lanes [52:00]

- Deadlines and emergencies sometimes demand 15–16 hour days, but choosing a career that always requires this puts material and spiritual life in head-on conflict.
- Material and spiritual life should run in parallel lanes; if work eats into the spiritual quota, one may need to reshuffle one's career plans.
- Overwork harms even materially — stress, disrupted biological clocks, night shifts. Devotees cannot run from responsibility "in the name of transcendence," but can choose wisely for the long term.

#### Regulation makes yoga fruitful [52:00 – 56:00]

- Without regulation in material activities, yoga practice is unfocused and does not lead to the destruction of misery.
- Squeezing in rounds while travelling, sleeping too little, distracted — acceptable in emergencies but not sustainable daily.
- Consciousness caught up in disproportionate indulgence in matter cannot focus on spirit; with regulation, one does not have to keep thinking about material things, and energy is freed.
- **The jigsaw alarm:** people who struggle to wake up for hours buy an alarm that breaks into jigsaw pieces and only stops ringing when reassembled; with a regulated decision about sleep, that whole conflict disappears. Likewise regulated eating ends the constant "should I snack now?" `56:00`.

#### The four bodily demands [56:00 – 58:00]

Roughly correlated:

- _Āhāra_ — eating.
- _Vihāra_ — recreation, primarily mating.
- Work — prescribed duty, which defends our arrangements for eating, sleeping and mating.
- Sleep — sleeping.

Prabhupāda's purport: extravagance in eating, sleeping, mating and defending can block advancement in yoga.

#### Time is more precious than money [58:00 – 60:00]

- We don't casually hand someone 1,000 rupees, yet we casually spend or take others' time.
- Cāṇakya: even crores of gold coins cannot buy back a single moment.
- Time is not only irrecoverable but un-storable — money can be banked; time is always being spent, and our only choice is how.

#### Purpose and urgency [60:00 – 62:00]

- Prabhupāda: a Kṛṣṇa conscious person cannot bear to pass a minute without being engaged in the Lord's service.
- A serious devotee is defined by a sense of **purpose** (this is my service) and **urgency** (I will do it now).
- The Gosvāmīs slept very little — keep that as an ideal, but don't physically imitate it, or "we will die."
- **Default to less:** in material matters (should I eat more?) assume the answer is no — the body signals when enough is enough; take less and ask for more rather than overeat to avoid wasting _prasāda_. In spiritual matters (one more round?) the answer is yes `62:00`.
- Prabhupāda's further explanation of how this is practised in the yogi's life will be discussed next class `62:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Regulation in eating, recreation, work and sleep is what makes yoga destroy misery: it frees the mind from constant bodily calculations and lets a devotee live with purpose and urgency, valuing time above money. [50:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
