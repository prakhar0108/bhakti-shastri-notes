### Bhagavad-gita 3.3 [00:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa's two paths — knowledge (jñāna-yoga) and work — are explained through the relationship between philosophy and religion `00:00`, `14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Philosophy_ — intellectual, systematic understanding; in this context, related to _jñāna_ `00:00`, `14:00`.
- _Religion_ — practice, doing something to move toward the experience; related to karma-yoga and bhakti `00:00`, `14:00`.

#### Each without the other [00:00 – 02:00]

- Religion without philosophy is sentiment — one simply believes, and can become fanatical `00:00 – 02:00`.
- Philosophy without religion is mental speculation — one thinks "it's like this, it's like that," but no transformation happens `02:00`.
- **The newspaper philosopher:** someone wonders, "There is so much order in the world, maybe God exists" — and then, having thought that much, simply picks up the newspaper `02:00`.

#### Spirituality as a science: theory and experiment [04:00 – 10:00]

- Science has theory and experiment; when both are present, science becomes a complete body of knowledge `04:00`.
- **The falling fruit:** many people had seen fruit falling before, but only one thought about why — and from that observation came the principle of gravity. Theory means observing something and trying to find the explanation `04:00`.
- The philosophy of spirituality: "I am not the body but the soul." I don't want to die — so where does my longing for immortality come from? It must come from a part of me that is actually immortal; by such logic we can point toward the soul `06:00`.
- Religion is like the experiment: if I practise service, I experience a higher happiness, by which lower desires go away `06:00 – 08:00`.
- **Confirmation:** unwanted habits I wanted to give up earlier but could not, I become able to give up after starting the practices — this is a confirmation for us `08:00`.
- When both philosophy and religion are present, spirituality becomes a higher-dimensional science `08:00`:
  - It deals with dimensions higher than modern science deals with.
  - It involves inner transformation — the experience of a higher reality.
- **Repeatability:** a good scientific theory is repeatable — gravity applies everywhere. Likewise anyone, in any part of the world and from any background, who practises experiences a higher happiness and becomes gradually free from the lower drives `08:00 – 10:00`.
- In the science of bhakti, the heart is the laboratory and chanting is the process `10:00`.

#### Theory alone, experiment alone [10:00 – 14:00]

- Philosophy without religion remains speculative and unverified — a theory with no experiment `10:00`.
- Someone who simply does experiments without knowing the theory finds them pointless `10:00`:
  - **Acid and alkali:** mixing acid and alkali gives salt and water; someone who keeps pouring acid without knowing the theory or the purpose learns nothing.
- When people do religion ritualistically, without deep conviction about what they are doing and why, something is done but there is no clear understanding `12:00`.
- People who go to temples and pray based only on feelings cannot last long when alternatives come up or obstacles arise `12:00`.
- With a background of philosophical understanding, we learn from whatever happens to us, and growth comes `14:00`.

#### The morning programme as balance [14:00]

- We need a balance of both. The morning programme has _darśana_ and _kīrtana_ — the religion part — and the class — the philosophy part; combining them moves our life forward `14:00`.
- In this context philosophy corresponds to _jñāna_, and religion to karma-yoga and bhakti `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Philosophy without practice is speculation and practice without philosophy is sentiment; like theory and experiment in science, the two together make spirituality a verifiable, transforming science whose laboratory is the heart. [00:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.4 [16:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.4 -->

**Context.** The goal is not achieved merely by adopting the external forms of renunciation; inner purification is required `16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _External forms_ — the outward adoption of renunciation `16:00`.
- _Inner purification_ — what actually must happen; external forms help only when it is present `16:00`.

#### External forms need inner purification [16:00 – 18:00]

- Achievement is not by external forms; there has to be inner purification `16:00`.
- When inner purification is there, adopting external forms helps; when it is not, adopting external forms does not lead to any change `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### The same danger in bhakti [18:00]

- In bhakti too we have external forms — a _dhotī_ and _kurtā_, devotional dress `18:00`.
- One may keep the intention of exploitation and adopt devotional dress so that one can exploit better and more easily — a problem discussed in the next verses `18:00`.
- The teacher cites a verse on devotion without the regulations of the _Pañcarātra_ in this connection `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Outer renunciation or devotional dress helps only when it accompanies inner purification; without it, the external form changes nothing — and can even become a cover for exploitation. [16:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.5 [20:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.5 -->

**Context.** Giving up activity is very difficult: everyone is helplessly forced to act `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Avaśaḥ_ — helplessly; the verse begins its point with this idea of being forced to act `20:00`.

#### Forced by the modes? [20:00 – 22:00]

- Giving up activity in principle is very difficult `20:00`.
- One may ask: are we helplessly forced to act because we are under the influence of the modes, so that once free from the modes we will no longer be forced to act? `20:00 – 22:00`.
- The purport counters this: it is not a question of embodied life; it is the nature of the soul to be active `22:00`.

#### The soul supplies energy; the modes give direction [22:00]

- Energy and action are not produced by the modes; they come from the soul. The direction is determined by the modes `22:00`.
- Because of the modes, the soul's energy is diverted toward materialistic things `22:00`.
- When embodied, the soul is active; when liberated, it is even more active `22:00`.

#### The trekker's sack [22:00 – 24:00]

- **Climbing with a heavy sack:** someone climbing a mountain with a big trekking sack on the shoulders is still running fast; if the sack were removed, he could run much faster `22:00 – 24:00`.
- So too the soul, burdened by the body, is already so enthusiastically active; free from the burden of the body, it is much more active `24:00`.
- The purport continues the theme that it is better to do devotional activities: even if one falls, there is no loss `24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Activity is the soul's own nature, not a product of the modes — the modes only direct it; so the solution is not to stop acting but to redirect the soul's energy toward Kṛṣṇa. [20:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.6 [24:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.6 -->

**Context.** One who restrains the working senses but keeps the mind dwelling on sense objects deludes himself and is called a pretender `24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karmendriya_ — the working senses, which such a person restrains `24:00`.
- _Mūḍhātmā_ — a deluded soul: one who thinks "I am advanced" `24:00`.
- _Mithyācāra_ — the pretender, whose external behaviour is false `28:00`.

#### Prestige without responsibility [26:00 – 28:00]

- Such pretence comes from wanting a position without taking up its responsibility and sacrifice — accepting the prestige without discharging the duties expected of the position `26:00`.
- **The absent ruler:** someone becomes the king, or a politician, collects taxes and enjoys, but when citizens come with grievances he is not available `26:00`.
- The false renunciate: people come and offer him things, and he privately engages in sense activity `26:00`.
- Every position brings some prestige and power, but along with them comes responsibility and sacrifice `26:00`:
  - A renunciate has to take up the responsibility of sharing knowledge.
  - He has taken a vow of giving up pleasures, especially sexual pleasure.
- The _Bhāgavatam_ says one should not become a guru unless one is capable of delivering one's dependants — guiding and training them so that they progress toward liberation. Accepting the respect given to a guru without doing the responsibility is the same fault `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### The picture gallery of the mind [28:00 – 30:00]

- We have a picture gallery in the mind of the sense pleasures we have enjoyed `28:00`.
- Sometimes a spontaneous, high-resolution flashback starts in that mental gallery `28:00`.
- Occasional thoughts that are not focused on are not the problem; _mithyācāra_ is the conscious, intentional cultivation of those memories — refreshing them without any external exposure `30:00`.

#### Kinds of practitioners [30:00 – 32:00]

- The teacher distinguishes several kinds of practitioners, from those who are not perfect but are trying for purification to those far from trying for purification `30:00`.
- Two of them are contrasted directly `30:00 – 32:00`:
  - One who has made an external change but continues the old ways under the garb of that change.
  - One who uses the external as a means for secretly indulging inwardly — very unfortunate; a cheater.
- Such a person may even speak great philosophy just to show spiritual advancement `32:00`.

#### Which path is Kṛṣṇa speaking of here? [32:00 – 36:00]

- Kṛṣṇa speaks at multiple levels; in this section He is primarily speaking not of bhakti-yoga but of jñāna-yoga and karma-yoga `32:00`.
- In jñāna-yoga there is inactivity and just meditation; if there is no purification, that meditation becomes a pretence `32:00`.
- This verse does not refer to a person who renounces home and is actually practising bhakti — that is discussed later `32:00`.
- In bhakti, renunciation is not an end in itself; the renounced order is a facility for _sādhana_ `34:00`:
  - We adopt renunciation on the path of bhakti so that we can practise bhakti better.
  - Not because renunciation by itself leads to purification — it is a tool.
  - Practising bhakti involves activity.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The pretender holds a position without its responsibility and nurses the mind's gallery of past pleasures behind an outer show of restraint; in bhakti, renunciation is only a tool for better service, never a substitute for inner purity. [24:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.7 [36:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.7 -->

**Context.** In contrast to the pretender of 3.6, Kṛṣṇa speaks of engaging the working senses: it is better to regulate the senses and practise karma-yoga with detachment `36:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karmendriya_ — the working senses, which are engaged here `36:00`, `38:00`.
- _Jñānendriya_ — the knowledge-acquiring senses; for the _yogīs_ and _jñānīs_ these should become inactive `36:00`.

#### Why the working senses are emphasised [36:00 – 38:00]

- The primary cause of bondage is not just perceiving sensorially stimulating objects, but acting on them `36:00`.
- **The delicious food:** I see a delicious food item, go there and stuff myself until I am full — that causes problems from the health point of view, and if the food is not offered to Kṛṣṇa, it causes bondage `36:00 – 38:00`.
- So Kṛṣṇa emphasises the _karmendriyas_, because acting with them primarily produces bondage; it is better to regulate the senses and practise karma-yoga with detachment `38:00`.

#### Out of sight, out of mind? [38:00]

- How can we practise karma-yoga if we have no level of detachment? `38:00`.
- Some feel it is better to go away from sense objects so they cannot fall prey; others feel they are in constant contact with the world `38:00`.
- "Out of sight, out of mind" is true to some extent — if we don't perceive sense objects, they may go out of mind `38:00`.
- But when something is deeply rooted in the mind, going out of sight makes it even more prominent, and we start searching, "Where can I find it?" `38:00`.
- So detachment must be there to some extent, and that comes from _jñāna_ — when we have proper philosophical understanding `38:00`.

#### The sincere sweeper and the charlatan meditator [40:00 – 42:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that a sincere sweeper in the street is far better than a charlatan meditator who meditates only for the sake of making a living `40:00`.
- Spiritual culture respects those who adopt the garb of renunciation; it should not be adopted because "I don't want to take responsibility" `40:00`.
- Some think: "I'll leave my job, wear saffron, and people will give me donations" `40:00`.
- Renunciation should not be taken as an escape from the hard work of material life or for easy living `42:00`.

#### How pretenders harm everyone [42:00 – 46:00]

- Such people have ruined spiritual culture: they exploit others, their exploitation gets widely publicised, and people form a negative perception of everyone in the renounced order `42:00`.
- Proper understanding is needed from both sides — those accepting the renounced order and those respecting it `42:00`.
- The saffron dress is to be respected, but if the person is not living properly we cannot associate with him `42:00`.
- The same applies to someone bearing devotional marks — chanting, wearing neck beads: if he is not behaving properly, we can respect him from a distance but should not associate `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Without proper understanding of what renunciation is and how it is to be practised, this misuse is perpetuated and multiplied `44:00`.
- It happens especially in holy places: pilgrims want to give donations and do service, so some people go there and adopt saffron just to get easy donations `44:00`.
- So we must be cautious from our side `44:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's safeguard and the real focus [44:00 – 48:00]

- Prabhupāda set a strong standard, with systematic understanding, so that people not serious about spiritual life will not come into the renounced order just for prestige and donations — a safeguard `44:00 – 46:00`.
- Still, we have to be careful `46:00`.
- Overall, our focus is not on renouncing but on connecting — developing a relationship with Kṛṣṇa, before and even after renunciation `46:00`.
- Developing that relationship means taking responsibility: Kṛṣṇa wants His mission to spread, and through taking responsibility we express our affection and devotion to Kṛṣṇa `46:00 – 48:00`.
- From the _varṇāśrama_ point of view, even a sweeper becomes purified if he does his activities as responsibility `48:00`.
- The externals are useful to the extent that they aid the internals `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A sincere worker who regulates his senses and acts with detachment is superior to a show of renunciation; outer forms deserve respect only when backed by inner life, and the devotee's real focus is not renouncing but connecting with Kṛṣṇa by taking responsibility in His service. [36:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.8 [48:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.8 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa gives another reason for prescribed duty: without it one cannot even maintain the body `48:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śarīra-yātrā_ — the journey or maintenance of the body; the soul travels from one place to another, and one lifetime is like a pilgrimage in the body `48:00 – 50:00`.
- _Akarmaṇaḥ_ — without work; one would not be able to maintain the body `50:00`.

#### Another reason to work [50:00]

- Here Kṛṣṇa gives another reason: maintaining the body. Without prescribed duty one cannot even survive `50:00`.
- Prescribed duty benefits oneself and benefits others `50:00`.

#### Varṇa as material dharma [50:00]

- In Vedic culture, by one's _varṇa_ one makes a contribution to society according to one's psycho-physical nature, and also earns one's living by it — that is material _dharma_ `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even keeping the body alive on its lifelong pilgrimage requires work, so prescribed duty — contributing to society according to one's nature while earning one's living — benefits both oneself and others. [48:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.9 [52:00 – 59:47]

<!-- vedabase:3.9 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa introduces work as sacrifice: when one does one's work as a sacrifice, it does not bind — one is liberated `52:00`, `58:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yajña_ — sacrifice; this is the first reference to the concept in the Bhagavad-gītā, and it recurs in this chapter `54:00`.
- _Āhuti_ — the offerings made into the sacrificial fire; part of the _form_ of _yajña_ `54:00`.

#### Form and principle [54:00]

- Like _yoga_, _yajña_ has a form and a principle `54:00`:
  - **Yoga** — the form is sitting in a particular posture; the principle is connection of the soul with the Supersoul.
  - **Yajña** — the form is the fire and the offerings into it; the principle is giving to Viṣṇu.
- People generally focus only on the forms `54:00`.
- When a fire sacrifice is performed properly according to scriptural prescription, the fire becomes the mouth of Lord Viṣṇu; the teacher quotes Vedic statements that Agni and Viṣṇu are connected and that Viṣṇu is the Supreme `54:00 – 56:00`.
- What is offered to Agni goes to the _devas_ — the universal government; ultimately everything goes to Viṣṇu. Details come in the next class `56:00`.

#### The credit-card analogy [56:00 – 58:00]

- I put ₹5,000 or ₹50,000 in the bank and get a credit card. Someone who does not know the modern financial system might think that putting money in and getting a card is a waste of money `56:00 – 58:00`.
- Similarly, someone might think that putting things into the fire is simply losing everything — but it is not `58:00`.

#### Sacrifice as a principle [58:00 – 59:47]

- In this verse Kṛṣṇa is speaking about the principle, not about a form of _yajña_ `58:00`.
- The principle: what I could have enjoyed, I give up for the Lord — that is sacrifice `58:00`.
- When you do your work as a sacrifice you will not be bound; you will be liberated `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Yajña's essence is not the fire but the principle of giving to Viṣṇu what we could have enjoyed ourselves; work done in that spirit, far from being a loss, frees us from bondage. [54:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
