## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 3.37 [00:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.37 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa answers: it is lust, born of the mode of passion, which transforms into anger — the all-devouring, sinful enemy of this world `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kāma_ — lust; _krodha_ — anger or wrath `00:00`.
- _Mahāśana_ — the great devourer; _mahā-pāpmā_ — extremely sinful `00:00`.
- _Vairī_ — the enemy `00:00`.
- _Iha_ — "in this world," a word used often in the Gītā, e.g. in Chapter 5 on those situated in equanimity `00:00 – 02:00`.
- Transmogrify — to change into an ugly form; lust transmogrifies into anger `02:00`.

#### Parts of spiritual bliss [02:00 – 04:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that living entities are parts and parcels of spiritual bliss — not just of the Lord. The usage indicates that we are meant to be blissful.
- Kṛṣṇa is independently blissful; we are dependently blissful. Being tiny, if we try to enjoy separately from Kṛṣṇa we get only tiny happiness; connected to Him, we get huge happiness.
- The desire to enjoy separately also disconnects us from Kṛṣṇa, so we seek enjoyment where there is none — in the objects of this world — causing bondage and suffering `04:00`.

#### From sense inquiry to Brahman inquiry [04:00 – 08:00]

- Prabhupāda: the material creation gives the conditioned souls facility to fulfil lustful propensities; when completely baffled by prolonged lustful activities, they begin to inquire about their real position `04:00`.
- While one is caught up in inquiring "where can I enjoy?", one has no impetus for _brahma-jijñāsā_. Culture, media and advertising keep offering new avenues of sense enjoyment `04:00`.
- **300 TV channels:** people are more eager while surfing than watching — "maybe the next is better" — but once watching, it's boring. Happiness is on no channel except the _bhakti_ channel — our connection with Kṛṣṇa `06:00`.
- _Brahma-jijñāsā_ is not idle armchair speculation followed by switching on music or TV; it is serious inquiry born of dissatisfaction: "what is in this world doesn't satisfy me; what lies beyond?" `06:00 – 08:00`.
- Realisations of the futility of sense pleasure don't stay, because the world keeps promising gratification; we need a channel to experience spiritual happiness so that we stop chasing change after change `08:00`.

#### "Human life is meant to become frustrated" [08:00 – 12:00]

- Prabhupāda told hippies at a festival in America that though others saw them as frustrated, he appreciated them: human life is meant to become frustrated — with material enjoyment `08:00`.
- After World War II America became enormously prosperous; the veterans' children had from birth the house, family, job and bank balance their parents had dreamed of — and felt there was nothing in it `10:00`.
- Seeking "something more," many turned to drugs; then drugs got into them and it became a search for drugs, not spirituality. To those who sought spiritual experience, Prabhupāda said chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa gives a far greater experience `10:00`.

#### Lust originates in the Supreme [12:00]

- Prabhupāda says the origin of lust, too, is in the Supreme, since everything comes from Him: the desire to love and be happy, which underlies male–female attraction, is present in the Supreme.
- In its original form it is meant to be directed to the Supreme; misdirected, it causes suffering. Directed downward it becomes anger; directed upward it becomes transcendent — Prabhupāda gives the example of Hanumān spiritualising anger.
- Lust is a misdirection of our original search for happiness.

#### All-devouring: nothing remains sacred [12:00 – 18:00]

- Once triggered, lust can go out of control and blind a person; the teacher lists progressively perverse forms, down to the abuse of children within families. Once lust is triggered, for many nothing remains sacred `12:00 – 14:00`.
- People fail to link what they eagerly watch in the media with what horrifies them in the news: the same force promoted in billboards and movies, gaining deadly momentum, becomes violence `14:00 – 16:00`.
- One may say consensual romance and brutal violence are different — there is a difference, but it is seen only when not blinded by lust; lust does not respect our moral boundaries `16:00`.
- Most people have too positive a self-image — "I'd never do such things." The teacher recalls Ajāmila: had someone told him, before he saw a _śūdra_ couple embracing, that within a week he would throw out his wife and parents and live with a prostitute, he would have been outraged — yet lust devastated him `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Lust is a misdirection of the soul's original desire for bliss, which comes from Kṛṣṇa; turned toward matter it becomes the all-devouring enemy that respects no moral boundary, and only frustration with material enjoyment opens serious inquiry into the Absolute. [02:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.38 [18:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.38 -->

**Context.** We usually picture enemies with guns or bombs; on the battlefield, Arjuna's enemies stood visibly before him. What kind of enemy is lust, and how does it attack? Kṛṣṇa gives three examples of how lust covers the living entity `18:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Fire covered by smoke; mirror covered by dust; embryo covered by the womb `20:00`.
- A covering — lust makes us spiritually forgetful and materially desirous `26:00`.

#### Three degrees of covering [20:00 – 22:00]

Prabhupāda explains in the purport, and an _ācārya_ elaborates in his commentary:

- **Fire covered by smoke — human species:** a thin, penetrable covering; blow and stoke properly and the smoke moves aside.
- **Mirror covered by dust — animal species:** the dust can be so thick that one doesn't even know there is a mirror; animals take time to rise to a consciousness that can pursue spirituality.
- **Embryo covered by the womb — plants:** a super-thick covering. The embryo is helpless and cannot come out prematurely; plants cannot make effort, so they must pass through their life course over time until they evolve to the human species `20:00 – 22:00`.

#### Is lust a thing or a feeling? [22:00 – 26:00]

- Both. There are lusty _impressions_ stored in the mind (from images seen or things heard) and lusty _desires_ triggered from them `22:00`.
- People have been exposed to different degrees; with internet pornography many have "so much garbage" in their minds `22:00 – 24:00`.
- The impressions are a thing — subtle, not gross like a table or wall, but subtle things are real. They provoke feelings: without them, men and women seeing each other would not be so agitated `24:00`.
- We should not assume we are pure devotees; we should assume the impressions are there. But we need not let the feelings take control — we can say no `24:00`.
- **External and internal stimuli:** we can't stop obscene hoardings or provocative dress, but we can avoid exposing ourselves too much and avoid succumbing. Likewise, past impressions push and prod "do this, do this" — and we can say no `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### Covering and dragging: the movie theatre [26:00 – 28:00]

- Māyā has two energies: one covers, making us forget our spiritual identity; the other drags us into material illusion.
- **Movie theatre:** first the lights go off, and people forget the reality around them; then the screen lights up, and they are dragged into the illusion. Lust does both — rips the soul out of reality and propels it into illusion `26:00 – 28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Lust covers the soul in degrees — thin smoke for humans, thick dust for animals, the womb for plants — so the human form is the opportunity to blow it aside; stored impressions will provoke feelings, but we can refuse to let them control us. [20:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.39 [28:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.39 -->

**Context.** Lust covers the knowledge of the knower — the soul — and Kṛṣṇa now calls it the eternal enemy, burning like insatiable fire `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jñānī_ — here the soul, who is by nature the knower; knowing is a faculty of the soul, and it is this awareness of spiritual identity that lust covers `28:00`.
- _Nitya-vairī_ — eternal enemy; "a frightening word" `28:00 – 30:00`.
- _Duṣpūra_ — insatiable `32:00`.

#### The worst enemy: longevity, identity, strategy [28:00 – 34:00]

- **Longevity:** a worldly enemy may torture or kill us, and the enmity ends; lust torments us lifetime after lifetime `30:00`.
- **Identity:** we don't even recognise the enemy. When lust is unfulfilled we become angry — not at lust, but at whoever obstructs it. Like a spy in our ranks: we aim outward while it blows a bomb under our feet from within `30:00`.
- **Strategy:** a normal enemy uses its own forces; lust uses _our_ forces to destroy us. No one forces the lusty person to do wrong — he does it himself, e.g. contracting sexually transmitted diseases `32:00`.
- **Computer virus:** it uses our own memory, electricity and programming to damage the computer. Or: someone ready to shoot the enemy is told, "turn the gun on yourself." So Ajāmila, a well-educated, cultured _brāhmaṇa_, destroyed himself `32:00`.

#### Insatiable fire [34:00 – 38:00]

- Even romantic songs — in English and Hindi films — acknowledge lust as fire. Their illusion is that indulgence will extinguish it; indulgence is fuel, and makes it blaze further `34:00`.
- In the Ninth Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_, Yayāti, captivated by Devayānī, enjoyed with her for 100 years yet was never satisfied, and concluded that lust is never satisfied by indulgence — like oblations poured into a fire `34:00 – 36:00`.

#### Then what is the point of gṛhastha-āśrama? [36:00 – 38:00]

- If indulgence always fuels lust, how does dharmic married life purify? It is not the indulgence that purifies but the observance of _dharma_ `36:00`.
- _Gṛhastha_ versus _gṛhamedhī_: the _gṛhamedhī_ is interested only in sense gratification; for the _gṛhastha_, sex is only one part of life, with _dharma_ and Kṛṣṇa at the centre.
- Practising _dharma_ gives a higher happiness, so one can compare and see that the lower is lesser. Without _dharma_, lust only grows — dissatisfaction with one partner leads to affairs `36:00 – 38:00`.
- There is a dharmic allowance, but under regulation, so that by _dharma_ one gradually gets a higher taste that frees one from it `38:00`.

#### Shackled by sex life [38:00 – 40:00]

- Prabhupāda: this world is like a prison, and the disobedient are shackled by sex life.
- No one thinks of sex life as a shackle, but one cannot enjoy it without thinking "I am the body" — misidentification is itself bondage, and acting on the bodily platform brings further bondage.
- The sex desire binds the soul to the body, because "this is how I want to enjoy, and for that I need the body." Thus souls unwilling to serve Kṛṣṇa are imprisoned in the bodily conception, with old age, disease, death, repeated birth and the threefold miseries `40:00`.

#### Advancement that increases bondage [40:00 – 44:00]

- Prabhupāda: advancement of material civilisation on the basis of sense gratification means increasing the duration of material existence `40:00`.
- In the past, a lusty person's options were limited; now one click brings images from all over the world, including the perverse fantasies of others, contaminating minds `40:00 – 42:00`.
- The teacher says the pornography industry earns more than the franchises of America's baseball and football leagues; pornography addiction is very serious `42:00`.
- **The teacher's engineering story:** interested in how nature works, he learned in his fourth year that a major application of electronics was better televisions. "If I use my intelligence to build a better idiot box that makes people idiots, then I'm an idiot." Soon after, he was introduced to Kṛṣṇa consciousness `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Scientists aren't free to research anything; funding comes mainly from the defence industry (defending) and the consumer industry (eating, sleeping, mating) `44:00`.

#### The feeling of happiness is the enemy [44:00 – 50:00]

- Prabhupāda's final sentence: while enjoying sense gratification there may be some feeling of happiness, but that so-called feeling of happiness is the ultimate enemy of the sense enjoyer — "a statement we can embed in our heart" `44:00`.
- Poets and movies hugely exaggerate it; the body's capacity to enjoy is very limited `46:00`.
- **Mouse and cheese:** there is pleasure in the cheese, but it is the very thing that traps the mouse and costs its life. **Fish and bait:** "so delicious" — then the hook drags it to its death. Likewise the drinker's relief `46:00`.
- A bitter pill: not just "don't do it," but that the happiness itself is the enemy `48:00`.
- People call morality relative, but consequences are not: if one contracts AIDS through illicit sex, there is no relativity in that. The obnoxious is obnoxious because it is noxious — poisonous `48:00`.
- **_Viṣa_ and _viṣaya_:** poison destroys only after it is consumed; sense objects destroy just by contemplation, contaminating consciousness. So _viṣaya_ is more dangerous than _viṣa_ `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Lust is the eternal enemy — lasting lifetimes, hiding its identity, turning our own strength against us, and blazing higher with every indulgence; its pleasure is the bait in the trap, so only dharma and a higher taste, not indulgence, bring freedom. [28:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.40 [50:00 – 58:18]

<!-- vedabase:3.40 -->

**Context.** Having described how the enemy acts — by covering intelligence — Kṛṣṇa says where it resides: in the senses, mind and intelligence, through which it covers the soul's knowledge and bewilders him `50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Senses, mind and intelligence — the sitting places of lust `50:00`.

#### Lust is within us, not in the object [50:00 – 52:00]

- Some men say, "If women dress inappropriately, we will naturally become lusty." But lust is not situated in the female form or dress.
- A baby in its mother's arms touches her body without any agitation. If lust were in the form, the baby would also be agitated; lust is provoked through the form but present in our own mind, intelligence and senses.
- So we cannot blame others for our lust.

#### Responsibility for wrongdoing [52:00]

- The teacher calls "blaming the victim" a very sad phenomenon: however a woman dresses, if she is forcibly violated, responsibility lies on the man, not on her. Even a prostitute is meant to be paid, not violated.
- No one can wash off responsibility with "people dress like that."

#### Dress sends messages [52:00 – 58:00]

- Another perspective: how we dress and behave sends messages, naturally and inevitably. Revealing, provocative clothing sends the message "see me as a sex object"; one should ask whether that is the message one wants to send `52:00 – 54:00`.
- **Money in a dark alley:** someone walks a dark, lonely alley with banknotes sticking out of every pocket and is robbed. Legally the thieves are criminals and must be punished; whether the money was shown or hidden doesn't change that. But it is a matter of common sense — turning it into a complicated rights debate muddies the discussion `54:00 – 56:00`.
- Lust is situated in female senses too, not only male; just as men must regulate their senses, women regulate theirs partly through modest, non-provocative dress. Responsibility is on both sides `56:00`.
- Since lust is in the mind, intelligence and senses, one must be freed from its influence there; how to control and conquer it is taught in 3.41–3.43, next class `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Lust resides in our own senses, mind and intelligence, not in the object — so we cannot blame others for it, the wrongdoer alone bears responsibility for violence, and everyone shares the duty of regulating their senses and the messages they send. [50:00 – 58:18]
</Callout>
