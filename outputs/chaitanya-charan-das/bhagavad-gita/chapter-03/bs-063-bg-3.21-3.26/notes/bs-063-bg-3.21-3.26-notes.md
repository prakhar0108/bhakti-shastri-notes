## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 3.21 [00:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.21 -->

**Context.** Whatever a great person does, common people follow, and whatever standard such a person sets, the world pursues `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ācarati_ — to behave, to act `00:00`.
- _Śreṣṭha_ — the great people: leaders, those influential in society, those with position `02:00`.
- _Pramāṇa_ — standard; the same word is used for the standard, authoritative ways of acquiring knowledge `02:00`.

#### A society is judged by its heroes [02:00 – 08:00]

- The calibre of a society can be judged in many ways, but one important way is by the calibre of its heroes — whom it considers ideal and exemplary `02:00`.
- In India even fifty years ago, the leaders striving for independence and social well-being were the role models; even if most were not spiritually minded in the proper devotional sense, they were exemplary in many other ways — for example, Gandhi, called the father of the nation and given the title _Mahātmā_, was seen as simple and widely concerned about others `02:00 – 04:00`.
- Today most people's models are film stars, sports stars or business magnates, or simply someone with a good career and salary; the level of models has declined and in some cases become distorted `04:00`.
- Entertainment fifty years ago was more holistic; now the hero is often reduced to a one-dimensional character focused on violence and sensuality, which indicates a decline in consciousness `04:00`.
- The same is true in the West: in America's formative years, the models were people concerned for society in general `06:00`.
- Best are models who are spiritually minded; next best, people in goodness whose concerns go beyond their own immediate gratification. Such models elevate people; otherwise people become degraded `06:00`.

#### "Bold" or shameless? [06:00 – 08:00]

- In today's society sin is made fashionable — modelling happens in subtle ways `06:00`.
- When film actresses appear in vulgar, revealing scenes, these are called "bold scenes"; actually it is not boldness, it is shamelessness. Once it is called bold, everybody wants to be bold `06:00`.
- Later in this chapter Kṛṣṇa will describe lust as the eternal enemy of the soul; when lust is glamorised as boldness, perhaps in ten or fifteen years modest dress will be called cowardly — but dressing modestly is intelligence and is desirable `08:00`.

#### Natural leaders and the universal standard [08:00 – 10:00]

- For a child, the parents, the teacher and the king are natural leaders; all of us were children once, so they create formative influences during our growing years `08:00`.
- To the extent they set a good example, people are moulded well; otherwise people are moulded in a distorted way `08:00`.
- What is exemplary behaviour? Changing social norms cannot decide it; there has to be a universal standard, and that standard comes from scripture `08:00 – 10:00`.
- Those who live according to scripture can influence others enormously, so scripture must be recognised as the standard and leaders should live by it `10:00`.

#### The power of a good example: George Harrison [10:00 – 12:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda quoted this very verse to the Beatles, among whom George Harrison was especially receptive: "You are so well known — chant Hare Kṛṣṇa and tell people about Kṛṣṇa; this will purify and benefit them" `10:00`.
- In his early days George Harrison had encouraged drugs, which many then thought were a means to higher consciousness `10:00`.
- When he visited places like New York and San Francisco, he saw that the hippies had not become free but more drug-addicted, living dirty, destitute lives; he felt a sense of moral responsibility `10:00`.
- Encouraged by Prabhupāda, he wrote many songs with the holy names; millions of people were introduced to the Hare Kṛṣṇa _mahā-mantra_ through them, and to some extent Caitanya Mahāprabhu's prediction that His name would be chanted in every town and village came about this way `12:00`.

#### The power of example versus the example of power [12:00 – 14:00]

- The power of example is far stronger than the example of power `12:00`.
  - **Power of example:** people see someone truly peaceful and joyful, chanting and serving, and want to know how.
  - **Example of power:** fanatical religions that say "convert or we will kill you / send you to hell" — this may force conversions, but the heart is not converted or purified `12:00 – 14:00`.
- Prabhupāda was the example: at around seventy he went to America and travelled tirelessly for his last ten years; hippies found him younger and more energetic than themselves because of his regulated life; beyond energy and wisdom, he radiated joy that came from his love for Kṛṣṇa `14:00`.

#### Models, not critics [14:00 – 18:00]

- This verse can be misused to find fault with leaders — "our temple leader doesn't come in the morning..." `14:00`.
- But every person given the opportunity to practise Kṛṣṇa consciousness is called upon to be a leader; as 7.3 says, among thousands hardly one endeavours for perfection, so by mercy we have already been made leaders, even with few followers `16:00`.
- Setting an example is the responsibility of every devotee, not only of leaders `16:00`.
- If some leaders behave unexemplarily, our own behaviour should be exemplary: be proper in _sādhana_ and enthusiastic in service. "Let us be models, not critics" — it is easier to be a critic, and we do not know the reasons behind another's behaviour `16:00`.
- In this way we become part of the solution, not part of the problem `16:00`.
- For Arjuna: if he leaves the battlefield, others facing difficult duties will say, "A great person like Arjuna abandoned duty — why shouldn't I?" `16:00 – 18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Society is shaped by whom it takes as heroes, and the standard for exemplary behaviour comes from scripture; since every devotee is already a leader by mercy, our task is to be models, not critics — the power of example transforms hearts where the example of power cannot. [02:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.22 [18:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.22 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa gives one more example of someone who acts to set an example — Himself: there is no prescribed duty for Him in all the three worlds, nothing He wants and nothing He needs to gain, yet He is engaged in duty `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Want_ and _gain_ — "want" is what one requires; "gain" is what one further desires; Kṛṣṇa has neither `18:00`.
- _Svābhāvikī_ — natural; Kṛṣṇa's knowledge, strength and activity happen naturally `22:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa points toward His position [18:00 – 20:00]

- In the flow of the Gītā, Kṛṣṇa has not yet explicitly revealed Himself as God in the first person, though in 2.61, by asking Arjuna to fix his mind on Him, He indicated that He is not just a teacher but the Supreme Lord `18:00`.
- Here He points toward it: He has no duty because He is the Supreme Lord, subordinate to no one `20:00`.
- Prabhupāda quotes a well-known Upaniṣadic verse in the purport: there are many controllers and many _devas_, but He is the supreme controller and the supreme _deva_ — the Master of all; no one is equal to or above Him to tell Him "do this, don't do this" `20:00`.

#### How things happen without duty [20:00 – 24:00]

- Not a blade of grass moves without God's sanction — so if He has no duty, how does everything happen? Through His many energies `20:00 – 22:00`.
- A very wealthy businessman can press a few buttons and money is transferred or a business started; Kṛṣṇa does not even need to press buttons — He simply desires, and things happen `22:00`.
- His knowledge (_jñāna_), capacity for action (_bala_) and action (_kriyā_) are all natural for Him `22:00`.
- Example: while we are looking at each other, if a fly sits on your thigh, your hand moves and brushes it away, almost without conscious attention; similarly, for Kṛṣṇa everything happens automatically, without struggle `22:00 – 24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Lord with no superior and nothing to gain, effortlessly accomplishes everything through His energies — and yet He still performs duty, to set an example. [18:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.23 – 3.24 [24:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.23 -->

<!-- vedabase:3.24 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says that if He did not engage in duty with great attention, all people would follow His path; the worlds would be ruined, He would be the cause of unwanted population, and all living beings would be destroyed. Here He turns around Arjuna's own concern from the first chapter about social disruption `24:00`, `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Atandritaḥ_ — engaged with great attention, attentively `24:00`, `26:00`.
- _Mama vartma_ — "My path"; all people would follow it. The same third and fourth lines (3.23 c–d) appear again in the more famous 4.11, but with a different meaning there `24:00`.
- _Saṅkara_ — disruption of society; unwanted population — Arjuna's concern in the first chapter `30:00 – 32:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa as an attentive householder [24:00 – 28:00]

- In the 69th–71st chapters of the _Bhāgavatam_'s tenth canto, Nārada Muni comes to see Kṛṣṇa's opulence and finds Him in each palace engaged responsibly in duties `26:00`:
  - arranging marriages of His daughters and sons and welcoming brides,
  - receiving visitors and attending to their needs,
  - hearing and resolving citizens' grievances,
  - welcoming _brāhmaṇas_ and giving charity.
- Kṛṣṇa was simultaneously present in all these palaces — it was no race in which Kṛṣṇa ran ahead of Nārada `26:00`.
- Jīva Gosvāmī observes in the _Sandarbhas_ that the time of day was different in different palaces — morning ablutions in one, lunch in another, evening with family in another, administrative duties in another — showing that Kṛṣṇa controls and pervades not only space but also time `26:00 – 28:00`.
- Though He can be anywhere and everywhere at any time, Kṛṣṇa still attentively performs His duties, and that is how He sets an example `28:00`.

#### Why Arjuna must act: the kṣatriya's role [30:00 – 32:00]

- If Kṛṣṇa did not act responsibly, others would get justification for irresponsibility, and if everyone acts irresponsibly society will crumble `32:00`.
- _Kṣatriyas_ are to set the example of chivalry in protection; if they become neglectful, the citizens will be exploited and there will be devastation `32:00`.
- So Arjuna must set this example; otherwise unrighteousness will prevail, ungodly people will take power and exploit society and women, leading to unwanted population and disastrous future generations `32:00`.

#### Butter-stealing and the rāsa dance: three answers [28:00 – 34:00]

- The objection: Kṛṣṇa stole butter and danced with the _gopīs_, who externally were other men's wives — are these exemplary `28:00`?
  1. **No leadership position:** in Vṛndāvana Kṛṣṇa was a small child and took no public leadership role; only later, as part of the Yadu dynasty, did He take an active role in protecting them `28:00`.
  2. **Arena of love, not arena of duty:** Vṛndāvana is the abode of love; there Kṛṣṇa does whatever intensifies the love in His devotees' hearts. In Mathurā, Dvārakā and Hastināpura He is in the arena of _dharma_ and sets that example; Vṛndāvana is the arena of _prema_ — and that too is an example, of a different kind: how to be constantly absorbed in Kṛṣṇa `28:00 – 30:00`.
  3. **Admire, don't imitate:** Prabhupāda points out in the purport that great personalities have powers we do not have; we can admire them but cannot imitate them `32:00`.

#### The example of Lord Śiva [34:00 – 38:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes _Bhāgavatam_ 10.33.30–31, near the end of the _rāsa-pañcādhyāya_ (chapters 29–33 of the tenth canto), where Parīkṣit Mahārāja asked how Kṛṣṇa could dance with others' wives at night; the answer says one who is not a controller should never imitate the controllers, even in the mind — the foolish imitator will be destroyed `34:00`.
- The example given is Rudra (Lord Śiva) drinking the poison born from the churning of the milk ocean `34:00 – 36:00`:
  - At the request of the _devatās_, under Lord Viṣṇu's guidance and with his consort's permission, he drank the deadly poison.
  - It stopped at his throat, which became bluish-black — hence _Nīla-kaṇṭha_ (blue throat) and _Kāla-kaṇṭha_ (black throat).
  - He held it there not out of fear of dying, but because, as the topmost devotee, he did not want the poison to come near his beloved Lord in his heart and inconvenience Him `36:00`.
- If we drink poison, it goes down — and so do we `36:00`.
- Some followers, unable to drink poison, take _bhāṅg_, claiming Lord Śiva did; but it is a _tāmasic_ intoxicant, and imitating a great personality without that authority simply leads to degradation `38:00`.

#### Two kinds of divine activity [38:00 – 42:00]

- When the Lord or great personalities come to this world, their actions serve two purposes `38:00`:
  - **To set an example** — e.g. Lord Rāma, the ideal king, son and husband.
  - **To demonstrate their position** — e.g. Kṛṣṇa showing the universal form, or showing the whole universe in His mouth to mother Yaśodā; we cannot do these.
- Kṛṣṇa's _rāsa_ is in the second category: it demonstrates His supremacy and is not to be imitated `40:00`.
- When the Supreme seems to violate laws, it demonstrates His superiority to those laws; if He were subordinate to the laws, the laws would be supreme — a deviant understanding `40:00`.
- We need laws because we are often moved by selfish desires; Kṛṣṇa has no lower self to regulate — He acts purely out of love and for everyone's supreme well-being `40:00 – 42:00`.
- If someone wants to imitate the _rāsa_: at seven Kṛṣṇa lifted Govardhana, and at eight He performed the _rāsa_ — "first lift Govardhana" `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa, though all-powerful, attentively performs His duties so that others will not be justified in abandoning theirs; His pastimes that transcend law demonstrate His supremacy and are to be admired, never imitated. [24:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.25 [42:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.25 -->

**Context.** Anticipating Arjuna's question — "Even if I set an example, won't I still be bound by reactions?" — Kṛṣṇa now distinguishes _karma-kāṇḍa_ from _karma-yoga_: as the ignorant act with attachment, the learned should act without attachment, to lead people on the right path `42:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Saktāḥ_ / _asaktaḥ_ — attached / unattached `42:00 – 44:00`.
- _Avidvāṁsaḥ_ / _vidvān_ — the ignorant / the learned and wise `44:00`.
- _Loka-saṅgraha_ — acting for the purpose of setting an example for others `44:00`.

#### Same action, different consciousness [44:00]

- The ignorant and attached refers to the _karma-kāṇḍīs_; the wise and detached to the _karma-yogīs_ `44:00`.
- Externally both perform the prescribed duties; but the _karma-kāṇḍīs_ think of heaven and material enjoyment, while the _karma-yogīs_ think of liberation and returning to the Supreme Lord `44:00`.
- One who acts with the purpose of becoming liberated and going back to Godhead never gets bound, but keeps moving forward and eventually returns to the Absolute Truth `44:00`.

#### An accessible example: the role of householders [46:00 – 48:00]

- Those who are attached need an example that is accessible and available to them; only then can they practise spiritual life `46:00`.
- In the early days of the movement, becoming a devotee practically meant dropping out of society and joining the temple full time — even householders lived in the temple `46:00`.
- Now the movement is primarily congregation-based. The renounced order has an important place, but for most people a realistic example of being spiritual while in the world must come from the _gṛhasthas_ `46:00`.
- When _gṛhasthas_ set a proper example, people see that spiritual life is not impractical or hypothetical `46:00`.

#### Two brigades in the same army [46:00 – 50:00]

- It is not a matter of superior and inferior: _brahmacārīs_ and _gṛhasthas_ are like two brigades in the same army — the army of Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Lord Kṛṣṇa — fulfilling Kṛṣṇa's mission `46:00 – 48:00`.
- A conflict between the orders came to a head in Māyāpur in 1976; Prabhupāda strongly made the point that one who knows Kṛṣṇa and is devoted to Him is advanced — we cannot label some _āśramas_ as lower and others higher `48:00`.
- The pyramid of example `48:00`:
  - _Brahmacārīs_ and _sannyāsīs_ demonstrate the vertical connection, showing that devotion to God brings real happiness, meaning and fulfilment.
  - _Gṛhasthas_ have both horizontal and vertical connections, and set an example for the broader society.
  - The broader society has only horizontal connections.
- With only _gṛhasthas_, people would feel spiritual life is a part-time affair; Prabhupāda's presentation is that _bhakti_ is not a part of our life — _bhakti_ is our life, continuing over many lifetimes `48:00 – 50:00`.
- Every _varṇa_ and every _āśrama_ plays an important, irreplaceable role `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The karma-kāṇḍī and the karma-yogī may act alike, but the yogī acts for liberation and so is not bound; living in the world with that consciousness — as householders especially do — gives others an accessible example, while every āśrama has an irreplaceable role. [44:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.26 [50:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.26 -->

**Context.** The learned should not disturb the minds of the ignorant who are attached to fruitive action, but should engage them in all sorts of work while acting in devotion themselves `50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Buddhi-bheda_ — disturbing the intelligence or mind of others `50:00`.
- _Dovetail_ — Prabhupāda's translation here, a word he often used: to dovetail activities in Kṛṣṇa's service `50:00`.

#### Dovetailing talents [50:00 – 52:00]

- The whole principle of _bhakti_ centres on this: a wise person should set an example of how to dovetail activities in devotional service `50:00 – 52:00`.
- Prabhupāda was very resourceful in engaging devotees according to their talents `52:00`:
  - artists painted pictures for his books and temples,
  - good cooks were asked to cook,
  - intellectually inclined devotees were asked to write.
- The perfection of our talents is to use them in Kṛṣṇa's service; we do not have to tell the world that everything is material and must be rejected `52:00`.
- The purpose of all Vedic rituals is to bring people to Kṛṣṇa; those who cannot come directly should be brought gradually `52:00`.

#### Renouncing from a "higher" responsibility [52:00 – 54:00]

- Kṛṣṇa acknowledges that the renounced order may be higher than acting in the world; still, if people see responsible persons giving up their actions, their minds will be disrupted `52:00`.
- Someone may argue: "I am not setting a bad example — I am renouncing out of compassion and a sense of higher responsibility, not frustration" `52:00 – 54:00`.
- Even so, one must act in a way people can understand; if they misunderstand, their minds get disturbed and they lose the scope for further elevation `54:00`.
- So set a good example, and encourage others to practise devotional service according to their level `54:00`.
- The next class will take up the vision of the ignorant and the vision of the wise `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The wise do not unsettle others by abruptly renouncing, even for lofty reasons; instead they set an example of dovetailing every talent in Kṛṣṇa's service and gently encourage each person at his or her own level. [50:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
