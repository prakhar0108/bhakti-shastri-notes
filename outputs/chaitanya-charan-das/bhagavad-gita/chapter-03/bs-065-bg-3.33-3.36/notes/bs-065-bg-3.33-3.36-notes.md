## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 3.33 [00:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.33 -->

**Context.** Everyone, even the learned, acts according to his nature; "what will repression accomplish?" The teacher notes this can seem confusing, since we usually say wrong desires must be suppressed `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prakṛti_ — here not lust, anger and illusion, but one's propensity within the four _varṇas_ — _brāhmaṇa_, _kṣatriya_, _vaiśya_, _śūdra_ — which cannot be repressed `02:00`.
- _Vāsanā_ — material desire, generated by _anarthas_; superficial to one's psychophysical nature and not harmonisable with _dharma_ `04:00`.

#### Prakṛti versus vāsanā [02:00 – 06:00]

- The contact of the soul with the body produces two kinds of urges: some are to be rejected and some dovetailed. Understanding the difference is vital for proper spiritual practice.
- _Prakṛti_ (material nature) is innate to our psychophysical nature and harmonisable with _dharma_. For an intellectually oriented _brāhmaṇa_, acting intellectually is natural — not innate to the soul, but to the soul embodied in that body — and not sinful in itself `04:00`.
- _Vāsanā_ is generated by _anarthas_, not by the soul–body contact. Anyone from _brāhmaṇa_ to outcaste can be infected by _anarthas_ and must regulate the resulting _vāsanās_; the level and method of regulation may differ `04:00`.

#### Varṇa is by qualities, and bhakti elevates all [06:00 – 08:00]

- A _śūdra_ is generally considered lower because he is less capable of sense control. If made to live like a _brāhmaṇa_ — a life of cleanliness and purity — he would be miserable, because that psychophysical nature is not suited to it.
- The discussion is about _varṇa_ by qualities, not by birth.
- A _śūdra_ can be elevated gradually by his duty in _varṇāśrama_, and rapidly by devotional service. As Mahāprabhu describes, one who understands Kṛṣṇa can even become a guru — both the inner understanding and the outer position are possible `06:00`.
- A _śūdra_ who becomes a devotee gives up pandering to lust, anger and greed, but need not give up his occupational duties `08:00`.

#### Hidden modes in so-called spiritualists [08:00 – 10:00]

- Prabhupāda: many so-called spiritualists outwardly pose as advanced, but inwardly or privately are under particular modes they cannot suppress.
- _Inwardly_ — they express it to no one; _privately_ — they express it, but hidden from public view.
- No one should suddenly give up prescribed duties to become an artificial "transcendentalist." Better to practise _karma-yoga_ in one's position, offer the fruits to Kṛṣṇa and practise devotional service — _karma-yoga_ culminates in _bhakti-yoga_ `10:00`.

#### Refuting the "indulgence" misreading [10:00 – 14:00]

- Some organisations — "materialists in spiritual garb" — make this verse the essence of the Gītā and say: "There's no need to repress; indulge in whatever you desire." The essence verses described by the _ācāryas_ are actually 10.8–10.11 `10:00`.
- **Refutation from context:** just three verses later (3.36), Arjuna asks what impels one to sin, and in 3.37–3.43 Kṛṣṇa explains how to control that propensity through intelligence and sense control, calling lust the eternal enemy to be conquered (3.43). If 3.33 licensed unrestricted enjoyment, there would be no reason for that question or that answer `12:00`.
- The real point: Arjuna, you have a _kṣatriya_ nature; trying to act artificially like a _sannyāsī_ won't work `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Must we transcend our nature? [14:00 – 16:00]

- The important thing is to be engaged productively in devotional service: internally satisfied and comfortable, and externally competent.
- Sometimes circumstances thrust us into services we are not comfortable with; then we must fight and control ourselves temporarily.
- It is a mistaken sentiment that "doing what I dislike proves my surrender." Kṛṣṇa does not want us miserable; He wants us happy.
- But we should not identify so completely with our nature that we think our current material nature is eternal. Hence _sādhana_ is a universal standard for all, beyond which we do our individual services `14:00`.

#### Discovering one's psychophysical nature [16:00 – 20:00]

- Today we cannot simply determine our _varṇa_ by birth family: one born in a _brāhmaṇa_ family may become an engineer or doctor through peer pressure, ambition, social trends or financial need.
- So a second self-discovery is needed: not only "I am a soul," but "what is my psychophysical nature, and how can I best serve Kṛṣṇa in this body?" `16:00`.
- Lack of job satisfaction can come from many factors — pay, hostile environment — but also from work incompatible with one's nature. Whether to change careers is an individual decision `18:00`.
- Devotees need not make discovering their _varṇa_ a priority and postpone Kṛṣṇa consciousness; it will be revealed as we practise and observe ourselves `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
One's varṇa nature (prakṛti) cannot be repressed and need not be resented; it is to be dovetailed in Kṛṣṇa's service through productive, comfortable engagement — while sādhana keeps us from mistaking this temporary nature for our eternal identity. [02:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.34 [20:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.34 -->

**Context.** Attraction and aversion are situated in the senses and their objects; one should not come under their control, for they are enemies — robbers on the path `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Rāga_ and _dveṣa_ — attraction (attachment) and aversion `20:00`.
- Robbers on the path — they steal our spiritual inclinations, our prospects for advancement and our capacity to live by _dharma_ `20:00`.

#### Contradiction with the previous verse? [20:00 – 22:00]

- 3.33 says nature cannot be repressed; 3.34 says don't come under its control. There is no contradiction: here Kṛṣṇa speaks not of _prakṛti_ but of _vāsanā_ — _rāga_ and _dveṣa_ in the senses and sense objects.
- Example: attraction to the opposite sex is situated in the male and female body and mind. It must be regulated, not controlling us.
- Marriage allows expression with regulation. Expression without regulation fails; repression also fails — that is the previous verse `22:00`.

#### How rāga and dveṣa rob us [22:00]

- A commentary explains: the conditioned soul wants sense gratification, so is attracted (_rāga_); when _sādhus_ say "don't engage in sense gratification," he feels aversion (_dveṣa_) toward them and wants them out of his life.
- Succumbing to attachment, or to aversion that drives us from devotee association, steals our opportunity for spiritual advancement.

#### Naturally reluctant [22:00 – 24:00]

- Prabhupāda: those in Kṛṣṇa consciousness are naturally reluctant to engage in material sense gratification — a profound statement, since most of us are naturally eager.
- In the conditioned stage, hearing and chanting are our prescribed duties, while eating, sleeping, mating and defending are our favourite pastimes.
- Devotees are reluctant because they experience a higher happiness, compared to which these pleasures seem insignificant.
- Others should follow scriptural rules, such as restricting sex to marriage `24:00`.

#### Accidents on the royal road [24:00 – 26:00]

- Prabhupāda: bodily necessities are allowed under rules, yet we should not rely on the control of such allowances.
- Marriage gives protection, but if its purpose — husband and wife together serving Kṛṣṇa — is forgotten, it can fuel sense gratification until desires burn away intelligence and dharmic restrictions too.
- Just as there can be accidents even on a royal road (the _śāstric_ arrangement for gradual purification), following regulations does not automatically protect us. If desires flare unrestrictedly, we stop wanting the safeguard, and they knock all safeguards out `26:00`.

#### A psychological necessity [26:00 – 28:00]

- Prabhupāda: sex enjoyment is a necessity for the conditioned soul, allowed under marriage. It is not a necessity for the soul, nor for the liberated soul.
- It is not a physical necessity like food, water or sleep — no one dies without it — but for most people a psychological necessity that is practically irresistible `26:00 – 28:00`.
- Hence renunciants are few: what we truly need is happiness, and one satisfied at the spiritual level no longer needs material happiness; but steady spiritual happiness needs substantial purification, so dharmic marriage is prescribed for most `28:00`.

#### Our suffering is due to detachment [28:00 – 30:00]

- Prabhupāda concludes: no one should be detached from Kṛṣṇa consciousness at any stage; the purpose of detachment from sense attachment is to be situated in Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- Some think all suffering comes from attachment; actually it comes from our _detachment_ — from Kṛṣṇa, of whom we are parts.
- The purpose of _bhakti_ is not primarily detachment but to rectify our detachment from Kṛṣṇa by reviving attachment to Him; that satisfaction makes material attachments fall away. So don't be too troubled by material attachments — focus on devotional attachment `30:00`.

#### Guilt and pseudo-guilt [30:00 – 36:00]

- After falling, we may feel "I'm good for nothing; I've chanted so long and nothing happens." Even guilt can be a temptation `30:00`.
- Temptation broadly means anything that takes us away from Kṛṣṇa: lust and greed by promising pleasure; guilt by building a wall — "I'm so fallen, what's the use?"
- Like lust, guilt makes us self-centred, not Kṛṣṇa-centred `32:00`.
- **Guilt as immune response:** fever signals sickness so that we take medicine. If the feeling of sickness makes us think the medicine is useless, it is not guilt but pseudo-guilt. The teacher mentions he has written an article on guilt and pseudo-guilt `32:00`.
- Anything that discourages us in devotional service is unfavourable to _bhakti_ and should be rejected `32:00`.
- The teacher cites devotional songs in which the singer laments a heart full of sinful desires but always concludes: "Kṛṣṇa, I take shelter of You; who else can deliver me?" — never "so I'll stop chanting" `32:00 – 34:00`.
- Don't be too "attachment-conscious" — "will I succeed or fail next time?" Focus on how to serve and remember Kṛṣṇa more; attachments will subside. Focus not on removing darkness but on turning on the light `34:00`.

#### Regulated expression is the middle way [36:00]

- In _karma-yoga_ we act according to our nature, but as _sādhakas_ we also sometimes do services not according to our nature.
- If we only do what we like, we develop a strong sense of doership and never discipline the mind, and so never develop taste for the core devotional activities — hearing and chanting.
- We need not hunt for uncomfortable services, but do what the situation demands; that expresses surrender without making ourselves miserable.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Attraction and aversion in the senses are robbers on the path: neither repress nor indulge them, but regulate them — and focus on reviving attachment to Kṛṣṇa, turning on the light rather than fighting darkness, never letting guilt drive us away from Him. [20:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.35 [36:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.35 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa returns to the point of acting according to one's _varṇa_: one's own duty, even if faulty, is better than another's duty perfectly done; even destruction in one's own duty is preferable, for another's duty is dangerous `36:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sva-dharma_ — one's own duty, which is better even if imperfect `36:00`.
- _Para-dharma_ — another's duty, which is fearful and dangerous `38:00`.

#### Misappropriated verses [38:00 – 40:00]

- Many verses of the Gītā are taken by people with their own agendas: the organisation mentioned earlier with 3.33; an Indian life-insurance corporation using a phrase from the Gītā for its own purposes `38:00`.
- Some Hindu organisations, agitated by conversions to Christianity in parts of India, run "homecoming" initiatives with the slogan "_para-dharma_ is dangerous" `38:00 – 40:00`.
- This is a decontextualised use: Kṛṣṇa's _dharma_ does not refer to Christianity, Hinduism or Islam — none existed when the Gītā was spoken, and the word "Hindu" came only a few hundred years ago `40:00`.

#### Exclusivist statements as focus [40:00 – 44:00]

- Returning to the Vedic fold is good in the sense that people at least appreciate Vedic culture `40:00`.
- Religions like Christianity and Islam tend to be exclusivist, though exclusive-sounding statements exist in all religions and need context. The teacher says a statement attributed to Jesus — no one comes to the Father except through me — refers in its original language to the present, not to all eternity `40:00`.
- **Doctor's focus:** when a patient keeps citing other doctors, a doctor says, "Forget what they said; just do what I say and you'll be cured." This creates focus; it is not a claim that all other doctors are wrong `42:00`.
- Taken as absolute truths and imposed on others, such statements make religions regard other gods as false — historically leading to destruction of places of worship, and converts being told to burn their images and develop antipathy toward past worship, which is very undesirable `42:00 – 44:00`.

#### Who is better placed? [44:00 – 46:00]

- Asked whether a Christian follower of Jesus or a demigod worshipper within the Vedic fold is better, Prabhupāda said the latter — because of fewer cultural and conceptual barriers to Kṛṣṇa consciousness `44:00`.
- **Cultural:** in Vedic culture deities are worshipped; in Abrahamic religions they are desecrated.
- **Conceptual:** that God can manifest through matter and make Himself accessible is largely alien to that tradition, though some Christian traditions accept it `44:00 – 46:00`.
- This is not a blanket statement that a materialistic demigod worshipper is better than a sincere Christian saint. The ultimate criterion is whether one aspires for and advances in love for God `46:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa's actual point [46:00 – 48:00]

- The verse is about _varṇa_ duties: a _brāhmaṇa_ should act as a _brāhmaṇa_; one acting as a _kṣatriya_ against his nature cannot sustain it.
- Prabhupāda notes exceptions — a _brāhmaṇa_ acting as a _kṣatriya_ and Viśvāmitra, a _kṣatriya_, acting as a _brāhmaṇa_ — but they are few and occur when one becomes transcendental `48:00`.
- Here Kṛṣṇa speaks primarily of material duties, not spiritual duties `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sva-dharma in this verse means one's varṇa duty, not one's religious label: acting according to one's own nature, even imperfectly, purifies, while imitating another's duty cannot be sustained — and exclusivist readings of scripture miss their context. [36:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.36 [48:00 – 55:20]

<!-- vedabase:3.36 -->

**Context.** Arjuna asks: by what is one impelled to sin, even unwillingly, as if engaged by force? `48:00 – 50:00`

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Carati_ — to move: one dwells on sin, moves about in sin, is moved toward it `50:00`.
- _Anicchan_ — not desiring; in one's normal state of consciousness one does not want to do it `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### Why the question arises here [48:00 – 50:00]

- _Karma-yoga_ requires regulating one's _karma_; otherwise there is only _karma_ without the _yoga_.
- In 3.34 Kṛṣṇa said attraction and aversion are enemies situated in the senses. This leads to Arjuna's question: we know we should do our duties, but some passion prevents us — where does it come from, and how can we be free of it?

#### "As if by force" [50:00 – 52:00]

- Are we truly engaged in sin against our desire? Not exactly. Overall we may not want to sin, but when temptation comes the desire overpowers us — and we say yes to it; without our "yes" we would not act.
- **The alcoholic:** "I couldn't resist; I was forced." The bottle didn't grow legs, walk home and pour itself into his mouth — a desire became irresistible at that moment `52:00`.

#### The enemy within [52:00 – 55:20]

- This is one of the most relevant sections of the Gītā today. People may not care about soul or God, but no one can deny that something within us sabotages us.
- Sports stars with great potential act on a passion, do something stupid and are disgraced.
- Addiction is a worldwide problem: not just alcohol, drugs and cigarettes, but TV, internet and eating, consuming time and mental energy and leaving one restless `52:00 – 54:00`.
- Arjuna asks specifically about lust, but the question is universal: what force impels me against my best interests, where does it originate, and how is it controlled? Kṛṣṇa answers in 3.37–3.43 — as important for _sādhakas_ as 2.54–2.72 — to be discussed next class `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We are not dragged to sin without consent; a desire becomes irresistible in the moment and we say yes. Arjuna's question names the "enemy within" that sabotages everyone — the subject of Kṛṣṇa's answer in 3.37–3.43. [48:00 – 55:20]
</Callout>
