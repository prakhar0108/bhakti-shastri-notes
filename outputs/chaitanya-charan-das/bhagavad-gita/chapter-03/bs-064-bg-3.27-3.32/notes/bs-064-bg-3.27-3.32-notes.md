## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 3.27 [00:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.27 -->

**Context.** A famous verse that Śrīla Prabhupāda quoted frequently: actions are done by the modes of material nature, and the soul deluded by false ego thinks "I am the doer" `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ahaṅkāra_ — the false ego by which the conditioned soul is deluded `00:00`.
- _Īśvara-bhāva_ — literally "the mood of a controller"; in the 18th chapter Kṛṣṇa lists it as a desirable quality of a _kṣatriya_ `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### If the modes do everything, why instruct Arjuna? [00:00 – 02:00]

- If everything were done by the modes, there would be no rationale for the Gītā telling Arjuna "act this way, don't act that way."
- Scripture gives philosophy within a context, and the context must be understood.
- The point: the soul is a doer, but not the only doer — it is one of the doers.

#### The woodcutter and the axe [02:00 – 04:00]

- Who cuts the tree — the woodcutter or the axe? Without the axe, the woodcutter cannot cut with his hands; yet the axe has no consciousness to decide whether to cut.
- The axe moves because a conscious being desires to cut. Both are important; which is emphasised depends on the context.
- Likewise in speaking: the organ of speech is the tool, and the soul is the person who desires to speak — both must come together `04:00`.

#### How the modes shape action and desire [04:00 – 08:00]

- How we are inclined to act is largely determined by the three modes: a mountaineer trains and climbs Everest, but it is the body — governed by material nature — that climbs `04:00`.
- The soul's range of desires is also generally set by material nature.
- **Exam with only wrong options:** "What is the capital of India — Moscow, Washington or London?" Whatever the student picks is wrong. Likewise, a soul disconnected from Kṛṣṇa is offered only ignorance, passion and goodness — all wrong for the soul, whose natural action is to love and serve Kṛṣṇa `06:00`.
- Non-human species simply act as nature impels them; in the human body, too, nature pushes us, and we cannot entirely resist or reject those pushes `06:00 – 08:00`.

#### Act by nature, within dharma [08:00 – 12:00]

- A _kṣatriya_ will be impelled to fight and administer; a _vaiśya_ will be money-minded. The nature cannot be given up.
- The _vaiśya_ is told: earn money, but by dharmic means.
- _Īśvara-bhāva_ is listed with heroic qualities like never fleeing from battle — not a negative quality but a fact of the _kṣatriya_'s nature, to be dovetailed in Kṛṣṇa's service `08:00 – 10:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's point to Arjuna: whether you think of yourself as the _kṣatriya_ fighter or the renouncing _jñāna-yogī_, your mind is impelling you. The impulse to renounce and go to the forest is not in harmony with your nature and will cause disruption `10:00`.
- Understand your nature and act accordingly — not surrendering to it, but dovetailing it in devotional service, or at least within _dharma_ (e.g. _karma-yoga_, below _bhakti-yoga_). Even that brings gradual purification `10:00 – 12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul is a doer, but not the only doer; the modes act through the body and mind. So we neither surrender to our conditioned nature nor try to suppress it — we act according to it within dharma and dovetail it in Kṛṣṇa's service. [02:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.28 [12:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.28 -->

**Context.** The knower of the truth understands that the modes are acting in relation to each other, and therefore does not become attached `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tattva-vit_ — the knower of the Absolute Truth; Kṛṣṇa used the word _tattva_ earlier in 2.16 `12:00`.

#### Acting by nature without pride [12:00 – 14:00]

- A _brāhmaṇa_ studying and teaching scripture may become proud: "I know so much more than others; see how nicely I give classes." That is misidentifying with one's psychophysical nature, and it causes bondage.
- If one understands that the senses and modes are interacting and action is being done according to one's nature, one does not become bound.
- Kṛṣṇa does not say "the modes are acting, therefore surrender to them," nor "completely resist them" — neither is possible. The knower of truth simply does not become attached `14:00`.

#### Default settings of the body [14:00 – 18:00]

- When a conditioned soul becomes a _sādhaka_, he understands: "I am trapped in a machine," and the machine has default pushes and pulls.
- **Computer default settings:** they activate automatically when the machine is turned on; they can be changed, but changing them requires conscious effort. Likewise, past actions have given the body and mind default tendencies `16:00`.
- By acting according to those default settings, within the limits of _dharma_, the soul moves step by step forward.
- If my nature is that of a _śūdra_, there is no need to resent it and imagine being a _brāhmaṇa_; _śūdras_ are not outlaws — they have their _dharma_, and by practising it one becomes elevated `16:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa offers a middle way: don't let the modes take you on a ride outside _dharma_, and don't try to resist them completely — regulate them `16:00 – 18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The knower of truth sees that the modes are acting according to his nature, so he neither takes pride in his actions nor resents his nature; he regulates his "default settings" within dharma and so advances without bondage. [12:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.29 [18:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.29 -->

**Context.** Continuing the same thread, Kṛṣṇa says those deluded by the modes become attached to activities impelled by the modes; the one who knows should not disturb such slow, partially-knowing people `18:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Guṇa-karma_ — here _guṇa_ means "modes," not "qualities": activities impelled and actuated by the three modes. Reading it as "qualities and activities" causes confusion `18:00`.
- _Manda_ — lazy `18:00`.
- "Do not disturb" — Kṛṣṇa repeats what He said at the start of this section, because disturbance disrupts people's gradual advancement `20:00`.

#### Gradual and rapid methods of advancement [20:00 – 24:00]

- The Vedic scriptures give two methods: gradual and rapid.
- In the gradual method, people's propensities are dovetailed: _karma-kāṇḍa_ gives material enjoyment through _dharma_, _artha_ and _kāma_; the cycle may go on for lifetimes before people seek _mokṣa_. Social order is maintained while people get regulated sense gratification `20:00`.
- For most people material pleasure is a necessity; even devotees can live purely on spiritual happiness only when very advanced `20:00`.

#### Spiritual happiness through material means [20:00 – 24:00]

- In the _sādhaka_ stage we have sweets, nice music, _prasāda_ and devotee association. If our pleasure were purely spiritual, the same tune could be sung every day for years — yet we notice who sings nicely and ask what _prasāda_ there is today `20:00 – 22:00`.
- This is not necessarily bad: there is variety in the spiritual realm too. The mood of _bhakti_ is not to "transcend taste" by eating the same food daily, but to spiritualise `22:00`.
- At our level, spiritual happiness manifests through material means; gradually we rise to where the material level no longer matters `22:00`.

#### Circles of dharma [22:00 – 24:00]

- _Dharma_ is like a big circle; within it are smaller circles, and as they narrow they bring one closer to Kṛṣṇa.
- In general _dharma_, people acknowledge and worship Kṛṣṇa but their goal is _artha_ and _kāma_; at the highest level, ordinary _dharma_ is pushed aside and the focus becomes love of Kṛṣṇa.
- Don't rush people to renunciation they aren't ready for: pushed prematurely toward _mokṣa_, they become hypocritical `24:00`.

#### Seriousness is not determined by āśrama [24:00 – 28:00]

- In the early days of the movement it was thought that a serious devotee had to be a renunciate. This is a misconception `24:00 – 26:00`.
- Seriousness is determined by conviction and commitment. At the end of life Kṛṣṇa will see not which _āśrama_ we lived in, but how much shelter (_āśraya_) of Him we took; the _āśrama_ is a facility for taking that shelter `26:00`.
- A renunciate proud of his renunciation, or a householder proud that "because of me these renunciates are maintained," both fail to take Kṛṣṇa's shelter. Advancement depends not on where we are situated but how — in consciousness `26:00`.
- Here Kṛṣṇa is comparing _karma-yoga_ with _jñāna-yoga_, not discussing _bhakti-yoga_: even if _jñāna-yoga_ is higher, people are not capable of it, so don't do it or instruct others to — act in _karma-yoga_, set a good example and become elevated by detachment `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Devotees are more kind than the Lord [28:00 – 32:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport: the Lord advises not to disturb such people, but the devotees of the Lord are more kind than the Lord; they take all kinds of risks, even approaching ignorant men, to engage them in Kṛṣṇa consciousness `28:00`.
- This is not contradicting Kṛṣṇa's will but fulfilling it: Kṛṣṇa ultimately wants all souls back with Him, through a progressive treatment `30:00`.
- **Child in hospital:** a parent wants the child home, but not by abandoning a months-long treatment midway; the child must be cured first `30:00`.
- **Slow and fast treatments:** the fast track demands strict diet and exercise; many prefer a one-year cure to a one-month cure with severe sacrifices. _Dharma_ is the slow track; _bhakti_ is the fast track, which devotees invite people to take `30:00 – 32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Those attached to mode-driven work should not be pushed into premature renunciation; they advance gradually through dharma. Yet devotees, more kind than the Lord, fulfil His ultimate will by offering everyone the fast track of bhakti — and what matters is not one's āśrama but how much shelter of Kṛṣṇa one takes. [18:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.30 [32:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.30 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says _karma-yoga_ too must be offered to Him: surrender all work to Me, in full consciousness of Me, without desire and possessiveness, free from lethargy — and fight. The teacher calls it one of the very important verses `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jvara_ — fever; it can mean lethargy (feverish lying in bed) or feverish hyperactivity — a mental "mania" `40:00`.
- Desires and possessiveness (attachment to positions) — the mode of passion `34:00`.

#### Military discipline [32:00 – 34:00]

- Prabhupāda says this is almost like military discipline: cadets told to wake early and run just do it, without questions, because on the battlefield obeying orders is a matter of life and death.
- Spiritual life is even more serious — eternal life versus repeated birth and death — so it deserves due commitment and training.

#### Structure of the verse [34:00 – 38:00]

- The verse has four lines; the primary verb is "fight."
- Its last line asks Arjuna to give up the mode of ignorance; the third line asks him to give up passion (desires and possessiveness); the remaining lines point beyond even goodness to transcendence, since in goodness one still has worldly attachment `34:00`.
- **Devotional vs. transcendental:** we can be in the three modes and still do devotional activities — we are devotional but still struggling to become transcendental `34:00 – 36:00`.
- Above the modes is transcendence, and within it there is _spiritual_ (anything to do with the soul, even impersonalism) and _devotional_ (centred on Kṛṣṇa), which is highest `36:00`.
- **The ladder of bhakti:** _bhakti_ stretches from the summit down to the valley — one in ignorance can practise it; but if one stays in ignorance forever, one is not letting _bhakti_'s potency transform one. Serious practice lifts one beyond the modes to pure devotion `36:00 – 38:00`.
- This is the first time in this chapter that Kṛṣṇa speaks of offering to Himself; in 2.61 He had said to control the senses and fix the mind on Him `38:00`.

#### The purpose of the Gītā [38:00 – 40:00]

- Prabhupāda's "classic purport" says this verse indicates the purpose of the Gītā: to offer everything to Kṛṣṇa.
- The living entity cannot be happy independent of the Lord's cooperation, because his eternal position is to be subordinate to the Lord's desires — just as a fish cannot be happy out of water.
- The teacher recalls a devotional verse listing nature's needs — land without water becomes desert, the hungry need food, the sick need medicine, flowers need sunlight — and concluding, "similarly, O Lord, I need You" `38:00 – 40:00`.
- Prabhupāda: any reluctance to execute such an order, out of consideration of so-called kinsmen, should be thrown off; one then becomes free from feverish mentality or lethargy `40:00`.

#### The mania of the modes [40:00 – 44:00]

- A World Cup brings mania: t-shirts of favourite stars, hoardings everywhere, constant talk — and soon after, it is completely forgotten. Fashions in hairstyles or shoes grip people in the same way `40:00 – 42:00`.
- The modes don't arrest us like police with handcuffs — then we would run away. They come wearing attractive masks: "enjoy, enjoy," and people become puppets of passion and ignorance `42:00`.

#### Work, watch, shop [42:00 – 50:00]

- Surveys show most of life goes in work; after work, time goes mainly in watching and shopping `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Families sit "united in illusion" before the TV, physically lulled and mentally dulled; the enjoyment is second-hand, so people snack to make something real — unconscious eating leads to overeating and obesity `44:00`.
- Television is run not by compassionate people but by passionate people out to earn money; advertisements say "your watch, phone, computer are old — take this new one" `46:00`.
- Shopaholism is an addiction like alcoholism; with credit cards people "shop till they drop," physically or financially `46:00`.
- The cycle: work → watch to relax → see advertisements → shop → need more money → work more → watch more. "WWS — the worldwide stupidity" — an example of the mode of passion captivating people `48:00`.
- Devotees may see these as trends of modernity, but essentially the mode of passion is taking a stronger grip on minds `48:00`.

#### Staying free from feverishness [50:00 – 52:00]

- We must do our prescribed duties in the world without being overcome by its frenzy, which can leave people senseless and bankrupt.
- To avoid feverishness, don't have too many desires or too much possessiveness: I came with nothing and will go with nothing; only my connection with Kṛṣṇa goes with me.
- **Socrates at the market:** a friend teased him for standing long looking at the wares; he said he was looking at all the things people buy that are unnecessary `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The purpose of the Gītā is to offer everything to Kṛṣṇa with military-like seriousness — free from desire, possessiveness and the feverish mania by which the modes, wearing attractive masks, trap people in the cycle of work, watch and shop. [32:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.31 [52:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.31 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says these are eternal truths, not words spoken merely to make Arjuna fight; those who follow them faithfully and without envy are liberated from the bondage of _karma_ `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Faith and non-envy — faithful adherence to Kṛṣṇa's instructions without resenting them `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### Liberated even if unable to execute [52:00 – 54:00]

- Prabhupāda: an ordinary person with firm faith in the Lord's eternal injunctions, even though unable to execute such orders, becomes liberated from the law of _karma_.
- In the beginning one may not fully discharge the injunctions, but if one is not resentful of the principle and works sincerely without consideration of defeat and hopelessness, one will surely be promoted to pure Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

#### Resentment is the wall [54:00 – 56:00]

- We may not be able to practise a principle, but we should not resent it; resentment creates a wall between us and Kṛṣṇa — "why am I the servant, why is Kṛṣṇa the enjoyer?"
- The word "servant" may sound negative, but service to the Lord is the most congenial form of intimacy; in transcendence the servant–master relationship is very sweet and based on love.
- **Pure and potential pure devotees:** pure devotees have no material desires; potential pure devotees accept the principle of pure devotion and gradually come to its practice.
- If I can't chant attentively and throw away my beads in frustration — that is resentment and hopelessness. Instead, keep continuing; purification will gradually happen `54:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa is pleased not only by our action but by our intention; intention brings purification, purification brings elevation, elevation brings satisfaction — and then, as discussed in 2.65, following the principles becomes easy `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Faith means accepting Kṛṣṇa's principles as true even when we cannot yet follow them, without resentment or hopelessness; Kṛṣṇa sees our intention, and sincere continuation brings purification, elevation and higher satisfaction. [52:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.32 [56:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.32 -->

**Context.** The contrasting verse: those who, out of envy, do not follow Kṛṣṇa's instructions are deluded, without Kṛṣṇa consciousness, and all their endeavours are ruined `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Rejection leads to ruin [58:00 – 60:00]

- If we resent and reject the principle that everything is meant to be offered to Kṛṣṇa, and withdraw into a shell of self-centred enjoyment, all our knowledge and endeavours are lost — because the soul without Kṛṣṇa cannot be happy.
- Many prominent atheist philosophers of the last century went mad or committed suicide toward the end of their lives.
- One philosopher held that there is no God, no soul, only this life, full of suffering; he wrote that the only philosophical question is whether to commit suicide today or tomorrow. The teacher calls this both gloomy and untrue.
- One who rejects Kṛṣṇa as a person will not achieve the ultimate perfection and stays in material bondage. Thus Kṛṣṇa urges Arjuna to continue _karma-yoga_ and gradually rise to _bhakti-yoga_ `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Envious rejection of Kṛṣṇa's instructions turns all knowledge and effort to ruin, because the soul cannot be happy without Kṛṣṇa — the gloom of atheistic philosophy shows where such rejection leads. [56:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
