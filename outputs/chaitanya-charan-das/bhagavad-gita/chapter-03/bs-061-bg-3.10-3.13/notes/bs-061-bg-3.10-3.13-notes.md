### Bhagavad-gita 3.10 [02:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.10 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes how, at the beginning of creation, the Lord of creatures sent forth generations of beings together with sacrifices, so that by sacrifice they would prosper and have their desires fulfilled `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prajāpati_ — the Lord of creatures; ultimately Lord Viṣṇu, though the word can also refer to Brahmā, who is the representative of the Supreme Lord `08:00`, `12:00`.
- _Yajña_ — the arrangement the Lord makes, right from the beginning of creation, to care for the living beings He creates; a system of cosmic cooperation between the _devatās_, the Supreme Lord and the living beings `04:00`.
- _Su-medhasaḥ_ — intelligent people; those with good intelligence, who perform the _yajña_ of _saṅkīrtana_ `12:00`.

#### Creation comes with an arrangement for care [04:00 – 06:00]

- When parents get a child, they arrange to take care of the child; similarly, when the Lord creates living beings, He makes an arrangement to care for them — the system of _yajña_ `04:00`.
- When a child is born, the Lord sends the child through the mother and also provides milk through the mother's breast `04:00 – 06:00`.
- In nature, different species have their own needs taken care of; the teacher links this to the Īśopaniṣad's teaching on the complete whole `06:00`.

#### Why humans carry a responsibility [06:00 – 08:00]

- Non-human living beings do not have a developed spiritual faculty; they act according to instincts — eating, sleeping and so on — and are not responsible in the same way.
- Human beings also need to eat and sleep, but they have advanced consciousness, freedom and the capacity to regulate these needs.
- Because of that freedom, humans have a responsibility to discharge.
- Through this system we move forward and upward toward the Lord `08:00`.

#### The purpose of creation [08:00 – 10:00]

- The Lord of creatures offers the conditioned soul the chance to come back home, back to Godhead — that is the ultimate purpose of creation.
- At the spiritual level, the goal of life is to know Lord Viṣṇu, Lord Kṛṣṇa.
- Lord Viṣṇu is the master (_pati_) of everyone; He is the _yajña-pati_, the Lord of sacrifice `10:00`.

#### Saṅkīrtana as the sacrifice for this age [10:00 – 12:00]

- The teacher connects this to a well-known _Bhāgavatam_ verse about Lord Caitanya (which he cites as 11.5.32): it speaks of one who always describes Kṛṣṇa, though His complexion is not like Kṛṣṇa's `10:00`.
- That verse says intelligent people worship Him by the performance of _kīrtana_ `12:00`.
- The central point: through _yajña_ our needs are fulfilled; _yajña_ is the means by which we harmonise with the Supreme Lord, and by which both material and spiritual needs are met `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Just as parents arrange care for a newborn, the Lord created living beings together with the system of yajña; for humans, who have freedom, yajña is the responsible way to have our needs met and to move upward toward the Lord — and in this age that yajña is saṅkīrtana. [04:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.11 [12:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.11 -->

**Context.** The word _anena_ ("by this"), used in the previous verse as well, refers to this sacrifice: by the practice of cosmic cooperation the _devatās_ become satisfied, and they in turn please us, so that by mutually satisfying each other everyone attains the highest good `12:00 – 14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Anena_ — "by this sacrifice"; also used in the previous verse `12:00`.
- _Devatās_ — the demigods, who become satisfied by the system of cosmic cooperation `12:00`.
- _Parasparam_ — mutually; satisfying each other `14:00`.
- _Śreyaḥ param_ — the supreme good attained through this cooperation `14:00`.

#### Science as a tool, not an opponent [16:00 – 18:00]

- The teacher makes a strong point that science has been hijacked by atheistic people; the problem is not science itself — science should be used in the service of the Lord `16:00`.
- Some scientists have campaigned against religion, portraying the concept of God as a virus that corrupts people's minds `16:00`.
- Science is a tool: depending on what people pick and on their existing biases, they see things accordingly `18:00`.
- Phenomena such as past-life memories and other experiences of consciousness indicate that the soul is different from the body and moves from one body to another, yet these are not considered in science; the teacher mentions there is a good amount of such evidence, with articles on his website and in his talks `18:00`.

#### Are demigods scientifically acceptable? The example of rain [18:00 – 26:00]

- The question taken up: is the whole concept of demigods scientifically acceptable `18:00`?
- Kṛṣṇa will soon speak about rain; scientists may explain how rain happens, but actually getting rain is very difficult `20:00`.
- Artificial rain is really cloud seeding `22:00`:
  - It needs clouds already in the sky; the water itself is not artificial — it evaporated into the clouds naturally.
  - We have very little control over where the rain will fall.
  - The amount of water in average clouds is not much.
- Most countries, even one as advanced as America, have considered artificial rain uneconomical `24:00`. Even with the best technology:
  - we cannot control where the rain will come,
  - we cannot produce rain if the clouds are not there,
  - and we cannot control the quantity of rain.

#### What "automatically" really means [26:00 – 30:00]

- "Automatically" does not mean the absence of a controller; it simply means the absence of awareness of the controller `26:00`.
- The automatic-door example: a person comes near, a sensor detects them, a motor works and the door opens — there is a controller and a design, we simply do not see it nearby `28:00`.
- Similarly, when science says something in nature happens "automatically," it means we are not aware of the controllers; there is a whole precise mechanism, and scientific explanation is a description of it `28:00`.

#### Supplication versus manipulation [30:00 – 34:00]

- There are two ways of trying to change nature `30:00`:
  - **Supplication** — understanding the higher beings who control nature and harmonising with them, asking for what we need.
  - **Manipulation** — looking only at the mechanism, without looking at the beings controlling it, and trying to use things for our own purpose `32:00`.
- Artificial rain is manipulative: the cloud is hanging there and not giving rain, so we think "I can just take it" `32:00 – 34:00`.
- The teacher compares nature to a water supply: there is an agency and an authority behind the supply `34:00`.

#### Our enormous dependence on nature [34:00 – 38:00]

- Whether or not we believe in such things, the fact remains that we are enormously dependent on nature — even with all our power, conquering space and so on `34:00`.
- This is best illustrated in money, the medium most valued in modern society: a study presented in the science magazine _Nature_ on 15 May 1997 put the world's bill to nature at 16 trillion to 54 trillion US dollars for the natural resources and raw materials humanity takes — food, water, lumber, rocks, metals, oil `34:00 – 36:00`.
- The energy the sun provides each day was also priced in billions of dollars per day `36:00`.
- If we had to pay nature's bill, we would immediately become bankrupt `36:00`.

#### Saṅkīrtana and rain [36:00 – 40:00]

- Rain can come through _kīrtana_, but it is not just playing _karatālas_ and singing some songs `36:00`.
- We do not do _saṅkīrtana_ to get rain; through Kṛṣṇa consciousness people learn to live in harmony with God, and to that extent the rains will come naturally `38:00`.
- The teacher mentions new-age gardening communities that seek communion with plants — a famous one in Scotland grows amazing flowers and vegetables in sandy soil — as one way people have sensed that higher principles are at work in nature `38:00`.

#### What "higher" means [40:00 – 42:00]

- Heaven is "up," but what does up mean? An Indian and an American both say heaven is up, yet they stand on opposite sides of a round earth `40:00`.
- So "up" is not simple geography: the universe has multiple levels, a higher karmic dimension; _svarga_ is higher in the sense that higher qualification is required to attain it `40:00`.
- We should not have simplistic notions about the Vedic teachings `40:00`.

#### Yajña as exchange with the cosmic government [42:00 – 44:00]

- The essential point of the Vedic worldview is that we are striving to harmonise ourselves with the cosmic government, and _yajña_ is the means `42:00`.
- The credit-card example: we put money in a bank and get a credit card to use for purchases; the method of exchange varies with the economic system, but the principle of exchange is universal `42:00`.
- The government (the _devatās_) gives rewards, and those rewards are in terms of the necessities of life `42:00`.
- When we do _kīrtana_ — chanting, dancing, singing — it is essentially the means by which we harmonise with the Supreme `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Nature's supply is "automatic" only in the sense that we do not see its controllers; rather than manipulating nature, the Gītā teaches supplication — harmonising with the higher beings through yajña, an exchange in which they supply the necessities of life. [26:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.12 [44:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.12 -->

**Context.** The _devatās_, satisfied by sacrifice, supply the necessities of life; one who enjoys these gifts without offering them back is a thief `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Necessities, not desires_ — Prabhupāda's point that the _devatās_ will provide the necessities of life when they are satisfied `46:00`.
- _Thief_ — one who uses the gifts without offering them back and without participating in cosmic cooperation `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Shortage as a sign of disharmony [44:00]

- If there is a shortage of water, it is because people are doing bad _karma_ and are not in harmony with nature `44:00`.
- Our focus is not on doing _yajña_ merely to get resources supplied `44:00`.

#### Taking without paying [46:00 – 48:00]

- If we do not offer back to the _devatās_ and do not participate in the system of cosmic cooperation, we become thieves using things for ourselves `46:00`.
- Like someone who takes the water supply but does not pay the tax — such a person is considered a thief `48:00`.

#### The demigods' proper place [48:00 – 52:00]

- The teacher refers to Vedic sacrifices and episodes in the _Bhāgavatam_ in which demigods became angry when not offered their share, including Indra and Lord Śiva `48:00`.
- Our sustenance depends on the earth; it is part of the system of cosmic cooperation, and if we do not play our part, it is ultimately displeasing to the Supreme Lord `50:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa told the residents to stop the worship of Indra, but He did so to check Indra's pride: the _devatās_ should accept respect on behalf of the Supreme Lord, not think that they themselves are supreme `50:00`.
- Ultimately the system is meant to help people get elevated `52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The necessities of life come through the devatās as part of a cosmic exchange; using them without offering anything back is like taking a city's water without paying the tax — it makes us thieves. [46:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.13 [52:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.13 -->

**Context.** Devotees who eat food first offered in sacrifice are released from sins, whereas those who prepare food for their own sense enjoyment eat only sin `52:00 – 56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prasāda_ — food offered to Kṛṣṇa `54:00`.

#### Inevitable violence in daily life [52:00]

- There are five primary ways in which people inevitably incur reactions through normal activities — for example, when we light a fire, when we walk along, or when we put down a pot of water, living beings get killed `52:00`.
- The specific practices for counteracting these can be adjusted according to culture; the point is that we are all inevitably part of this system `52:00`.

#### Food as the arrangement of higher beings [54:00]

- The food we get is the arrangement of higher beings; it does not come just by our own efforts, though our efforts are important to produce food `54:00`.
- So we offer some kind of prayer to the higher beings; the teacher also mentions a Christian practice in this connection `54:00`.
- Food that is offered to Kṛṣṇa is called _prasāda_ `54:00`.

#### Prasāda as today's yajña [56:00 – 58:00]

- When we offer food, we are freed from the reactions; this is why vegetarianism is important `56:00`.
- For people from a Western background, the principle of _yajña_ is practised through taking _prasāda_ `58:00`.
- In Vedic _karma-kāṇḍa_ contexts, the principle of _yajña_ was practised through fire sacrifices, and people took the remnants of the fire sacrifice; in our situation, we have the Deity and we offer food to Him `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Everyday life inevitably involves harm to other beings, and food itself comes by the arrangement of higher powers; by offering our vegetarian food to Kṛṣṇa and taking it as prasāda, we practise yajña in our own situation and are freed from those reactions. [52:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
