## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 3.14 – 3.15 [00:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.14 -->

<!-- vedabase:3.15 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes a cycle: living beings depend on food, food comes from rain, rain from sacrifice, sacrifice from prescribed duty, prescribed duty from the Vedas, and the Vedas from the Supreme — so the all-pervading Absolute is situated in acts of sacrifice `02:00`, `18:00`, `22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Parjanya_ — rain; the teacher notes it is also a personal name in the tradition `00:00`.
- _Karma_ — prescribed duty; it is prescribed in the Vedas `02:00`.
- _Brahma_ — in 3.15 the word refers to the Vedas; the related _akṣara_ refers to the Supreme `02:00`, `24:00`.
- _Sarva-gatam_ — all-pervading; the Lord, though not visible to us, is accessible everywhere through sacrifice `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### The billiards example [04:00 – 08:00]

- Imagine a camera placed overhead, looking down on a billiards table: one would practically never see the players — only sticks moving forward, hitting balls, balls hitting other balls and going into the holes `06:00`.
- The motion of the balls follows the laws of mechanics; from the angle and force of the stick we can explain why a ball went into a hole `06:00`.
- That explanation is correct but not complete: the force was applied at that angle because players with particular training, expertise and desire are playing the game `06:00 – 08:00`.
- Similarly, rain falls through a mechanism — water evaporating and condensing through the clouds — and that mechanism is correct but not complete `08:00`.

#### Horizontal duty is not complete duty [08:00 – 14:00]

- Taking care of one's children and family members is something animals also do; it is not the complete scope of human duty `08:00 – 10:00`.
- Human duty involves rising to the higher; if we only take care of our own bodies and the bodies of our loved ones, we are doing duties but not complete duties `10:00`.
- Parents' love for their children is natural and necessary, but not by itself spiritual; nature arranges what is necessary through feelings of affection. This is not to minimise the sacrifice parents make for their children `10:00 – 12:00`.
- Ethology — the study of animals' natural behaviour in their habitat — shows this `12:00 – 14:00`:
  - When a bird is expecting, the pair search for a good hollow in a tree and cover it with branches so that snakes cannot get in.
  - After the eggs are laid, food is brought to the mother and later to the chicks.
  - When the parents drop food into the hollow, the bigger chicks first give it to the smaller ones and only later eat themselves.
- So affection and even a spirit of sacrifice exist among animal species too `14:00`.

#### The vertical relationship [14:00 – 16:00]

- Our duty is to take care of family members, but we also have to connect with God `14:00`.
- Relationships are horizontal (with others) and vertical (with God); only in the human form can we pursue the vertical relationship `14:00`.
- When we perform _yajña_, we acknowledge the higher authority, ultimately the Supreme `14:00`.
- It is not just "I am a good person"; as human beings we have a duty to take care of both the material and the spiritual side `16:00`.

#### Where duty comes from: scripture [16:00 – 20:00]

- We cannot know what our _karma_ is by our own speculative efforts `16:00`.
- The Lord is not directly visible to us; Kṛṣṇa and the spiritual realm become accessible to us in the material world through scripture `16:00`.
- Scripture is not just theoretical but transformational: it gives a practical process of _yoga_ by which we can rise from the material level to the spiritual level `16:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa is the source and the goal of everything; since He is not visible to us, He has given the Vedas `18:00`.
- In sacrifice, for the pleasure of the Absolute, we give up what we could have used for our own pleasure — and this is how we connect to the Absolute Truth `20:00`.

#### The government analogy [20:00 – 24:00]

- The government gives water supply, health services and schools; if we stop paying taxes, the government stops giving water. For the cycle to go on, we have to keep paying `20:00`.
- The higher universal authority is like the government, and we are the citizens `20:00`.
- A donkey does not have the intelligence to follow laws and is not expected to; human beings have higher intelligence and are expected to `22:00`.
- In practice, the cycle runs: living beings need food; food comes from rain; rain from sacrifice; sacrifice from prescribed duty. Doing our part is like saying "thank you very much" — and we get grains; in this way the cycle is maintained `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Like a billiards game seen only from above, nature's mechanism is a correct but incomplete explanation; behind it is a cycle rooted in the Lord, and humans — unlike animals — are expected to do their part in it through scripturally given duty, connecting vertically with God and not only horizontally with family. [04:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.16 [24:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.16 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa states what happens to one who does not follow the cycle so established: such a person, delighting only in the senses, lives in sin and lives in vain `24:00 – 26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pravartitam_ — established; the cycle has been set in motion `26:00`.
- _Indriya-ārāma_ — one who delights in the senses `24:00 – 26:00`.
- _Living in vain_ — living without knowing, or caring to know, the ultimate goal of life `36:00`.

#### The Vedas come from the Lord [24:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport compares one who does not maintain the cycle to hogs and dogs; such a person goes on increasing sinful reactions `24:00`.
- The scriptures come from the Lord: the teacher quotes that the Vedas came out from the breathing of the Supreme Personality of Godhead `24:00`.

#### When law departs from scripture [28:00 – 34:00]

- The scriptures are like the government law books of the state; if the government acted according to scripture, crime and sin would be the same `28:00`.
- When the government does not act according to scripture, there is a systematic programme of misinformation — people are not given proper information by which they would consider, for example, that leather shoes involve sin `28:00`.
- Unfortunately, according to today's laws, slaughterhouses and abortion are not considered crimes, and society performing them is incurring karmic reactions `28:00`.
- The teacher describes a documentary showing how a child is killed in abortion, which he calls very horrifying `28:00 – 32:00`.
- Because of such social values, most people go on doing immoral, sinful activity thinking it is all right; they pursue _dharma_, _artha_ and sense gratification without considering the scriptures or the higher laws of God `32:00`.

#### Bridging the difference [34:00 – 38:00]

- The difference between man's laws and God's laws has to be bridged — not by changing God's laws to conform with man's laws, but by elevating human understanding so that it harmonises with the divine `34:00`.
- The teacher connects this to the third mantra of the Īśopaniṣad, which describes such people as killers of the soul `34:00`.
- They live in vain because they do not know the ultimate goal of life and do not care to know it, living in violation of the law of the Supreme Lord `36:00`.
- Different _devatās_ may be worshipped, but ultimately the Lord is the enjoyer (_bhoktā_) and the ultimate goal; the worship of others ultimately goes to Him — a point the chapter returns to later `36:00 – 38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
One who breaks the cosmic cycle for sense enjoyment lives in vain; when society's laws depart from scripture, the remedy is not to bend God's laws to man's laws but to raise human understanding to harmonise with the divine. [24:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.17 – 3.18 [38:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.17 -->

<!-- vedabase:3.18 -->

**Context.** A new section (3.17 – 3.24) begins: even if one is qualified for _jñāna-yoga_, one should still practise _karma-yoga_ to set an example `38:00`. Kṛṣṇa describes the person who is self-satisfied, for whom there is no duty, who gains nothing by doing duty and loses nothing by not doing it, and who depends on no living being `40:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ātma-ratiḥ_, _ātma-tṛptaḥ_, _ātma-santuṣṭaḥ_ — the word _ātma_ ("self") appears three times: one who takes pleasure in, is illuminated in, and is satisfied in the self `40:00 – 42:00`.
- _Artha_ — purpose; such a person has no purpose to fulfil by doing duty `42:00`.
- _Sarva-bhūteṣu_ — "on all living beings"; such a person is not dependent on anyone `42:00 – 44:00`.

#### Reciprocity creates obligation [40:00]

- As long as we take something from someone, we are expected to give something back — this is how we live in society `40:00`.
- Parents take care of children; when the children grow up, they are expected to care for their parents `40:00`.
- A self-satisfied person is not seeking anything `40:00`.

#### Free from external obligation [42:00]

- When we do not seek anything externally, we are free from external obligation; for such a person there is no duty — an elevated stage `42:00`.
- For such a person, doing duty and not doing duty are both equal; there is no purpose in doing it and no reason not to do it `42:00`.

#### Horizontal, not vertical [44:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is not talking about _bhakti_ here; it is not introduced much in this chapter `44:00`.
- There is an indirect reference to the Supreme Lord in 3.9 through the word _yajña_, but until now no direct reference to Him in the third chapter `44:00`.
- Ultimately everyone depends on the Supreme Lord; but here Kṛṣṇa is speaking not of vertical interactions but of horizontal ones — such a person takes nothing from anyone and so is not dependent on anyone `44:00`.

#### Levels of detachment from society [44:00 – 48:00]

- A _brāhmaṇa_ is still part of society and receives charity; at a big sacrifice a king may say to a _brāhmaṇa_, "You have honoured me by gracing this place — I give my whole kingdom to you" `44:00 – 46:00`.
- _Sannyāsīs_ are at the top of the system, yet moving further outward from it `46:00`.
- _Jñāna-yogīs_ seeking liberation take nothing from anyone and give nothing either `46:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa turns the argument around [48:00 – 52:00]

- What Arjuna is being told is: do your duty `48:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's point: "If you think you are so self-satisfied, fine — you don't need your family, you could go to the jungle and be satisfied there. But then it should make no difference to you whether you are in the forest or in the city" `48:00`.
- So, since externals do not matter much for such a person, better to stay in society and act there `52:00`.
- Material difficulty is not a sufficient qualification for spiritual life `50:00`:
  - Sometimes life is based on feelings — finding material life difficult, one thinks "I will become a renunciant"; but what happens when problems arise there?
  - "Today I am fed up with life, so I'll start practising" is different from practising month after month and year after year — that steady practice is the test and the demonstration `52:00`.
  - When that is present, one can renounce worldly desires and focus on spiritual life `52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The self-satisfied transcendentalist has no duty — but for the same reason gains nothing by abandoning it; since forest or city makes no difference to such a person, it is better to remain in society and act, while mere frustration with material life is no qualification for renunciation. [40:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.19 [52:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes that one should therefore act as a matter of duty, without attachment, and by working without attachment one attains the Supreme `52:00 – 54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Param_ — the supreme destination; this is the first time Kṛṣṇa uses the word in the sense of referring to Himself — an indirect, not direct, reference to the Supreme `52:00`.

#### Attaining the Supreme through duty [52:00 – 54:00]

- Just by doing one's duty, one can attain the Supreme `52:00`.
- By performing prescribed duty steadily, one moves forward and eventually becomes liberated `54:00`.
- It is not the activity itself that binds; it is one's internal mentality — whether one is detached `54:00`.
- The teacher also previews the theme of acting for _loka-saṅgraha_, the welfare of the world, which follows next `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Activity itself does not bind — attachment does; by performing prescribed duty steadily and without attachment, one moves toward liberation and the Supreme, which Kṛṣṇa here names for the first time as param. [52:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
