### Bhagavad-gita 11.17 [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.17 -->

**Context.** As Arjuna looks around, he begins to discern specific features — crowns, maces and discs — but above all an immeasurable, blinding effulgence `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kirīṭa_ — helmet; can refer to crowns `00:00`.
- _Gadā_ and _cakra_ — mace and disc; can be taken as a reference to Viṣṇu `00:00`.
- _Durnirīkṣya_ — that which is difficult to see `02:00`.
- _Anala_ — fire; _dīpta_ — burning, blazing (used twice in the verse); _arka_ — the sun; _dyuti_ — effulgence `02:00`.

#### Blindingly effulgent [00:00 – 04:00]

- The form is well adorned; Arjuna sees the form of Viṣṇu there too, among many other things he will describe in later verses.
- All directions are ablaze with its effulgence — like blazing fire, like the sun — immeasurable.
- When there is a very bright light we cannot see anything clearly; Arjuna is saying, "I see crowns, maces and discs, but basically it is so effulgent I can't see anything."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna can just make out crowns, maces and discs — signs of Viṣṇu — but the overriding experience is an effulgence so blinding that the form is hard to see at all. [00:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.18 [04:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.18 -->

**Context.** As Arjuna understands that this is a manifestation of the Absolute Truth, his description starts becoming a glorification: You are the supreme, the ultimate object to be known, the supreme basis of the universe, inexhaustible, the eternal protector of religion, the eternal Supreme Person — "this is my opinion" `02:00 – 04:00`.

#### From perception to identification [04:00 – 06:00]

- The description of the perception is changing into a glorification and an identification.
- As discussed in the previous class, people blind from birth who receive eyes cannot make sense of anything at first; gradually — when a carpenter touches a saw — they identify it.
- Identification happens when perception is linked with pre-existing conceptions. Arjuna first sees something blinding and all-pervading; now he identifies it: "You are the Supreme Person."
- It is not yet directly a prayer, though there is a slight mood of prayer; it is description with glorification inside it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
When perception links with what one already knows, it becomes identification; Arjuna moves from "something blinding" to "You are the Supreme Person," and description begins to turn into glorification. [04:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.19 [06:00 – 09:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.19 -->

**Context.** Arjuna repeats that the form has no beginning, middle or end and unlimited glories; the sun and moon are like its eyes, blazing fire comes from its mouth, and its effulgence heats the whole universe `06:00 – 08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vaktra_ — mouth `06:00`.
- _Tapantam_ — making hot; the universe is blazing, becoming hot `06:00`.

#### The description sharpens [06:00 – 08:00]

- What was described earlier is slightly repeated, but now becomes more specific: earlier just effulgence all around; now, discerning the face, blazing fire from the mouth.
- The form is frightening. An angry person frightens us; if fire came out of his mouth, it would be far more so — even jugglers who seem to eat fire are frightening to watch.
- Until now only sight was described; now the sensation of touch — heat — is also described.

#### Repetition from ecstasy [08:00]

- The _virāṭ-rūpa_ is impossible for us to conceive or describe, what to speak of depict; any picture is a partial depiction indicating what Arjuna saw — many faces, as Sañjaya also said.
- Prabhupāda writes in the purport that the repetition is because of ecstasy and amazement: what is happening is so extraordinary.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
As the vision grows clearer — a fiery mouth, sun-and-moon eyes, heat felt as well as seen — Arjuna repeats himself, and Prabhupāda explains the repetition as the natural overflow of ecstasy and amazement. [06:00 – 09:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.20 [08:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.20 -->

**Context.** The form alone pervades the space between sky and earth and all directions; seeing this wonderful and terrible form, the three worlds are perturbed `08:00 – 12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dyāv-āpṛthivyoḥ_ — between the sky and the earth; the vertical dimension, along with all directions `10:00`.
- _Adbhutam_ — wonderful; _ugram_ — dreadful, which Prabhupāda translates as "terrible" `10:00`.
- _Mahātman_ — "O great one"; the word Kṛṣṇa and Sañjaya used earlier, now used by Arjuna for Kṛṣṇa `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### Transported beyond the battlefield [10:00]

- At this time Arjuna does not see the battlefield or the warriors waiting for the conversation to end; the divine vision transports him into a different arena of perception.
- He sees only this one form.

#### Wonderful and dreadful at once [10:00 – 12:00]

- Normally we do not bring wonderful and dreadful together — one is a positive emotion, the other negative.
- Conflicting emotions are going through Arjuna's mind because the form is so extraordinary.
- Arjuna also sees that others — Brahmā, the _devas_ and other celestial beings — are seeing this form and are troubled by it.

#### Overview of Arjuna's description [12:00 – 14:00]

- BG 11.15 – 11.20 — vision of the universe.
- BG 11.20 – 11.23 — the effect on the universe: other beings in other arenas of existence, such as the Rudras and Ādityas, are affected by the fury of the form.
- BG 11.24 – 11.25 — the effect on Arjuna.
- BG 11.26 – 11.30 — in the form Arjuna sees the Kurukṣetra battlefield itself and the warriors being devoured, which really shakes him.
- BG 11.31 — he asks, "Who are You?" The terrifying description ends with a question.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The form is at once wonderful and dreadful, pervading every direction; caught between conflicting emotions, Arjuna sees that even the higher worlds are shaken by it. [08:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

#### Even the suras and sages are amazed [14:00]

- In BG 10.2 Arjuna heard that even the _suras_ and sages do not know Kṛṣṇa's origin; here their reaction is described — they are amazed by the _virāṭ_.

#### Svasti and its misuse [14:00]

- _Svasti_ is depicted diagrammatically by a symbol.
- Hitler appropriated that symbol for the Nazi regime but twisted it: he wanted inauspiciousness for everyone except the Nazis — to rule the world, and the complete annihilation of groups like the Jews and the gypsies.

#### Praise when the Lord appears [16:00]

- Whenever the Lord appears in a manifestation, praises are offered; it is appropriate to praise the glorious, worshipable Lord.
- The _devas_ are shocked and fold their hands; the sages offer prayers, trying to pacify the Lord.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">

### Bhagavad-gita 11.24 [20:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.24 -->

**Context.** The transition to Arjuna's own emotions: seeing the form touching the sky, many-coloured, with open mouths and large glowing eyes, Arjuna cannot keep his composure; he addresses the form as Viṣṇu `20:00 – 22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nabhaḥ-spṛśam_ — reaching right up to the sky `20:00`.
- _Dīpta_ — effulgent; a word Arjuna uses repeatedly because the sight is so blinding `20:00`.

#### Large eyes, but not sweet eyes [20:00 – 22:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is said to have large eyes, but His eyes are sweet, full of curiosity and love, bringing joy to the heart.
- These eyes are scary; Arjuna cannot maintain his peace.

#### Identifying the form as Viṣṇu [22:00 – 24:00]

- Until now Arjuna identified the form as the Supreme Person; here he identifies it as Viṣṇu (the word comes once more later).
- In the pastime of Nṛsiṁhadeva, when no _deva_ could pacify the angry Lord, Brahmā asked Lakṣmī; she hesitated — even she had not seen her Lord in such a mood.
- Prabhupāda explains in his purport that Lakṣmī eternally resides with the Lord in the spiritual world and knows Him, but _yogamāyā_ covered her; the whole pastime was orchestrated to highlight Prahlāda's singularly exalted devotion — when everyone else had submitted to Hiraṇyakaśipu, Prahlāda did not; when everyone else failed to pacify the Lord, Prahlāda could.
- Similarly, Arjuna knows Kṛṣṇa and knows Kṛṣṇa and Viṣṇu are the same; intellectually he identifies the form as Viṣṇu, yet he will still be so overwhelmed as to ask, "Who are You?"

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even knowing intellectually that this is Viṣṇu, Arjuna loses his composure — just as Lakṣmī hesitated before Nṛsiṁhadeva — because these blazing eyes are not the sweet, loving eyes of the Kṛṣṇa he knows. [20:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.25 [24:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.25 -->

**Context.** Seeing the terrible teeth and the fire like that of universal destruction, Arjuna loses all sense of direction and begs the Lord of lords, the refuge of the worlds, to be pleased `24:00 – 26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Daṁṣṭrā-karāla_ — terrible teeth `24:00`.
- _Kālānala_ — the fire that comes at the time of universal destruction `24:00`.
- _Deveśa_ — Lord of all the gods; _jagan-nivāsa_ — shelter of the worlds `26:00`.

#### Losing orientation [24:00 – 26:00]

- Earlier the fire and the teeth were described separately; now, as everything becomes clear, both together — fearsome teeth and fire that is no ordinary fire but the fire of annihilation.
- "I don't know which way is right or left, top or bottom." When we are too overwhelmed by emotion, we lose our voice and our orientation; that is why people with tremendous emotions sometimes fall down.
- Arjuna, having seen all the _devas_ folding hands, prays: "Please have mercy on me; please be pleased with me."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Overwhelmed past the point of orientation, Arjuna turns from description to plea — "be pleased with me" — addressing the form as Lord of the gods and shelter of the worlds. [24:00 – 26:00]

### Bhagavad-gita 11.26 – 11.27 [26:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.26 -->

<!-- vedabase:11.27 -->

**Context.** The vision becomes even more frightening: Arjuna sees the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra with the allied kings, Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Karṇa and the chief warriors of his own side rushing into the form's terrible mouths; some are caught between the teeth with their heads smashed `28:00`, `34:00 – 36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Avani-pāla_ — protectors of the earth, the kings `28:00`.
- _Sūta-putra_ — Karṇa `28:00`.
- _Kāla-rūpa_ — the form of time, within the _virāṭ-rūpa_ `28:00`.
- _Karāla_ and _bhayānaka_ — terrible and fearful; two words used because the sight is so unnerving `34:00`.
- _Uttamāṅga_ — the best part of the body, the head `36:00`.

#### From a distant vision to an immediate one [26:00 – 28:00]

- The fury of the form is bad enough; now Arjuna sees it is not far away but right here.
- Imagine watching a horror movie and suddenly seeing on screen the very theatre you are sitting in, and the spectators around you being consumed: a distant vision acquires an immediate urgency that causes panic.
- Arjuna now sees everything happening on the battlefield itself.

#### Even the pious and even our side [28:00]

- Not only the vicious Kauravas and allied kings, but even those Arjuna respects and reveres — Bhīṣma and Droṇa. In the first chapter, Kṛṣṇa placed the chariot before Bhīṣma, taking Arjuna's confusion to a far more insoluble level.
- Karṇa is there, and even warriors from Arjuna's own side.

#### The kāla-rūpa: space and time [28:00 – 30:00]

- The universe has two dimensions: space (up, down, left, right — already shown as pervaded) and time, which destroys everyone.
- Arjuna had asked to see the _viśva-rūpa_ but did not expect the _kāla-rūpa_; Kṛṣṇa had said he would see something more which he might like to see.
- In time everyone is destroyed. Life is a journey from which nobody comes out alive at the material level — good and bad alike.

#### Why would God show something gruesome? [30:00 – 32:00]

- People go to God for something good, peaceful, sweet and soothing; they go to a horror movie for something ghastly. If they saw blood, fire and teeth in a temple they would say, "I didn't come to the temple to see this."
- The Vedic understanding of God is not one-sided but all-sided: material nature has two aspects — stability and change — and stability is largely an illusion.
- As the teacher speaks, the cells of his face and vocal cords are ageing, dying and being replaced; the chair under us seems solid, yet at the subatomic level electrons whizz around and specific forms come into and go out of existence.
- Destruction is integral to material existence, and any conception of God that excludes it is incomplete.

#### God destroys the temporary to make way for the eternal [32:00 – 34:00]

- This does not mean God likes to be like this or is inherently destructive. He is the supremely all-attractive, loving person; even in this chapter Arjuna will ask to see His sweet form instead.
- But destruction is an aspect of material nature; denying it does not drive away its reality. It is one of God's energies.
- When God destroys, it is not cruelty; as long as our hearts are attached to the temporary, we remain in the cycle of birth and death.
- The purpose is not destruction itself but to show in stark terms that nothing here lasts, so that we redirect our affection from the world to its source, Kṛṣṇa.
- God is the eternal lover and beloved; when we turn away from Him we place ourselves in the arena of destruction.

#### Crushed between the teeth [34:00 – 36:00]

- Earlier fire was coming out of the mouths — frightening enough. Now the opposite: like a suction pump, everyone is being pulled inside.
- The path is not unobstructed; the heads of some crash against the teeth and are crushed — like a head-on accident in which bones come out, but far worse.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The kāla-rūpa brings destruction home — to the very battlefield and even to the revered and to Arjuna's own side; it reveals that destruction is God's energy, used not out of cruelty but to turn our hearts from the temporary to the eternal. [26:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.28 – 11.29 [36:00 – 53:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.28 -->

<!-- vedabase:11.29 -->

**Context.** Arjuna uses two metaphors: as the many waves of rivers flow into the ocean, the great heroes of human society enter the blazing mouths; and as moths rush with full speed into a blazing fire to their destruction, so do these people `36:00 – 40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vega_ — force, speed `36:00`, `38:00`.
- _Nara-loka-vīra_ — great heroes of human society `38:00`.
- _Pataṅga_ — moths `38:00`.
- _Samṛddha_ — endowed with, full of; normally positive (prosperity), here used negatively `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### Speed is not always good [38:00 – 40:00]

- Speed in an animal is usually praised; but a moth rushing into fire, or a car veering off the road into a valley at speed — the greater the speed, the greater the destruction.
- The better our technology becomes, the more fossil fuel we can extract; but reserves are not unlimited, and when they are exhausted there will be a serious crisis.

#### Involuntary and voluntary [40:00 – 42:00]

- The _ācāryas_ have analysed the two metaphors: the river goes to the ocean naturally — involuntarily; the moth goes to the fire voluntarily.
- **River → ocean:** Bhīṣma and Droṇa were not malicious or evil-minded and did not willingly court destruction; by the force of circumstances beyond their control they had to fight on the Kaurava side and would be destroyed.
- **Moth → fire:** Duryodhana, his brothers and his network of friends — including Śakuni and Karṇa — knew what they were doing was wrong.

#### Duryodhana's defiance [42:00 – 46:00]

- Duryodhana was so overconfident that he wanted war. Just before the war Maitreya Ṛṣi came and told him he could not stand against the Pāṇḍavas, and certainly not against Kṛṣṇa.
- **Negligence versus defiance:** negligence is not paying attention to advice; defiance is doing the opposite — even more disrespectful.
- Duryodhana slapped his thigh before the sage, as wrestlers do to show strength. The sage cursed him: the very thigh he was proud of would be broken and become the cause of his death — Bhīma would break it.
- Dhṛtarāṣṭra sobered up and begged forgiveness; the sage said the curse would not act only if Duryodhana accepted his counsel and refused to fight.
- Earlier, when Draupadī was dishonoured in the assembly, Duryodhana had exposed his thigh to her, and Bhīma had vowed to break it; the curse reinforced that vow.

#### Salt on the wound [46:00 – 48:00]

- Before the war, Duryodhana sent Śakuni's son with insulting messages to the Pāṇḍava leaders:
  - Yudhiṣṭhira — a fool who gambled away himself, his wife and his kingdom.
  - Arjuna — a mere dance teacher, a eunuch, his power only in words.
  - Bhīma — nothing but a cow.
- The Pāṇḍava assembly roared with fury; the messenger, frightened, reminded them that a messenger should not be harmed.
- The war was coming anyway, but Duryodhana's insolence was like opening a wound and putting salt on it. Like a moth, he rushed into the fire by provoking the Pāṇḍavas, whose wrath would destroy him completely in eighteen days.

#### The deeper meaning: two kinds of death [48:00 – 50:00]

- The teacher refers to a prominent systematiser-_ācārya_ of the Śrī tradition and two later successors, one of whom is called a jewel among logicians; paraphrasing that logician: materialists die and spiritualists die — so what is the difference, and why practise spiritual life?
- A river reaching the ocean remains water; its nature continues. Devotees serve Kṛṣṇa here and, after death, continue to serve Him in the spiritual world — their essential nature does not change.
- A moth entering fire is burned to ashes. The soul is never destroyed, but that level of existence is reduced to nothing; only karma carries to another body, and all positions are lost.
- For devotees death is a transition and continuation; for materialists, everything is destroyed.

#### Beneficial on the way, beautiful at the end [50:00 – 53:00]

- A moth moving towards fire does no good to anyone; a river moving towards the ocean nourishes all the land, plants, animals and people along its banks.
- Devotees, moving towards the Lord, benefit others — setting an example of how life is meant to be lived and sharing Kṛṣṇa consciousness by words and actions. In the twelfth chapter Kṛṣṇa will describe devotees as well-wishing friends of all.
- Afterwards the devotee continues to serve without the problems of the material world — no body that limits, no mind that distracts.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Everyone enters death, but not in the same way: the river goes involuntarily and remains water, nourishing all along its course; the moth rushes in willingly and is consumed — so a devotee's life is beneficial on the way and beautiful at its end. [36:00 – 53:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.30 [53:00 – 57:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.30 -->

**Context.** Arjuna, using the word Viṣṇu a second time, sees the form licking its lips as it devours people on all sides with blazing mouths, its fierce effulgence pervading and scorching the universe `52:00 – 56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Lelihyase_ — licking `54:00`.
- _Ugra_ — fierce `56:00`.

#### A vision demonstrating a principle [54:00]

- When we eat something delicious, like a _rasagullā_, we lick up every bit of syrup; here the form licks up everything.
- This may seem disturbing — we are meant to be vegetarian — but this is a vision. It is not that there is a form of God that consumes everyone; it demonstrates a principle: destruction in this world is inevitable and within God's jurisdiction.

#### Kurukṣetra as a yajña [54:00 – 56:00]

- Recall BG 4.24 and the different kinds of sacrifice described from BG 4.25 onwards: the principle of sacrifice is not limited to physical fires and can include all activities.
- The battle is like a _yajña_: Kurukṣetra is the place of sacrifice, the Kauravas are the oblations, and Arjuna's Gāṇḍīva bow is the ladle; the Mahābhārata also uses this metaphor in one place.
- In a fire sacrifice the fire is considered Viṣṇu's mouth, and all the ghee should go into the fire, not be spilled. Likewise the form accepts every oblation: whatever Arjuna offers at Kurukṣetra, Kṛṣṇa will accept.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The gruesome licking is a vision of a principle, not a literal appetite: the battle is a sacrifice in which Arjuna's arrows are the offering and Kṛṣṇa the one who fully accepts it. [53:00 – 57:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.31 [57:00 – 60:31]

<!-- vedabase:11.31 -->

**Context.** Arjuna asks the fearsome form who He is, offers obeisances, begs Him to be pleased, and asks to know His intention `57:00 – 60:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ugra-rūpa_ — fearsome form `58:00`.
- _Bhavān_ — the respectful second-person address, as against _tvam_ `58:00`.

#### "Yes, You are Viṣṇu — but who are You?" [57:00 – 58:00]

- In the previous verse Arjuna called the form Viṣṇu, yet now asks who He is: "I have never seen this kind of form of Viṣṇu."

#### From tvam to bhavān [58:00 – 60:31]

- Even when he surrendered to Kṛṣṇa, Arjuna used _tvam_ — "you"; _bhavān_ is more respectful, like the Hindi _tum_ and _āp_. English does not distinguish them.
- If someone calls and we think it is a senior devotee or spiritual master, _tum_ suddenly becomes _āp_ — the whole mood changes.
- Here Arjuna uses the respectful address twice: "I offer my obeisances; please be pleased with me; I want to know You — who are You and what is Your intention?"
- With this inquiry Arjuna falls silent; Kṛṣṇa's response will be discussed in the next class `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The terrifying vision ends in a question and a change of relationship: the intimate friend becomes a respectful supplicant, asking the Lord who He is and what He intends. [57:00 – 60:31]
</Callout>

## Cross-Shloka Conceptual Progression (BG 11.17 – 11.31)

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

- No separate audience Q&A appears in the captions. The teacher raises and answers questions himself:
  - Why would God reveal a gruesome form? Because destruction is a real aspect of material nature and one of God's energies; a conception of God that excludes it is incomplete, and it serves to turn us from the temporary to the eternal `30:00 – 34:00`.
  - Everyone dies, materialist and spiritualist alike — so why practise spiritual life? The river and moth metaphors show the difference in both the process and the end `48:00 – 52:00`.
  - Is the form literally licking blood? No — it is a vision demonstrating a principle `54:00`.

### High-Yield Facts & Terminological Anchors

- Arjuna's description: 11.15 – 11.20 vision of the universe; 11.20 – 11.23 effect on the universe; 11.24 – 11.25 effect on Arjuna; 11.26 – 11.30 the battlefield devoured; 11.31 "Who are You?" `12:00`.
- _Kirīṭa_, _gadā_, _cakra_ — can point to Viṣṇu `00:00`.
- _Adbhutam_ and _ugram_ — wonderful and dreadful together `10:00`.
- Arjuna names the form Viṣṇu twice (BG 11.24, BG 11.30) `22:00`, `52:00`.
- _Kālānala_ — the fire of universal destruction `24:00`.
- Space and time: the _viśva-rūpa_ shows pervasion of space; the _kāla-rūpa_ shows time's destruction `28:00`.
- God destroys the temporary to make way for the eternal `32:00 – 34:00`.
- River → ocean = involuntary (Bhīṣma, Droṇa); moth → fire = voluntary (Duryodhana and allies) `40:00 – 42:00`.
