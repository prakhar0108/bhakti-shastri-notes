### Bhagavad-gita 11.36 – 11.38 [02:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Having now identified the time-form, Arjuna recognises it as the same Viṣṇu, the Supreme Lord, and returns to familiar forms of address. He makes sense of what he saw and glorifies the Lord `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- "Rightly done" — everything happening is appropriate, in its right position `02:00`.
- _Hṛṣīkeśa_ — the Lord who controls the senses `02:00`.
- _Brahmā_ — the word can mean _brāhmaṇas_, Brahman, the _brahma-jyoti_ or Lord Brahmā; here it means Lord Brahmā `06:00`.
- Knower and knowable — Kṛṣṇa knows everything, and He is the one to be known by everyone `08:00`.

#### Three typical reactions to the Lord's glories — verse 36 [02:00 – 06:00]

- When the Lord is glorified, two things happen naturally for the piously inclined: joy increases and attachment to the Lord increases.
- Three reactions:
  - General people become delighted.
  - The demoniac (_rākṣasas_) flee fearfully in all directions.
  - The perfected beings and saints offer obeisances.
- Arjuna had twice described everyone as disturbed during his vision; now he reflects that these typical reactions may not happen when the Lord appears in a fearsome form, but in general this is what happens `04:00`.
- In Vedic culture there are protective mantras against ghosts and _rākṣasas_; this verse is part of such a mantra, said to make _rākṣasas_ flee `04:00`.

#### Why everyone should glorify Him — verse 37 [06:00 – 08:00]

- He is greater than Brahmā: Brahmā is a creator, but He is the Supreme Creator.
- He is unlimited, the controller of all the gods and the shelter of the whole universe.
- Imperishable and transcendental: in this world causes produce effects that become causes in a chain, but He is not mixed up in it.

#### The Supreme object of knowledge — verse 38 [08:00 – 12:00]

- He is the original, primal person and the supreme shelter.
- Kṛṣṇa is omniscient — present as Supersoul in every heart, directly and indirectly cognisant of everything.
- **The microphone example:** one could do a PhD on a microphone — its composition, alignment, resonance. Such knowledge is useful but not ultimate; it cannot give supreme happiness or take us out of material existence. Only by knowing Him does one go beyond `08:00 – 10:00`.
- The universe is pervaded by Him. The Lord is always the Supersoul of the universe (the teacher contrasts the Viṣṇu who is Supersoul in each body with the Viṣṇu who is Supersoul of the whole universe) — but in this revelation He is not only pervading; He is also manifesting as this unlimited form `10:00 – 12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord's glories naturally bring joy to the pious, fear to the demoniac and reverence from the saints; among all possible objects of knowledge, only knowing Him can carry us beyond material existence. [02:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.39 – 11.40 [12:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.39 -->

<!-- vedabase:11.40 -->

**Context.** After three verses of glorification, Arjuna begins offering obeisances `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Unlimited strength and unlimited force — the force by which wonderful things are done; "You are incredibly powerful" `16:00`.
- "You are everything" — not a simplistic equation of everything with God: He pervades everything and is therefore everything `16:00`.

#### All powers are His manifestations — verse 39 [12:00 – 14:00]

- The powers in this world — wind, Yama the controller, fire, the god of water, the moon, Brahmā and the great-grandfather — ultimately are the Supreme Lord.
- This is not absolute oneness but a connection: they are His opulences, and His power is manifest through them.
- All these gods are worshipable, but He is the source of their power; so one obeisance is not enough — Arjuna offers thousands of obeisances, and again and again.

#### Obeisances from every direction — verse 40 [14:00 – 18:00]

- In a temple we bow before the deity; but the universal form is everywhere — front, back, right, left. So where does one bow?
- Hence Arjuna offers obeisances from the front, from behind, from all directions.
- Prabhupāda quotes the _Viṣṇu Purāṇa_: whoever approaches the Lord — even a demigod — He is the creator of the whole universe and of everyone in it; being Creator and controller of everything, He is all-pervading `16:00 – 18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every power in the universe is a manifestation of His power, and before a form that is everywhere one can only bow in every direction, again and again. [12:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.41 – 11.44 [18:00 – 35:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.41 -->

<!-- vedabase:11.42 -->

<!-- vedabase:11.43 -->

<!-- vedabase:11.44 -->

**Context.** Now Arjuna reflects that he behaved intimately with the Absolute Truth, treating Him like a friend, and offers apologies `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Presumptuous address — Arjuna addressed Him as "O Kṛṣṇa," "O Yādava," "O friend" `18:00`.
- Madness or love — the two possible causes Arjuna names for his behaviour `20:00`.
- _Viśrambha-sakhya_ — intimate friendship, rarely if ever covered by awareness of Kṛṣṇa's divinity (as in Vṛndāvana) `22:00`.
- _Gaurava-sakhya_ — friendship with awareness of the friend's greatness (as Arjuna shows here) `22:00`.

#### "I thought I was doing You a favour" — verse 41 [18:00 – 22:00]

- A commentator's explanation cited by the teacher: Arjuna thought, "I am a member of the ruling dynasty and a king; You are just one member of the royal family among the many Yadus, not the king — I did You a favour by befriending You."
- "O Yādava" thus meant "just one of the Yadus," not the greatest among them.
- Arjuna calls this a terrible error of judgment.
- Arjuna does not make another presumption by claiming "I love You"; he says it could have been love or it could have been madness — whatever it was, he begs forgiveness `20:00`.

#### The mood changes: two kinds of friendship [20:00 – 22:00]

- In Vṛndāvana the cowherd boys never apologise to Kṛṣṇa; they tease Him, wrestle with Him, sometimes defeat Him.
- In a Gosvāmī's work cited by the teacher: after Kṛṣṇa defeated Kaṁsa's champion wrestlers, He wrestled with His friends in the arena and let them defeat Him — to increase Kaṁsa's vexation that children who killed his champions were beaten by other children.
- Arjuna bowing, praying and seeking forgiveness shows _gaurava-sakhya_; the cowherd boys show _viśrambha-sakhya_.
- From our point of view, friendship with Kṛṣṇa itself is very exalted; the distinction helps us appreciate how intimate the Vṛndāvana relationships are.

#### Joking alone and in public — verse 42 [22:00 – 26:00]

- They lay down, played, sat and ate together; sometimes, alone or even in front of others, Arjuna teased, made fun of and derided Kṛṣṇa — out of love, but not appropriately.
- Friendly banter is natural, but there are also serious occasions.
- "You are infallible, I commit mistakes — please forgive me."

#### Why You should forgive me — verse 43 [26:00 – 28:00]

- He is the father of all the moving and non-moving; the most worshipable among the worshipable.
- No one is equal to Him, so how can anyone be greater? "How foolish I was to think You are just a Yadu and I am greater than You."
- His power is immeasurable, without parallel in all three worlds — as the universal form has shown.
- Implication: "You are the father, I am the child — forgive me."
- Prabhupāda quotes verses establishing that no one is equal to or greater than Him, including a well-known verse from the Gītā about knowing Him in truth `28:00`.

#### Three examples of forgiveness in love — verse 44 [28:00 – 35:00]

- Arjuna lays down his body in obeisances and gives three examples:
  - A father forgives a son who behaves slightly inappropriately.
  - A friend forgives a friend's taunts and teasing.
  - A lover forgives the beloved's strong or teasing words spoken in intimacy.
- Words and tone: "You are a rascal" said with love carries no anger; "I love you" said angrily means nothing `30:00`.
- **Trust and intentions** `30:00 – 34:00`:
  - With trust, we see intentions beyond actions and overlook wrong actions.
  - Without trust, even good actions are suspected as flattery or manipulation, and bad actions are assumed to come from bad intentions.
  - A low-trust relationship is like walking through a minefield: one step and an explosion.
  - When there is love, there are naturally good actions — sweet speech, kindness, care — and occasional bad actions are forgiven.
- The three examples resemble three _rasas_ — friendship and conjugal love clearly, but the first is the opposite of _vātsalya_ (where the Lord is the son); here God is the father and we are His children, an idea present in Western religions and not alien to the Indian tradition `34:00`.
- **Application:** act positively, build good trust, so that accidental slips don't matter much and we keep moving forward `34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
When awe of the Lord's greatness dawns, intimate friendship turns into apology; and the basis of forgiveness is love and trust, which look past wrong words to the heart behind them. [18:00 – 35:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.45 – 11.46 [35:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.45 -->

<!-- vedabase:11.46 -->

**Context.** Arjuna now makes a request. He echoes the earlier statement that this form had never been seen before: "You have shown me something unique, and I am delighted — yet my mind is tormented by fear" `35:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Never seen before — the same idea Kṛṣṇa had used when introducing the form; Arjuna now confirms it `36:00`.
- Crown, club, disc in hand — symbols of Viṣṇu; by naming these, the other symbols are understood too `40:00`.
- Thousand-armed / thousands of mouths — the present universal form, from which Arjuna asks to see the four-armed form `40:00`.

#### Gratitude and fear together — verse 45 [36:00 – 38:00]

- He is tormented because of the time-form: blood, heads smashed, blood being licked, teeth protruding.
- Before the time-form there was amazement; it changed into agitation, trepidation and then mortal fear.
- "Please show me Your form as the Supreme Personality of Godhead."

#### Bigger is not better for a devotee [38:00]

- In the material world, more is better: someone with a two-channel TV happily upgrades to a hundred channels.
- Having seen a form with two hands and then one with thousands of arms, a materialist would prefer the bigger.
- Prabhupāda: a devotee is not interested in a godless display of opulence; one cannot reciprocate love with it.

#### Why ask for the four-armed form — verse 46 [38:00 – 42:00]

- Arjuna wants to remove any doubt about Kṛṣṇa's divinity: by showing both the universal form and the Viṣṇu form, Kṛṣṇa makes clear He can manifest all forms and is the original Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- "From among Your thousands of arms, please display the form with four arms."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna is grateful for the rare universal form, but his heart seeks the form of the Supreme Person; a devotee values not the size of the display but the possibility of loving exchange. [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.47 – 11.48 [42:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.47 -->

<!-- vedabase:11.48 -->

**Context.** Before revealing the form, Kṛṣṇa responds — in a sense teasing Arjuna: "You asked for this form; it is so rare no one else has seen it — why do you want to see another?" The speaker is still the universal form `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- By My internal (mystic) potency — how the form was shown `44:00`.
- Not by Vedic study, sacrifice, charity, pious activities or severe austerities — the means by which this form cannot be seen `46:00`.
- Material vs materialistic — "material" can mean non-spiritual; "materialistic" usually refers to those in passion and ignorance `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### The implied dialogue [42:00]

- Kṛṣṇa: "This form is so rare." Arjuna: "I'm grateful, but it was a mistake; it's so fearful, I don't want to see it any more." Kṛṣṇa: "It cannot be had by Vedic study." Arjuna: "I'll never ask for it again — please don't show it."
- **Horror-channel analogy:** watching a horror film where the creature starts devouring the walls and people of your own room — you try to change the channel and the remote freezes.

#### Rare and shown only to you — verse 47 [44:00]

- Kṛṣṇa uses epithets like those Arjuna used: a great, transcendental, effulgent form pervading the universe.
- Prabhupāda mentions that what was shown to Duryodhana was different — he was not shown the time-form.

#### Not attainable by Vedic means — verse 48 [46:00 – 48:00]

- Not by study, sacrifice, charity, pious activities or severe austerities can one see this form, which is still manifest at this point.
- No one on the earthly planets has seen this except Arjuna.
- Kṛṣṇa is building up to the point that visions of the Lord do not come by material endeavours — only through mercy.
- Those in goodness have human sensitivity and discrimination; their activities can be material or spiritual — both possibilities exist for everyone — while those in passion mostly act materialistically.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The universal form cannot be earned by study, sacrifice, charity or austerity; Arjuna sees it only by mercy — and still, as a devotee, he would rather not keep it. [42:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.49 [48:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.49 -->

**Context.** Arjuna has inwardly pleaded, "Please free me from this"; this verse is Kṛṣṇa's response `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Bewilderment and fear — let them go away `48:00`.
- Peaceful, satisfied mind — what follows when fear goes `48:00 – 50:00`.

#### Reassurance [48:00 – 50:00]

- "You are My dear devotee; there is no need to be overwhelmed or fearful — now see the form that will pacify you."
- Prabhupāda's purport: the devotee is not much interested in the universal form, because it does not enable one to reciprocate loving feelings.
- The universal form is so vast and all-pervading: whom do I love, how do I converse? Kṛṣṇa is a person — we can talk with Him and offer love through practical service.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord removes His devotee's fear and turns him toward the form with which love can be exchanged, since the universal form offers no room for personal reciprocation. [48:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.50 [50:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.50 -->

**Context.** For the third time in the chapter, the "camera" moves back to Sañjaya — first from verse 9, then for one verse at 35, and now here — because actions must be described by an observer `50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vāsudeva_ — here not the Vāsudeva expansion of the spiritual world, but Kṛṣṇa Himself, the son of Vasudeva `52:00 – 54:00`.
- One's own form — Kṛṣṇa revealing His own form `52:00`.
- _Saumya_ — mellow, soft, sweet; the two-handed form of Kṛṣṇa `54:00`.

#### Why the narrator changes [50:00 – 52:00]

- **Cricket commentary:** players busy playing cannot also give a running commentary; an observer describes the action.
- A conversation alone cannot reveal action, so the "camera" moves to another conversation where the action is seen and described.

#### Three forms in one verse [52:00 – 56:00]

- At the start of the verse there is the universal form.
- By the end of the first two lines it becomes the four-armed Viṣṇu form.
- By the end of the third and fourth lines it becomes the sweet two-handed Kṛṣṇa form.
- The teacher recalls an earlier diagram of Kṛṣṇa's expansions to show that the Viṣṇu forms come from Kṛṣṇa.
- **Doctor analogy:** to an anxious patient, a doctor says, "Everything will be all right; we know how to treat this" — so the Lord reassures fearful Arjuna `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
In a single verse the universal form gives way to the four-armed form and then to Kṛṣṇa's sweet two-handed form — showing that all forms come from Kṛṣṇa, who reassures His devotee like a doctor calming a patient. [52:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.51 [56:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.51 -->

**Context.** Arjuna confirms: "Now I am seeing Your human-like, gentle form; I am settled and restored to my own nature" `56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Human form — two hands, two legs, a head; not a canine or bovine form. Calling Kṛṣṇa's form human is not offensive; calling it material is incorrect `56:00`.
- _Saumya_ — the same word Sañjaya used in verse 50; Arjuna confirms the gentle form `56:00`.
- Own nature — materially, the nature of a fearless _kṣatriya_; spiritually, a loving devotee rejoicing in the Beloved Lord's presence `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Three statements about Kṛṣṇa's form [56:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's form is human — correct; He can incarnate in any form, but His normal original form is two-handed.
- Kṛṣṇa's form is material — incorrect.
- Kṛṣṇa's form is an illusory cover, to be transcended to reach the formless — not just incorrect but offensive.

#### Peace after a nightmare [58:00]

- After the nerve-wracking scene of everyone entering the mouth and blood all around, seeing the normal form brings relief.
- In life too, sudden destabilisations — a relative's disease, losing a job, health problems — disturb us; but our relationship with Kṛṣṇa has not changed. To the extent we see the unchanging reality underlying the changing, we become peaceful.
- Kṛṣṇa wanted to reassure Arjuna; Arjuna confirms, "Yes, everything is all right now."
- The concluding verses on _bhakti_ are left for the next class `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Seeing Kṛṣṇa's familiar two-handed form restores Arjuna to his own nature; likewise, amid life's upheavals, seeing our unchanged relationship with Kṛṣṇa brings back peace. [56:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
