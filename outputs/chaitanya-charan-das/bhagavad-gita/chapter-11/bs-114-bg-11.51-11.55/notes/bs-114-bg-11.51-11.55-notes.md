## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 11.51 [02:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.51 -->

**Context.** Arjuna replies to what Kṛṣṇa has just done: he is now seeing the human, gentle form, he is calmly settled, and he has returned to his own nature `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Saumya_ — soft, gentle, soothing `02:00`.
- _Janārdana_ — can mean chastiser of enemies or protector of people in general `02:00`.
- _Prakṛti_ — basically nature or energy; there is material and spiritual _prakṛti_, both acting by the Lord's will. Here it means Arjuna's own nature. Kṛṣṇa uses the word in a spiritual sense in 9.13 `04:00`.
- _Mānuṣam rūpam_ — human form; not mortal, but two-handed, transcendental and eternal `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### Two levels of Arjuna's relationship [02:00 – 04:00]

- Externally: two _kṣatriya_ friends returning to their friendship.
- Internally: the Lord and His devotee returning to their intimate relationship — both transcendental.

#### When life shows the time-form [04:00 – 08:00]

- Losing a loved one, an immobilising accident, a big financial loss, an earthquake, tsunami or hurricane — in one sense we are seeing Kṛṣṇa's time-form.
- Even a devotee can be bewildered. Being spiritual does not mean being dead to emotion: besides material vs spiritual, there is also human response.
- We exist at three levels — physical, mental and spiritual; the physical and mental are material and they do affect us.
- Arjuna grieved bitterly when Abhimanyu was killed — not because he forgot the philosophy, but because of a father's natural affection.
- "Time I am, the destroyer of all the worlds": not only Kurukṣetra but all destruction is the time-form — not a pleasant form, an agitating one `08:00`.

#### Knowing what to do when we don't know what to do [08:00 – 10:00]

- One definition of intelligence: knowing what to do when we don't know what to do.
- The specific "what to do" may be unclear, but the generic one is clear: pray to Kṛṣṇa for guidance — "keep me engaged in Your service and guide me how I can serve You."
- The guidance may not come immediately, but it will surely come.

#### From the time-form to the gentle form [10:00 – 12:00]

- Devotees don't think tragedies are wonderful and don't want anyone to suffer, but they understand Kṛṣṇa is the caretaker of all and has a plan.
- **Tough love:** a mother is tough with a child playing with fire; the time-form is Kṛṣṇa's tough love, and Gītā wisdom connects the tough part with the love part.
- The time-form destroys the temporary to make way for the eternal: destruction forcefully reminds us that everything here is destructible, impelling us to redirect affection from the world to Kṛṣṇa — and our vision shifts from the time-form to the gentle form.

#### Where is my inner shelter? [12:00 – 16:00]

- When externals change, we pray for Kṛṣṇa to set them right; but the shelter is internal — our connection with Kṛṣṇa.
- The degree to which externals disturb us shows how far our inner shelter lies elsewhere.
- Examples:
  - **Money:** if the rupee falls or the stock market crashes and I am tremendously agitated, my inner shelter is in money, not Kṛṣṇa.
  - **Position:** if, as a counsellor, I am criticised as not caring, competent or advanced enough and I am disturbed, my shelter is in the prestige, not the service.
- We do need some external stability to serve — but first see things at the internal level. External changes are primarily meant to help us make internal changes; when Kṛṣṇa sees that, He can change the externals far more effectively than our plans `16:00`.
- This is not passivity about externals, but not acting only at the external level.
- Moving our shelter is not easy and won't happen overnight — but it will never happen if we don't try. Times of being shaken are opportunities to realign the inner compass toward Kṛṣṇa and return to calmness, poise and happiness `16:00`.

#### Anthropomorphism vs theomorphism [16:00 – 20:00]

- Prabhupāda's whole commentary here focuses on the Lord's original form being human — two-handed, like ours.
- Some ask why God should have a form like ours; but it is not that God has a form like ours — we have a form like His.
- _Anthropomorphism_ — the idea that we ascribed a human form to God because we are human; a self-centred analysis taking ourselves as the reference point.
- _Theomorphism_ — God's original form is reflected in ours. The Bible says man is made in the image of God, and in 15.7 Kṛṣṇa says we are His sparks.
- This two-handed form is not mortal or material but transcendental and eternal.

#### The original form and the original nature [20:00]

- It is far easier to develop affection for the two-handed Kṛṣṇa than for the time-form — before the universal form, even offering obeisances was difficult ("where should I bow?").
- Relating with the original form is the original nature of the living entity; going instead to the impersonal manifestation leads to trouble and frustration, as the 12th chapter discusses.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
When life shows Kṛṣṇa's time-form, even a devotee may be shaken; such moments reveal where our inner shelter lies and invite us to move it to Kṛṣṇa, whose gentle two-handed form — the original of our own — restores us to our natural position. [04:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.52 [20:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.52 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now speaks: the form Arjuna is seeing is extremely difficult to see; even the gods constantly aspire to see it `20:00 – 22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Very difficult to see — the form "as you are seeing Me now" `22:00`.
- Opening of spiritual eyes — Prabhupāda's explanation of how the Lord can be seen, not by ordinary means `24:00`.

#### Even the gods are surprised [22:00 – 24:00]

- In the 10th Canto, the gods offered prayers when Kṛṣṇa was in the womb, knowing Viṣṇu had promised to appear; yet when He appeared, even they were surprised — Brahmā and Indra became bewildered — because they too had not seen this form.
- The cosmology the teacher describes: lower planets, earthly planets, heavenly planets, then the ocean of milk where Viṣṇu resides, and beyond that the spiritual world.
- The gods go to the ocean of milk but generally do not even see Viṣṇu; they pray, and He usually replies through a message in Brahmā's heart. Viṣṇu sometimes comes to fight on their behalf, but seeing Kṛṣṇa is very rare.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's two-handed form is rarer than even the vision of Viṣṇu, longed for by the gods themselves; it is seen only when spiritual eyes are opened. [22:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.53 [24:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.53 -->

**Context.** Having said this form is very difficult to see, Kṛṣṇa lists the ways by which one cannot see Him `24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Study of the Vedas — recitation and memorisation of the Vedas `26:00`.
- Austerity — e.g. by willpower `26:00`.
- Charity — good, in goodness, but not necessarily transcendental `28:00`.
- Worship — in this Vedic context, fire sacrifices in which Agni accepts offerings on behalf of the gods `28:00`.

#### The Badrinath analogy [24:00]

- To say a place is hard to reach, we list the ways that won't work: not by plane, not by train, not by car, not by boat — you must walk to the top.
- Likewise Kṛṣṇa first says how He cannot be known; the next verse says how He can.

#### Seeing this form is itself rare [26:00]

- Impersonalists say a form is for the less intelligent, and the intelligent go beyond it to a higher reality.
- But Kṛṣṇa says seeing this form — "as you are seeing Me" — is itself rare; He does not say going beyond it is rare.
- The normal Vedic process of elevation gives piety and can take one to heaven, but not beyond.

#### Examples for each disqualified means [26:00 – 30:00]

- **Austerity:** a _brahmacārī_ who drank only milk — Śrī Caitanya said the Supreme Lord is not obtained by austerity like that; pure devotion is required.
- **Vedic learning:** a scholar very learned in the Vedas who still did not understand the ultimate goal of life.
- **Charity:** King Hariścandra is glorified for truthfulness, but that does not automatically make him transcendental; goodness alone does not reveal the Lord.
- **Worship:** the _yajña_-performing _brāhmaṇas_ ignored the cowherd boys sent by Kṛṣṇa — impersonally, dehumanisingly — while their wives recognised Him as the Lord of their hearts and went to Him.

#### Seeing is not just seeing [30:00 – 32:00]

- Śrī Caitanya, displeased with Mukunda, said he would not see Him for ten million lifetimes; Mukunda danced in joy that after ten million lifetimes he would see Him, and Śrī Caitanya, pleased by his faith, humility and devotion, called him back.
- Mukunda then prayed: even demons like Śiśupāla, Dantavakra, Kaṁsa and Jarāsandha saw You — and wanted to shoot arrows at the all-attractive form adored by Brahmā, Śiva and Lakṣmī. Real seeing is seeing with devotion; please grant me devotion.
- So by these good, virtuous Vedic activities one may even see the Lord but not understand the significance of the vision — as if one had not seen Him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Vedic study, austerity, charity and sacrifice are virtuous, but none of them reveals Kṛṣṇa; even seeing Him is not truly seeing without devotion. [24:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.54 [32:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.54 -->

**Context.** Now Kṛṣṇa states the means: by devotional service He can be known as He stands now, seen, and entered into `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ananya_ (undivided) — directed to no one else and containing nothing else; not mixed with fruitive activity or speculative knowledge `32:00`.
- Enter — not into the _brahma-jyoti_; Prabhupāda translates it as entering into the mysteries of Kṛṣṇa's understanding `34:00`.

#### No reality beyond Kṛṣṇa [34:00]

- Kṛṣṇa makes no impersonal reference: He does not say there is a truth higher than Him to go beyond to.
- One will understand, see, know and enter — and so progress in spiritual life.

#### Why only devotion works [34:00 – 38:00]

- The Lord is compassionate and wants to reveal Himself, but He relates with all beings in love, and on that love all reciprocations, revelations and relationships of the spiritual world rest.
- We have body, mind, intelligence, ego — and we are the soul, the seat of emotions (what we mean by "heart"), which has a natural relationship with Kṛṣṇa.
- Other paths activate other parts of our being:
  - _Karma_ — mainly physical.
  - _Jñāna_ — intellectual.
  - _Yoga_ — mental.
- The disqualified means of verse 53 are external: speaking with the mouth, money for charity, ghee and grains for worship — where is the heart invested?
- In undivided devotion the heart is purely offered; body, mind and intelligence are used but not distracted to other things — and Kṛṣṇa reciprocates by revealing Himself.

#### The train-ticket analogy [36:00 – 38:00]

- A ticket inspector asks for your ticket; you say, "I have a degree from IIT, I was a gold medallist… a degree from MIT… a best-teacher award." "That's good — but show me your ticket."
- A particular path needs a particular qualification; charity, austerity and Vedic study may contribute, but they must ultimately contribute to _bhakti_.

#### Other processes in the service of bhakti [38:00 – 40:00]

- Prabhupāda describes Śrī Caitanya as the most munificent, freely giving _bhakti_.
- Charity given for Kṛṣṇa is no longer just charity; it becomes _bhakti_.
- For beginners, deity worship in the temple is essential.
- Prabhupāda quotes a famous verse: for one who has devotion to the Lord and the guru, the meaning of scripture is revealed.

#### The two-handed form is the highest [40:00 – 44:00]

- Prabhupāda emphasises that the two-handed and four-handed forms are completely different from the temporary universal form.
- Sequence: Kṛṣṇa → universal form → four-handed Nārāyaṇa form → Kṛṣṇa's own two-handed form; all forms come from Kṛṣṇa.
- Those who mistake bigness for greatness — like someone with a hundred-channel TV who can't understand asking for two channels — cannot see why two arms are greatest. Greatness lies in the intimacy of love the form reciprocates.
- Prabhupāda quotes verses describing Mahā-Viṣṇu, from whose pores universes come and go within one breath, as just one expansion of the original Lord; and verses calling Kṛṣṇa the Supreme Brahman descended in a human-like form.
- Conclusion: the devotee is attracted not to the universal form but to the two-handed Kṛṣṇa; there is no reality higher than this form, and it is known only by _bhakti_ `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Only undivided devotion — offering the heart itself — lets one know, see and enter into the mystery of Kṛṣṇa; all other qualifications are useful only as they become bhakti, and there is no reality beyond His two-handed form. [32:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.55 [44:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.55 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa summarises the chapter; Prabhupāda says this verse is the sum and substance of the Gītā `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mat-karma-kṛt_ — doing My work: actively serving Kṛṣṇa, e.g. deity worship, book distribution, missionary activities `46:00 – 48:00`.
- _Mat-parama_ — making Kṛṣṇa the supreme goal of life `48:00`.
- _Mad-bhakta_ — become My devotee; the central part `52:00`.
- _Saṅga_ — can mean association and attachment; external association creates internal attachment `52:00`.
- Not envious of any living being `54:00`.

#### "Me" again and again [46:00]

- Against the idea of some truth beyond Kṛṣṇa, the very next verse says "you will come to Me"; the word for "Me" occurs several times, all referring to Kṛṣṇa — as both the means and the goal.

#### Levels of working for Kṛṣṇa [46:00 – 48:00]

- In 9.27 Kṛṣṇa says offer the fruits of whatever work you do — a good level, by which one also comes back to Him, but not the highest. The teacher notes that an _ācārya_ analyses such levels and does not consider this a very elevated platform.
- Here Kṛṣṇa says do _My_ work — direct service.

#### Intermediate goals and the ultimate goal [48:00 – 52:00]

- Parīkṣit Mahārāja ruled — policies, conflicts, politics — as his God-given duty; but when told he would die in seven days, he immediately renounced the world and heard the _Bhāgavatam_ for seven days.
- We may set intermediate goals that contribute to devotion: becoming a good speaker to share Kṛṣṇa consciousness, a good mother to raise children in it, a competent businessman to have resources to serve.
- Whether Kṛṣṇa is really our ultimate goal is tested when an intermediate goal conflicts with Him; then we must courageously sacrifice the intermediate for the ultimate.
- That doesn't mean everyone must renounce like Parīkṣit — perhaps just move something to the background and bring Kṛṣṇa to the foreground.
- Prabhupāda's examples: a builder can build a temple, a person can grow flowers for Kṛṣṇa or clean the temple `52:00`.

#### Association and attachment [52:00 – 54:00]

- Contemplating sense objects breeds attachment, then desire.
- External association leads to internal attachment: associating with drunkards gradually makes one a drunkard.
- Internal contaminations are of two kinds — fruitive activity and impersonal speculation — both distracting from Kṛṣṇa.

#### Fight, yet be envious of none [54:00 – 58:00]

- A jolting contrast: Arjuna has just seen the Kauravas devoured and been told to fight and conquer — yet now he is told to be envious of no living being, which includes the Kauravas.
- The mystery of purified consciousness: Arjuna has nothing personal against them; they are against _dharma_ and he is the Lord's servant.
- A devotee is a person with personal emotions but no self-serving agenda, so he serves faithfully without animosity.
- **Rāma and Vibhīṣaṇa:** after Rāvaṇa's death, Vibhīṣaṇa refused to perform his last rites because of Rāvaṇa's sins; Rāma told him the soul is always pure, deserving neither hatred nor anger, and asked him to perform the rites for an auspicious destination. Rāma fought and killed Rāvaṇa without animosity.
- **Prabhupāda's "rascals":** not used in anger or hatred; in a lecture he prays, "Kṛṣṇa, these rascals are suffering in forgetfulness of You; give me the intelligence to help them come back to You." It is like a parent or grandparent telling a child not to be mischievous. Prabhupāda would say he could use such words, but others using them might not be understood — he had the advantage of age and genuine compassion `58:00`.
- We should be well-wishers of all living beings.

#### How the last three verses fit together [58:00 – 60:00]

- Verse 53 — by other paths one cannot know Him.
- Verse 54 — by undivided _bhakti_ one can know Him.
- Verse 55 — how to come to that level: by these practical activities we gradually reach undivided devotion, know Kṛṣṇa and return to Him.
- Kṛṣṇa concludes the chapter with the glory of devotion `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The sum and substance of the Gītā is practical: do Kṛṣṇa's work, make Him the goal over every intermediate goal, avoid association that breeds contaminating attachment, and wish everyone well — this leads to undivided devotion and back to Him. [44:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
