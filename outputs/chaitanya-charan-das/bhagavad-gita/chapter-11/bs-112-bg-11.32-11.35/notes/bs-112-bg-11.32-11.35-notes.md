## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 11.32 [00:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.32 -->

**Context.** The speaker is introduced as the Supreme Lord — it is Kṛṣṇa Himself speaking as the universal form. In the first line itself He answers both of Arjuna's questions: who He is (time) and what He has come for (destruction of the worlds) `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kāla_ — time; the Lord identifies Himself as time, the destroyer of all the worlds `02:00`.
- "All the worlds" — the principal kings of the world, who will be engaged in mutual destruction `02:00`.
- _Kāla-rūpa_ — the time-form; ferocious for materialists but not for devotees `06:00`.
- The verse's qualifying word about Arjuna — the teacher explains it can mean "except for you" or "without you"; both readings are developed `02:00`, `08:00`.

#### Reading one: "except for you" — the war's survivors [02:00 – 06:00]

- The Kauravas were defeated and the Pāṇḍavas victorious: after Karṇa fell on the 17th day, Śalya commanded on the 18th and was killed by Yudhiṣṭhira; Duryodhana fled, hid under a lake, and was challenged and mortally wounded by Bhīma.
- That night Aśvatthāmā, with Kṛtavarmā and Kṛpa, slaughtered the sleeping Pāṇḍava warriors in their camp. The five Pāṇḍavas survived because, by tradition, victors slept on the battlefield to claim the land they had won.
- Survivors on the Pāṇḍava side: the five Pāṇḍavas, Sātyaki and Kṛṣṇa. Sātyaki — a Yadu warrior, Arjuna's close friend and student — and Kṛtavarmā (who came with the army Duryodhana had chosen) were the only Yadu warriors who fought.
- Survivors on the Kaurava side: Aśvatthāmā, Kṛpa and Kṛtavarmā — plus one half-brother of the hundred Kauravas, born of Dhṛtarāṣṭra through a maidservant, who had defected to the Pāṇḍavas before the war and performed the Kauravas' final rites `06:00`.
- So practically nobody survived — "except for you" everyone was destroyed.

#### The time-form is not ferocious for devotees [06:00 – 08:00]

- Lord Nṛsiṁha roared fiercely at the demons but became like a gentle, purring cat before Prahlāda, placing His hand on Prahlāda's head.
- The teacher cites a verse from a commentator devoted to Nṛsiṁha: just as a lioness is fierce to everyone except her own cubs, the Lord is fearsome to all except His devotees.
- In the same way, the universal form does not destroy the Pāṇḍavas.

#### Reading two: "without you" — destruction already ordained [08:00]

- Even if Arjuna does not fight, the Lord as time will still destroy everyone.
- Arjuna may think "I won't fight, I won't incur sinful reactions, I won't kill my relatives" — but their killing is already ordained by divine arrangement and cannot be stopped.

#### Is this a "cannibalistic" God? [08:00 – 16:00]

- The teacher cites an Upaniṣadic verse describing everyone being consumed like a meal by the Supreme — admittedly a "scary" verse `08:00`.
- Cannibalism is barbaric, outlawed, and abhorrent for any civilised person — what to speak of attributing it to God `10:00`.
- A court case in Russia tried to ban _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_, arguing that its devotees worship a God who drinks blood and eats people `10:00`.
- The teacher calls this a weak, distorted, out-of-context argument: read literalistically, every scripture can be faulted — he gives examples of violent conquest narratives from the Old Testament and from the Quran, which different people interpret and contextualise in different ways `10:00 – 12:00`.
- Answers given in the Gītā's own context `14:00 – 16:00`:
  - The _Mahābhārata_ goes to great lengths to show how cruel the Kauravas were and how tolerant and conciliatory the Pāṇḍavas were; even Kṛṣṇa's peace mission failed, so war became a necessity — and the world needed godly administrators.
  - The Gītā is a philosophical discussion, and this is a revelation within it, not to be taken literalistically.
  - The universal form is not eternal — there is Goloka and Ayodhyā in the spiritual world, but no planet of the universal form with eternal worshippers. It is a temporary revelation demonstrating how the Lord pervades everything.
  - The destruction is metaphorical, showing that everything in nature is within the Absolute Truth's jurisdiction.

#### Destruction within God's jurisdiction [16:00]

- A virtuous king still has a prison; the prison does not contradict his virtue.
- Destruction is a reality of this world and cannot be divorced from God, whose jurisdiction covers everything.
- God has no vendetta and is not cannibalistic; people are destroyed because of their own bad _karma_, not God's malevolence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord reveals Himself as time, which destroys the temporary; this destruction is not divine malevolence but a sign that nothing lies outside His jurisdiction — and for His devotees the same fearsome Lord is affectionate, like a lioness with her cubs. [02:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.33 [18:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.33 -->

**Context.** Having established that destruction is coming, the Lord says "therefore" arise: what was implied before is now explicit — the others will die, Arjuna will not, so he will emerge victorious `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nimitta-mātram_ — merely an instrument `24:00`.
- _Savya-sācin_ — one who can shoot with both hands; Arjuna was ambidextrous, the best archer in the world, able to hit targets even in darkness `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### "Enjoy the kingdom" — devotees are not meant to be deprived [18:00 – 20:00]

- Devotees are meant to serve, not enjoy — true — but there is enjoyment within service; Kṛṣṇa does not want us deprived.
- Real enjoyment is spiritual, in our natural activity as parts of Kṛṣṇa who lovingly serve Him.
- Regulation and restriction are not meant to deprive us of enjoyment; they are meant to end our deprivation of it, by freeing consciousness from the material level.
- Even in this world a devotee finds joy in service — e.g. building a big temple for the Lord's glorification `20:00`.

#### "Already killed by My arrangement" — battle fixing? [20:00 – 24:00]

- The teacher raises the comparison with sports match fixing: is Kurukṣetra "battle fixing"?
- The real point is not fixing the battle but fixing our consciousness. The whole purpose of the Gītā is to fix Arjuna's consciousness in devotion.
- Kṛṣṇa needs no one: He can build temples and spread His message Himself; one inspiration from the Supersoul can transform a person more than the best preacher's lecture.
- Kṛṣṇa is concerned not just with material results but with spiritual transformation.
- "Match fixing" also means arranging marriages — and the eternal romance is between the soul and the Lord. The match Kṛṣṇa wants to fix is our revived relationship with Him; all external situations are facilities for that.

#### Assurance, not licence [24:00]

- Knowing the opponents are already killed is not a licence for passivity. Arjuna did not say, "You've already killed everyone, so You do it; I'll sit and drink _bhang_" — a phrase the teacher attributes to Prabhupāda mimicking some so-called religious people.
- A licence means "do whatever you want"; an assurance means "you should do this, and if you hesitate about success, I assure you of it."

#### Why becoming an instrument is not demeaning [24:00 – 28:00]

- Some feel being "just an instrument" is demeaning — "I am my own person."
- But our intelligence is never as good as God's, and our own plans often create more problems than they solve.
- Ecology example: the best thing scientists can do is often to stop interfering — nature can heal itself, while much of our activity (exploitation, deforestation) wounds it. The teacher clarifies this is not a call to reject all scientific progress, which needs broader discussion.
- Our plans and intelligence are finite, so their results are at best mediocre; as instruments of Kṛṣṇa's plan, our intelligence is complemented and perfected by His.
- Kṛṣṇa does not minimise Arjuna: He immediately acknowledges his skill as _savya-sācin_. Devotees' abilities — singing, managing, speaking — attain perfection when used in Kṛṣṇa's service `28:00`.

#### How do I know Kṛṣṇa's plan? [28:00 – 30:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that people think everything happens by chance, but everything happens by plan; perfection is to understand and cooperate with it.
- We may not always have direct instructions from the spiritual master. Take care of the broad aspects — direct connection with Kṛṣṇa, proper study of scripture, proper _sādhana_ — and the specific aspects take care of themselves.
- When service is clear, do it; when it is unclear, Kṛṣṇa will guide externally and internally.
- Avoid "paralysis by analysis": take the service opportunities at hand; the more sincerely we serve, the more Kṛṣṇa reveals.

#### Mission Impossible → Mission Unstoppable [30:00 – 34:00]

- Sharing Kṛṣṇa consciousness can feel like "Mission Impossible" when so many are uninterested.
- But it is Kṛṣṇa's mission, not ours. The teacher cites the Bible ("when God is for you, who can be against you") and reverses it: we should be for God.
- Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu predicted His name would be chanted in every town and village; Prabhupāda took up that responsibility with vision and faith and got the glory.
- "Unstoppable" does not mean every plan succeeds: Prabhupāda's League of Devotees had to be shut down, and his attempt to assist a godbrother's mission did not succeed `32:00`.
- Like the Gaṅgā flowing to the ocean: when an obstacle is too strong, it flows over, under, left or right — a devotee keeps finding a way to serve.

#### Why devotees sometimes fail [34:00 – 36:00]

- Success can infatuate us, make us proud, and lead us away from God in the name of His service.
- Internally too — battling lust, greed, anger — we may be overpowered despite taking shelter. This is not because the holy name doesn't work; Kṛṣṇa wants us to realise victory comes by grace, and to beg for it sincerely.
- After long struggle, success shows itself as Kṛṣṇa's grace: "if it came by my effort, it would have come much earlier" — so we stay dependent.
- Sometimes Kṛṣṇa gives a failure in order to give a greater victory.

#### Jaṭāyu: lost the battle, won the war [36:00 – 42:00]

- Rāvaṇa consulted an astrologer, who named a period within which the abduction of Sītā would succeed; he planned for Mārīca to draw away Rāma and then Lakṣmaṇa.
- Jaṭāyu fought Rāvaṇa for hours, so that auspicious period expired — within the pastime, he succeeded in delaying the abduction.
- Vyāsadeva consoled Yudhiṣṭhira after Abhimanyu's death: Yudhiṣṭhira felt he had sent a 16-year-old to slaughter when Jayadratha blocked the other Pāṇḍavas from following him into the military formation, and six Kaurava great warriors killed him. Vyāsadeva said that dying in a righteous war in the Lord's mission is a devotional perfection; Abhimanyu had reached an abode from which temptation can never pull him down — lament for us who remain `38:00 – 40:00`.
- Likewise Jaṭāyu, a vulture — considered an inauspicious, lower bird — served the Lord, died in His lap chanting His name, and had the Lord perform his last rites as for His own father; he went back to the spiritual world `40:00`.
- A battle is one unit of events; a war is the larger confrontation — like battles within World War I `40:00 – 42:00`.
- We may lose fights but should not lose the fighting spirit, which rests on Kṛṣṇa being with us and the ever-present opportunity to serve Him.

#### Beyond karmic limits [42:00 – 44:00]

- In this world one rises only as far as one's karmic credits allow; many work hard, yet few become billionaires, because success also needs circumstances arranged by _karma_.
- A devotee's success is not limited by karmic credits — it can go as far as Kṛṣṇa wishes to empower, and He wants to empower unlimitedly.
- Prabhupāda told devotees: if you firmly believe Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, He can give you the world in eighteen days, as He gave it to Arjuna.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Divine arrangement is an assurance, not a licence: the Lord already has the result in hand, and asks us to act as His instruments so that our consciousness becomes fixed in Him; we may lose battles, but with Him we never need lose the fighting spirit. [20:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.34 [44:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.34 -->

**Context.** The Lord names specific enemies already killed by His arrangement and tells Arjuna to give up his disturbance, fight, and conquer `44:00 – 46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Disturbance, fear, apprehension — what Arjuna is told to destroy and give up `46:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's purport: perfect in success or failure [44:00]

- One in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness, devoted to the Lord's transcendental service, is perfect; fighting in accordance with the Lord, he is perfect in either success or failure and successful by the Lord's arrangement.

#### Why Jayadratha is named [46:00]

- In his description of the universal form, Arjuna mentioned Bhīṣma, Droṇa and Karṇa, but not Jayadratha.
- Jayadratha was not a prominent hero; when Duryodhana listed the warriors on both sides, he did not even bother to mention him.
- Kṛṣṇa names him because he will later play a critical role: he will cause Abhimanyu's death, Arjuna will vow to kill him or enter fire, and Duryodhana will arrange practically the whole army to protect him — a herculean, nearly impossible task Arjuna will accomplish by the Lord's will.

#### Our own battles [48:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that the plans of the devotees are as good as the Lord's plans; one should follow such plans and be victorious in the struggle for existence.
- We may not fight at Kurukṣetra, but we have our own battles.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
By naming even the warrior whose death would later seem nearly impossible, Kṛṣṇa assures Arjuna in advance; a devotee who acts by the Lord's plan can give up fear, because success is by His arrangement. [44:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.35 [48:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.35 -->

**Context.** The "camera" moves back to Sañjaya, who describes Arjuna trembling with folded hands, offering obeisances again and again and speaking falteringly; the universal form spoke encouraging, action-calling words, yet remained fearsome to see `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kirīṭī_ — the crown-wearer; Prabhupāda translates it as helmet or crown, worn on the head for both protection and ornament. It can refer to any crowned _kṣatriya_, but is specifically one of Arjuna's names `48:00 – 50:00`.
- Folded hands — Arjuna holds his hands folded while offering obeisances and bowing down `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### Why the greatest warrior trembles [48:00 – 50:00]

- _Kṣatriyas_ are trained to see blood and violence and to face death without flinching; the 18th chapter lists not fleeing the battlefield among their qualities.
- Yet the time-form is so fearsome that even Arjuna, the greatest of _kṣatriyas_, trembles.

#### The Lord can overwhelm anyone [50:00]

- Lord Śiva could not be agitated even when Pārvatī tried; but on seeing Mohinī-mūrti he ran after her, forgetting Pārvatī and his own worshippers.
- What nothing in this world can do, the Lord can do: the most composed can be bewildered and the most fearless made fearful — the Lord's omnipotence.

#### From description to prayer [52:00]

- Until now Arjuna was describing; now he begins a prayer, which goes through phases that will be studied next class.

#### Conceptualization: the universal form in the Bhāgavatam [52:00 – 56:00]

- The Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam describes the universal form elaborately; the basic idea is to connect the universe with God and see its manifestations as His form, spiritualising our vision.
- Meditation on it is called the first step in God-realisation, because in material consciousness what is big attracts respect — as the biggest, strongest lion is respected as king.
- The meditation: mountains as the Lord's bones, sun and moon as His eyes, rivers as water in His navel, trees as His bodily hair, higher planets as His head, lower planets as His legs.
- Prabhupāda explains this is a conceptualisation — the Bhāgavatam uses a word related to _kalpanā_ (imagination). Imagination is not bad if it connects us with God; it makes people God-conscious at a preliminary level.
- The Bhāgavatam's revelation is progressive: from the universal form to the Supersoul in the heart, to Bhagavān, culminating in Śrī Kṛṣṇa `56:00`.

#### Revelation: the universal form in the Gītā [56:00 – 58:00]

| Bhāgavatam's universal form | Gītā's universal form |
| --- | --- |
| Conceptualisation, guided by scripture | Revelation — everything flashes before Arjuna |
| _Sādhana_ for beginners | _Kṛpā_ (mercy) that Arjuna receives |
| Bottom-up | Top-down |
| Time-form not shown | Time-form specially shown |

#### Why the time-form was never shown before [58:00 – 60:00]

- Kṛṣṇa had shown versions of the universal form earlier: to Mother Yaśodā when she opened His mouth after He was said to have eaten mud, and to Duryodhana when Duryodhana tried to arrest Him during the peace mission.
- Duryodhana's vision was scary and contained practically all beings, but he did not see himself and his brothers being consumed — no blood, no devouring.
- The time-form was shown only to Arjuna, both to fulfil his request and to reassure him of victory — hence the description of it as never seen before.
- This answers why Arjuna asked "who are you?" even after identifying the form as the universal form and as Viṣṇu (verses 16, 24, 30): the time-form within it had never been seen `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The time-form was a unique revelation of mercy, not a meditation built from imagination; it was so fearsome that even the greatest kṣatriya trembled — showing that the Lord can overwhelm anyone, and preparing Arjuna's turn from description to prayer. [48:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
