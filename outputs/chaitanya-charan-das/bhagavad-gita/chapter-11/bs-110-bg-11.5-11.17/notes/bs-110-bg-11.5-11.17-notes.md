## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 11.5 [02:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.5 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to behold not one form but many forms — divine, of many colours and shapes — within one majestic form `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nānā-varṇa_ — many colours `04:00`.
- _Ākṛti_ — shape `04:00`.

#### Many forms in one form [02:00 – 04:00]

- The universal form is one majestic form in which many forms exist in cohesion and synthesis.
- Those forms are divine and connected with God, and they vary in colour and shape — this is the mysterious vision Kṛṣṇa is revealing to Arjuna.

#### We see with our intelligence [04:00 – 08:00]

- This is not just a philosophical point but a practical reality: we cannot make sense of things unless our intelligence has some framework.
- The teacher contrasts congenital blindness (from birth) with acquired blindness (losing sight after having had it).
- Medical journal articles describe people blind from birth who received sight through surgery after fifteen or twenty years:
  - One said that on opening his eyes he saw only patterns of dark and bright colours; because he knew a voice comes from a face, he guessed that what he was seeing was a face.
  - A carpenter, blind for many years, had used tools to cut wood. When he first saw a saw he could not make sense of it by sight, but the moment he touched it he knew the blade and the handle, and could use it — better with touch than with sight.
- Knowledge proceeds from the familiar to the unfamiliar; with no prior frame of reference, such people need a long period of acculturation to function with their eyes.

#### Arjuna's orientation course [08:00 – 10:00]

- Arjuna is going to get a vision for which he has no prior connection — nobody has seen anything like it; later in the chapter it is called something never seen before.
- So these verses give Arjuna a basic framework. Like a new employee's orientation course — "this is this department, this person is in charge here" — Kṛṣṇa first describes, through these four verses, the view that will follow.

#### Why Arjuna asked [10:00 – 12:00]

- Arjuna is not personally interested in seeing the form. As with his question in the previous chapter — asked so that people who cannot see spiritual reality could move from matter to spirit — here the purpose is for people to know who is God.
- One who is God has extraordinary power; one thing He can do is display the universal form.
- Kṛṣṇa sets a precedent: anyone who claims to be God must be able to show the universal form. Arjuna's request is primarily for people in general, so that they have a standard for knowing who is and who is not God.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Because we see only through a framework, Kṛṣṇa first orients Arjuna with a description before showing the vision — and the vision itself sets a lasting standard for recognising who is truly God. [02:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.6 [12:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.6 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to see the Ādityas, Vasus, Rudras, Aśvinī-kumāras and Maruts — wonders never seen before `12:00 – 14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Adṛṣṭa-pūrva_ — never seen before `08:00`.

#### Two kinds of illusion [12:00]

- Thinking an ordinary person to be God, and not believing the real God to be God.
- In the eighteenth chapter, among the characteristics of knowledge in ignorance, Kṛṣṇa describes perceiving the exact opposite of reality. Kṛṣṇa — and Arjuna — want to protect people from that misfortune.
- So it is not just Kṛṣṇa speaking about Himself; He demonstrates it, so that people know He is not merely speaking words.

#### Powerful beings in one form [14:00]

- These are powerful personalities in the universe, not easily visible to human beings.
- Seeing all of them together in one form is a revelation of the Absolute Truth — something extraordinary, never seen before.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The universal form protects people from both illusions — taking an ordinary person for God and denying the real God — by showing within one form even the great cosmic beings that humans never see. [12:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.7 [16:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.7 -->

**Context.** Everything moving and non-moving in the universe can be seen at one place, in Kṛṣṇa's body — and whatever else Arjuna wishes to see `16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Eka-stha_ — at one place `16:00 – 18:00`.
- _Yac cānyad draṣṭum icchasi_ — "whatever else you wish to see" `16:00`.

#### Whatever else you wish to see [16:00 – 18:00]

- Arjuna is about to fight a fierce war and would naturally want to know the outcome — who will win and who will lose.
- He did not specifically ask for this, but Kṛṣṇa will show him that too. Thus Kṛṣṇa gives Arjuna a temporary, provisional omniscience.

#### Seeing everything at one place [18:00 – 20:00]

- In one place we normally see only what is visible there. Through high-tech gadgets and the internet we can see other places, but that is secondary, dependent on intermediate sources — people writing, cameras showing.
- God is all-pervading and has vision and knowledge of everything.
- In the universal form Arjuna sees not every detail of the universe, but its prominent beings — the whole universe, in Kṛṣṇa's body.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa grants Arjuna a provisional share of His own all-seeing vision — the whole universe and even the future, at one place — something no gadget can give, because only the all-pervading Lord truly sees everything. [16:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.8 [20:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.8 -->

**Context.** Arjuna cannot see this with his present eyes, so Kṛṣṇa gives him divine eyes to behold His mystic opulence `20:00 – 22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Divya-cakṣu_ — divine eyes `20:00`.
- _Yogam aiśvaram_ — mystic opulence; the same expression as in BG 9.5 `20:00 – 22:00`.

#### "If it is possible for me" — it is not [20:00 – 22:00]

- In BG 11.4 Arjuna said, "if it is possible for me to see." Kṛṣṇa answers that, actually, it is not possible with the eyes he has — so He will give divine eyes.
- In BG 9.5 Kṛṣṇa described how He sustains all beings, and Arjuna still did not fully understand; now he will see.
- Divine vision is not like an organ transplant, where something is taken from one person and fitted into another; it is not that kind of thing.

#### Levels of access [24:00]

- The universe is like a mainframe computer with a huge amount of information; different users have different levels of access — some only certain files, some everything.
- Human beings have a particular level of access; Kṛṣṇa has complete access.
- Like a parent who gives a child access on the computer, Kṛṣṇa gives Arjuna access to the highest level — not just permission, but the ability to see.

#### Is the universal form spiritual? [24:00 – 26:00]

- A commentator says something interesting: the _virāṭ-rūpa_ is itself not spiritual but material, because it concerns the universe, which is material.
- By another definition, anything connected with Kṛṣṇa is spiritual.

#### The Lord conceals His power out of love [26:00 – 30:00]

- Out of love, Kṛṣṇa conceals His supreme power and nature because He wants to reciprocate and relish love — this is the divine intimate vision.
- Others saw Him just like a human being — normal human vision. Arjuna had a purely devotional vision; seeing the universal form is actually a downgrading, not an upgrading.
- Kṛṣṇa is attracted by loving features, not by godly displays of opulence. God is not interested in a display of power — "I am so great."
- He reveals His divinity sometimes so that people can come out of illusion, give up their rebellion and forgetfulness of Him, and return to connection and reunion with Him.
- Devotees already accept Kṛṣṇa. It is very difficult to love the _virāṭ-rūpa_; Arjuna himself will soon ask, "Who are You? Please tell me."

#### Not a form for intimate reciprocation [30:00 – 34:00]

- With the _virāṭ-rūpa_ one cannot have the loving exchanges of _rasa_, such as feeding Him.
- It displays God's opulence in terms of power and pervasiveness — evoking awe — but is not conducive to intimate, loving, devotional reciprocation.
- Different people perceive God differently. The cowherd boys, by their accumulated pious credit, are able to play with Kṛṣṇa as a friend.
- Certain perceptions are favourable to devotion; the _virāṭ-rūpa_ perception is not very favourable to intimate reciprocation.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Divine eyes let Arjuna see Kṛṣṇa's cosmic power, but this is a step down from his intimate vision: Kṛṣṇa conceals His majesty out of love and reveals it only to free people from illusion — love, not awe, is what attracts Him. [20:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.9 [34:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.9 -->

**Context.** Sañjaya now speaks to the king: having said this, the great Lord of mystic power, Hari, revealed His supreme opulent form to Arjuna `36:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mahā-yogeśvara_ — the great Lord of mystic power `36:00`.
- _Paramaṁ rūpam aiśvaram_ — the supreme opulent form `38:00`.

#### The camera zooms out [34:00 – 36:00]

- In a film, to convey action properly the camera sometimes zooms in close — on two people fighting hand to hand — and sometimes pulls back to show the overall direction of the battle.
- Here the camera "goes backwards": from Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna to Sañjaya, who describes what is seen.
- By this method not only the vision but also Arjuna's reactions — his thoughts, emotions and bodily reactions — are conveyed through Sañjaya.

#### Mahā-yogeśvara [36:00]

- What Kṛṣṇa is going to do is not ordinary. Arjuna called Him _yogeśvara_ in BG 11.4 — "You can show me because You are _yogeśvara_."
- Sañjaya highlights that point with _mahā-yogeśvara_: no one else can do this; Hari alone has the mystic power to reveal the _virāṭ_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The narration steps back to Sañjaya so that both the vision and Arjuna's reactions can be seen, and Sañjaya confirms Arjuna's own word — only the great Lord of mysticism can reveal such a form. [34:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.12 [42:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.12 -->

**Context.** If a thousand suns rose at once in the sky, their effulgence might resemble that of the Supreme Person in that universal form `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sūrya-sahasra_ — a thousand suns `42:00`.
- _Mahātmā_ — "great soul," used here for the Supreme `42:00`.

#### The suns at universal destruction [42:00]

- At the time of the universe's destruction, one of the factors that triggers and accelerates destruction is the increasing temperature of the sun.
- Like that, if thousands of suns were blazing at once, that might give some indication of the effulgence.

#### Words are pointers, not describers [44:00 – 46:00]

- The reach of words is not as much as the scope of the experience; the vision is so beyond that words cannot reach it.
- In such situations words are pointers, not describers — indicators.
- A mental image cannot invoke the Supreme Personality of Godhead; the Supreme Lord is perceived when He reveals Himself.
- What we describe through words is very small compared with what He is.

#### Glorification that falls short [46:00 – 48:00]

- Calling the emperor of a whole country "the landlord of the village" would, from the perspective of his actual position, be almost an insult.
- Similarly, our words cannot adequately describe the Lord of lords.
- Still, we try to speak His glory — for our purification and to communicate something about Him to the world.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even "a thousand suns" is only a pointer; words can never contain the Lord, who is seen only when He reveals Himself — yet we keep glorifying Him for our purification and for others' benefit. [42:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.13 [48:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.13 -->

**Context.** Sañjaya says that Arjuna, the son of Pāṇḍu, then saw the whole universe situated in one place in the body of the God of gods `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Deva-deva_ — God of gods `48:00`.
- _Śarīra_ — body; here it need not mean a material body — it can be a transcendental body `48:00`.

#### Sañjaya's choice of address [48:00 – 52:00]

- The scene is told from Sañjaya's perspective: Arjuna sees the universe in the body of the Supreme.
- Sañjaya speaks of Arjuna as a Pāṇḍava. From Dhṛtarāṣṭra's point of view, Pāṇḍu's sons are not his sons — a particular sentiment is there, and it underlies how Sañjaya addresses Arjuna.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna sees the entire universe in one place within the Lord's body — a body that need not be material — and Sañjaya's way of naming Arjuna quietly reminds the blind king whose side Kṛṣṇa is revealing Himself to. [48:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.14 [52:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.14 -->

**Context.** Arjuna's emotional reaction to the vision is amazement, and from here he begins to speak `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vismaya_ — amazement; Arjuna's emotional reaction `52:00`.

#### From Sañjaya back to Arjuna [52:00 – 56:00]

- From the chapter overview: BG 11.15 – 11.31 is Arjuna's description of the form; until now Sañjaya has been describing.
- Kṛṣṇa first described what He would reveal (so Arjuna would see something he had not even asked to see); Sañjaya then confirmed that the great mystic revealed the form, underscoring its many faces, many hands and many weapons.
- Sañjaya elaborates in a couple of verses what Kṛṣṇa said in BG 11.5; he gives only a quick overview, not the details — those come in Arjuna's description.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sañjaya hands the narration back to Arjuna at the moment of amazement; the overview is over, and the detailed description will come from the one actually seeing. [52:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.15 [56:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.15 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says he sees in the Lord's body all the gods and the various living beings — from Brahmā at the top of the universe down to Śeṣa-nāga at the bottom `56:00`.

#### Top to bottom of the universe [56:00 – 58:00]

- Arjuna sees many forms of different kinds of living beings.
- Brahmā is at the top of the universe; there are various sages; Śeṣa-nāga is at the bottom.
- So the first thing Arjuna describes is the scope — from the top of the universe to the bottom: everything is seen.
- Arjuna thus confirms what Kṛṣṇa said in BG 11.7 — "at one place you will see the whole universe" — "Yes, I am seeing it."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's first words confirm Kṛṣṇa's promise: from Brahmā at the top to Śeṣa at the bottom, the whole universe is visible within the Lord's body. [56:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.16 [58:00 – 61:50]

<!-- vedabase:11.16 -->

**Context.** Arjuna sees a form with many arms and many other features, expanding everywhere — no beginning, middle or end — and addresses the Lord as _viśveśvara_ and _viśva-rūpa_ `58:00 – 60:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Viśveśvara_ — the Lord of the universe, its controller `60:00`.
- _Viśva-rūpa_ — the form that pervades the whole universe `60:00`.

#### Water everywhere [58:00]

- Like being in the middle of the ocean: wherever we look there is only water, water everywhere; we do not know where it began, where its end is, or where the middle is.
- Arjuna is overwhelmed in the same way.

#### Lord of the universe and form of the universe [60:00 – 61:50]

- The important point is the two words Arjuna uses: "My dear Lord, You who are the controller of the universe, You have a form which pervades the whole universe — that is the form You are revealing to me."
- Queen Kuntī, in her prayers, also describes Kṛṣṇa in three ways: He is inside the universe, He is the universe, and He is outside the universe.
- Here Arjuna speaks of two aspects: the Lord of the universe, and the Lord manifesting as the universe.
- How Arjuna's vision and emotions change as he sees more will be discussed in the next class `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Lost in a form without beginning, middle or end, Arjuna holds on to two truths: Kṛṣṇa is the Lord of the universe and also the form of the universe. [58:00 – 61:50]
</Callout>
