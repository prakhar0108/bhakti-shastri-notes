## Topic-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### The front matter of a book [00:00 – 06:00]

**Context.** Before discussing the Gītā itself, the teacher explains the kinds of material that precede a book's main text, and which of them Śrīla Prabhupāda's edition contains `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prologue_ — usually in a novel; tells part of the storyline beforehand to set the scene `02:00`.
- _Foreword_ — normally written by someone other than the author, whom both the author and the audience respect; it is a bridge that introduces the author and the book to the audience, just as a speaker is introduced by someone else before a class `02:00`.
- _Setting the Scene_ — in _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_, written by the publishers; it gives the historical background of the war between the two branches of the Kuru dynasty, written especially for Western readers to whom a book opening on a battlefield would otherwise seem odd `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Introduction_ — written by Prabhupāda with the book; focused directly on the content `04:00`.
- _Preface_ — written by Prabhupāda just before publication; much shorter, focused on the historical context and the audience `04:00`.

#### Why "As It Is" [04:00]

- Several people had commented on the Gītā in improper and incorrect ways.
- Prabhupāda wanted Kṛṣṇa's message presented as it was originally intended — hence _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Both the Preface and the Introduction exist to put the reader in the right mood before the first verse; the title "As It Is" itself announces that the Gītā will be presented without distortion. [04:00]
</Callout>

### The Preface: how the book was published [06:00 – 16:00]

**Context.** The Preface was not in the first published edition; it tells the "adventurous" background of how the book came out `06:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's situation in America [06:00 – 08:00]

- Before leaving India in 1965, Prabhupāda had published the First Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_ in three volumes; with these he felt equipped with "transcendental ammunition" against illusion.
- In India he was a respected, published author — copies had gone to government libraries, the Prime Minister and the President, who wrote positive statements.
- In America he was relatively unknown; his followers (from 1966 onwards) were sincere but mostly hippies without material contacts or influence.
- He still wrote his commentaries diligently, especially in the early morning hours, and wanted his books published.

#### The Macmillan episode [08:00 – 12:00]

- American publishers doubted who would take such a book and how it could be distributed.
- A letter from Macmillan raised the devotees' hopes, but it turned out only a clerk wanted a Gītā for himself.
- While Brahmānanda was there, a senior officer of Macmillan came out and asked about the Swami: Macmillan was publishing a collection of the world's scriptures and had nothing on Hinduism. Hearing that a full Gītā commentary existed, he took it up quickly.
- Condition: books in that series had a standard length (roughly 400–500 pages), so the purports had to be cut. Hayagrīva Prabhu later wrote of the "doleful task" of deciding which purports were unnecessary.
- The abridged edition carried forewords, including one by Allen Ginsberg, received positive reviews from scholars, and gave Prabhupāda respectability.
- With demand growing, Prabhupāda started his own publishing house (then ISKCON Press, later renamed the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust) and published the full Gītā with all purports — the edition that carries this Preface `12:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's stated purpose [12:00 – 16:00]

- Parents of his students appreciated how their children's lives had been transformed; the hippies had rebelled against material values and sought "something more," but drugs were taking them down, not up.
- Prabhupāda took no credit: he had only repeated the message of his spiritual master.
- People misinterpret the Gītā — a point he repeats throughout the purports.
- His only purpose: present the Gītā as it is, to guide the conditioned soul to the same purpose for which Kṛṣṇa descends once in a day of Brahmā (every 8.6 billion years) `14:00`.
- The teacher notes how Prabhupāda moves seamlessly from a historical context Western readers relate to, into the reality of the tradition he represents `14:00`.
- Central point: we are parts of Kṛṣṇa, meant to serve Him; without serving Him we are miserable, and by serving Him we become happy — "the Lord wants it and the Lord demands it" `14:00`.
- Closing line of the Preface: if even one person becomes a pure devotee of the Lord, the attempt is a success `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prabhupāda's goal in presenting the Gītā was not fame or scholarship but Kṛṣṇa's own purpose — that souls return to Him by becoming pure devotees; even one such devotee would make the effort successful. [14:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### The Introduction: the Gītā as a prescription [16:00 – 20:00]

**Context.** The Introduction opens with prayers — including a prayer to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, which the teacher notes is the only direct reference to her in the whole of _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_ `16:00`.

#### The prescription analogy [16:00 – 18:00]

- The Gītā is like a doctor's prescription: its potency works only when followed as the doctor meant it.
- Misreading examples the teacher gives:
  - "Take three times a day — morning, afternoon, evening" → "morning is different in India, Africa and America, so morning means evening."
  - "Don't take sugar" → "sugar is just a white substance, so coat it in another colour and it's allowed."
- Interpreting and misinterpreting the prescription this way means we will not get the benefit.

#### The mood of devotion [18:00]

- Prabhupāda presents the Gītā according to the prescription given in the Gītā itself, quoting the fourth chapter: it must be understood in a mood of devotion, otherwise its import remains lost.
- He introduces the five _rasas_ and the eternal relationship between the eternal soul and the eternal Lord.
- Arjuna himself accepts Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Lord.
- Unless one reads the Gītā in a submissive spirit — here the same as a devotional spirit — it is very difficult to understand, because it is a great mystery.
- Prabhupāda first offers devotional prayers himself, and only then explains the Gītā — modelling the mood he asks of the reader.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Gītā is a prescription: its benefit comes only by following it as the speaker intended, and the Gītā itself says it must be received in devotion — which is why Prabhupāda's Introduction begins with prayers. [16:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Purpose of the Gītā and the five truths [20:00 – 26:00]

**Context.** Having set the mood, Prabhupāda states the purpose: to deliver humanity from the nescience of material existence `20:00`.

#### The tigers of nescience [20:00]

- Everyone is in misery, yet few inquire about spirituality; we are "about to be devoured by the tigers of nescience."
- The teacher explains the metaphor: we have forgotten our spiritual identity, and the Gītā is meant to rescue us from that.
- The Lord is merciful to all living entities, especially human beings.

#### Analysis and synthesis [20:00 – 24:00]

- Prabhupāda draws on Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's commentary (_Gītā-bhūṣaṇa_), which describes the Gītā as dealing with five basic truths — an analytical framework.
- _Analysis_ — taking things apart; _synthesis_ — seeing how the parts connect.
- Without synthesis, points become like a news bulletin: a cricket score printed above "139 killed in earthquake," with no flow from one item to the next.
- A child learns language synthetically — speaking first; grammar (nouns, subjects, predicates) is analysis that comes later.
- Scripture presents knowledge synthetically, as a living culture; the _ācāryas_ analyse it to give us a conceptual foothold, after which we integrate it for a better understanding.

#### The five truths [24:00 – 26:00]

- _Īśvara_ — the Supreme Controller; Prabhupāda shows Kṛṣṇa is the _īśvara_.
- _Jīva_ — the living entity, a part of the _īśvara_: a particle of gold is also gold; a drop of ocean water is also salty.
- _Prakṛti_ — material nature, which also belongs to Kṛṣṇa; the modes are introduced here.
- _Kāla_ — time.
- _Karma_ — activity done under _prakṛti_ for some desire.
- _Jīva_ and _īśvara_ are conscious; _prakṛti_ and _kāla_ are eternal like them; only _karma_ is temporary.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The five truths are an analytical foothold given by the ācāryas; real understanding comes when we synthesise them — seeing how the soul, the Lord, nature, time and action fit together into one living picture. [20:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### The conditioned soul and its relationship with the Lord [26:00 – 30:00]

#### Not the same as the Lord [26:00]

- The living entity is conscious but cannot be supremely conscious; from the very beginning Prabhupāda distinguishes the _jīva_ from the _īśvara_, refuting _māyāvāda_.
- A devotee is transcendental to the material modes.

#### What "conditioned" means [26:00 – 28:00]

- As in "conditions apply" or the conditions of a treaty: the soul's happiness depends on many conditions outside its control.
- For a conditioned soul to enjoy:
  - the body must be healthy,
  - the sense objects must be available and of good enough quality,
  - the circumstances must be suitable,
  - and even then enjoyment is limited by the body's limited capacity.
- Liberation is not becoming formless; it is being reinstated in one's constitutional position as the Lord's servant `28:00`.
- Contaminated consciousness is "I am the lord, I am the enjoyer, I am the controller" `28:00`.

#### Philosophy in a nutshell [28:00 – 30:00]

- The Introduction gives practically the whole philosophy of Kṛṣṇa consciousness in condensed form.
- The stomach-and-hands example: the hands nourish themselves by feeding the stomach, not separately.
- The Lord's form is _sac-cid-ānanda_; He is not impersonal.
- There is complete facility for the small complete units (souls) to realise the complete — similar to the Īśopaniṣad Invocation; all incompleteness ("I don't have this, I don't have that") comes from incomplete knowledge of the complete `28:00 – 30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We are "conditioned" because our happiness rests on conditions we do not control; liberation is not losing identity but being restored to service — the one relationship in which our incompleteness ends. [26:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Sanātana-dharma and the goal of life [30:00 – 34:00]

#### Karma and rising above the animal platform [30:00]

- Like the Īśopaniṣad introduction, Prabhupāda explains the infallibility of Vedic knowledge and how humans should rise from the animal to the human platform — i.e. what kind of _karma_ to do in the world.

#### Dharma versus religion [30:00 – 32:00]

- _Sanātana-dharma_ is not a sectarian process; it is the eternal function of the eternal living entity in relationship with the eternal Supreme Lord.
- The Gītā does not teach "Hinduism": it was spoken thousands of years ago, while the word _Hindu_ entered Indian vocabulary less than a thousand years ago.
- _Dharma_ means what is intrinsic — unchangeable, eternal; _sanātana_ emphasises that further: no beginning, no end, no change.
- Our _sanātana-dharma_ is that we are all servants of the Lord.

#### Back to the spiritual world [32:00]

- Demigod worship does not lead to eternal results; only the worship of Kṛṣṇa does.
- This material world is a reflection of the spiritual world; the goal is to return there by surrendering to Kṛṣṇa.
- Surrender is practised by constantly remembering Kṛṣṇa while serving Him — the practical core of the Gītā.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Gītā teaches not a sectarian religion but sanātana-dharma — the soul's eternal nature as the Lord's servant — and its practical method is constant remembrance of Kṛṣṇa joined with service. [30:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### The art of constant remembrance [34:00 – 42:00]

#### A startling example [34:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda cites an example given by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu to Rūpa Gosvāmī on how to remain absorbed in the Lord while engaged in the world: like a married woman attached to a lover.
- The teacher raises the obvious question: Mahāprabhu was extremely strict about morality — once, running toward the sound of the _Gīta-govinda_ being sung, He stopped when His servant said it was a woman singing, and said He would have died had He gone further. Why would He use such an example?

#### Why the example works [36:00 – 38:00]

- The _ācāryas_ use examples that convey the point unforgettably; extramarital attraction is simply a fact of life, not a recommendation.
- Few things capture attention like male–female interactions, so the example is memorable.
- The point conveyed: such a woman does her household duties even better, so her husband does not suspect, while her mind stays on her lover.
- Likewise a devotee has two sets of duties — material and spiritual. Material duties must be done well at our level, but the material is foreign to our nature; devotion is our true nature.
- The essence is not morality or immorality but the _intensity_ of remembrance that strong attraction produces.
- Prabhupāda then writes: when the mind is engaged in Kṛṣṇa's service, the senses are automatically engaged; this is the art and secret of the Gītā — total absorption in the thought of Kṛṣṇa `38:00`.

#### Not two lanes of consciousness [38:00 – 40:00]

- We do not have to split our consciousness into one lane for work and another for Kṛṣṇa; _bhakti_ integrates everything.
- By serious _sādhana_ we become fixed in devotion; then, with the mind attached to Kṛṣṇa, everything becomes connected to Him: "not just my job — Kṛṣṇa's service; not just my family — Kṛṣṇa's family; not just my house — Kṛṣṇa's house."

#### The moon challenge [40:00 – 42:00]

- Prabhupāda observes that modern man struggled hard to reach the moon but has not tried hard to elevate himself spiritually.
- Reaching the moon is often called the crowning achievement of the 20th century, built on decades of effort by thousands of scientists; if so much effort is given to material elevation, why not to spiritual elevation?
- Absorbing the mind in Kṛṣṇa is not easy, but the process is available — and open to all: the Gītā itself says women, _vaiśyas_ and _śūdras_ can practise it; no material disqualification bars anyone `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The art of the Gītā is total absorption in Kṛṣṇa while doing one's duties well; it is achieved not by splitting consciousness but by sādhana that connects everything — job, family, home — to Kṛṣṇa. [36:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Glories of the Gītā (Gītā-māhātmya) [42:00 – 46:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes the _Gītā-māhātmya_ from the _Padma Purāṇa_ `42:00`:
  1. One who studies the Gītā is not affected by past sinful reactions.
  2. Future sinful reactions do not come.
  3. More important still, sinful desires themselves go away.
- When the Supreme Lord Himself has spoken the Gītā, why do we need anything else? The teacher's application: be focused on the fundamentals of this one book; the _Bhāgavatam_ and _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ then elaborate the same principles `44:00`.
- One who drinks the Gītā's nectar does not take birth again `44:00`.
- All the Upaniṣads are like a cow, and the Gītā is the milk — the essence of all Vedic wisdom `44:00`.
- Conclusion of the _māhātmya_: let there be one scripture (the Gītā), one work (service to Kṛṣṇa), one God (Kṛṣṇa) and one mantra — which Prabhupāda identifies as the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Gītā is presented as self-sufficient — the milk of all Vedic wisdom — so the student's task is to grasp its fundamentals deeply rather than scatter across many books. [44:00]
</Callout>

### The Gītā in the Gauḍīya tradition [46:00 – 52:00]

#### Summary of the Introduction [44:00 – 46:00]

- Sets a devotional mood → states the purpose (rescue from material existence) → the five truths and how they interact → _karma_ aligned with _sanātana-dharma_ (contrasted with non-eternal _dharmas_) → glories of the Gītā from other scriptures.
- Prabhupāda's edition has become by far the most widely distributed Gītā in English and an important work in modern Gītā scholarship, representing a vast tradition `46:00`.

#### The Gītā's role in the sampradāya [46:00 – 48:00]

- The Gītā is a broad Vedic book: it establishes Kṛṣṇa's supremacy, also discusses other paths, and finally establishes the supremacy of _bhakti_.
- In the tradition it is quoted in the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ and especially by Jīva Gosvāmī in the _Sandarbhas_.
- Earlier commentaries outside the Gauḍīya line include those of Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja and Madhva, among many others `48:00`.

#### Gauḍīya commentaries [46:00 – 52:00]

- **Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura** — the first Gauḍīya commentary on the Gītā, giving its essential meaning; rich in _rasa_ `46:00`, `50:00`.
- **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa** — _Gītā-bhūṣaṇa_, focused on _tattva_ and philosophy `48:00 – 50:00`.
  - A disciple generally does not comment on a book already commented on by his guru unless a specific purpose requires it.
  - Baladeva's purpose: for a _sampradāya_ to be accepted as authorised in Vedic circles, it needs commentaries on _śruti_ (the Upaniṣads), _smṛti_ (the Gītā) and _nyāya_ (the _Vedānta-sūtra_). He wrote on all three, establishing the Gauḍīya line's credibility.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — two works based on the earlier commentaries `50:00`:
  - _Vidvad-rañjana_ (based on Baladeva) — the delight of the wise.
  - _Rasika-rañjana_ (based on Viśvanātha) — the delight of relishers of _rasa_.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda** — _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_ draws primarily on Baladeva's commentary, integrates other sources and his own insights, and gives specific instructions for ISKCON devotees, presenting everything for the modern context `50:00`.
- Prabhupāda establishes the credibility of his presentation by giving the disciplic succession at the very beginning; it systematically and repeatedly shows _bhakti_ as the essence of the Gītā `50:00 – 52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prabhupāda's Gītā stands in a line of Gauḍīya commentaries — Viśvanātha's rasa, Baladeva's tattva, Bhaktivinoda's two digests — and presents that line for today, consistently showing bhakti as the Gītā's essence. [46:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>
