## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 15.7 [00:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.7 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says that all living beings in this conditioned world are His eternal parts, and that they are struggling hard with the six senses, which include the mind `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mamaivāṁśaḥ_ — "My part"; the stress on "My" is important `00:00`, `04:00`.
- _Sanātana_ — eternal: we are eternally parts `02:00`.
- _Jīva-loke_ — the world of conditioned life; the word that connects the verse's two parts `00:00`, `04:00`.
- _Karṣati_ — struggling and suffering; struggling or grappling hard `02:00`, `14:00`.
- _Indriyāṇi_ — the senses, here six including the mind `02:00`, `06:00`.
- _Prakṛti-sthāni_ — situated in material nature `10:00`.

#### Two parts, one truth [02:00]

- The verse has two parts; both are important, and so is their combination:
  - **First part** — a philosophical truth: the living entity is eternally a part of Kṛṣṇa.
  - **Second part** — the living entity is struggling and suffering in material existence because of the senses, including the mind.

#### Eternally a part: refuting māyāvāda [02:00 – 04:00]

- Impersonalists hold that we will eventually become one with God and merge into His existence; but the verse says clearly that we are parts — so how can the part ever become the whole? `02:00`
- Earlier verses already said the soul cannot be cut or changed by the elements; if the soul cannot be cut, then if it is a part it has always been a part and will always be a part. That could be inferred before, but the word _sanātana_ makes it emphatic and unambiguous `02:00`.
- **Whose part?** Not just parts of _brahman_ — not just sparks of spirit — but parts of the Supreme spiritual Person `04:00`.
  - The flame-and-sparks example illustrates the qualitative oneness of all living beings, but no more: fire, at least as we perceive it, has no personality, and neither do sparks.
  - The absolute reality is a person; Kṛṣṇa has said that there is no truth higher than Him `04:00`.
  - So "My" (_mama_) is important: "they are all My parts" — not temporary emanations from _brahman_ — and eternally so. This is a very strong refutation of _māyāvāda_ `04:00`.

#### Suffering aggravated by the mind and senses [06:00 – 10:00]

- That the material world is a place of misery Kṛṣṇa has said earlier (the teacher cites verses from the eighth, ninth and thirteenth chapters); this verse places slightly more emphasis on how _we_ are miserable here `06:00`.
- The addition here is that the struggle happens _because of_ the mind and senses `06:00`.
- **The mind as a sense:** the senses are often counted as five, but in the Gītā the mind is generally grouped with them — e.g. among the elements of the field in the thirteenth chapter the eleven senses include the mind, and in the tenth chapter Kṛṣṇa says, "Among the senses I am the mind" `06:00`.
- **Why it matters** `08:00`:
  - Most people think of the senses as sources of happiness — "with my eyes I'll see beautiful things, with my tongue I'll eat delicious things."
  - Kṛṣṇa says the opposite: the soul, part of Kṛṣṇa, has His blissful nature — so why are we suffering? Because of the senses.
  - The material world is itself a place of suffering, and that suffering is aggravated, multiplied, worsened by entanglement with the mind and senses.
  - Old age, disease and death are largely inevitable; but uncontrolled mind and senses create great attachment to worldly things, and through that one suffers far more than one needs to.
- **The alcoholic:** all the suffering that comes upon an alcoholic — to health, career, family and society — is self-induced. When we make wrong choices, we sentence ourselves to suffering; why would anyone do that? That is the insidious effect of the mind and senses `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### We are the aliens [10:00 – 12:00]

- The mind and senses alienate us from Kṛṣṇa — "alienate" means to make aliens `10:00`.
- People are fascinated by aliens — life forms from other planets that might come and explore the earth. The _Bhāgavata_ philosophy turns that telescope around and points it at us: it is we who are the aliens — not just on earth but in the whole of material existence `10:00`.
- To be situated in perishable, miserable material nature is not the soul's nature; it is an unnatural situation (_prakṛti-sthāni_). Being aliens in foreign territory, we suffer `10:00 – 12:00`.
- The way to break free is by returning to our original, spiritual nature `12:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's purport: expansions and identities [12:00 – 14:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that even the Supreme Absolute Truth is not a homogeneous oneness: there is diversity within it — the Lord's personal expansions, His many incarnations, which are His own expansions and keep their identities eternally `12:00`.
- The living entities are His separated expansions: they do not have the same potency as His personal expansions, but they are also parts of the Lord and also keep their identities eternally — just as the incarnations have eternal abodes in the spiritual world `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Chains subtler than iron [14:00]

- The conditioned soul is bound up as though shackled by iron chains. "I don't see any iron chains" — these chains are subtler than iron: our own desires shackle us `14:00`.
- Prabhupāda's purport: the soul is bound by false ego, and the mind is the chief agent driving him in material existence `14:00`.
- Correlated with the modes `14:00`:
  - mind in goodness — activities are good;
  - mind in passion — activities are troublesome;
  - mind in ignorance — he travels in the lower species of life.

#### The virtual-reality analogy [14:00 – 20:00]

- **The picture** `14:00 – 16:00`:
  - I am the spectator — the soul.
  - The screen in front of me — the mind.
  - A tunnel- or pipe-like structure from my head to the screen locks my vision so I cannot look anywhere else — the false ego, which riveted my consciousness to bodily reality.
- This is based on the allegory of Purañjana in the Fourth Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_. An allegory is more elaborate than an analogy: philosophical points are illustrated through characters and the dynamics between them `16:00`.
- One of Prabhupāda's scientist disciples published a paper on it, "The City of Nine Gates," also printed in _Back to Godhead_; to explain the allegory he uses the virtual-reality concept, describing the mind as a multimedia computer that integrates inputs from all the senses and displays them for the soul to behold and respond to `16:00`.
- **False ego binds, the mind drives** `18:00`:
  - If nothing were playing on the screen, we would soon get bored and look for a way to get free, even with our head locked to it.
  - But if a captivating movie is playing, we become more and more riveted.
  - The mind drives by presenting objects for enjoyment — "this looks good, this tastes good, this is soft to touch; let me enjoy."
- **Channels and the modes** `18:00 – 20:00`: a TV has many channels — some educational, some filled with sexuality and passion, some perverse with brutal violence:
  - mind in goodness — activities reasonably good, though still in material existence;
  - mind in passion — the screen is filled with sexuality and passion; activities become troublesome and our bondage and complications increase;
  - mind in ignorance — one goes down to lower species, as in 14.18 `20:00`.
- Even in goodness one must undergo old age, disease and death; but there are degrees of suffering depending on the mode one is in `20:00`.

#### Perception after liberation [22:00 – 24:00]

- The teacher discusses a passage, in connection with the purport, describing the liberated soul's experience: the liberated soul sees with eyes and hears with ears `22:00`. It conveys two points:
  - **Even the liberated soul has perceptions** — sensory perceptions.
  - **There is something to perceive** — if _brahman_ were undifferentiated oneness, there would be nothing to perceive. There is a great deal to perceive because the highest reality is a person, and the liberated living entities are also persons endowed with senses `22:00`.
- In the spiritual world the living entity takes _darśana_ of the Lord and relishes Kṛṣṇa's sweet speech; in the _Gopī-gīta_ the _gopīs_ say His voice is so sweet and His words so charming that even the wise become bewildered — captivated `22:00 – 24:00`.
- That is the real life of the spiritual world: reciprocation between the living entity and the Supreme Lord `24:00`.
- Prabhupāda further explains that everyone in the spiritual world has a form like the Lord's four-armed form, though Viṣṇu alone has certain distinctive characteristics; there are other forms as well `24:00`.
- Parts cannot be cut; and as a particle of gold is also gold, we are godly as parts of God, but cannot be God in His entirety `24:00`.

#### Be a part, not apart [24:00 – 28:00]

- The two parts of the verse are integrated by the phrase: **"Be a part, be not apart."** Let us live as parts of Kṛṣṇa, cooperate with Him, and by living in harmony with the whole we will be happy `24:00 – 26:00`.
- **Limited souls, unlimited happiness:** as souls we are limited; if we seek happiness independently of Kṛṣṇa, our happiness will be limited. But Kṛṣṇa is all-attractive and infinite, so connection with Him can give happiness forever — we need not be limited by our finiteness in our capacity to experience happiness `26:00`.
- Being a part does not mean becoming a slave; it means serving Kṛṣṇa according to our individuality — even in the spiritual world — which pleases Him; He reciprocates, and there is supreme joy `26:00`.
- Being a part is our nature; our present situation is unnatural: instead of "a part" we have become "apart" — one word — and then we suffer (_karṣati_), because nothing else can satisfy us. "Supreme" by definition means one; there cannot be any substitute for the Supreme `26:00`.
- The mind and senses offer objects that promise to be substitutes — "why don't you enjoy this, and this?" — but we cannot actually enjoy them. So suffering here is inevitable, created by our own _karma_, and entangled with the mind and senses we do further bad _karma_ and suffer more `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We are eternally Kṛṣṇa's parts, not sparks to be merged; separated from Him — "apart" instead of "a part" — we become aliens in matter, and the mind and senses, which we take as sources of happiness, actually multiply our suffering. [02:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.8 [28:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.8 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains how the soul, here called _īśvara_, carries its conceptions of life from one body to another, as the air carries aromas `28:00 – 30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Īśvara_ — controller; used here to show that the soul can choose `28:00`, `40:00`.
- _Vāyu_ / the wind carrying aromas — the soul acquiring and carrying conceptions of life `30:00`.

#### The wind and the aroma [30:00]

- If the wind blows through a fragrant flower garden, it carries that fragrance; if it blows past an outhouse with a repellent odour of excreta, it acquires that odour `30:00`.
- Similarly, the soul acquires different conceptions of what life is and how it is meant to be enjoyed `30:00`.

#### Perverse conceptions of enjoyment [30:00 – 34:00]

- Some people are sadists — they get pleasure by causing others pain, such as pouring scalding water on ants `30:00`.
- Mṛgāri, the hunter, would half-kill animals — wounding them so the wound was fatal but only gradually `30:00`.
- Such deliberate slow wounding is also done in wars and by terrorists and brutal people who want their victims to die slowly `30:00 – 32:00`.
- After a failed conspiracy against Hitler, those involved were hanged with piano strings, which cut slowly through the neck — a very brutal way of killing in which the person could feel the pain for days `32:00`.
- How can anyone get pleasure from others' pain? That is the perversity of the mode of ignorance `32:00`:
  - **Goodness** — I want to be happy, and I want others to be happy too.
  - **Transcendence** — I may even give up my happiness for others' happiness; that is actually love.
  - **Passion** — I want to be happy; what happens to others doesn't matter.
  - **Ignorance** — my happiness should be exclusive: I should be happy and others miserable.
- Even Hitler's soul is pure originally; a person acquires terrible misconceptions of life based on circumstances `34:00`.

#### Is the soul then not responsible? [34:00 – 36:00]

- The wind isn't responsible for the odour it picks up; is it the same with us — we were exposed to certain experiences, so we became conditioned? Not necessarily: the soul has free will `34:00`.
- **"We may not be able to choose our experiences, but we can choose our experiences."** The word "experience" means two things `34:00`:
  - the external situation we go through — e.g. if a crowded local train is the only way to travel, we have to go through it;
  - what we emotionally go through — if I keep focusing resentfully on the pushing crowd, that becomes my emotional experience and it becomes torturous. But I can choose to think of something else.
- Spiritual consciousness enables us to think of a higher reality by which we transcend worldly situations; physical experiences will affect and shape us, but they don't have to define or determine us `36:00`.
- **Childhood abuse:** some who were abused in childhood become abusers; others choose instead to work for the rehabilitation of those who were abused. The experience is the same; the responses differ — which shows we have free will `36:00`.

#### Shaped, not determined [36:00 – 40:00]

- The verse's emphasis, however, is that the soul is generally affected by its circumstances: repeated exposure habituates us, and habituation makes us accept that kind of action as normal and act accordingly `36:00`.
- **Baseball:** an Indian who knows nothing of baseball goes to college in America, where everyone is mad about it; exposed to it, he may become infatuated too `36:00 – 38:00`.
- We acquire conceptions of how we are going to enjoy; we are shaped by our experiences but not determined by them — this is not fatalistic or deterministic. Determinism, a philosophical school, says external factors determine us; the Gītā's understanding is not deterministic, as the chapter will show `38:00`.
- **Practical point:** we should therefore choose our circumstances as much as possible — rather than hanging out with people with undesirable traits, or in circumstances that drag out our lower self, choose association and a social environment that brings out our higher side `38:00`.
- The verse expands on _karṣati_: the struggle is the cycle of birth and death undergone in the hope of material enjoyment. Prabhupāda's purport says ignorance not only covers us but traps us, and the senses are pathways to destruction: going along them, we go faster and faster towards misery, repeated birth and death, and hellish situations `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### Īśvara: we direct our transmigration [40:00 – 42:00]

- Why do I have one kind of body and you another? Because in past lives we chose to develop particular conceptions, and accordingly got a particular mindset and a particular physical body `40:00`.
- The word _īśvara_ indicates that the soul can choose. Transmigration we will have to go through, but where we go — that choice we have. What kind of body the soul takes depends on the conceptions it acquires, and we can decide how we respond to our experiences `40:00`.
- Prabhupāda: the minute independence is there; the change the body undergoes depends on him. If you want a dog's body, you'll get a dog's body `40:00 – 42:00`.
- _Karṣati_ covers both going from one body to another — death, the post-mortem journey and birth — and the various troubles while in the body `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Like the wind carrying aromas, the soul carries the conceptions of enjoyment it acquires into its next body; we are shaped by our experiences but not determined by them, so we should choose our association and our responses — that is how the soul, as īśvara, directs its own transmigration. [30:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.9 [42:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.9 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the five senses — ears, eyes, skin, tongue and nose — centred around the mind, through which the soul, having taken a body, enjoys sense objects `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Adhiṣṭhāya_ — centred around, situated around, supervised by `42:00`.
- _Viṣayān_ — sense objects; the soul tries to enjoy them `42:00`, `54:00`.

#### The carpenter's toolkit [42:00 – 44:00]

- According to the conception of life we have acquired, we are given the next body `42:00`.
- A carpenter has a toolkit with all the tools needed for his job. The soul is like a carpenter out to do the job of sense gratification; the senses — the whole body — are the toolkit `42:00 – 44:00`.
- But this toolkit itself causes misery, and the job causes further misery `44:00`:
  - sense gratification gives a little pleasure, followed by suffering;
  - the toolkit — the body — grows old, gets diseased and dies.
- We think we are enjoying, but we are serving — enslaved by the spell of the senses `44:00`.

#### Dragged by many wives [44:00]

- The teacher cites prayers in the ninth chapter of the Seventh Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_ that compare the soul to a man with many wives, each dragging him — "come with me, do this for me, get this for me" `44:00`.
- Sometimes all the senses are attracted to one sense object, but quite often to different objects, and it becomes very difficult for the soul to survive in such a situation `44:00`.
- Just as five fingers are centred around the palm, the five senses are centred around the mind `44:00`.

#### The mind as a multimedia computer [44:00 – 48:00]

- Returning to the virtual-reality example: the mind is like a multimedia computer whose screen shows output, while inputs come from many sensors `44:00`.
- Right now, as the teacher speaks, there are many inputs at once — the sound of his own voice, the feel of the chair he sits on, and many more `46:00`.
- Not every input reaches the monitor: what is displayed is like the conscious mind; what goes into the hard disk without being displayed is the subconscious mind `46:00`.
- Sigmund Freud pioneered the systematic study of the subconscious, though the idea existed before; the teacher describes him as more a creative thinker in the garb of a scientist, with many speculative theories. Carl Jung, his protégé turned rival, studied patterns of behaviour that seem common to all people at the subconscious level `46:00`.
- The mind brings in all the inputs, integrates them, and presents them for display and action. Which input to focus on and how to respond is decided by the soul `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### The integration problem [48:00 – 54:00]

- Neurology faces a problem it considers intractable: the "integration problem." Different areas of the brain correlate with different functions `48:00`.
- **The parrot:** if a parrot flies in front of me, four inputs come in — colour (green with a red beak), shape (bird-like), motion, and sound (chirping). These go to four different parts of the brain `48:00 – 50:00`.
- Yet I don't perceive them as discrete inputs; I get one synchronous composite picture `50:00`.
- **Out-of-sync video:** in a poorly recorded home video, the sound of a punch comes before the fist lands — and it becomes a joke. Asynchrony shows that inputs come from different sources and that integration is vital to proper perception `50:00`.
- From the point of view of neuroscience this is intractable not just in practice but in principle: there is no single area of the brain where everything comes together; removing one part, though it may cause serious or incapacitating damage, does not make everything else dysfunctional `52:00`.
- So the integration does not happen in the physical brain; it happens in the mind. Signals from the brain are routed to the subtle body, which integrates them and presents them to the soul `52:00`.
- **The cricket broadcast:** a match's inputs — motion and sound — come from various cameras and sensors, even sound sensors in the stumps to detect whether the ball nicked the bat. They are integrated not on the field but elsewhere, and displayed on the TV screen. Likewise, the physical body and senses are in the physical world; inputs go to the physical brain, from there to the mind, where everything is integrated and presented to the soul for display and response `52:00 – 54:00`.
- Then, with the senses centred on the mind, the soul tries to enjoy sense objects accordingly `54:00`.
- Prabhupāda writes that consciousness is originally pure like water, but water mixed with a certain colour changes; similarly the soul is pure but changes according to the circumstances it is exposed to `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The body is a toolkit of senses given for the job of sense gratification, all centred on the mind, which integrates their inputs and presents them to the soul; thinking we are enjoying, we are actually being dragged in many directions — while the choice of what to focus on and how to respond remains ours. [42:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.10 [54:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.10 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa introduces an important concept, to be continued in the next class: the foolish cannot see how the soul quits one body and enjoys another under the spell of the modes, but one with eyes of knowledge can see it `54:00 – 56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vimūḍhāḥ_ — the foolish, who cannot see `56:00`.
- _Jñāna-cakṣuṣaḥ_ — one who has the eyes of knowledge `56:00`, `58:00`.

#### Who sees and who doesn't [56:00]

- The teacher links this to 13.35: one should use the eyes of knowledge to see the difference between the soul and the body `56:00`.
- With the eyes of knowledge one sees that the soul is entangled in material existence and suffering; without them one thinks "I am enjoying" `56:00`.
- **The drunkard:** he thinks "I am drinking," but a seer sees that he is shackled by an addiction that makes him run from post to pillar to fulfil its surges. So the soul, under the spell of the modes, moves here and there, while the foolish person thinks "I am enjoying, I am doing this, I am doing that" `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Suffering happiness [58:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that the living entity is suffering different kinds of happiness and distress under the illusion of sense enjoyment `58:00`.
- How can one "suffer happiness"? Happiness is part of the package: overall the package is suffering, and within it a little happiness comes along — and that happiness traps us. A drinker gets a little happiness, but the overall product is misery `58:00`.
- We can become free of this illusion by taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa. How one acquires those eyes of knowledge, and the difference between those who can see and those who can't, will be discussed in the next class `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The foolish take the soul's entanglement for enjoyment, as a drunkard thinks he is enjoying his drink; eyes of knowledge reveal that even the happiness in material life is part of a package of suffering — an illusion we escape by taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa. [56:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
