## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 15.15 [00:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.15 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says He is situated in everyone's heart, that from Him come remembrance, knowledge and forgetfulness, and that by all the _Vedas_ He is to be known — He is the compiler of _Vedānta_ and the knower of the _Vedas_ `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Caitya-guru_ — the indwelling Supersoul, the guru from within; the same Kṛṣṇa also appears outside as _śikṣā_- and _dīkṣā-guru_, through guru, _sādhu_ and _śāstra_ `04:00`.
- _Jñāna_ — the knowledge Kṛṣṇa gives; it is meant not for "pooling" (accumulating in the head) but for "pulling" us toward Kṛṣṇa `04:00 – 06:00`.
- _Responsible / responsive_ — "we are responsible, Kṛṣṇa is responsive": we are able to choose our response, and He responds to our desires `08:00`.
- _Compiler and knower_ — Kṛṣṇa as compiler of _Vedānta_ and knower of the _Vedas_ `02:00`, `26:00`.

#### Two units in one verse [02:00 – 04:00]

- First unit: Kṛṣṇa's indwelling presence and how He acts through it.
- Second unit: His connection with the _Vedas_ — the goal, compiler and knower of them.
- Earlier verses about the Lord in the heart focused on Him as overseer and maintainer; here the focus is on how He acts internally.
- Unlike us, who may forget what we ourselves said a year ago, Kṛṣṇa never forgets what He has spoken.

#### Knowledge, then choice [04:00 – 08:00]

- Kṛṣṇa gives knowledge — for example, "I am not the body, I am the soul, meant to love and serve Kṛṣṇa, and I will be far happier in that than in trying to enjoy separately from Him."
- Whatever _dharma_ one performs, its purpose is to increase attraction to Kṛṣṇa; if attraction is not increasing, it is a waste of time `06:00`.
- After receiving knowledge we choose: if we desire to turn toward Kṛṣṇa, one trajectory follows; if not, another.
- When sense objects come before us, we often forget everything we heard from guru, _sādhu_ and _śāstra_ — as Ajāmila forgot his _dharma_ as a son, as a _brāhmaṇa_ and as a member of a respected society, and capitulated to his attraction `06:00`.

#### Can we blame Kṛṣṇa for forgetfulness? [08:00 – 12:00]

- A student who blacks out in an exam cannot blame Kṛṣṇa because "Kṛṣṇa gives forgetfulness."
- The teacher points back to 2.62–2.63: forgetfulness comes at the end of a sequence that we set in motion:
  1. contemplation of sense objects,
  2. attachment,
  3. lust (_kāma_),
  4. anger (_krodha_),
  5. delusion,
  6. bewilderment of memory.
- When we keep entertaining a desire, we are effectively telling Kṛṣṇa, "I have heard Your teachings, but I want to enjoy"; He then lets us act in forgetfulness of reality.
- **"Entertain" = enter + retain:** we let the desire enter and do not dismiss it; it gains a foothold, then a stronghold, then a full hold, and our consciousness is abducted `10:00`.

#### Remembrance for those who choose Kṛṣṇa [12:00 – 18:00]

- If we hold on to the desire to love and serve Him, Kṛṣṇa gives stronger remembrance of the message of guru, _sādhu_ and _śāstra_, and in time forgetfulness of material enjoyment — we no longer feel we are missing anything.
- Prahlāda Mahārāja's prayer: he has no anxiety about crossing material existence because he is absorbed in the nectar of glorifying the Lord; he laments only for those who carry a great burden for illusory happiness `12:00 – 14:00`.
- The teacher explains the word for these fools as "empowered fools" — people with a lot of power for foolish activities; our desires empower us, positively or destructively `14:00`.
- **Sense enjoyment as a sandwich:** two thick loaves of bitter bread — hankering before ("when will I get it?") and lamentation after ("why did it end so quickly?") — around a thin delicious filling `14:00 – 16:00`.
- For a serious devotee Kṛṣṇa inverts the normal dynamics: instead of forgetting Kṛṣṇa and remembering _māyā_ when sense objects appear, the devotee forgets sense enjoyment and remembers Kṛṣṇa's sweetness, glory and beauty, so the struggle for sense control becomes much less `16:00`.
- We should not be "desirers of desires" but desirers of Kṛṣṇa `16:00`.

#### Even forgetfulness serves our return [18:00 – 22:00]

- When Kṛṣṇa gives forgetfulness, it is not perennial: the infatuation with material enjoyment fizzles out in anticlimax, and the soul comes back to Kṛṣṇa, who then guides it `18:00`.
- When remembering is relatively easy — in devotee association, studying scripture, chanting — we should remember Kṛṣṇa earnestly; then, when temptation comes, He will give remembrance, and if we respond we will be protected `18:00`.
- **End the forgetfulness of our forgetfulness:** sometimes we are so deep in illusion that we do not even notice we have forgotten (for example, spacing out while chanting); as soon as we notice, we bring ourselves back on track `18:00 – 20:00`.
- **The aeroplane off course:** a plane flying from Mumbai to Kolkata is off track about 90% of the time due to wind and pressure, yet reaches its destination because the pilot keeps bringing it back `20:00`.
- Experience confirms this: cherished enjoyments, such as a taste for meat, become insignificant after one gets the higher taste of _prasāda_ — Kṛṣṇa has given forgetfulness of them; persevering in devotional service, the same will happen with the remaining ones `20:00 – 22:00`.

#### By all the Vedas I am to be known [22:00 – 26:00]

- Kṛṣṇa gives knowledge internally, and He gives it externally through the _Vedas_.
- Three senses of the word _Veda_:
  - **Restrictive** — the four _Vedas_, which primarily discuss _karma-kāṇḍa_.
  - **Generic** — knowledge itself, whose ultimate goal is to know Kṛṣṇa.
  - **Moderate** — the body of literature derived from the _Vedas_, including the _Purāṇas_ and _Itihāsas_ (discussed in the Īśopaniṣad introduction), which progresses from _karma-kāṇḍa_ to _jñāna-kāṇḍa_ to _bhakti_.
- When studying a book full of information, we must know its purpose; otherwise we get lost in details `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### Goal, compiler and knower [26:00 – 30:00]

- Kṛṣṇa relates Himself to the _Vedas_ in three ways: their goal, their compiler (as Vyāsadeva, the literary incarnation of the Lord) and their knower.
- To know a big book's purpose, ask the author; Kṛṣṇa is the author, so He knows the goal.
- The teacher contrasts this with deconstructionism, where later reviewers produce interpretations contradicting the author's intention; such deconstruction is of little use, and Kṛṣṇa, unlike human authors, does not forget why He spoke `26:00 – 28:00`.
- **Link between the two units:** the first part shows Kṛṣṇa acting internally (knowledge, remembrance, forgetfulness as Supersoul); the second shows Him acting externally; together they show Kṛṣṇa tirelessly striving to elevate us `28:00`.
- His ultimate mercy is not maintaining this world of old age, disease and death, but taking us beyond it to the spiritual world, for which He provides abundant arrangements `28:00 – 30:00`.

#### Close of the jñāna-cakṣu section and the purport [30:00 – 34:00]

- This concludes the section that began at 15.10: even a person not perceptive enough to see the soul can see Kṛṣṇa's arrangements at the material level and gradually His spiritual arrangements `30:00`.
- Arjuna's implicit question — "if You compiled and know the _Vedas_, what is their essence?" — leads to the "_tri-gītā_," the essence of _Vedānta_ in three verses (15.16–15.18) `30:00`.
- From the purport `30:00 – 32:00`:
  - The Lord is all-pervading as Brahman and localised in every heart as Paramātmā.
  - He is the supplier and digester of foodstuff, the witness, the giver of knowledge as the _Vedas_, and the teacher of the Gītā — "God is all good, God is all merciful."
  - When the soul enters a new body (for example, from a human to a dog's body), it forgets, but the Lord remembers and guides it how to act; a newborn knows to suckle — science calls it instinct, but where instinct comes from remains a mystery (to be revisited at 18.61 with bird migration).
- The _Vedānta-sūtra_ (1.1.4) is quoted on _samanvaya_ — reconciliation or harmonisation: the first four _sūtras_ are inquire about Brahman, Brahman is the source of everything, it is known through _śāstra_, and _śāstra_'s many teachings are harmonised hierarchically with knowledge of Kṛṣṇa at the top `32:00 – 34:00`.
- Prabhupāda's last sentence of the purport: the purpose, understanding and goal of the _Vedas_ is ultimately Kṛṣṇa `34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We are responsible, Kṛṣṇa is responsive: He gives knowledge from within and through the Vedas, then grants remembrance or forgetfulness according to the desire we choose to hold — so earnestly remembering Him when it is easy, and ending our "forgetfulness of our forgetfulness," invites Him to invert the pull of māyā. [08:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.16 [34:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.16 -->

**Context.** The first verse of the three-verse summary of _Vedānta_: there are two kinds of beings (_puruṣas_), the fallible and the infallible `34:00 – 36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kṣara_ — fallible; the conditioned souls, situated everywhere in the material world `36:00`.
- _Akṣara_ — infallible; the liberated souls, transcendentally situated and not dragged down by matter `36:00`.
- _Kūṭa-stha_ — situated high up, in oneness; the teacher explains the oneness as oneness in quality, not total identity `36:00`.

#### What "oneness" means here [36:00]

- As discussed in the Īśopaniṣad, the oneness of liberated souls is qualitative.
- To understand such a word, one must look at the full flow and context, not only at a single word.
- Prabhupāda's purport: oneness does not mean the souls have no individuality but that there is no disunity — they all agree with the purpose of creation, which is to love and serve Kṛṣṇa.
- **Oneness is the resolution of disunity, not the dissolution of individuality;** resolving disunity brings harmony.
- The spiritual body is not made of matter, so the six changes of material existence do not apply — hence the liberated are infallible.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa first divides all souls into two — conditioned and liberated — and the liberated souls' "oneness" means harmony in serving Him, not loss of individuality. [36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.17 [38:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.17 -->

**Context.** Besides these two, there is the Supreme Person, the Supreme Soul (Paramātmā), who enters the three worlds and maintains them `38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Uttama-puruṣa_ — the Supreme Person, beyond even the liberated souls `38:00`.
- _Anya_ — "someone else"; the teacher stresses its significance: the Supreme is different even from the liberated `38:00`, `42:00`.
- _Paramātmā_ — the Supreme Soul, a word Kṛṣṇa has used earlier, for example at 13.23 `38:00`.

#### Building up the message of Vedānta [38:00 – 40:00]

- The _Vedānta-sūtra_ itself speaks primarily in terms of Brahman, so here Kṛṣṇa also speaks in the third person.
- At 15.13 Kṛṣṇa said, "I enter the planets and maintain them"; now He says the Paramātmā enters and maintains the worlds — putting two and two together, Kṛṣṇa is the Paramātmā `40:00`.
- He does not state the conclusion at the beginning; He develops the thought — third person here, first person in the next verse `40:00`.

#### Three things the tri-gītā establishes [40:00 – 42:00]

1. The Supreme Person is different from both the conditioned and — especially — the liberated souls.
2. It is not the liberated souls who are all-pervading; it is the Supreme Soul who enters and maintains creation. This answers the idea that merging into Brahman makes one everything.
3. That Supreme Person is Kṛṣṇa Himself (stated in the next verse).

- Prabhupāda quotes a favourite verse from the _Upaniṣads_: among the many living beings, one is special and supreme `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Supreme Person is "someone else" — beyond even the liberated souls — and He alone pervades and maintains the worlds; liberation does not make the soul the all-pervading Absolute. [38:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.18 [42:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.18 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now speaks in the first person: because He is beyond the fallible and even beyond the infallible, He is celebrated in the world and in the _Vedas_ as the Supreme Person `42:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Puruṣottama_ — the Supreme Person; the chapter is therefore called _Puruṣottama-yoga_, the yoga of the Supreme Person `44:00`.
- _Aham_ — "I"; its use makes clear that the verse refers to the Bhagavān aspect, the Lord who has His own abode and associates and performs pastimes `48:00`.

#### Beyond liberation one must still seek the Person [44:00]

- In the banyan-tree section Kṛṣṇa said that after disentangling from material existence one must seek the Supreme Person.
- Merely attaining the spiritual platform is not enough; there too one must connect with the Supreme Lord — beyond Brahman one must rise to Bhagavān.

#### The strongest refutation of Māyāvāda [44:00 – 48:00]

- The teacher calls these three verses the strongest, most devastating rebuttal of _Māyāvāda_; there is very little scope for interpretation, only for verbal "prestidigitation" — intellectual trickery `44:00 – 46:00`.
- Prabhupāda would say, "Let Kṛṣṇa speak for Himself" — which is why he called his explanation _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_ `46:00`.
- The sequence is plain:
  1. There are two kinds of persons — conditioned and liberated.
  2. There is a Supreme Person beyond both, who maintains all the worlds.
  3. "I am that Supreme Person."
- Therefore when we are liberated we do not merge into Kṛṣṇa or become a homogeneous oneness; Kṛṣṇa remains the Supreme reality `46:00`.
- Prabhupāda correlates the word _puruṣa_ with other scriptural references in the purport `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Viśvanātha Cakravartī's reading: Brahman, Paramātmā, Bhagavān [48:00 – 52:00]

- 15.18 → **Bhagavān**: transcendental to everything and everyone, indicated by _aham_.
- 15.17 → **Paramātmā**: the one who enters all the worlds and maintains them — the Lord's Viṣṇu expansions enter the creation, each universe, the hearts of all living beings and even every atom `50:00`.
- 15.16 → **Brahman**: the word _akṣara_ can refer to Brahman (as in Arjuna's question and Kṛṣṇa's answer at the start of chapter 8); Brahman is also _kūṭa-stha_, unchanging and situated in transcendence. Prabhupāda's translation takes _akṣara_ there as the living entity; Viśvanātha offers another way of looking at it `50:00 – 52:00`.
- The teacher does not go into the full technical analysis; the point is that a progression through the three aspects leads to the ultimate conclusion — understand Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Person.

#### Summary of the three verses [52:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is not the same as the liberated _puruṣa_; He is beyond even the liberated.
- Kṛṣṇa is the _puruṣottama_ and the Paramātmā.
- Therefore Kṛṣṇa is the highest manifestation of the Absolute Truth; none is higher.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
In three steps — two kinds of souls, a Supreme Person beyond them, "I am that Person" — Kṛṣṇa establishes that the liberated soul does not merge into Him; read through Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān, the verses climb to Kṛṣṇa as the highest reality. [44:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.19 [52:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.19 -->

**Context.** An unambiguous, emphatic verse: one who, without doubt, knows Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Person knows everything and therefore worships Him with all his heart `52:00 – 54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Without delusion_ — the conclusion one should come to `52:00`.
- _Knower of everything_ — one who has complete knowledge `52:00`.
- _With all the emotions_ — how such a person worships Kṛṣṇa `54:00`.

#### What "complete knowledge" means [52:00 – 54:00]

- At 7.2 Kṛṣṇa promised to give complete knowledge, after which nothing more remains to be known.
- This does not mean every technical detail of every branch of knowledge, but that the essential principles are given completely.
- The essential principle of knowledge is to know Kṛṣṇa as the _puruṣottama_ — and this is transformational, not merely intellectual.

#### Knowledge intensifies emotion [54:00 – 56:00]

- The result of such knowledge is devotion: the knower worships Kṛṣṇa with all the emotions of the heart.
- Kṛṣṇa said the same at 10.8 — the wise who know Him as the source of everything worship Him imbued with emotion.
- The _Māyāvāda_ idea: knowledge reveals that everything is one, so there is no one to love, and one must go beyond emotions.
- Kṛṣṇa's teaching: knowledge does not dissolve emotions — it intensifies them, because they become concentrated on the all-attractive, sweetly reciprocal Kṛṣṇa.
- **River and ocean:** Kṛṣṇa's love is like an ocean and ours like a river flowing unstoppably toward Him; Queen Kuntī prays that her consciousness flow toward Kṛṣṇa as the Gaṅgā flows to the sea `56:00`.
- A devotee holds nothing back, and nothing holds a devotee back.

#### From the purport [56:00 – 58:00]

- Knowing that it is Kṛṣṇa who is ultimately to be known, the devotee does not waste valuable time on all the various Vedic processes but engages directly in devotional service.
- Prabhupāda also addresses commentators who give wrong understandings, and concludes that speculation over hundreds and thousands of years that does not arrive at Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, to whom one must surrender, is a useless waste of time.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Real knowledge of Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Person does not dissolve emotion but concentrates all of it on Him — so the knower becomes a wholehearted devotee rather than a detached speculator. [52:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.20 [58:00 – 62:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.20 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes the chapter with another superlative verse: this is the most confidential part of the scriptures; one who understands it becomes wise and his endeavours meet with perfection `58:00 – 60:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Guhyatamam_ — most secret, most confidential `58:00`.
- _Buddhimān_ — truly wise, endowed with intelligence `58:00`.
- _Success in all actions_ — whatever such a person does attains success `60:00`.

#### Why this is the most confidential knowledge [58:00]

- The _Vedas_ do speak of Indra, Sūrya, Agni and many other beings, but their ultimate purpose is to direct us to Kṛṣṇa.
- When Brahmā studied the _Vedas_, he first took the conclusion to be _karma-kāṇḍa_, then _jñāna-kāṇḍa_, and only on the third hearing understood it to be _bhakti_.
- Kṛṣṇa speaks it to Arjuna because he is non-envious — resembling the opening of chapter 9.

#### Supreme success [60:00]

- We may have different conceptions of success, but objective supreme success is that which leads to supreme, everlasting happiness: eternal love for the eternal object of love, Kṛṣṇa.
- Prabhupāda's purport: devotional service is a process of spiritual understanding — it does not come overnight — and wherever devotional service exists, material contamination cannot exist.
- **Weakness of the heart:** the first fall-down comes from the desire to lord it over material nature, giving up the Lord's service; the second weakness is attachment to that position. The teacher explains weakness of the heart as giving in to lower desire instead of being determined to serve Kṛṣṇa; by praying and solidifying our connection with Him, it is overcome.

#### The flow of the whole chapter [60:00 – 62:00]

- The chapter starts analytically — the upside-down banyan tree, detachment, cutting the tree, transmigration, the need for _jñāna-cakṣu_.
- From verse 12 the mood becomes intensely devotional: see Kṛṣṇa's hand maintaining us materially and striving to liberate us.
- The movement from _jñāna_ to _bhakti_ is most beautifully illustrated in the _tri-gītā_, where _Vedānta_ leads to a devotional conclusion.
- Verses 19–20 establish unambiguously that Kṛṣṇa is the highest reality and _bhakti_ to Him the highest _sādhana_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The most confidential conclusion of all scripture is that Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Person and the goal of the Vedas; one who grasps this becomes truly wise, and by strengthening the heart's determination to serve Him reaches life's supreme success. [58:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
