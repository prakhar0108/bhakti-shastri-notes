## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 15.1 [04:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.1 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa introduces an imperishable banyan tree whose roots are above and branches below, whose leaves are the Vedic hymns; one who knows this tree is the knower of the Vedas `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ūrdhva-mūlam_ — roots upward `04:00`, `08:00`.
- _Adhaḥ-śākham_ — branches below `04:00`.
- _Aśvattha_ — the banyan tree; the teacher gives two further meanings (below) `04:00`, `10:00 – 12:00`.
- _Avyaya_ — imperishable `04:00`, `10:00`.
- _Chandāṁsi_ — the Vedic hymns, which are the leaves (_parṇāni_) of the tree `04:00`.
- _Veda-vit_ — a knower of the Vedas `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### Knowing only the leaves is not enough [04:00 – 08:00]

- Knowing the Vedic hymns alone does not make one a _veda-vit_; one has to know the complete tree — what it is, its nature and its purpose `06:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa made the same point earlier:
  - In 2.45, the Vedas speak of things within the three modes — so go beyond the three modes, Arjuna `06:00`.
  - In 6.43–44, the transcendentalist on a multi-lifetime journey, coming close to perfection, is attracted by the principles of spirituality and goes beyond the ritualistic activities (_karma-kāṇḍa_) of the Vedic literature `06:00`.

#### What the tree represents [06:00 – 10:00]

- The tree is a metaphor for material existence — not merely the existence of matter (matter is not aware of its own existence) but the existence of the soul within the arena of matter `06:00 – 08:00`.
- Here things are upside down — the opposite of how they should be:
  - Kṛṣṇa is not the centre;
  - the soul, a part of Kṛṣṇa, wants to become the centre `08:00`.
- The teacher notes that this inversion is discussed further in 15.7 `08:00`.

#### The tree reflected in water [08:00 – 10:00]

- Normally roots go down and branches go up; where do we see an upside-down tree? In a reflection.
- A beautiful tree on the bank of a river is reflected upside down in the water.
- Likewise our world is a reflection of the spiritual world. The world we live in is presently ours, but it is not actually our world; our world is Kṛṣṇa's world.
- In between lies the struggle — to go from here to there, or the struggle to find a better place here `10:00`.

#### Avyaya and aśvattha [10:00 – 12:00]

- **Avyaya:** material existence itself is eternal — sometimes manifest, sometimes unmanifest, but imperishable `10:00`.
- **Aśvattha — "that which will not last":**
  - We are not attracted to material existence as such, but to material forms and beautiful objects, and those are temporary.
  - Because they are temporary, there cannot be lasting happiness in relationships with people or things of this world.
  - We are generally attracted not to the soul but to outer bodily forms and appearances; those appearances will disappear, and so we will suffer `10:00 – 12:00`.
- **Aśvattha — "where a horse stands":** a horse habitually stands on three feet; so this world is situated unsteadily `12:00`.
  - The material world is _eka-pāda-vibhūti_ — just one-fourth of the Lord's total opulence.
  - Three parts (_tri-pāda-vibhūti_) exist in the spiritual world `12:00`.

#### Desire is the cause [12:00 – 14:00]

- In the purport Prabhupāda explains that the fruits of the tree — _dharma_, _artha_, _kāma_ and _mokṣa_ — come about from the Vedic hymns, the leaves `12:00`.
- Prabhupāda writes that desire is the cause of things being situated in this reflected material light `14:00`.
- Just as one must look away from the real tree to see its reflection, the soul desires to enjoy separately from Kṛṣṇa and to delight in material things; that misdirected desire causes material existence `14:00`.
- This reflection is to be cut off; one who knows how to cut off attachment to the material world is the real knower of the Vedas `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The material world is an upside-down reflection of the spiritual world, unsteady and temporary in its forms; the real knower of the Vedas is not one who knows the hymns alone, but one who knows how to cut the attachment that keeps the soul looking at the reflection. [04:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.2 [14:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.2 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa adds detail: the tree's branches spread both downward and upward, nourished by the modes, with sense objects as twigs, and its roots also extend downward, binding to fruitive action in human society `14:00 – 16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Adhaḥ ca ūrdhvam_ — downward and upward `16:00`.
- _Guṇa-pravṛddhāḥ_ — nourished by the modes `16:00 – 18:00`.
- _Viṣaya-pravālāḥ_ — _viṣaya_ are sense objects, _pravāla_ are twigs: the sense objects are the twigs `18:00`.
- _Karmānubandhīni_ — becoming bound by _karma_ `20:00`.
- _Manuṣya-loke_ — in the world of human beings `20:00 – 22:00`.

#### Branches going up and down [16:00]

- The tree is not simply upside down; things also go up and down.
- This represents how the living entity moves up and down in material existence:
  - creation begins from Brahmā — the first created being — so the roots are above, and from there things move downward;
  - souls fall, stay in material existence, and go through various species in various directions;
  - within transmigration, one can go downward or upward.

#### Watered by the modes [16:00 – 18:00]

- This connects with the previous chapter: every tree needs water for nourishment, and this tree is nourished by the modes.
- We choose particular desires according to the three modes; accordingly our material existence expands, and accordingly we move forward in life `18:00`.

#### Roots that also go downward [18:00 – 20:00]

- The tree is very complicated, because the word "root" has many senses:
  - **Where things begin** — our ancestors; looking at ourselves, our root is the spiritual world.
  - **The roots of our actions** — our desires `18:00`.
- "Thought is the ancestor of the deed; the thought is the seed of the deed": if we want to change our actions, we have to change our thoughts `18:00 – 20:00`.
- Conversely, the actions we do also affect our thoughts. So the roots — the concepts that make us act in particular ways — also change and go downward `20:00`.
- By good or bad _karma_ we go upward or downward; our conceptions change according to how we act `20:00`.

#### Why "manuṣya-loke" [20:00 – 22:00]

- All beings are ultimately under the jurisdiction of the Supreme Lord, and the principle of cosmic accountability applies even on the higher planets.
- But the specifics of the law of _karma_ do not apply to other species in the same way they apply to humans `20:00`.
- From heaven one enjoys and comes back; in hell one exhausts karmic reactions through suffering and then comes back up; future _karma_ is created in human life, where there is freedom for action `22:00`.
- In the purport: the lower branches are the lower species (animals such as cows, dogs and cats), the upper branches are the higher forms of life such as demigods and _gandharvas_; the twigs are sense objects such as sound, form and touch; and the planet of human beings is considered the field of activities `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The tree of material existence keeps growing because desires chosen in the modes water it; since thoughts are the seed of deeds and deeds in turn shape thoughts, the human form — where future karma is made — is the place to change direction. [16:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.3 – 15.4 [22:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.3 -->

<!-- vedabase:15.4 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says the real form of this tree cannot be perceived in this world; its strong roots must be cut with the weapon of detachment, after which one must seek the place from which no one returns, and surrender to the original person from whom everything has extended `22:00 – 24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Rūpam_ — the form of this tree, which Kṛṣṇa says cannot be perceived (_upalabhyate_) in this world `24:00`.
- _Su-virūḍha-mūlam_ — very strongly rooted `24:00`.
- _Asaṅga-śastreṇa_ — by the weapon of detachment; _asaṅga_ is the opposite of _saṅga_ `26:00`.
- _Dṛḍhena_ — with determination `26:00`.
- _Chittvā_ — cutting down `26:00`.
- _Padam_ — the destination `26:00`.
- _Prapadye_ — surrender `28:00`.
- _Pravṛttiḥ prasṛtā purāṇī_ — from whom the whole of nature has manifested `28:00`.

#### Don't try to figure it out — get out [22:00 – 24:00]

- Kṛṣṇa says, in effect, "Don't get too confused by this" — because it is confusing.
- The tree is so complex that its form cannot be understood from within this world. The teacher compares the _viśvarūpa_ of the eleventh chapter, where Arjuna said he could see no beginning, middle or end of the Lord's universal form `24:00`.
- The important thing is not to figure the tree out but to get out of it.
- What we can understand is the basic principle: we are bound here by our attachments, and those attachments are very deep-rooted `24:00`.

#### The axe of detachment, held with determination [24:00 – 26:00]

- The weapon normally used to break down a tree is an axe — so this is the axe of detachment `26:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa uses the word _saṅga_ several times; the teacher recalls a key "seed statement" (_bīja-vākya_) earlier in this section: the bondage of the soul in matter — of _puruṣa_ in _prakṛti_ — is due to association with the modes. For the soul to come out of matter, _asaṅga_ is required `26:00`.
- Detachment is not easy to develop and takes time; hence _dṛḍhena_ — one has to be determined:
  - "Even if I don't become detached today, I will become detached gradually."
  - "Let me just keep persevering; let me go on determinedly." `26:00`

#### Not the happy ending yet [26:00 – 28:00]

- After cutting, one must seek the destination from which, having gone, one does not come back.
- Is that the end — "everything is hunky-dory, happy ending"? No; Kṛṣṇa goes further: surrender to that original person, from whom all of nature has manifested `28:00`.

#### The prisoner's sentence [28:00 – 30:00]

- What keeps us going on the journey of material existence is our _saṅga_; the modes provide the water by which the tree grows.
- We cannot end the tree of material existence, but we can end our entanglement in it.
- A prisoner cannot destroy the prison, but by proper behaviour can end his own sentence and be released. Similarly, by detachment we cut off the shackles that bind us to this world `28:00 – 30:00`.
- Material existence will always remain a place of suffering — birth, death, old age and disease cannot be changed — but how much we suffer can change: greater identification brings more suffering; lesser identification brings less `30:00`.

#### Surrender to a person, not a stepping stone [30:00 – 32:00]

- This is a very clear indication of the Supreme Person to whom we must surrender — and He is not someone to be dropped later `30:00`.
- The teacher contrasts the _māyāvādī_ idea of an _iṣṭa-devatā_: use a worshippable deity of one's choice to get rid of attachment to material things, and then merge into _brahman_ — going beyond Bhagavān to _brahman_ `30:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa says detachment is required, but even after one becomes detached and attains a position from which one will not fall back, that is not the perfection; after that one has to surrender to the original person `32:00`.
- Here Kṛṣṇa refers to Himself in the third person, but the point is the same as in the tenth chapter, where He says He is the source of everything `32:00`.

#### Two directions of one process [34:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda's point in the purport is that it is a reciprocal process:
  - we become detached from this world and then surrender to Kṛṣṇa; or
  - we surrender to Kṛṣṇa and by that become detached from the world. Both are possible `34:00`.
- **The gradual route:** struggle to detach oneself from the world; having succeeded, find out what reality lies beyond it; then come to know about Kṛṣṇa and surrender. This takes multiple lifetimes — _karma-yoga_, _jñāna-yoga_, the yoga path, and then after that _bhakti-yoga_; "after many births" `34:00`.
- Since this is the _jñāna_ section, Kṛṣṇa gives that analytical process: see how the world is false, become detached from its false pleasures, then turn towards the reality beyond `34:00`.
- **Prabhupāda's merciful route:** wherever we are, we can just surrender to Kṛṣṇa and He will take us beyond everything. Prabhupāda cites a verse stating that by _bhakti_ to Vāsudeva, detachment and knowledge quickly arise `34:00`.
  - Such knowledge does not require a material cause — one does not have to be super-intellectual; one just needs a receptive heart `34:00`.
  - It is not that we have to be detached in order to surrender; no matter how attached we are, we start the process of surrender from the beginning `36:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We cannot destroy the prison of material existence, but we can end our own sentence: cut deep-rooted attachment with the axe of detachment held with determination — and know that surrender to Kṛṣṇa, begun wherever we are, itself produces that detachment. [24:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.5 [36:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.5 -->

**Context.** This verse elaborates the previous one: having said one must surrender to the original person, Kṛṣṇa now explains how that surrender is done — the qualities of those who reach the eternal kingdom `36:00`, `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nirmāna_ — without _māna_, prestige or false pride `36:00`, `42:00`.
- _Moha_ — illusion `36:00`, `46:00`.
- _Jita-saṅga-doṣāḥ_ — having overcome the fault of association with the modes `36:00`, `48:00`.
- _Adhyātma-nityāḥ_ — always in spiritual consciousness `48:00`.
- _Vinivṛtta-kāmāḥ_ — completely retired from lust `38:00`, `50:00`.
- _Dvandvair vimuktāḥ sukha-duḥkha-saṁjñaiḥ_ — free from the dualities of happiness and distress `40:00`.
- _Amūḍhāḥ_ — unbewildered `40:00`.
- _Padam avyayam_ — the imperishable destination `40:00`.

#### Pravṛtti, nivṛtti and bhakti [36:00 – 38:00]

- In the Vedic tradition there are two paths:
  - _Pravṛtti-mārga_ — the path of attachment, roughly equated with _karma-kāṇḍa_.
  - _Nivṛtti-mārga_ — the path of detachment: _karma-sannyāsa_, giving up activity, practising _jñāna-yoga_, disengaging oneself from the world `38:00`.
- Beyond both is _bhakti_: being in the world but fixing the heart on an object beyond the world `38:00`.

#### Renounce the desire, not the world [38:00 – 40:00]

- We do not have to renounce the world, but we do have to renounce the desire to enjoy the world `38:00`.
- _Nivṛtti_ is also used for retirement — "so-and-so retired on such-and-such a date." _Vinivṛtta-kāmāḥ_ means becoming detached — retired — from the desire for sensual enjoyment `38:00`.
- It is this desire that binds the soul: the teacher recalls the earlier verse explaining that the soul is situated in _prakṛti_ because of the desire to enjoy the modes `38:00`.
- **The movie analogy:** a person watching a movie starts feeling its emotions — anger, fear, terror, joy. But when the person no longer wants to watch, those emotions no longer come. Similarly, when we give up the desire to enjoy, we give up our entanglement with material desire `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### Equipoise and the imperishable abode [40:00 – 42:00]

- _Dvandvair vimuktāḥ_ — one stays equipoised in happiness and distress `40:00`.
- _Amūḍhāḥ_ — the material energy makes one _mūḍha_; the person with this consciousness is unbewildered, well situated, not caught up in the material energy `40:00`.
- _Padam avyayam_ — Kṛṣṇa refers to this abode elsewhere too; the teacher recalls the eighth chapter, where it is called unmanifest, infallible, the supreme destination and Kṛṣṇa's supreme abode `40:00`.
- That supreme abode can be attained by surrender `42:00`.

#### Nirmāna: strong enough to admit we are not strong [42:00 – 44:00]

- Prabhupāda describes the qualifications carefully; the first is not being deluded by pride `42:00`.
- Why? Because I am not actually the controller:
  - material nature is making me dance like a puppet;
  - I think "I can do this, I can do that," but I am so frail and powerless — like a leaf tossed in the waves of material nature;
  - to go beyond the waves I need an anchor beyond their reach — the Supreme Lord `42:00`.
- "We need to be strong enough to admit that we are not strong enough." In the world, "strong" means never admitting any weakness — staying calm, putting on an "unflappable" profile `42:00`.
- In some professions one does need such a face, but we cannot stay that way artificially: a mask of peacefulness falls off in the face of the adversities of this world unless there is some shelter within `42:00 – 44:00`.
- For the conditioned soul, admitting "I am weak" is very difficult; the conditioning says "I am great, I am supreme." Isolated self-contemplation, not supervised by a responsible guide, leads to problems `44:00`.

#### Healthy self-confidence [44:00 – 46:00]

- Everyone needs some level of self-confidence, and _bhakti_ gives it by telling us about the real self:
  - the self is part of Kṛṣṇa, filled with divine potential;
  - the self is loved by Kṛṣṇa;
  - Kṛṣṇa's omnipotence is available for His devotee `44:00`.
- Self-confidence based on understanding the self is fine. Prabhupāda had a lot of self-confidence — confidence in Kṛṣṇa: "Kṛṣṇa is going to fulfil this; I might be the instrument." That gave him the courage of conviction to become successful all over the world at an advanced age, sleeping very little and working so hard — an example of surrender `44:00 – 46:00`.
- **The Jaladuta song:** in the song Prabhupāda wrote on board the Jaladuta, he called himself the most insignificant and unfortunate, and prayed that Kṛṣṇa make his words understandable to the people, make him dance according to His tune, and manifest as _śabda-brahma_ — for the revival of spiritual happiness, of our awareness of our spiritual identity, and the reconnection of our heart with Kṛṣṇa, the source of unlimited happiness `46:00`.
- As long as I think "I am strong; I can deal with my problems and _anarthas_," Kṛṣṇa cannot really be there to help. So humility is important `46:00`.

#### Moha: proprietor or caretaker [46:00 – 50:00]

- Here _moha_ is not specifically the _tāmasic_ illusion spoken of in the fourteenth chapter; it is the general illusion of thinking "I am the body" and that things related to me are mine — that I am their proprietor rather than their caretaker `46:00 – 48:00`.
- _Jita-saṅga-doṣāḥ_ — the modes create dualities, and those dualities bind us in material existence; one must be freed from that `48:00`.
- Prabhupāda writes that one should cultivate knowledge of what is actually one's own and what is not `48:00`.
- What is actually our own is our spiritual advancement — our _bhakti_, our connection with Kṛṣṇa. The teacher cites Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's song: "I am Yours and You are mine; what need have I of any other wealth?" `48:00`
- Our sense of possessiveness (_mamatā_) is not to be rejected but redirected; Kṛṣṇa Himself becomes the property of His devotee `48:00`.
- Our connection with Kṛṣṇa is our most precious property: all other wealth will come and go, but if we hold on to this wealth sincerely throughout life, by Kṛṣṇa's grace we will attain eternal existence `50:00`.

#### Completely retired from lust [50:00]

- _Vinivṛtta_ means totally retired — no chance of coming back `50:00`.
- Lust arises from dualities — "this form is attractive, that one is not" — and dualities are caused by the hope of pleasure and the fear of misery `50:00`.
- We must be convinced that lust is our worst enemy, because it makes us act in anti-devotional, anti-spiritual ways `50:00`.
- Vedic culture does not encourage divorce — but it does encourage the "divorce" of the soul from matter, which is essential for our happiness `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Surrender is made concrete through humility (admitting we are not strong enough), freedom from the illusion of proprietorship, and retirement from lust; what we hold on to instead — our connection with Kṛṣṇa — is the one wealth that is truly our own. [36:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.6 [50:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.6 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now speaks in the first person: His supreme abode is not illumined by the sun, moon, fire or electricity, and those who reach it never return to this material world `50:00 – 52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sūrya_ — the sun `52:00`.
- The moon — like the sun and fire, not needed for illumination there `52:00`.
- _Pāvaka_ — fire; Prabhupāda translates it as fire or electricity `52:00 – 54:00`.
- _Dhāma paramaṁ mama_ — My supreme abode `52:00`.

#### From third person to first person [52:00]

- In the preceding verses Kṛṣṇa spoke of the original person in the third person; here, as the _puruṣottama_, He speaks of Himself and His abode in the first person: "that is My abode" `52:00`.

#### Self-luminous [52:00 – 54:00]

- The abode is self-luminous — it does not depend on anything for its illumination.
- In this world, without the sun, moon and fire there would be darkness, and darkness brings confusion and helplessness.
- In the spiritual world there is no darkness; everything is self-luminous. The moon may be in the sky — for example on a full-moon night during pastimes — but simply to enhance the sweetness of the _rasa_, to make the setting of the pastime more wholesome, appealing and enriching. Sun and moon may be there, but they are not needed for illumination `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### Why "electricity"? [54:00 – 58:00]

- One may wonder how Prabhupāda includes electricity when it is not mentioned in the verse `54:00`.
- In an Īśopaniṣad purport, the teacher recalls, a similar statement is quoted from an Upaniṣad (he thinks the Muṇḍaka) that also includes _vidyut_ — electricity. So in that sense it is not wrong `54:00`.
- The verse gives a representative, not exhaustive, list: Kṛṣṇa is not listing every source of light ("maybe a matchstick is there?") — He names the major sources and says none is needed in the spiritual world `54:00`.
- In Vedic times electricity might not have been common — when people wanted, they could use mantras to create luminosity — but today, when people talk about light, most think first of the power switch, not the sun `54:00`.
- Electricity is ultimately recycled sunlight: hydel and thermal power plants could not exist without the sun; so the list is inclusive `56:00`.

#### Precision and relevance in translation [56:00 – 58:00]

- Translating any ancient book means balancing two aspects:
  - **Precision** — what the text accurately, technically means.
  - **Relevance** — how it bears on the values and life of the current reader and how it benefits them `56:00`.
- Prabhupāda in some cases leans more toward relevance: people have little inclination to read, so he makes sure that on every page they get the essential message of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, repeatedly stressed in his purports and hammered into our consciousness until it becomes deep-rooted `56:00`.
- Including electricity serves the point of the verse — no external source of illumination is needed — rather than a precise inventory of light sources `58:00`.

#### A symbolic reading [58:00]

- The verse can also be taken symbolically:
  - **Sun** — the light by which we see: the abode cannot be perceived by the eyes.
  - **Moon** — associated with the mind: it cannot be conceived by the mind.
  - **Fire** — associated with speech: it cannot be described by words.
- Such is Kṛṣṇa's abode — invisible, inconceivable, indescribable — and the characteristic of it is that one who goes there does not come back `58:00`.

#### Captivated by the destination [58:00 – 60:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that one should be captivated by this information `58:00`.
- **The America analogy:** many people from India go to America because they hear it is a land of plenty, prosperity and freedom; when we hear seriously about something attractive, a desire to go there develops `58:00 – 60:00`.
- Similarly, hearing about the spiritual world should naturally develop attraction for it; by surrendering to Kṛṣṇa one goes there, with no coming back to this miserable material world `60:00`.
- Prabhupāda writes that one cannot become detached from attraction to the world simply by dressing in saffron cloth; one must become attached to the Lord's devotional service. Advancement is a matter of the heart, not of dress — the rest can help develop the right disposition of heart `60:00`.
- The purpose of _sannyāsa_ is to become more attached to Kṛṣṇa. Mahāprabhu quoted the song of the Avantī _brāhmaṇa_: "I shall cross over the insurmountable ocean of nescience by holding firmly to the devotional service of the Lord" `60:00`.
- The more we become attached to Kṛṣṇa, the more we become liberated, and ultimately we attain the spiritual world — the ultimate perfection of life `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's abode needs no sun, moon, fire or electricity because it is self-luminous; hearing about it seriously should captivate us, and attachment to devotional service — not a change of dress — is what carries us there for good. [52:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
