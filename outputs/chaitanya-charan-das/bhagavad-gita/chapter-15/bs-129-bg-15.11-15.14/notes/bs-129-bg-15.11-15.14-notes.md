## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 15.11 [00:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.11 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa contrasts two kinds of endeavourers: yogis who strive and, being situated in the self, can see the soul's entanglement; and others who also strive but, not being situated in the self and with undeveloped minds, cannot see `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yatantaḥ_ — endeavouring; the word applies to both groups `00:00 – 02:00`, `04:00`.
- _Enam_ — "this": the reality of how the soul is trapped in material existence and dragged around by the modes `00:00`.
- _Paśyanti_ — they see; logically connected with the "eyes" (_cakṣu_) of the previous verse `02:00`.
- _Ātmani avasthitam_ — situated on the spiritual platform, at the level of the _ātmā_ `00:00 – 02:00`.
- _Akṛtātmānaḥ_ — not adequately situated on the spiritual platform `02:00`.
- _Acetasaḥ_ — not conscious of what one should be conscious of `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Endeavour must transform [02:00 – 04:00]

- It is not enough to desire and strive to perceive spiritual reality; the endeavour must lead to a transformation by which one becomes situated on the spiritual platform `02:00`.
- **The blind man and the painting:** Prabhupāda gives the example that willpower alone is not enough. A blind person standing before the world's most beautiful painting — the Mona Lisa, say — will not see it no matter how long he stares, glares and peers `02:00`.
- A process is required — a surgery or healing by which he develops eyes to see. So endeavour must be guided endeavour, involving a treatment by which one gets eyes and then sees `04:00`.

#### Perceive or pursue — which comes first? [04:00 – 06:00]

- This can seem circular: to see the soul we must be on the spiritual platform, but if I can't see the soul, I won't feel inspired to advance to that platform `04:00`.
- Rephrased: does _perceiving_ (seeing) come first, or _pursuing_ (going after)? `04:00`
- What comes first is submissive hearing. By hearing, we understand there is a higher reality, and that triggers both: we get some perception of it, which inspires us to pursue it further; pursuing more, we perceive more, and that inspires further pursuit `04:00 – 06:00`.
- Hearing is itself the initial pursuit — seeking with a little faith (_śraddhā_). That faith may be just a serious inquisitiveness, even a positive, favourable curiosity — not yet life-transforming, which comes later — but it begins the process `06:00`.

#### Consciousness kidnapped [06:00 – 10:00]

- One cannot perceive because consciousness is caught at the material level. Kṛṣṇa makes a similar point elsewhere: consciousness is _apahṛta_ — abducted, kidnapped. A child coming home from school wants to go one way, but kidnappers take him elsewhere `06:00`.
- What causes this kidnapping? Attachment to matter `08:00`.
- **"Don't be a kid, and don't take naps"** `08:00 – 10:00`:
  - **Don't be a kid** — don't be naive or childish. Children are casual and frivolous, ruled by feelings — one hobby today, another tomorrow. Spiritual life is a serious pursuit. At work, if the boss asks why a task wasn't done, we won't say "I didn't feel like it" — or he'll show us the door. Adults are meant to rule their feelings to some extent — not switch them off, but know when to be guided by them and when to control them.
  - **Don't take naps** — don't become lazy and drift off. Rising to the spiritual platform needs continuous, steady pursuit; if we lower our guard at any moment, _māyā_, waiting close by, will pounce and drag us away like a kidnapper `10:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa consciousness is not just momentary peaks of ecstasy but a steady climb. Sometimes we feel high — "I feel like dancing and singing" — sometimes dry; either way we keep going `10:00`.

#### What "spiritual platform" means [10:00 – 12:00]

- It is not an abstract concept, nor a stage one walks onto at a programme `10:00`.
- **Acting at the bodily level** — letting bodily desires and drives rule life: the eyes see something attractive, the tongue wants something delicious, and we go after it `10:00 – 12:00`.
- **Acting at the spiritual level** — letting the needs and opportunities of the soul guide our life, shape it and mould our decisions `12:00`.
- One need not be fully self-realised: we cannot be entirely on the spiritual platform now, but we can act at the spiritual level to a significant extent by choosing not to let bodily drives rule our life `12:00`.

#### Acetasaḥ: conscious, but of the wrong thing [12:00 – 14:00]

- For those ruled by bodily desires, consciousness gets dragged away from Kṛṣṇa. Kṛṣṇa used the word earlier (3.31–3.32): those envious of Him or without faith in His words lose even the knowledge they have, because their consciousness is dragged away `12:00`.
- We are always conscious of something. "Absent-minded" doesn't mean having no mind — the mind is absent from where the body is. Likewise _acetasaḥ_ doesn't mean lacking consciousness, but not being conscious of what one should be conscious of — because of excessive, unregulated material desires one is conscious only of matter `14:00`.
- **In the temple:** the Deities may be beautiful, and our eyes may even be on Kṛṣṇa; but if we are agitated by lust and fantasising about other things, we are in front of Kṛṣṇa but not looking at Him `14:00`.

#### Misinformed vs well-informed endeavour [14:00 – 16:00]

- In the second case, _yatantaḥ_ refers to misinformed endeavour — like the blind person staring at the painting: unproductive. Well-informed endeavour is productive `14:00`.
- The difference is proper versus improper endeavour, determined by whether it is guided or not `16:00`.
- Prabhupāda explains that though they endeavour in a so-called yoga system, they are not self-realised and cannot understand the transmigration of the soul. Their hazy conception of the soul keeps them from tangible steps toward self-realisation — so there is neither comprehension nor resulting transformation `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Eyes of knowledge come not from endeavour alone but from guided endeavour that begins with submissive hearing and steadily shifts us from acting at the bodily level to acting at the soul's level — seriously (not as kids) and vigilantly (without naps), so māyā cannot kidnap our consciousness. [02:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.12 [16:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.12 -->

**Context.** A new section begins: how Kṛṣṇa assists us in our material existence (15.12–15.14) and in our spiritual endeavour (15.15). Kṛṣṇa first says that the splendour of the sun, the moon and fire, which illuminates the whole universe, comes from Him `16:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jagat_ — the whole universe, illuminated by the sun `20:00`.
- _Āditya_, _candramā_, _agni_ — the sun, moon and fire: the same three objects as 15.6, under different names `20:00`.
- _Tejaḥ_ — effulgence; "that effulgence comes from Me" `20:00 – 22:00`.

#### If one can't yet perceive the soul [16:00 – 18:00]

- The previous verse said one should have eyes of knowledge to perceive the _ātmā_. But what if a person is at the bodily level and not developed enough to perceive the soul? Is spiritual advancement blocked for him? No `16:00`.
- Eyes of knowledge can work in a different way: instead of perceiving the _ātmā_, we can try to perceive the hand of God — Kṛṣṇa's role in our life `18:00`.
- Perceiving the _ātmā_ requires critical thinking, intellectual sophistication and depth of contemplation, which is not always easy. But even at the material level of consciousness one can see the hand of God in material things — whatever we have, we cannot get by ourselves; it comes by the Lord's arrangement `18:00`.

#### Four levels of arrangement [18:00 – 20:00]

- Getting _jñāna_ by perceiving the higher arrangement in the material world `18:00`:
  1. **15.12 — cosmological:** the universe.
  2. **15.13 — terrestrial:** the earth.
  3. **15.14 — physiological:** the body.
  4. **15.15 — spiritual.**
- These correspond to the coverings around the _ātmā_: broadly we are in the material world; within it, on a particular planet that must provide facilities for living; and within that, in a body that must be maintained `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### Representative sources of light — and more [22:00]

- In the spiritual world these are not required, because everything there is self-luminous; in the material world reality is not self-luminous, so we need sources of illumination. These three are representative sources `22:00`.
- They are not only sources of illumination: the sun is also a source of nutrition — by its energy photosynthesis happens and we get food. At a universal level, without the sun's energy nothing would be possible `22:00`.

#### The arrangement and the Arranger [22:00 – 24:00]

- "Their effulgence comes from Me" — modern science will say some mechanical process makes the sun give light, though its explanations are sometimes debated; so where does God come in? `22:00`
- The mechanical process is a partial explanation. What started it, and what sustains it? That is God's arrangement `22:00`.
- We may study the arrangement, and that can be helpful; but beyond the arrangement is the Arranger, beyond the design is the Designer `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### Animals ask "how"; humans can ask "why" [24:00]

- Prabhupāda begins the purport: the unintelligent cannot understand how things are taking place `24:00`.
- Animals simply live by the sun: birds chirp when it rises, animals wake and move; they think only of how to get food, a mate, shelter `24:00`.
- Humans can ask not only "how" but "why." Science largely answers "how" — the mechanism by which the sun gives light — but why does the sun exist at all? `24:00`

#### The earth's position [24:00 – 26:00]

- Even taking modern cosmology's view that life needs oxygen, water and a particular temperature range: if the earth were in Venus's position it would be too hot for life as we know it; in Mars's position, too cold `26:00`.
- The Vedic view is that life exists everywhere, in all possible conditions, because the _ātmā_ is not affected by any material condition — that is correct in principle. But here we are looking from the standpoint of people at the material level of consciousness, to help them begin to appreciate God `26:00`.

#### Science, deism and theism [26:00 – 30:00]

- From the start of the Scientific Revolution — Newton, Galileo — and through later centuries — Kelvin, Pasteur — almost all leading scientists believed in God `26:00`.
- Their belief was not merely religious (Christianity was "taking a beating" then); their study of science uncovered such majestic order, harmony and intricacy that it reflected a superhuman mind, a supreme intelligence `26:00 – 28:00`.
- Only later, after Darwin's theory of evolution and subsequent ideas, did scientists become overconfident and reject God `28:00`.
- **Three positions** `28:00`:
  - **Theism** — God is both the creator and the controller.
  - **Deism** — God is the creator but not the controller.
  - **Atheism** — God is neither; God doesn't exist.
- Science can at least move us towards deism — things cannot have started on their own; pursued further, it reveals the theistic conception too, which will be seen in 15.15 `28:00`.
- **The clock:** deism sees the universe as a clock — it needs a maker, but once handed to the customer, the maker has no control; it runs by its mechanism `28:00 – 30:00`.
- Theistic scientists don't deny mechanisms; but the mechanisms are set up by the Supreme Lord, and things work under His supervision `30:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa is overseeing: He doesn't directly touch material nature, but His sight alone is enough for nature's actions to take place `30:00`.

#### Background arrangements and provocation [30:00 – 32:00]

- Even at the material level, existence and enjoyment need many background arrangements, which come not by chance but by divine plan; recognising that plan, one can appreciate the Supreme `30:00`.
- Prabhupāda: without His mercy there could be no sun, moon or fire, and without them no one could live — "these are some thoughts to provoke Kṛṣṇa consciousness in the conditioned soul" `30:00`.
- "Provoke" is usually used negatively — provocative sights and sense objects — but the idea is a force that shakes one up: serious contemplation forcefully shakes one up `30:00 – 32:00`.
- The teacher recalls (attributing it, he thinks, to Pascal, and noting he has written about it in his book on science and spirituality) that a little science takes a man away from God, but immersion in science brings him back: a little science intoxicates — "I know everything; we can explain everything without God" — but going deep one realises God is essential `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
One who cannot yet see the soul can still see the hand of God: the sun, moon and fire that make life possible are arrangements, and beyond every arrangement stands the Arranger — contemplating this seriously "provokes" Kṛṣṇa consciousness even in the conditioned soul. [16:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.13 [32:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.13 -->

**Context.** Moving to the terrestrial arrangement, Kṛṣṇa says He enters the planets and by His energy keeps them, with their living entities, in orbit; and, becoming the moon, He supplies the juice that nourishes all vegetables `32:00 – 34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Āviśya_ — by entering into them `32:00`.
- _Ojasā_ — by My energy `32:00`.
- _Oṣadhīḥ_ — conversationally used for medicine, but generally plants, vegetables and herbs, all of which have medicinal components `32:00 – 34:00`.
- _Soma_ — the moon, through which Kṛṣṇa nourishes the vegetables `34:00`.
- _Rasātmakaḥ_ — supplying the juice that nourishes the vegetables `34:00`.

#### Gravity: description, not explanation [34:00 – 38:00]

- Why are the planets sustained in their orbits? Because of gravity — so what does "it is I who sustain them" mean? Ultimately gravity, like all laws of material nature, acts by Kṛṣṇa's will; He has arranged the force of gravity `34:00`.
- Scientists often admit that what they call explanations are often descriptions `34:00`:
  - **Description** — saying how something is.
  - **Explanation** — giving causes.
- A generic definition: gravity is the force that keeps planets in orbit; and what keeps planets in orbit? Gravity. That is circular — naming a pre-existing phenomenon is not an explanation `34:00 – 36:00`.
- The equation is useful, but even Newton admitted its limits: his contemporary Leibniz said the idea of gravity smacked of occultism — through what medium does a force travel from one planet to another? Newton frankly replied that he was not speaking of ontology, of the source of the force, but giving a mathematical formulation for an observed phenomenon `36:00`.
- Einstein later described gravity as a characteristic of space-time — but that too piles up concepts: an invisible concept (gravity) is posited to explain phenomena, then another (space-time) to explain that. What is solidly rooted at the bottom of that chain of inference? Nothing `36:00 – 38:00`.
- The teacher notes he does not want to go into science too much, since the class is studying the Gītā `36:00`.

#### Asking the wrong question [38:00 – 40:00]

- **Darkness and light (Prabhupāda's example):** normally, when walking in light and the light suddenly goes off, we understand darkness is the absence of light — maybe the power was switched off. But someone who has always lived in darkness, suddenly seeing light, may ask "How is darkness producing light?" From his limited experience the question seems valid, but it is all wrong: darkness never produces light; it is only light's absence `38:00`.
- Similarly, trying to explain everything at the material level — including the intricacy of all the arrangements — without an ultimate cause is an invalid understanding; we have got the questions wrong. Consciousness comes first and arranges matter; matter does not produce consciousness `38:00`.
- There may be some material mechanism, but it is Kṛṣṇa's presence within everything that sustains it; by His strength and energy all moving and non-moving things stay in place `38:00 – 40:00`.
- Prabhupāda's purport cites the Vedic statement that because of the Supreme Person the sun shines and the planets move steadily; were it not for Him, the planets would scatter like dust in the air and perish `40:00`.

#### The tiny soul makes the body light [40:00 – 42:00]

- Matter is heavy, but the soul's presence makes it light: one tiny soul makes a body run, dance, skip and play `40:00`.
- When the soul departs, the body is so heavy that one person cannot lift it; in funeral processions four people are needed, and even they keep changing shoulders as they ache `40:00`.
- A living person can swim; when the soul departs, the body drowns. In the same way, everything in the material universe floats and is sustained by the Lord's presence `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### Nourishment through the moon [42:00 – 44:00]

- Modern science says photosynthesis transforms sunlight into edible food through plants — true; but Kṛṣṇa is speaking specifically of nourishment — the juice (_rasa_) that comes by His arrangement through the moon `42:00`.
- Science is never complete and is always evolving; ideas considered bedrock today may be rejected tomorrow. History shows that no man-made idea considered infallible has survived the test of time `42:00`.
- So we need not put undue faith in such explanations; they may be correct from a limited perspective but not from the ultimate perspective. If the Lord says He nourishes through the moon, there is some mechanism we don't yet know about `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Some researchers report that crops grow less when the night sky is cloudy and the moon hidden, and more when the moon shines. Such empirical research is ultimately indecisive — it may reach right or wrong conclusions — so we focus on _śabda_ (scriptural testimony) and use empirical research only to the extent it helps us understand _śabda_ `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Science largely names and describes — "gravity," "space-time" — without reaching the ultimate cause; the Gītā reverses the question: consciousness arranges matter, and it is Kṛṣṇa's presence that keeps the planets in orbit and nourishes the plants, just as the tiny soul keeps a heavy body afloat. [34:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 15.14 [44:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:15.14 -->

**Context.** At the physiological level, Kṛṣṇa says He becomes the fire of digestion in the bodies of all living entities and, joining the outgoing and incoming airs, digests the four kinds of food `44:00 – 48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vaiśvānara_ — the deity who presides over digestion; Kṛṣṇa says "I become _vaiśvānara_" `44:00 – 46:00`.
- _Prāṇinām_ — of all living entities, not just humans `46:00`.
- _Prāṇa_ and _apāna_ — the outgoing and down-going airs, kept in balance for digestion `48:00`.
- _Catur-vidham_ — the four kinds of food: chewed, swallowed, sucked/licked and drunk `48:00`.

#### "I become vaiśvānara" [44:00 – 46:00]

- This doesn't mean complete oneness between Kṛṣṇa and _vaiśvānara_; as in the _vibhūti-yoga_ chapter, some correlation is described — there it was more "the one among the many, the one above the many"; here it is that _vaiśvānara_ gets the power to digest by the Lord's grace, just as the sun gets the power to give light by His arrangement `46:00`.

#### Sheltered by the body — which is sheltered by Kṛṣṇa [46:00]

- As 15.7 said, living entities are Kṛṣṇa's parts, meant to live in His shelter; instead they have taken shelter of the body and try to enjoy at the bodily level `46:00`.
- We think "this is my body" — even "I am the body" — but we cannot maintain the body by our independent effort `46:00`.

#### The airs and digestion [46:00 – 48:00]

- Modern science speaks of chemicals and of peristalsis — the motions in the alimentary canal that move chewed and swallowed food down. True, but how do those motions happen? `46:00 – 48:00`
- The Vedic worldview on which the Gītā is based explains that they happen by the movement of the airs in the body; constipation, for instance, occurs when there isn't adequate air to excrete waste. The airs play a vital role in all bodily functions, including digestion `48:00`.
- The four kinds of food are also mentioned in the _Maṅgala-ārati_ prayer: the spiritual master prepares the four kinds of food, offers them to Kṛṣṇa and distributes them `48:00`.

#### "God gives and forgives; we get and forget" [48:00 – 50:00]

- We can eat, but after that we don't know what happens; we cannot digest the food ourselves. It just happens, as far as we know `48:00 – 50:00`.
- The only time we think about our digestive system is when it doesn't work. "God gives and forgives; we get and forget" `50:00`.
- Providing food for the body is part of our duty, but however competently we do it, it is not enough; digestion happens by Kṛṣṇa's arrangement through _vaiśvānara_ `50:00`.

#### A chain of factories [50:00 – 54:00]

- Some may not believe in a deity supervising digestion; but even scientifically, digestion is very complex, involving many reactions that turn food into usable energy `50:00`.
- In this age of transplants and artificial limbs, researchers have tried to make an artificial digestive system, but found the process so complex that a machine won't suffice — it would need a factory, even a chain of factories extending over several miles `50:00 – 52:00`.
- **Voluntary and involuntary functions** `52:00`:
  - Voluntary — raising my hand, speaking: to some extent in my control.
  - Involuntary — blood circulation, respiration, digestion: vital for survival, but we don't do them; Kṛṣṇa does.
- **Artificial ventilation:** some bodily functions can be sustained externally for a dying patient, but it costs thousands of rupees to keep someone alive even a day, and much more over several days. We cannot do these functions ourselves, and even with machines it costs so much — so who normally does them? `52:00`
- Dismissing it as "chance" or "nature" is no explanation: it is too well designed for chance, and nature itself has no consciousness or purpose; there must be a being with consciousness and purpose overseeing it `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### Always resting on Kṛṣṇa [54:00 – 56:00]

- We take shelter of the body, but the body is ultimately sheltered by Kṛṣṇa; we can never move away from His shelter. Even when turning away from Kṛṣṇa we are still resting on Him, dependent on His merciful arrangements to live at the material level `54:00`.
- **Rāvaṇa and Sītā:** in the _Rāmāyaṇa_, flattering Sītā before kidnapping her, Rāvaṇa says her beauty is so great that the Creator must have retired after making her, thinking He could not make anyone more beautiful. Even Rāvaṇa recognised he did not make that beauty — yet he thought it was meant for his enjoyment `54:00`.
- Even to enjoy sense objects, we don't make them; the Supreme Lord does. He is the creator, sustainer, maintainer, the caretaker of the body and the arranger of all its internal necessities `54:00`.
- Whether for survival or enjoyment at the material level, we can do neither by ourselves. Seeing the Lord's benedictory arrangements, even someone whose consciousness is at the material level can develop appreciation for and begin connecting with Kṛṣṇa, the supreme spiritual reality who maintains the material reality. This is the first step in developing eyes of knowledge `54:00 – 56:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa does not only arrange for our material life; His primary purpose is to elevate us to the spiritual level of consciousness — the subject of the next verse, in the next class `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We cannot even digest our own food — Kṛṣṇa, as the fire of digestion, does what a chain of factories could not; recognising that every survival and enjoyment rests on His arrangements is the first step in developing eyes of knowledge. [44:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
