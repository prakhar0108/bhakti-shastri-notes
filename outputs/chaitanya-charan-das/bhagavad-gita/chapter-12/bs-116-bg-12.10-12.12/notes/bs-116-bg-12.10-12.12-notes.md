## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 12.10 [00:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.10 -->

**Context.** If one is incapable even of the practice described in 12.9, Kṛṣṇa says: become dedicated to working for Him, and by that also one will attain perfection `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Dedicated to Kṛṣṇa versus working for Kṛṣṇa — being dedicated to Him means the heart, soul, mind and intelligence, the whole inner being, are offered to Him; working for Him means we do various works but treat the work done for Kṛṣṇa as most important and try to make all work an offering. The first is a higher level `02:00`.
- _Siddhi_ — literally "perfection" `04:00`.

#### The rope lowered into the well [02:00 – 04:00]

- A person is trapped at the bottom of a well; a rope is sent down.
  - "Stretch your hands up, stand on your toes and catch it." — "I can't."
  - The rope comes lower: "Just raise your hands a little." — "I can't."
  - The rope comes lower still: "It's around your waist — just tie it and I'll pull you out."
- Likewise Kṛṣṇa extends His mercy lower and lower.

#### Which perfection? [04:00]

- Kṛṣṇa uses _siddhi_ several times in the Gītā; most relevant is 7.3: out of thousands few endeavour for perfection, and of those, fewer still achieve it — perfection meaning the quest for transcendence.
- _Siddhi_ can also mean mystic perfections (such as becoming tiny or light), but those are material; this is a _bhakti-yoga_ chapter, so it means devotional perfection, culminating in pure love for Kṛṣṇa.

#### Two sub-levels of working for Kṛṣṇa [04:00 – 06:00]

1. **Direct — do His work:** render service to His mission, e.g. going out in a book-distribution marathon, sharing Kṛṣṇa's message, serving in the temple.
2. **Indirect — offer Him the fruits of our work:** in one's job or profession, give charity from one's earnings and thus spiritualise the work.

- For a devotee even family life is integrated: family members, devotees or not, are parts of Kṛṣṇa, placed with us by His arrangement, and we serve Kṛṣṇa by caring for them materially and spiritually `06:00`.

#### Land, capital, organisation and labour [06:00 – 08:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport cites the management principle that every endeavour needs land, capital, organisation and labour — infrastructural, financial, organisational and human resources.
- Example: a builder needs land to build on, capital for materials and workers, labour to build, and trained people to oversee the project.
- All of these can be used for Kṛṣṇa. Prabhupāda writes that the only difference is that in materialism one works for sense gratification, while the same work done for Kṛṣṇa's satisfaction is spiritual activity — publication work, for instance `08:00`.
- Such voluntary service to the cause of Kṛṣṇa consciousness raises one to a higher state of love for God, whereupon one becomes perfect `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa lowers the rope of mercy: if one cannot yet fix the mind on Him, one can work for Him — by doing His work directly or by offering the fruits of one's own — and that voluntary service itself leads to perfection. [02:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.11 [10:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.11 -->

**Context.** One might expect Kṛṣṇa to reject someone who cannot even work for Him. Instead He accommodates even those who do not accommodate Him, encouraging a general spirit of sacrifice: give up the fruits of all work and be self-situated `10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Giving up the fruits of all work — cultivating a general spirit of sacrifice `10:00`.
- _Ātmavān_ — "possessor of the self," as in 2.45, where Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to go beyond the three modes and be free from gain and loss by holding on to the _ātmā_ `12:00`.
  - The suffix means "possessor," as in "possessor of wealth" or _bhagavān_, "possessor of fortune" `12:00`.

#### How can one possess oneself? [12:00 – 16:00]

- Normally the possessor and the possession are different — "I possess this computer."
- If I am the _ātmā_, how can I be both possessor and possessed? The teacher says this points to a link at the level of consciousness.
- A person with Alzheimer's may own a house without knowing it; similarly, a forgotten secret bank account is as good as not possessed.
- At the level of the soul we are all like princes — beloved children of Kṛṣṇa, and so fabulously wealthy by connection with the supremely wealthy person.
- We feel bereft, deprived and needy because we have forgotten that connection. To become _ātmavān_ is to become aware of ourselves as the _ātmā_ and of the irreplaceable wealth available at that level.
- What makes us not _ātmavān_ is material infatuation: the more one is obsessed with material things, the more oblivious one becomes of the spiritual reality.

#### Finding ourselves by losing ourselves [16:00 – 20:00]

- By starting to sacrifice, one gradually develops a spirit of selfless sacrifice, which gradually makes one self-aware.
- The only way we can find ourselves is by losing ourselves in something greater than ourselves.
- When "I" is the centre of consciousness, it becomes like a black hole sucking us in:
  - comparing and seeing others as better → inferiority complex and misery;
  - seeing ourselves as better → superiority complex, pride and arrogance.
- When we pour ourselves into a greater cause, self-obsession breaks, and the inspiration brings out our latent divinity, spiritual potential, skills and grit. The best such cause is loving and serving Kṛṣṇa.
- A devotee may think "I am not the body" when temptations come, but the primary focus is: "I am Kṛṣṇa's servant — how can I serve Him and share His wonderful message?" `20:00`

#### Self-consciousness is not self-realisation [20:00 – 22:00]

- Someone who can chat for three hours informally may struggle to speak three minutes on a podium, because in the public eye we become self-conscious.
- Self-consciousness is about my dress, looks, facial expression, smile — how others perceive us — and never leads to self-realisation.
- A devotee stops worrying about how the world perceives him and engages in activities that purify him and please Kṛṣṇa; by Kṛṣṇa's grace spiritual awareness awakens.
- We do not need to be self-conscious; to become self-realised we must be Kṛṣṇa conscious.

#### Sacrifice for a good cause [22:00 – 26:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport: one may be unable even to sympathise with Kṛṣṇa consciousness because of social, family or religious considerations or other impediments; such a person is advised to sacrifice the accumulated results of his activities to some good cause.
- The point is to cultivate the ethos of selflessness and the value of sacrifice.
- The teacher recalls an earlier class on humanitarian work and social service: the concern for others seen in social-welfare work is a significant step in the evolution of consciousness that will gradually bring one to Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- If one's family is very anti-devotional, one need not give up everything: keep practising discreetly as far as possible, and develop the spirit of sacrifice and selflessness at the heart of Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

#### Engaging one's propensities [24:00 – 28:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes another verse: by worshipping, through one's work, the Lord from whom all beings emanate and by whom everything is pervaded, one attains perfection.
- "Propensities" here are not sinful propensities but those of one's _varṇa_ situation — a _brāhmaṇa_ propensity, a _śūdra_ propensity.
- These propensities sit in the subtle body; the soul is spiritual. I cannot wish them away, but I can accommodate and elevate them by engaging them in Kṛṣṇa's service with a spiritual understanding.

#### Seeing the good in others [28:00 – 30:00]

- Jīva Gosvāmī teaches that one should not accept a spiritual master on ecclesiastical considerations — e.g. because someone is the Gosvāmī of one's caste. "Ecclesiastical" comes from church hierarchy; position in a hierarchy does not by itself make one spiritually advanced.
- So someone with no external spiritual affiliation who has selflessness and concern for others deserves appreciation.
- We could list what such a person lacks — _ātma-jñāna_, _bhakti_, regulative principles — but we can also see what he has.
- A saying the teacher cites: take gold even from a filthy place.
- This was Prabhupāda's vision: the hippies were unclean, unregulated, unemployed, rebellious and undisciplined, yet he saw a spark of spirituality in them and helped it grow.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa accommodates even those who cannot yet work for Him: by giving the fruits of work to a good cause one develops selflessness, finds oneself by losing oneself in something greater, and gradually becomes self-situated — so we too should see and nurture the spark of good in others. [10:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.12 [30:00 – 62:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.12 -->

**Context.** The teacher calls this the single most complicated verse of the Gītā, with different ācāryas giving different commentaries. The verse ranks practice (_abhyāsa_), knowledge, meditation and renunciation of the fruits of action, ending in peace `30:00 – 32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Abhyāsa_ — practice; a non-specific word, which can be the practice of _karma-yoga_, _jñāna-yoga_, _dhyāna-yoga_ or _bhakti-yoga_ (as in Kṛṣṇa's well-known reply to Arjuna's question on the restless mind: by practice and detachment it can be controlled) `38:00 – 40:00`.
- _Jñāna_ — knowledge, contemplative or philosophical analysis `32:00 – 34:00`.
- _Dhyāna_ — meditation, generally on the Supersoul `34:00`.
- Renunciation of the fruits of action — the last term in the verse `32:00 – 36:00`.
- _Śānti_ — peace `42:00`, `58:00`.
- The two comparative words in the verse — "better" and "superior" `42:00`, `56:00`.

#### The problem: an inverted hierarchy [32:00 – 36:00]

- The Gītā's usual hierarchy:
  1. _Karma-yoga_ — detaching from matter without a clear conception of spirit.
  2. _Jñāna-yoga_ — contemplative analysis, seeking _brahman_.
  3. _Dhyāna-yoga_ — meditation, focusing on the _paramātmā_.
  4. _Bhakti_ — focusing on _bhagavān_ (with its own stages up to pure, spontaneous devotion).
- 12.12 appears to turn this upside down: _abhyāsa_ (the _bhakti_ practice of 12.9) is lowest, then knowledge, then meditation, and renunciation of the fruits of work — normally the _karma-yoga_ level — is highest.
- The teacher asks students first to grasp the problem, then to appreciate how brilliantly the ācāryas resolve it.

#### Prabhupāda: changing the referent [36:00 – 42:00]

- The ambiguous antecedent: if a class begins "he said he is very angry with everyone," no one knows who "he" is; even mid-conversation a pronoun can point to someone else in the speaker's mind.
- Prabhupāda explains that 12.12 refers back not to 12.11 but to 12.10.
- Words are multivalent; their meaning must agree with the book's overall message, not just the dictionary. So:
  - _abhyāsa_ here refers to the practice of _niṣkāma karma-yoga_, not the _bhakti_ practice of 12.9;
  - the renunciation of fruits refers to the work offered to Kṛṣṇa in 12.10, i.e. _bhakti_.
- The hierarchy then makes sense: knowledge is better than that practice; meditation better than knowledge; work offered to Kṛṣṇa better than meditation; and from it comes supreme peace, as Kṛṣṇa also says elsewhere `42:00`.
- Prabhupāda does not state this very explicitly; one must study his purport repeatedly. A devotee's book, _Surrender Unto Me_, elaborates on it `42:00`.

#### Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa: accessibility, not superiority [42:00 – 46:00]

- The comparative words do not necessarily mean "higher in the hierarchy" but "easier to perform for the unqualified" — more accessible.
- Kṛṣṇa is accommodating different levels of people, so "better" is from the seeker's point of view, not the Absolute's.
- Read this way, the verse gels with the Gītā's hierarchy:
  - _Karma-yoga_ is most accessible — for one who knows nothing of _brahman_, _paramātmā_ or _bhagavān_, only that this world entangles.
  - Then _dhyāna_: in Chapters 2–5 Kṛṣṇa speaks of _karma-yoga_ and then of _dhyāna-yoga_ rather than _jñāna-yoga_ directly.
  - Then _jñāna_ — realised self-knowledge.
  - Then the practice of remembering Kṛṣṇa, which is very steady after _jñāna_; cf. 7.16–7.18, where among four kinds of people who approach Him, the _jñānī_ is called best.
- Why is _karma-yoga_ "most accessible" when _bhakti_ is usually called most accessible? _Bhakti_ becomes accessible by mercy, and mercy is not available to everyone all the time; when it is not, _karma-yoga_ is the best way forward `46:00`.

#### Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura: five levels of bhakti in 12.8–12.11 [46:00 – 54:00]

- Prabhupāda puts all of 12.8 on one level — one's whole inner life offered to Kṛṣṇa — a perfectly valid explanation. Viśvanātha separates mind and intelligence into two levels.
- On the spiritual path it is easier to be intellectually convinced than to be mentally purified: I may understand that sense gratification leads to misery yet still desire it. Once the mind accepts it, one is more or less realised — a very steady platform `48:00`.
- Remembrance versus contemplation `48:00 – 50:00`:
  - Spontaneous recollection — a mother seeing baby paraphernalia at a friend's house immediately thinks of her baby; Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, seeing any river, would think of the Yamunā.
  - Conscious contemplation — like solving a mathematics problem for an exam: not out of love, but because one understands one must think it through.
- The five levels:

  | Level | Verse | Type | Description |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 1 | 12.8 (mind) | Internal | _Smaraṇātmika_ — the mind spontaneously attracted to Kṛṣṇa, lost in remembrance |
  | 2 | 12.8 (intelligence) | Internal | _Mananātmika_ — consciously fixing the mind on Kṛṣṇa through intellectual contemplation |
  | 3 | 12.9 | Internal | _Abhyāsa_ — striving to fix the wandering mind on Kṛṣṇa |
  | 4 | 12.10 | External | Working for Kṛṣṇa — serving with the external senses, e.g. preparing flowers, cooking, cleaning the temple, even if the mind wanders |
  | 5 | 12.11 | External | Doing whatever work circumstances require and offering its fruits to Kṛṣṇa |

- On 12.11: since the sections before and after are about _bhakti-yoga_, this section is too; even without the word "Me," offering to Kṛṣṇa is understood `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### Viśvanātha's resolution of 12.12 [54:00 – 58:00]

- _Jñāna_ is the intellectual faculty → _mananātmika_; _dhyāna_ involves the mind → _smaraṇātmika_.
- Better than _abhyāsa_ (struggling to fix the mind) is _jñāna_ (the intelligence convinced, so the mind is fixed); better still is _dhyāna_ (the mind spontaneously attracted and remembering Kṛṣṇa).
- The third and fourth lines contain no comparative word, so Viśvanātha reads them not as a comparison but as a causal chain: from meditation comes renunciation, and from renunciation comes peace.
- When the mind spontaneously meditates on Kṛṣṇa, one becomes detached from everything else and has no other desire; renunciation naturally follows, and with it peace — a person is peaceful when he has no independent desire apart from serving Kṛṣṇa `58:00`.

#### Head and heart in the ācāryas' commentaries [58:00 – 62:00]

- Some devotees have a romantic idea that studying the previous ācāryas means discovering esoteric pastimes that make the hair stand on end, or new stories to impress others.
- Pastimes are part of our legacy, but the ācāryas also offered their heads to Kṛṣṇa — using all their intelligence in serious philosophical study. Studying them is hard work.
- The Gītā is clear in its conclusions but has technical complexities; there is abundant intellectual nectar within devotional service itself.
- The doctor analogy: the doctor simply prescribes — chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, practise devotional service, become purified and eventually liberated — but behind that prescription lies complex medical science.
- Prabhupāda gives an "emergency-mode" presentation that sticks to fundamentals; those overwhelmed by technicalities can relish the simplicity and potency of his process, while those who want depth have ample room within devotional service `60:00 – 62:00`.
- The teacher notes he has kept to these primary commentators and not gone into others `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
12.12 seems to invert the Gītā's hierarchy, but the ācāryas resolve it — Prabhupāda by its referent, Baladeva by accessibility, Viśvanātha by levels of bhakti ending in a chain from meditation to renunciation to peace; the conclusion stays simple even when the technicalities are deep. [32:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
