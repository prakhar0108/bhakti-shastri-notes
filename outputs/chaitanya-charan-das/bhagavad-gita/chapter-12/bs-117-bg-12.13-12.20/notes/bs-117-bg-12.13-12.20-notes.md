## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 12.13 – 12.14 [00:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.13 -->

<!-- vedabase:12.14 -->

**Context.** 12.13 and 12.14 are given as a single verse. Several verses in this section end with the same refrain — such a devotee is dear to Me — like a chorus `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānām_ — not envious towards any living being `12:00 – 16:00`.
- _Maitraḥ karuṇaḥ_ — a compassionate friend `14:00`, `16:00`.
- _Nirmama_ — free from the possessive mentality `16:00 – 18:00`.
- _Nirahaṅkāra_ — free from false ego, not letting bodily designations become one's self-definition `16:00`.
- _Sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ_ — equipoised in happiness and distress `18:00 – 20:00`.
- _Kṣamī_ — forgiving: if someone offends a devotee, it is no big deal `20:00`.
- _Santuṣṭaḥ satataṁ yogī_ — always satisfied, delighting in the inner connection with Kṛṣṇa; a _yogī_ strives for connection, and a devotee strives for it through _bhakti-yoga_ `20:00 – 22:00`.
- _Yatātmā_, _dṛḍha-niścayaḥ_ — self-controlled and firmly determined `22:00`, `24:00`.
- Mind and intelligence offered to Kṛṣṇa — those who strive this way are topmost and dear to Him `24:00`.

#### Love and trust: why Kṛṣṇa has special devotees [02:00 – 04:00]

- Kṛṣṇa earlier said (Chapter 9) that He values even the sinful: even one who is terribly sinful but devoted should be considered saintly, and Arjuna should declare that His devotee never perishes.
- That is an expression of love — but love has many levels.
- A mother loves her baby but does not trust her with a knife; she takes it away because the child cannot use it properly. When the girl grows into a responsible, intelligent daughter, she can use the knife to cook excellently — now the mother has love _and_ trust.
- Kṛṣṇa loves all, even the sinful; He is not partial but reciprocal. Devotees who reciprocate more attract more of His attention and get a greater place in His heart by their devotion `04:00`.

#### Devotional service is not a whitewash [04:00 – 08:00]

- To whitewash is to make someone look completely blameless — "no faults at all."
- We should not let devotional service become our whitewash, assuming we are faultless simply because we are devotees.
- _Bhakti_ does have the potency to truly whitewash us — not just externally clean, but internally pure in our desires — but that is a process, and we must let it act on us.
- We are all works in progress. Without Kṛṣṇa's connection, progress may take many lifetimes, with regress in between — "works in regress."
- The dough analogy: when _purīs_ are still flour, or dough being rolled, no one's mouth waters; it is still a process.
- Kṛṣṇa works on us, and to the extent we cooperate, we progress faster towards perfection; yet it is we who must take responsibility for progressing — nobody else can `06:00 – 08:00`.

#### Do the demigods really have good qualities? [08:00 – 12:00]

- The teacher cites a _Bhāgavatam_ verse explained by Prabhupāda: all the good qualities of the demigods manifest in a devotee, while a non-devotee cannot keep good qualities, being on the mental platform and dragged away by the uncontrolled mind.
- An objection: Indra tried to seduce Ahalyā, the wife of Gautama Ṛṣi, and Brahmā ran after his daughter. Do the demigods really have good qualities?
- The teacher's answer: those are very rare misdeeds. They are told because they are so extraordinary and improbable:
  - A three-month-old baby crying is normal and never makes the news.
  - A thirty-year-old man crying because he lost a bet is unusual.
- Demigods are normally virtuous and religious; their misdeeds illustrate how great the power of illusion is, that it can overcome even such personalities.

#### Why bhakti is the sustainable foundation [10:00 – 14:00]

- That _Bhāgavatam_ section glorifies the process of _bhakti_: its emphasis is that _bhakti_ engenders good qualities.
- A non-devotee may temporarily have positive qualities, but they lack a sustainable foundation: they rest on worldly concepts — "this is good for me, good for others, how I should behave." When circumstances change, the idea of morality changes too.
- _Bhakti_ is the means for sustainable development of good qualities, because it helps us understand that, as parts of Kṛṣṇa, behaving virtuously is our nature and the best way to advance and do good to ourselves and others.
- When we act virtuously, we allow Kṛṣṇa to act through us; when we do not, we block Him, and we and others suffer — we make a mess of our lives.
- So while _bhakti_ helps develop qualities, we must not imagine it will just whitewash us; within devotional service we strive to cultivate these qualities as much as possible `12:00`.

#### Non-envious and positively friendly [14:00 – 16:00]

- Envy arises when someone has a better car, house or job.
- A devotee understands that no asset in this world brings true happiness: our happiness depends not on what we have but on what we do with what we have.
  - Wealth used for drinking poisons one's body and ruins one's future.
  - Wealth used constructively can be a source of happiness; _bhakti_ engages it in Kṛṣṇa's service, giving a deeper connection with Kṛṣṇa — the basis of lasting happiness.
- "That person has a better car" — but a car is just a tool for going from one place to another, not for making me happy.
- Being non-envious is only non-negative; the devotee's vision goes further, to the positive: friendship and compassion, because everyone is part of the one family of the Supreme Lord `16:00`.

#### Free from possessiveness and false ego [16:00 – 18:00]

- Kṛṣṇa spoke of these two words earlier, in 2.71, while discussing giving up material desires.
- Giving up the possessive mindset, and the ego of "I am this body," so that bodily designations do not become our self-definition, is the process of liberation from illusion.
- A devotee also has a possessive tendency, but understands that the ultimate possession is Kṛṣṇa — as in a song of self-surrender (_ātma-nivedana_) by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura: "Lord, I am Yours and You are mine; what other wealth do I want?"

#### The coin and the five crore [18:00 – 20:00]

- If a one-rupee coin is all I have, I get agitated when it falls or is stolen and excited when someone gives me another.
- If I hold five crore rupees in the other hand, losing the coin hardly matters — I might even toss it to a beggar.
- The coin is our material pleasures, possessions and position; the five crore is our spiritual wealth — our devotional connection with Kṛṣṇa.
- To the extent the devotional connection is weak, material ups and downs shake us: success makes us jubilant, failure dispirited. To the extent our vision is fixed on Kṛṣṇa and we regularly experience the fulfilment of that connection, these dualities do not affect us `20:00`.

#### Qualities are earned, not inherited [22:00 – 26:00]

- We should not imagine these qualities come easily. Seeing a very advanced devotee — humble, tolerant, gentle-mannered — we may think, "That level is beyond me; let me stay where I am," as if their qualities were congenital assets.
- Even material skills are not congenital: a phenomenal tennis or cricket player must practise; a leg-spin bowler was not born out of his mother's womb spinning a ball. Even talent requires work to express.
- "They did so much _bhakti_ in previous lives" — perhaps, but they still had to struggle. And perhaps in my previous life I said the same thing and lost my chance; if I repeat it, I will say it again next life `24:00`.
- Apart from a few exceptional, eternally connected devotees from the spiritual world, every advanced devotee became advanced by practice and by struggling against _anarthas_; we should not expect a struggle-free path.
- Developing good qualities requires hard work, and a devotee must be ready for it. Those who work this way and offer mind and intelligence to Kṛṣṇa are topmost and dear to Him `24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa loves all but reciprocates more with those who reciprocate; devotional service is not a whitewash but the sustainable way to cultivate real virtues — non-envy, compassion, freedom from possessiveness, equanimity and determination — which must be earned through practice and struggle, as even talent must. [02:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.15 [26:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.15 -->

**Context.** The devotee is one who does not disturb anyone and is not disturbed by anyone, free from the agitating emotions `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Harṣa_ — happiness, excitement `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Amarṣa_ — distress, dejection `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Bhaya_ — fear `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Udvega_ — anxiety `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Mukta_ — free from these agitating forces `28:00`.

#### The gamut of emotions [26:00 – 28:00]

- Excitement, dejection, fear and anxiety represent the whole range of emotions the mind can go through.
- A devotee does not allow the mind to come between himself and Kṛṣṇa.

#### "But devotees do disturb people" [28:00 – 30:00]

- One may wonder: devotees' lifestyle differs from others', and outreach sometimes agitates people. Does that mean we are not doing outreach properly?
- Not exactly; we need both proper intention and proper execution, with the knowledge to check whether the intention is right.
- The competent surgeon: superficially he cuts a patient's stomach and may cause more pain than before the surgery, but the goal of the surgical knife is not to kill but to heal; some cutting is part of healing `28:00 – 30:00`.
- A devotee has no intention of disturbing anyone; he simply wants to practise devotional service and help others do so.

#### An instrument of the Supreme Doctor [30:00 – 32:00]

- That is best done by staying purely connected with Kṛṣṇa and becoming a puppet in His hands — as Prabhupāda prayed to the Lord: like a wooden puppet, make me dance.
- A compounder or nurse may not know every technical detail of a case, but by learning the precautions from the chief doctor, he or she can care for the patient much better.
- Likewise the devotee acts as an assistant of Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Doctor, praying, "My dear Lord, please use me as Your instrument" — and so is not considered anyone's enemy.

#### Not disturbed by others [32:00 – 34:00]

- A devotee understands that everything he possesses belongs to Kṛṣṇa; other people, and their assets, belong to Kṛṣṇa too.
- People act according to the three modes — like puppets of the modes — so the devotee does not let himself get disturbed.
- This does not mean passivity: the teacher recalls that Prabhupāda, faced with problems such as construction delays, wrote strong letters highlighting the specifics of what was right and wrong (see flags).
- As we let Kṛṣṇa use us and experience how He does, we feel more inspired and dedicated; devotional service engenders more qualities and frees us more from the world's agitation.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A devotee neither disturbs nor is disturbed: like a surgeon he may cause temporary discomfort only to heal, acting as an instrument of the Supreme Doctor, and he sees others as acting under the modes, so excitement, dejection, fear and anxiety do not come between him and Kṛṣṇa. [26:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.16 [34:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.16 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa lists further qualities of a devotee; the teacher notes some were discussed earlier in the course, so he does not dwell on all of them `36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Anapekṣa_ — not dependent on, not taking sides `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Śuci_ — clean, externally and internally; internal cleanliness comes by cultivating good thoughts `36:00`.
- _Dakṣa_ — expert: excelling in the branch of service allotted to one `36:00`.
- _Sarvārambha-parityāgī_ — renouncing all selfish endeavours `36:00`.
- _Gata-vyatha_ — carefree, without unnecessary cares `38:00`.

#### No "theo-politics" [34:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda often said devotees need not take part in the "theo-politics" that takes place in metropolitan countries — a mixture of theology and politics.
- In it people take over others' projects for their own pursuit of power and position, jockeying for power while claiming to act in the name of God.
- A devotee takes no such sides and seeks no political alignment; he simply wants to serve Kṛṣṇa. Prabhupāda's purport likewise says a devotee never takes the side of any particular party `38:00`.

#### Expertise in service [36:00]

- Prabhupāda was expert in many things; in the early days his disciples said the Swami's knowledge of cooking recipes was as vast as his knowledge of Sanskrit verses.
- Whatever a devotee does is ultimately for everyone's good, and he tries to be expert in it.
- This does not mean a devotee will be expert in every material skill. The ultimate expertise is a sincere desire to serve Kṛṣṇa; Kṛṣṇa reciprocates and gives guidance that helps the devotee find out what He wants done.

#### From selfishness to supreme selflessness [36:00]

- Spiritual advancement is a journey from selfishness to supreme selflessness — absorption in service to Kṛṣṇa.
- Everyone has to take this journey, and renouncing selfish endeavours contributes to it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The devotee stays out of power politics, keeps clean inside and out, and excels in his allotted service — the ultimate expertise being a sincere desire to serve, which Kṛṣṇa answers — as he journeys from selfishness to supreme selflessness. [34:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.17 [38:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.17 -->

**Context.** The devotee neither rejoices nor hates, neither laments nor desires, and renounces both auspicious and inauspicious things `38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Renouncing the auspicious and the inauspicious — rising above the mental conceptions of good and bad `38:00`.

#### Why both good and bad are renounced [38:00]

- Everything in this world is temporary, so ultimately nothing here is auspicious.
- The auspicious and the inauspicious are both undesirable; the difference is only one of degree.
- What we consider desirable now will later become undesirable; what we call desirable is just higher up on the same continuum of undesirability.
- Kṛṣṇa enables the devotee to stay transcendental to both conceptions — "this is good, this is bad" — so that mental energy is not drained by them.

#### Only service matters [40:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that nothing is important except the discharge of devotional service to Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Because everything material is temporary, worldly "good" and "bad" sit on one continuum of undesirability; the devotee rises above both and holds only service to Kṛṣṇa as important. [38:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.18 – 12.19 [40:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.18 -->

<!-- vedabase:12.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the devotee who is equal towards enemy and friend, honour and dishonour, cold and heat, happiness and distress, praise and blame; satisfied with anything; without a fixed residence; fixed in mind; and full of devotion — such a person is dear to Him `40:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Saṅga-vivarjita_ — _saṅga_ is attachment, _vivarjita_ is free from: free from attachment `42:00`.
- _Santuṣṭo yena kenacit_ — satisfied in whatever situation `42:00`.
- _Aniketa_ — without a house (_niketana_ means house) `42:00`.
- _Sthira-mati_ — fixed in mind `42:00`.
- _Bhaktimān_ — one who has attraction for Kṛṣṇa `42:00 – 44:00`.

#### Equanimity in changing times [40:00 – 42:00]

- Kṛṣṇa speaks of most of these qualities in terms of an equipoised attitude.
- The challenge is applying a timeless spiritual culture practically in current times.
- Recognising that spiritual happiness is eternal, the devotee resolves: whatever changes happen externally at the material level, let them not affect my spiritual practice and advancement; let me focus on the steady practice of devotional service.
- Honour and dishonour are part of day-to-day conduct `42:00`.

#### Satisfied, because situated in Kṛṣṇa [42:00]

- The devotee is satisfied in whatever situation, because he is situated not in the situation but in his connection with Kṛṣṇa — the basis of supreme fulfilment.

#### The inner home [42:00]

- A devotee's body may have no fixed residence — a travelling preacher, for example, may have no house.
- But by virtue of being a devotee he has an inner home: his connection with Kṛṣṇa, which remains.
- Such a person, with attraction to Kṛṣṇa, is most dear to Him `42:00 – 44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Equanimity in honour and dishonour, praise and blame, comes from being situated not in circumstances but in one's connection with Kṛṣṇa — an inner home that remains even without a fixed residence. [40:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.20 [44:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.20 -->

**Context.** The last verse of the chapter sums up: those who follow this nectar of religion with faith, making Kṛṣṇa the supreme goal, are very especially dear to Him `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dharmāmṛta_ — the nectar of religion `44:00`.
- _Śraddhā_ — faith `44:00 – 48:00`.
- _Atīva_ — very especially, much greater; i.e. most dear `44:00`.

#### Faith at the beginning and end [44:00 – 46:00]

- In 12.2, answering Arjuna's question, Kṛṣṇa spoke of those endowed with transcendental faith.
- Now, at the end of the chapter, He returns to faith — a symmetry between the first and last verses of the discussion.

#### What makes a devotee dear? [46:00 – 48:00]

- Many things might seem to: speaking expertly, catchy lines, personal charisma.
- But faith is the greatest quality: all the other qualities endear one to Kṛṣṇa, but faith endears one most — accepting the principle of devotional service and practising it with faith.
- Beyond that, the path is not a whitewash: one must practise spiritual life sincerely and seriously, focusing on the fundamental point of practice and service.

#### Everything else is taken care of [48:00 – 50:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport: one need not bother about how to keep body and soul together, because by the Lord's grace everything is carried out automatically.
- Some companies or government institutions provide perks — accommodation, transport, medical insurance. Working there, if I just do my work properly, my other needs are taken care of.
- If I board a plane after paying, I do not check the fuel meter; I only make sure I have a proper ticket with a respected airline.
- Similarly, if a devotee just serves Kṛṣṇa, other things are taken care of, and he can fix himself wholeheartedly on Kṛṣṇa.
- Even if one lacks good qualities or has many bad ones, it does not matter: we can practise devotional service, aspire for elevated qualities, develop them by steady practice and become dear to Kṛṣṇa `50:00`.

#### Review of the bhakti section, Chapters 7–12 [50:00 – 56:00]

- **Symmetry:** the _bhakti_ section began at 7.1 and ends in Chapter 12 with "such a devotee is very dear to Me." At the end of Chapter 6 the most elevated _yogī_ was the one who fixes the mind on Kṛṣṇa; Chapters 7–12 present _bhakti-yoga_ as the way to that perfection, and from Chapter 13 Kṛṣṇa will discuss _jñāna-yoga_ and how it culminates in _bhakti-yoga_ `50:00 – 52:00`.
- **Chapter 7** — knowledge of the Absolute: how Kṛṣṇa pervades the material world, and how people respond — some surrender directly, some indirectly, some deride Him, some approach inquisitively. It gives the intellectual foundation for a devotional life: His immanence, His transcendence and the superiority of _bhakti-yoga_ `52:00`.
- **Chapter 8** — compares _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ with _bhakti-yoga_ and establishes _bhakti_ as preferable because it is easier; a devotee need not worry whether he dies at the right time — the Lord ensures everything works out `52:00`.
- **Chapter 9** — the most confidential knowledge of pure devotional service: various kinds of people (_māyāvādīs_, exclusive devotees, three kinds of performers of the sacrifice of knowledge); how devotional service elevates one despite inherent faults and incidental falls; how easy it is to perform; and its eternal results compared with demigod worship `52:00 – 54:00`.
- **Chapter 10** — _vibhūti-yoga_: Kṛṣṇa is rare to know but can be known through _bhakti-yoga_, which attracts His mercy; the _catuḥ-ślokī_ — four verses of the Gītā — come here; Arjuna speaks for the first time not in a questioning mood but in an acknowledging mood; Kṛṣṇa describes how He pervades nature to help materially minded people think of Him `54:00`.
- **Chapter 11** — Kṛṣṇa promises to show Arjuna whatever he wants to see, now or in future, and reveals the universal form; at Arjuna's request He withdraws it, shows the four-armed form and returns to His original form; _bhakti_ is the best way to see Him `54:00`.
- **Chapter 12** — against the common conception that _bhakti_ leads to _jñāna_, Kṛṣṇa says practising _bhakti_ is far better; worship of the personal is preferable to the impersonal, which is much more difficult; out of extraordinary compassion He offers multiple levels of _bhakti_ (12.8–12.12) and then the qualities that endear one to Him `54:00 – 56:00`.
- Chapters 10, 11, 12 and also 13 each begin with a question from Arjuna; Chapter 13 opens the discussion of _jñāna-yoga_ and how _jñāna_ ultimately leads to _bhakti_, for a future class `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Of all the qualities, faith endears one to Kṛṣṇa most; practising devotional service sincerely with faith, one can leave the rest to Him — like a passenger who need not check the fuel meter — and develop every other quality along the way. [44:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>
