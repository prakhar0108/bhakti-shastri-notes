## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 12.1 [04:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.1 -->

**Context.** Arjuna opens the chapter by asking which of two kinds of transcendentalists is most perfect in _yoga_. The teacher stresses that 12.1 is not linearly connected with 11.55; it explores a discussion begun at 9.15, with Kṛṣṇa inspiring Arjuna from within to ask the relevant questions `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tamāḥ_ — a superlative: "best"; Arjuna asks who are the most perfect in _yoga_ `06:00`.
- Unmanifested — that which is not perceivable; as an object of worship it refers to the impersonal aspect of the Absolute Truth `06:00`.
- Impersonal form — the teacher notes this is a paradox, since form is normally personal; more precisely, it is the impersonal _aspect_ `00:00`.

#### Flow of Chapter 12 [04:00]

1. 12.1–12.7 — worship of the personal form is superior to worship of the impersonal _brahman_.
2. 12.8–12.12 — the progressive stages of _bhakti_.
3. 12.13–12.20 — qualities that endear one to Kṛṣṇa, qualities He likes in His devotees.

#### What Arjuna is really asking [06:00 – 08:00]

- Both groups are on the transcendental path; Arjuna grants that both seem good but asks who is best.
- The first group is clearly devotional: the words used and Arjuna's "You" refer to the devotees.
- The second group worships the unmanifested — the impersonal aspect.
- More precisely: which worshippers are better situated, more intimately united with Kṛṣṇa — those of the impersonal aspect or those of the personal aspect?

#### Another reading of the question [26:00 – 32:00]

- The teacher presents an alternative explanation from a commentator (see flags): the attributes of the unmanifested listed in 12.3 are strikingly similar to the attributes of the _ātmā_ given in Chapter 2 (around 2.23–2.27).
- On this reading Arjuna asks: between one who fixes the mind on the _ātmā_ ("I am not the body, I am the soul") to become detached from matter, and one who fixes the mind on Kṛṣṇa, who is better?
- This fits the structure of the Gītā:
  - In Chapters 1–6 the word "Me" (Kṛṣṇa referring to Himself) is relatively infrequent — the teacher lists a few places such as 2.61, 3.38, 4.1–4.15, 4.35, 5.29, 6.14–6.15, 6.30–6.31 and 6.47.
  - In Chapters 7–12 it comes almost every alternate verse.
  - Chapters 2–6 focus on _ātma-jñāna_ — detaching from the body, since the seeker may not yet know that the Absolute Truth is a person; 6.47 then declares the topmost _yogī_ to be one who fixes the mind on Kṛṣṇa, and from 7.1 the focus shifts to fixing the mind on Him.
- So the question becomes: of the path of _ātma-jñāna_ (Chapters 2–6) and the path of contemplating the Lord (Chapters 7–12), which is better? Either way, Kṛṣṇa answers that those who fix their mind on Him are better situated `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's question is not idle comparison: having seen the universal form, he asks which worshippers are most intimately united with Kṛṣṇa — those of His personal form or those of the unmanifested — and the rest of the section answers that precisely. [06:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.2 [08:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.2 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa answers by establishing the personal aspect so explicitly that no ambiguity is left `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- "Fixing the mind on Me" / "Me" — Kṛṣṇa repeats the first-person words, picking up Arjuna's own "You" `08:00 – 10:00`.
- Transcendental faith — faith that persists even in the transcendental phase `10:00`.

#### Words that remove ambiguity [08:00 – 10:00]

- When an answer is not clearly tied to the question's words, many interpretations become possible.
- Here Kṛṣṇa reuses the words of Arjuna's first line and says "Me" and "on Me," making the referent unmistakable.
- Answer: those who fix their mind on Kṛṣṇa and worship Him constantly with transcendental faith are the most intimately united with Him.

#### No "going beyond" the form [10:00 – 12:00]

- The teacher addresses the idea that one worships a form only until reaching transcendence and then goes beyond the form to the formless.
- The Gītā does not support this: "transcendental faith" means that even after coming to transcendence one keeps fixing the mind on Kṛṣṇa.
- It is not that we transcend Kṛṣṇa at some point and go to the impersonal.

#### "My personal form" [12:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation says "fix their minds on My personal form." There is no Sanskrit word for "personal form" in the verse, the teacher concedes.
- Yet the context makes it implicit: it is not an impersonal light speaking, but Kṛṣṇa in His personal form standing before Arjuna and saying "Me."
- Had Kṛṣṇa meant "worship some light beyond Me; I am only its temporary manifestation," He would have said so.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa answers with crystal-clear words: those who fix the mind on Him — the person speaking — with transcendental faith are most intimately united with Him; there is no stage at which devotion to His form is left behind. [08:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.5 [16:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.5 -->

**Context.** Two words in this verse are clearly negative — trouble and difficulty — describing the path of those attached to the unmanifested `16:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kleśa_ — trouble, misery `18:00`.
- Attached to the unmanifested — consciousness attracted to the impersonal aspect, making it the goal of life `18:00`.
- Embodied — those in bodies, for whom such progress is especially troublesome `18:00`.

#### Why it is troublesome: thinking of the formless [18:00]

- It is difficult to concentrate on that which has no form.
- The teacher recalls an earlier picture: material form is on the negative axis, spiritual form on the positive axis, and impersonal _brahman_ is like the zero point.
- Giving up attraction to material forms and fixing the mind on "nothing" — the attributeless, activity-less _brahman_ — is hard for everyone, and especially for the embodied, who are used to thinking in forms.

#### The vacuum analogy [20:00]

- It is extremely difficult to create a vacuum and extremely easy to destroy one.
- Emptying a glass of water still leaves air; removing the air is laborious, and the moment any opening appears, air rushes back.
- Meditating on the impersonal is like creating a vacuum in consciousness: all material forms are rejected as entangling, but there is no positive spiritual conception of form, activities or attributes to replace them — so what is there to think about?

#### Not a statement about the Ultimate Reality [20:00 – 24:00]

- An impersonalist might argue: "It is only hard while we are embodied; beyond the body the impersonal is the ultimate reality."
- The teacher answers that such a reading is possible if one wants it, but the Gītā does not support it:
  - Kṛṣṇa nowhere says _bhakti_ is only for the less intelligent.
  - He nowhere says that the more intelligent go beyond the personal form to something higher.
  - He nowhere says the trouble ends once there is no body.
- 12.5 only says that, being embodied and habituated to form, we find the formless hard to think of. It is both difficult _and_ not the ultimate truth — the latter shown by the many verses where Kṛṣṇa says those who think the impersonal is higher than the personal are mistaken and misled `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### Does goodness make it easier? [24:00 – 28:00]

- In goodness, one is less obsessed with material forms and more contemplative, so one can think more about certain features of the Absolute Truth.
- A saying of three kinds of people, which the teacher loosely correlates with the modes:
  - Some make things happen — goodness: they think, and with control of mind and senses they act effectively.
  - Some watch things happen — passion: they rush to make things happen, lose control, and ask "what should I do?"
  - Some wonder what happened — ignorance: caught in their own world, they do not understand.
- Goodness helps contemplation of _any_ aspect of the Absolute Truth, including the personal: a devotee in goodness can think of Kṛṣṇa much better than a devotee in passion or ignorance.
- So this is a comment on our conditioning, not on which aspect of the Absolute Truth is highest; that must be settled from the broader scriptures.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Fixing the mind on the formless is like creating a vacuum in consciousness — hard to create and harder to sustain — yet 12.5 describes only the difficulty for embodied souls, not any superiority of the impersonal aspect. [18:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.6 – 12.7 [32:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.6 -->

<!-- vedabase:12.7 -->

**Context.** After 12.2 (most intimately united), 12.3–12.4 (they also come) and 12.5 (but with difficulty), 12.6–12.7 make Kṛṣṇa's preference clear again `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Without deviation — a word that appeared earlier in 9.22; the mind steadily fixed on Kṛṣṇa `34:00`.
- _Yoga_ — here, the practice `34:00`.
- Deliverer — Kṛṣṇa becomes the swift deliverer of His devotees `36:00 – 38:00`.
- _Sulabha_ and _susukham_ — "easily" and "happily," words the teacher connects with 8.14 and 9.2 `46:00`.

#### Who is being described [34:00]

- 12.2 begins and 12.7 ends with consistent wording, so both refer to the same transcendentalist — the one who fixes the mind on Kṛṣṇa.
- Such devotees offer all their works to Him, renounce the fruits for Him, make Him the ultimate goal, meditate on Him and worship Him.

#### The impersonal is not reciprocal [36:00]

- In the impersonal aspect the Lord's personality is not manifested: it is quality-less, attributeless and in a sense emotionless, so it cannot reciprocate or extend help.
- The rope-in-the-well analogy:
  - If a rope hangs from an unconscious object, the fallen person must laboriously climb up alone.
  - If a person holds the rope, that person cares, has emotions and pulls us up — we only need to hold on.

#### "I personally intervene" [38:00 – 40:00]

- The essential point: this process is easier because Kṛṣṇa intervenes personally in favour of His devotees; here the sweetness of His love shows, and His clear preference for the devotional path.
- The familiar picture: a person drowning in the ocean, and the Lord descending on Garuḍa, extending His hand and lifting the person up.
- One commentator explains the deliverance as Kṛṣṇa personally giving the knowledge by which the devotee becomes disentangled. Other commentators do not particularly appreciate this: Kṛṣṇa does not only give knowledge but intervenes with His personal being as well (see flags).

#### Prabhupāda's purport: work, done for Kṛṣṇa [40:00 – 42:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that this is "explicitly stated": devotees are very fortunate to be delivered very soon from material existence by the Lord.
- It does not matter what kind of work one does, provided it is only for Kṛṣṇa — Arjuna's work was war, involving bloodshed.
- This is not a licence for sinful activity; the context is _varṇāśrama_ — whatever one's prescribed duties, done faithfully for the Lord with the mind fixed on Kṛṣṇa.

#### No need to wait [42:00 – 44:00]

- The devotee need not wait to become very experienced before being transferred to the spiritual sky — the Lord Himself takes him.
- The beings who guide the soul in its post-mortem journey are not required for a devotee; the Lord takes him personally.
- The desire of the Lord's heart is to take the devotee back without distraction, obstacle or delay. The Lord is to the devotee like a parent caring for a child.

#### The Mahābhārata verse [44:00 – 46:00]

- The _Mahābhārata_ is a compendium of wisdom with knowledge of _dharma_ at many levels, not all transcendental.
- Within it, the Gītā, the _Viṣṇu-sahasra-nāma_ and a further section the teacher names are considered purely devotional.
- Prabhupāda quotes a verse from it: one who takes shelter of the Supreme Lord attains the supreme destination simply by that shelter — easily and happily, which is said of no other path.

#### Informational and instructional verses [46:00 – 48:00]

- Some verses are informational — e.g. the verse that there is no truth higher than Kṛṣṇa and that all things rest on Him like pearls on a thread; we can draw a lesson, but it is not a direct instruction.
- Other verses are instructional, and these are more important.
- Likewise, Prabhupāda's purports are usually analytical, concluding that devotional service is best; occasionally he becomes directly instructional — as here: "Let everyone take shelter of the Supreme omnipotent Godhead, Kṛṣṇa; that is the highest perfection of life."
- Since the Lord promises to deliver soon, why should one not take shelter of Him immediately?

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The impersonal cannot reciprocate, but Kṛṣṇa is a person who cares: for those who offer Him their work and mind, He Himself becomes the swift deliverer — so the instruction is to take shelter of Him without delay. [36:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.8 [48:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.8 -->

**Context.** With 12.1–12.7 complete, the next section (12.8–12.12) describes the stages of _bhakti_. 12.8 gives the highest level: fix the mind and intelligence on Kṛṣṇa and you will live in Him, without doubt `48:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Mind and intelligence fixed on Kṛṣṇa — the topmost level, where the mind is naturally attached to Him `50:00`, `54:00`.
- "You will live in Me" — we live where our subtle body is `50:00`.

#### We live where our subtle body lives [50:00 – 52:00]

- We do not live where our gross body lives; we live where our subtle body is.
- An addict may sit in a comfortable home or a well-furnished office, but his mind is with the object of addiction — alcohol, drugs — and he has no peace, being dragged and pinched.
- So if our subtle body is with Kṛṣṇa, we are with Kṛṣṇa; if not, we are not.
- We bring the subtle body to Kṛṣṇa by offering our mind — the "art of Kṛṣṇa consciousness" Prabhupāda described in the Introduction: always fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa wherever one is situated.

#### A descending ladder [52:00 – 54:00]

- From the first to the sixth chapter there was a gradual development culminating in _bhakti_; in Chapters 7–12 Kṛṣṇa established _bhakti_ as the highest — better than demigod worship, _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_, _karma-yoga_, _jñāna-yoga_ and impersonal worship.
- The natural doubt: "_Bhakti_ is best, but am I qualified? Perhaps I must practise other paths first."
- Kṛṣṇa's answer: _bhakti_ can be practised at multiple levels, so we begin wherever we feel equipped and rise upward.
- Earlier chapters were like a gradual incline upward; now Kṛṣṇa lowers a ladder of devotion — a ladder of love — from the top all the way down. 12.12, the teacher notes, is a very complicated verse.

#### The rungs, from the top [54:00]

1. Offer the mind entirely to Kṛṣṇa and thereby live in Him (12.8).
2. Practise fixing the mind on Him and thereby increase the desire for Him (12.9).
3. Work for Kṛṣṇa (12.10, discussed next class).

#### Pure devotion in any place [56:00]

- 12.8 is the level of pure devotion.
- Prabhupāda was physically in America, but internally he was not "in America": we may take a pure devotee out of Vṛndāvana, but not Vṛndāvana out of a pure devotee — Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet were in his heart wherever he was.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We live where our mind lives; by fixing mind and intelligence on Kṛṣṇa we live in Him wherever the body is — the top rung of a ladder Kṛṣṇa lowers so that everyone can begin bhakti from where they stand. [50:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 12.9 [54:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:12.9 -->

**Context.** If the mind lacks natural attachment to Kṛṣṇa, one must _practise_ fixing it on Him — the second rung `54:00 – 56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Abhyāsa-yoga_ — the _yoga_ of practice: the mind wanders and one brings it back, as in _japa_ meditation `56:00`.
- _Dhanañjaya_ — Arjuna as the conqueror of wealth, now told of the supreme wealth `58:00`.
- Desire to attain Kṛṣṇa — the result of the practice `58:00`.

#### 12.8 versus 12.9 [54:00 – 56:00]

- 12.8 — the mind is directly attached to Kṛṣṇa, so it stays fixed on Him.
- 12.9 — the mind does not yet have that attachment; it goes here and there, and one brings it back again and again.

#### The mother and the restless child [58:00]

- The mind may stray, but let it not _stay_ away.
- Just as a mother gently but firmly brings her restless child back, we bring the mind back to Kṛṣṇa.

#### The supreme wealth [58:00]

- Arjuna is addressed as the conqueror of wealth, but the supreme wealth is attraction to and remembrance of Kṛṣṇa.
- No wealth of this world helps at death; this wealth takes us back to the spiritual world, where we are eternally happy.
- We have many desires but not a strong desire to attain Kṛṣṇa; getting that desire is the supreme perfection of life.
- We may wear a devotee's dress and go through a devotee's actions, yet our desire for Kṛṣṇa is not very strong — this practice is how it grows.

#### How practice grows into desire [60:00]

- At first it is not easy to fix the mind on Kṛṣṇa; we do it as a discipline.
- As we do, we taste peace and joy, while other things feel irritating, agitating and entangling.
- The desire to remember Him increases; once it is strong, we think of Him naturally and constantly — rising to the level of 12.8.
- The further rungs are left for the next class.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
If the mind is not yet attracted to Kṛṣṇa, keep bringing it back like a mother bringing back a restless child; such practice gives a taste of joy that grows into the desire to attain Him — the supreme wealth. [54:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
