## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 13.1 [02:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.1 -->

**Context.** Arjuna asks the meaning of six terms. Before going into Kṛṣṇa's answers, the teacher gives a map of the chapter, discusses why this verse is missing in some versions of the Gītā, and explains what kind of analysis the chapter undertakes `02:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kṣetra_ — the field (of activity) `04:00`.
- _Kṣetrajña_ — the knower of the field `04:00`.
- _Jñāna_ — literally, knowledge `04:00`.
- _Jñeya_ — that which is to be known `04:00`.
- _Prakṛti_ and _puruṣa_ — nature and the enjoyer; familiar Vedic terms whose exact meaning Arjuna wants to know `04:00`.
- _Sāṅkhya_ — analysis of material nature into its components, so that one can go beyond it to what lies beyond matter; the word has appeared earlier, especially in Chapters 3 and 5 `16:00`.
- Recension — a different rendition of the same manuscript arising when scribes copied scripture by hand `08:00`.

#### Map of Chapter 13 [02:00 – 06:00]

- Kṛṣṇa answers Arjuna's six questions progressively, not in the order asked:
  1. What are _kṣetra_ and _kṣetrajña_ (the third and fourth questions) — 13.2–13.7.
  2. What is _jñāna_ — 13.8–13.12.
  3. What is _jñeya_ (completing the fifth and sixth questions) — 13.13–13.19.
  4. Then Kṛṣṇa returns to the first two questions — what are _prakṛti_ and _puruṣa_, how they interact and how it all comes together.
  5. 13.27–13.35 is "like a bonus section": Kṛṣṇa gives _jñāna-cakṣu_, the eyes of knowledge, to see reality beyond how it appears at the level of material perception.

#### Copyists and recensions [06:00 – 12:00]

- In traditional culture scribes recopied scriptures by hand as manuscripts decayed — there were no photocopy machines — so small differences of reading could arise between versions.
- Example: in the first of Prahlāda Mahārāja's prayers to Lord Nṛsiṁhadeva (where he says that if demigods like Brahmā, whose words flow like a stream, could not satisfy the Lord, how can he, low-born, satisfy Him), one word differs between recensions, yet the overall meaning stays the same `08:00`.
- A traditional commentator would first address the variant readings and choose one — because it is older, more coherent, or more authorised by having come down in the tradition and been commented on `08:00 – 10:00`.
- Comparing recensions minimises the human error factor; once a commentary is preserved in a tradition, the verses it comments on are preserved with it `10:00`.
- Evidence of fidelity `10:00 – 12:00`:
  - Śrīdhara Svāmī's _Bhāgavatam_ commentary, nearly a thousand years old, comments on practically the same verses we have today.
  - Śaṅkarācārya, about 1,400 years ago, commented on the same Gītā verses we have today.

#### Why the Gītā's text is so stable [12:00 – 14:00]

- Scripture is not only text on leaves or paper; it is living wisdom in the hearts of those who have internalised it and moulded their lives by it `12:00`.
- Even secular scholars acknowledge the Gītā as one of the most stable scriptures across recensions `12:00`.
- A Kashmir Śaiva commentator tried to prove the Gītā concludes that Śiva is the supreme Absolute Truth — an untenable conclusion, since the word "Śiva" hardly appears in the Gītā — yet even his verses hardly differ `12:00 – 14:00`.
- With many copies in circulation and serious intellectual competition — even "warfare" — between schools, each having to establish itself by logic and scripture, nobody could tamper with the text without being caught `14:00`.

#### Is 13.1 authentic? [14:00 – 18:00]

- Although the Gītā is very stable, 13.1 — Arjuna's question — is absent in some recensions; the widely distributed Gita Press edition in India often omits it `14:00 – 16:00`.
- The teacher's position:
  - Even if Arjuna never asked, what Kṛṣṇa speaks does not change.
  - Without the question there is a sudden jump in topic; the question gives context and continuity, so it is tenable that Arjuna asked it.
  - Our _ācāryas_ generally include it as the start of Chapter 13, but there is no need to debate or be insistent; what matters is understanding the concepts.
- If the question were absent, the reason for the shift would be that Kṛṣṇa, having shown _bhakti_ superior to _jñāna_ and impersonal worship, now wants to clarify further terms `16:00`.

#### Why analyse matter [16:00 – 18:00]

- The variety of the material world is captivating and bewildering; we get caught in forms and appearances.
- When we see those forms are made of fundamental elements, the elements themselves are not captivating — moving air or a glass of water does not fascinate us; we simply drink water when thirsty.
- Analysing the world into its components frees us from being captivated, and lets us look for the real substance that is the basis of life and love — the soul and ultimately the Supersoul.

#### Nature, the enjoyer and consciousness [18:00 – 20:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda titles the chapter "Nature, the Enjoyer and Consciousness" `18:00`.
- Of the six terms, _prakṛti_ and _kṣetra_ are not conscious; _jñeya_, _puruṣa_ and _kṣetrajña_ are conscious `18:00 – 20:00`.
- Three entities underlie the terms — _jagat_, _jīva_ and _jagadīśa_ `20:00`:
  - _Jagat_ — material nature (_prakṛti_, _kṣetra_).
  - _Jīva_ — can be the _puruṣa_ or the _kṣetrajña_.
  - _Īśvara_ — can also be the _kṣetrajña_ and the _puruṣa_.
- In the chapter title, "nature" is material nature, "the enjoyer" is the Supreme Lord, and "consciousness" is the _jīva_ `20:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Whether or not Arjuna's question appears in a given edition, Kṛṣṇa's teaching is the same: Chapter 13 analyses matter and its interaction with consciousness so that we stop being captivated by forms and seek the soul and the Supersoul behind them. [14:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.2 [06:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.2 -->

**Context.** Pointing to Arjuna's body, Kṛṣṇa says this body is called the field, and the one who knows it is called the knower of the field — quoting "those who know" the subject, without yet saying exactly who the knower is `06:00`. The teacher returns to the verse in detail at `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kṣetra_ — a part of _prakṛti_: the area of material nature within one's jurisdiction of influence; at a preliminary level, the body `20:00`.
- _Kṣetrajña_ — the one who knows the field; the awareness "I am a man/woman, I am an Indian" is the awareness of a _kṣetrajña_ `30:00`.

#### The field as an area of influence [20:00 – 24:00]

- Material nature is all around us, but we control only some parts: "I can move my hand up and down, but I can't move your hand." `20:00`
- Even over the body our control is partial — breathing, circulation and digestion happen involuntarily — yet we have some influence over what happens to it and what we do with it `20:00 – 22:00`.
- Different living entities have different _kṣetras_: an amoeba, given by past _karma_ a very small field, still has mechanisms for eating, defending and reproducing `22:00`.
- "Field" has broad meanings — a farmer's field, or one's professional field (engineer, doctor, executive), indicating an area of expertise and mastery. Scripture here indicates broad principles, not specifics `22:00 – 24:00`.
- An eagle's body, given by past _karma_, lets it fly fearlessly; all non-human entities act within their fields for eating, sleeping, mating and defending, with no further metaphysical quest `24:00`.

#### The brain as gadgetry, the soul as user [24:00 – 30:00]

- In the human form the _kṣetra_ is more evolved; scientists may say we have more brain matter with more processing power, but that is a facility used by the conscious soul `24:00`.
- A child can use a toy's remote, but given a sophisticated laptop, the child's limited intelligence limits what it can do `24:00 – 26:00`.
- The monkey experiment `26:00 – 28:00`:
  - Based on a claim that monkeys typing long enough would produce _Hamlet_ by chance, scientists placed a dozen monkeys in a sealed room with computers.
  - Some played with the computers, some urinated on them, some beat them; a few typed — and after seven full days all that came out was garbage.
  - The teacher has written about this in an article, "When monkeys started typing," in his science and spirituality book.
- Lesson: along with gadgetry there must be an evolved intelligence to use it. The brain is a tool; the soul is the source of consciousness that uses it `28:00`.
- The soul's "more developed consciousness" in the human form means the subtle-body covering of lust and ignorance is less than in subhuman bodies — so the human _kṣetra_ has greater capacity for spiritual inquiry and realisation `30:00`.

#### Field and knower are different [30:00]

- The body and the soul operating it are different; one should not confuse them.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda's painter example, which makes this point, is taken up with the next verse.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The body is our field — a limited, temporary area of influence given by karma — and we are its knower; the human field is precious because its lighter covering lets the soul inquire into spiritual reality. [20:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.3 [30:00 – 59:35]

<!-- vedabase:13.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says He too is the knower — of all fields — and that knowing the field and its knower is knowledge in His opinion. The teacher explains the two knowers, refutes the impersonalist reading, and then introduces individual-to-universal reasoning and the five _koṣas_ `30:00 – 59:35`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mataṁ mama_ — "My opinion"; Kṛṣṇa's opinion is final because it completely coincides with reality `32:00`.
- "Super-knower" — Śrīla Prabhupāda's coinage (like "Supersoul") for the Lord as knower of all bodies `38:00`.
- _Hṛṣīkeśa_ — Kṛṣṇa as the ultimate controller of the senses `40:00`.
- _Vyaṣṭi_ and _samaṣṭi_ — two Upaniṣadic ways of acquiring knowledge: from individual to universal, and from universal to individual `46:00`.
- _Koṣa_ — a sheath or level; "_maya_" means "full of" `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa's "opinion" is reality [32:00 – 34:00]

- "In My opinion" might seem to lessen the weight, as if it were just one opinion among many.
- But Kṛṣṇa is the Absolute Truth: our opinions coincide with reality to varying degrees, while His coincides completely.
- Illusion never covers Kṛṣṇa because illusion is His potency — as clouds do not cover the sun.

#### Knowledge means matter, soul and Supersoul [34:00]

- Modern education studies matter in its combinations and permutations, with little spiritual knowledge.
- Kṛṣṇa says knowledge is a broad understanding of all reality — matter and spirit.
- Since there are two knowers, complete knowledge means knowing three things:
  - matter,
  - the soul, who knows its own body (if my back hurts, I know),
  - the Supersoul, who is present in all living beings and so knows all bodies.

#### "Also" means two different knowers [34:00 – 38:00]

- Impersonalists conflate 13.2 and 13.3 to say "the knower is I," i.e. both refer to one reality — the teacher calls this misleading and wrong, grammatically and logically.
- Practically we are knowers only of our own body, and even then only partially; if we were knowers of all bodies, doctors would need no instruments or conversations to diagnose `36:00`.
- Grammatically, "X is in the room and I am also in the room" means X and I are two different entities; the word "also" in Kṛṣṇa's statement shows He is speaking of two different persons `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### King, citizens and tenants [38:00 – 40:00]

- Under a monarchy, the king owns the whole kingdom while various areas belong to various people within his overarching ownership.
- Our bodies belong to us in a partial, temporary sense of control, but ultimately belong to the Lord.
- We are tenants, not owners: a tenant controls a house but can be evicted any time; death can evict us from this body at any moment, and we must go wherever we are sent.

#### Seeds sown in the field [40:00 – 42:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda quotes a Vedic verse (the teacher notes no reference is given): as seeds are sown in a field, our auspicious and inauspicious activities become like seeds, and the one who knows this is the _kṣetrajña_.
- This can refer to the _ātmā_ or the _paramātmā_: we do not necessarily know what is auspicious and inauspicious, but the Lord does and gives rewards accordingly — acting indirectly, as a judge does.

#### Painter, painting and easel [42:00 – 44:00]

- The painter — the Supreme Lord, the ultimate doer.
- The easel and canvas — material nature.
- The painting — the living entity with a particular _kṣetra_.
- The teacher notes the example is approximate: a painting has no free will, while the living entity has agency; the verse's emphasis is that there is a supreme knower, the Supersoul, who is also the ultimate doer without whose sanction nothing can happen `44:00`.
- The _jīva_, the Supreme Lord and material nature can all be called Brahman, being different manifestations of Brahman; Śrīla Prabhupāda explains that they are not one, and taking them as entirely one misunderstands the Absolute Truth `44:00`.

#### Consciousness locked in matter [46:00]

- The teacher contrasts the soul's original consciousness with consciousness rooted in the material world, promising to return to the distinction later.
- Our consciousness, capable of perceiving spiritual reality, is locked in matter — like a moviegoer so absorbed in the screen that he perceives nothing else in the theatre. This is how the soul becomes connected with its physical _kṣetra_.

#### Individual to universal, universal to individual [46:00 – 50:00]

- _Vyaṣṭi_ — individual to universal: as Śrīla Prabhupāda would argue, "I am a person, you are a person, our fathers are persons — therefore God must also be a person."
- _Samaṣṭi_ — universal to individual: looking at the totality of creation to understand what the individual must be like.
- Science works both ways: from specific observations (Newton seeing a fruit fall) to general laws of motion, and then interpreting further motion through those laws `48:00`.
- Different people have different fields — a prime minister can declare an emergency for a whole country, a child "declares an emergency" in the backyard over pets — but every field is conditional; even a prime minister may die of a heart attack `48:00 – 50:00`.
- Applied here: as we are knowers, the Lord is the supreme knower — what we know in an infinitesimal quantity He knows infinitely; qualitatively we are the same `50:00`.

#### The five koṣas [50:00 – 59:35]

- In a purport Śrīla Prabhupāda describes the _koṣas_ as objective levels of God's energy and subjective levels of individual realisation `50:00`.
- Citizenship analogy: being an Indian citizen is an objective fact, but subjective awareness of it varies — a dog living in India has no conception of belonging to India `50:00 – 52:00`.
- The levels:
  1. _Annamaya_ — everything is defined as food. A newborn finds nourishment and security at the mother's breast; thereafter babies put fingers and anything else in their mouths `52:00 – 54:00`.
  2. _Prāṇamaya_ — everything is judged by its survival value; a baby that bites a candle cries and learns. Most animals live here; the grazing deer is also afraid of the tiger. It is in the modes of passion and ignorance. Darwin's "survival of the fittest" fits here — though Śrīla Prabhupāda rebutted that even the fittest do not survive, since everybody dies `54:00 – 56:00`.
  3. The next level (the teacher notes that nomenclature differs between versions of the books) — one becomes a philosopher, aware of ideas, the subtle body and one's own thoughts; "all conscious beings think, but not all can think about their thinking" — e.g. stepping back from anger to see nothing warranted it. This and higher levels are available only to humans `56:00`.
  4. _Vijñānamaya_ — awareness of the _ātmā_ as the basis of consciousness and of all activity in the body `56:00 – 58:00`.
  5. _Ānandamaya_ — consciousness of the Supreme Lord, and supreme happiness in relationship with Him, available on purification `58:00`.
- The higher we rise, the deeper our stability, security and satisfaction — like peeling an onion's layers to reach the nutritious part, or excavating buried treasure: beyond all coverings is the soul who loves Kṛṣṇa and whom Kṛṣṇa loves `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul knows its own field partially; Kṛṣṇa — "also" the knower — knows all fields fully, and real knowledge is seeing matter, soul and Supersoul as distinct while rising through levels of consciousness to loving relationship with Him. [32:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
