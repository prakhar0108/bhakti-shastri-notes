## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 13.20 [00:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.20 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa begins the discussion of _prakṛti_ and _puruṣa_ — standard terms that form the heart of all discussions in _sāṅkhya_ — by declaring both beginningless and locating all changes within material nature `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prakṛti_ — material nature; that which is insentient, unconscious and not intrinsically active `04:00`.
- _Puruṣa_ — that which is conscious, living, the active principle; it includes both the soul and the Supersoul, and in Chapter 15 Kṛṣṇa is distinguished as the supreme _puruṣa_ `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Anādi_ — beginningless; said of both _prakṛti_ and _puruṣa_ `00:00`.
- _Vikāra_ — changes `00:00`, `10:00`.
- _Guṇa_ — the three modes of material nature `00:00`.
- _Prakṛti-sambhava_ — born of material nature `00:00 – 02:00`.

#### Two points of the verse [00:00 – 02:00]

1. Both _prakṛti_ and _puruṣa_ — broadly, matter and spirit — are eternal.
2. The changes we see in material nature are born from matter.

#### Sāṅkhya and jñāna [02:00 – 06:00]

- _Sāṅkhya_ and _jñāna_ are closely related: the path of _jñāna-yoga_ uses _sāṅkhya_ analysis extensively, and in Chapter 5 Kṛṣṇa treats the two as almost interchangeable `02:00`.
- They may be divergent in their content — what the path comprises — but convergent in their intent: both seek the Absolute Truth `04:00`.
- In _sāṅkhya_, everything that happens is an interaction of _prakṛti_ and _puruṣa_. In some later developments of that line of thought, _prakṛti_ is associated with Pārvatī (non-different from Durgā, in charge of material nature) and _puruṣa_ with Śiva `04:00 – 06:00`.
- The Supreme Lord is not merely in charge of one universe; He is Mahā-Viṣṇu, the source of all existence. Here, however, Kṛṣṇa speaks in terms of concepts, not personalities, which is why He does not bring Śiva or Durgā into the discussion `06:00`.

#### "Things" are temporary, the material "thing" is not [06:00 – 08:00]

- Specific things — a mic, a laptop, a chair — are temporary.
- Matter itself, the substratum of existence, is an energy of God and is eternal; it is sometimes manifest and sometimes unmanifest.
- A building is constructed and demolished, but its components continue to exist; the teacher compares this with the law of conservation of energy in physics.
- Misery and bondage come from attachment to the temporary forms and tastes; attraction to spirit — the soul's eternal nature and Kṛṣṇa's eternal form — can bring eternal happiness `08:00`.

#### The screen and the three colours [08:00 – 12:00]

- Changes "coming from _prakṛti_" means they happen in the arena of _prakṛti_ — like events on a movie screen, which happen within the film, not to the spectator `08:00`.
- The three modes are like three fundamental colours (red-green-blue or red-yellow-blue): from their combinations come a beautiful sunset, a disaster scene after a calamity, a night sky or a cold-blooded crime — images that evoke radically different emotions `08:00 – 10:00`.
- Likewise, everything we see in this world, every attractive form or object, is made of the three modes; and the modes themselves are material `10:00`.
- The _vikāras_ of the field produce reactions: we recoil from a ghastly scene and feel drawn to a spectacular sunrise `12:00`.
- This raises the question: if changes come from _prakṛti_, who is experiencing the emotions, and how? That is answered in the next verse `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Matter and spirit are both eternal, but every change — every form, attraction and emotion-provoking image — happens within the arena of matter, built from the three modes like pictures from three colours; bondage comes from attachment to these temporary forms. [00:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.21 [14:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.21 -->

**Context.** The teacher calls this an enigmatic verse: material nature is the cause in the matter of cause and effect, while the _puruṣa_ is the cause of the experience of happiness and distress `14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kārya_ and _kāraṇa_ — effect and cause `14:00`.
- _Hetu_ — cause; since the word "cause" appears twice, the teacher suggests reading _prakṛti_'s role as the "substratum" or basic arena `14:00`.
- Enjoying mentality — the _puruṣa_'s desire to enjoy, which the teacher identifies as the cause of _sukha_ and _duḥkha_ (happiness and distress) `14:00`, `20:00`.

#### The spectator and the movie [14:00 – 18:00]

- The desire to watch a movie brings some happiness and some distress; one who decides not to watch feels neither `14:00 – 16:00`.
- Two people before the same screen: one watching, the other blind or unable to see after an eye operation — the movie affects only the first `16:00`.
- Even with no observer, events still happen on the screen; their cause is the projection — that is, _prakṛti_ `16:00 – 18:00`.
- So events happen in material nature regardless, but our enjoyment or distress depends on how much we identify with them `18:00`.

#### Examples from nature and from cricket [18:00 – 20:00]

- Seasons change: extreme cold sends some creatures into hibernation, and the creatures that feed on them go without food; a storm diverted from one place to another affects different beings.
- All this happens within material nature, but it is the soul who, because of the enjoying mentality, has placed itself in that arena and so experiences pleasure and pain.
- For urban listeners: when India beats Pakistan at cricket, something on a sports ground shown on a TV screen makes us happy only because we identify with the Indian team `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### The body determines what is possible [20:00 – 22:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport mentions the 8.4 million species and the two birds in the body `20:00`.
- Once placed in a body, the living entity acts according to that body's laws: a soul in a dog's body must act like a dog — it cannot mew like a cat or fly like a bird `20:00 – 22:00`.
- The body's functions are largely determined by material nature, but the emotional consequences come from the soul's desire to enjoy, which is how it got into that body `22:00`.
- A human recoils from stool and wants to flush it away; a hog rushes towards it — because of the enjoying mentality the hog has `22:00`.
- All the panorama of emotions — pleasure, pain, excitement, irritation, fear — arises because of this fundamental desire to enjoy `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Material nature runs the events; the soul's own enjoying mentality is what turns those events into happiness and distress — the same match, screen or object affects us only to the extent we identify with it. [14:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.22 [22:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.22 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa elaborates the enjoying mentality further. The teacher calls this one of the most important verses — the "seed" verse of the Gītā's knowledge section `22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Puruṣa_ situated in _prakṛti_ — the soul becomes situated in material nature because it desires to enjoy `24:00`.
- Desire to enjoy — the verse's word for enjoying has essentially the same meaning as the enjoying mentality of the previous verse `24:00`.
- _Guṇa-saṅga_ — association with the modes; _saṅga_ means association or attachment — association brings attachment and attachment leads to further association `26:00`, `30:00`.
- _Yoni_ — literally the womb; some beings come from eggs rather than wombs, so here the word refers in general to species `26:00`.

#### The sequence of entanglement [24:00 – 26:00]

1. The soul desires to enjoy.
2. The objects of enjoyment are the products of material nature in the form of the three modes.
3. Because of association with the modes, the soul takes birth in different species, meeting good and bad.

#### Raw elements are not attractive; combinations are [24:00 – 26:00]

- A computer palette of 100, 500 or 10,000 colours attracts no one, but the same colours made into beautiful images draw eager viewers.
- Earth, water, fire, air and ether are not irresistibly attractive in themselves, but made into objects they become so:
  - a sunrise — _sāttvika_;
  - the form of the opposite sex — _rājasika_;
  - alcohol — _tāmasika_.

#### From happiness and distress to transmigration [26:00 – 30:00]

- 13.21 said the desire to enjoy leads to happiness and distress; 13.22 is more expansive: it leads even to transmigration into species with more or less misery `28:00`.
- This will be elaborated in 14.18: those in goodness move upwards, those in passion stay where they are, those in ignorance go downwards `26:00`.
- Extending the movie example: someone who enjoys the one or two horror scenes in a mainstream movie may go to a multiplex or the internet, click on horror, and get caught among thousands of horror movies — so the soul's specific enjoying mentality takes it into a species suited to that enjoyment `28:00 – 30:00`.
- Our bodies are given according to the kind of desires we have `30:00`.

#### Gradually and proportionately [30:00 – 32:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda writes that as long as one is captivated by this false manifestation, one must keep transmigrating `30:00`.
- The change can be effected only by hearing from authoritative sources; one who submits to hearing gradually loses the "long-cherished desire to dominate material nature" `30:00 – 32:00`.
- "Long-cherished": it has been going on so long that it is almost beginningless.
- "Gradually and proportionately": we cannot expect to give up the enjoying mentality immediately, but as we reduce it, we come to enjoy spiritual happiness proportionately `32:00`.

#### Why it is called the seed verse [32:00 – 36:00]

- In Chapter 15, Kṛṣṇa describes material existence as an upside-down tree; every tree grows from a seed, and the whole tree of material existence grows from the soul's enjoying mentality `32:00`.
- The teacher maps the following chapters onto the tree `34:00`:
  - Chapter 13 — the seed (the enjoying mentality).
  - Chapter 14 — the water that nourishes the tree (the three modes).
  - Chapter 15 — the tree itself: its branches, and how attachment binds while detachment cuts it down (15.3–15.4).
  - Chapter 16 — the upper and lower parts of the tree: the divine and demoniac natures.
  - Chapter 17 — the intermediate parts: people neither explicitly devoted to God and scripture nor brazenly defiant of them.
  - Chapter 18 — the summary of everything.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul's desire to enjoy is the seed of the whole tree of material existence: through association with the modes it carries the soul from species to species, and it is reduced only gradually, through hearing from authority, as spiritual happiness grows proportionately. [22:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.23 [34:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.23 -->

**Context.** After the _puruṣa_ who is sentenced to wander through species, Kṛṣṇa describes another kind of _puruṣa_ — the Supersoul `34:00 – 36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Attributes of this _puruṣa_ — overseer, permitter, maintainer, enjoyer and the great controller (_maheśvara_) `36:00`.
- _Paramātmā_ — the Supersoul; the word appears several times earlier (e.g. 6.7, on one who has controlled the mind), but this is the first place the Gītā explicitly defines who the _paramātmā_ is `36:00`.
- _Para_ — transcendental: this _puruṣa_ is in the body yet transcendental to material existence `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Two different puruṣas [38:00 – 40:00]

- None of these attributes apply to us: we are not the overseer or permitter — if we were, we could do whatever we wanted; actually we are controlled and depend on something beyond ourselves `38:00`.
- Contrast with 13.22: that _puruṣa_ wants to enjoy, gets caught in _prakṛti_ and is subjected to birth in various species; this _puruṣa_ is transcendental, an overseer, not caught in anything — _maheśvara_ `38:00`.
- The same contrast appeared in 13.2–13.3 (a knower of one body and a knower of all bodies). The teacher notes that _māyāvādīs_ try to read those as one knower, an interpretation already shown to be unintelligible; here, in one continuous flow about _puruṣa_, the two are even more clearly distinct `40:00`.

#### Supersoul verses compared [40:00 – 44:00]

| Verse | Main point conveyed |
| --- | --- |
| 6.31 | Understanding the oneness of the Supersoul in all hearts and Kṛṣṇa — the yogī so situated is always in Kṛṣṇa |
| 13.23 | The transcendence of the Supersoul, spoken in the third person |
| 15.15 | Identity: "I am that" — Kṛṣṇa Himself is situated in all hearts |
| 18.61 | The Supersoul's role as guide, directing the wanderings of all living beings |

- The guiding aspect is not specifically mentioned in 13.23; to understand the Supersoul properly we must combine all these verses, but here, within the analytical flow, the emphasis is transcendence `44:00`.

#### The Supersoul's concern [44:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda adds the devotional conclusion: the Lord is always eager to take the soul back from the material to the spiritual energy; the misuse of independence is the cause of the soul's material strife.
- The Supersoul's message is, in effect, "give it up and turn your faith to Me, then you will be happy" — the ultimate purpose of His presence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Within the same body there are two puruṣas: the soul, entangled by its desire to enjoy, and the Supersoul — overseer, permitter and great controller — who remains transcendental yet stays present out of concern to bring the soul back. [36:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.24 [44:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.24 -->

**Context.** A summary verse on why all this knowledge matters: one who understands _puruṣa_, _prakṛti_ and the modes will not take birth again `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### Real life beyond the movie [46:00]

- One will not take birth again because one understands that real life is transcendental, beyond material existence.
- Once we realise that what we are watching is only a movie, and that our real life is far more exciting than the movie, we stop watching; likewise, understanding "I am a soul with a real life in the spiritual arena" turns one away from this world.
- Kṛṣṇa includes the modes because they produce the substances that attract us.

#### Whatever one may be doing [46:00 – 48:00]

- Kṛṣṇa says that one properly situated in this understanding is not entangled, whatever one may be doing; the teacher draws the implication that even fighting a war need not entangle one who has proper philosophical understanding.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda states the purpose of knowledge: to understand distinctly that the living entity has fallen into material existence by chance and must revert to spiritual existence — to disentangle oneself from matter `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge of matter, spirit and the modes frees one from rebirth because it reveals that our real life lies beyond this world — and with that understanding even one's outward activities, even fighting a war, need not entangle. [46:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.25 [48:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.25 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now gives the means of coming out of matter's entanglement: three methods here and a fourth in the next verse `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dhyāna_ — meditation `48:00`.
- The word _ātmā_ appears three times with three meanings `48:00 – 50:00`:
  1. the self (the soul);
  2. the Supersoul;
  3. the mind — Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation does not say "mind" directly but uses "meditation," whose whole focus is concentrating and directing the mind.

#### Three methods [50:00 – 52:00]

- **Meditation** — turning the mind inwards illumines the inner territory, where one perceives the soul and the Supersoul; this refers to Chapter 6, which culminates in the topmost _yogī_ being one who sees Kṛṣṇa `50:00`.
- **_Sāṅkhya_ analysis** — analysing the twenty-four elements, seeing consciousness in none of them, and going beyond to the twenty-fifth element and finally the twenty-sixth `50:00`.
- **_Karma-yoga_** — work without fruitive desire `50:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa has covered all three in the Gītā itself: Chapters 2–5 discuss _karma-yoga_ in contrast with _sāṅkhya_, Chapter 6 _dhyāna-yoga_, and this section _jñāna-yoga_ `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### Why "childish activities" [52:00 – 54:00]

- In the last line of the purport Śrīla Prabhupāda speaks of those who try to satisfy the Lord by childish activities, referring to _haṭha-yoga_ `52:00`.
- The teacher explains: _haṭha-yoga_, _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ and _dhyāna-yoga_ often refer to the same process; _haṭha-yoga_ here refers to the initial, physical stage `52:00`.
- Children boast: "I can balance on my head — you can't"; people likewise get impressed by physical gymnastics or mystic feats such as producing ash `52:00`.
- Being enamoured of the capacity to do what others can't is childish; the major distraction on the path of _yoga_ is infatuation with _siddhis_ and the popularity they bring — it is not eternal and leads to nothing eternal `52:00 – 54:00`.
- If one moves beyond that and searches for the Supersoul within, the path progresses `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Meditation, analysis and selfless work are all ways to perceive the Supersoul, but on the yogic path the great danger is childish infatuation with powers others lack — real progress lies in searching for the Lord within. [48:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.26 [54:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.26 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa recommends the _bhakti_ process: those who do not know all these technicalities, but hear from others and begin to worship, also go beyond death `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śruti-parāyaṇa_ — dedicated to hearing. The teacher compares other _parāyaṇa_ phrases: those wholly devoted to Nārāyaṇa, and, in Chapter 16, the demoniac who have given themselves to lust and greed `54:00 – 56:00`.
- _Śruti_ — can mean the _śruti_ scriptures that discuss _karma-kāṇḍa_ (as in 2.52–2.53, where one fixed in transcendence is not disturbed by them), or the function of hearing, especially _śravaṇam_ about Kṛṣṇa; since the result here is going beyond material existence, it clearly means hearing about Kṛṣṇa `56:00`.
- _Anye_ — "others," used twice with different meanings `56:00 – 58:00`:
  1. others apart from the three earlier groups (meditators, analysts, _karma-yogīs_);
  2. the spiritual authorities from whom they hear.
- _Upāsanā_ — worship; centrally a feature of _bhakti_ `58:00`.

#### A fourth category [56:00 – 58:00]

- These people do not know the technicalities of _prakṛti_ and _puruṣa_, but they hear from authority.
- Hearing is not the end: it transforms them, and they become devoted to and begin worshipping the Supreme.

#### Crossing beyond death [58:00 – 60:00]

- Kṛṣṇa used similar language in 7.14: those who surrender to Him cross beyond His illusory energy; here too one goes beyond `58:00`.
- Hearing about the Supersoul leads to devotion and worship, and by that one transcends material existence `58:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda explains this hearing as chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa _mahā-mantra_ and following scriptural authorities: though the common man is not as capable as so-called philosophers, faithful hearing from authority helps him go back home, back to Godhead `58:00 – 60:00`.
- The chapter's technical categories may intimidate us — "I thought Kṛṣṇa consciousness was simply chant, dance and go back" — but even within the _jñāna_ section Kṛṣṇa reassures that hearing and then worshipping is enough to cross the cycle of birth and death `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
One need not master the technicalities: hearing about the Supreme from authorities — above all chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra — transforms the hearer into a worshipper and carries even the common person beyond birth and death. [54:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
