### Bhagavad-gita 13.5 [02:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.5 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says this knowledge of the field and its knower has been described by the sages in various ways and in the _Brahma-sūtra_ `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ṛṣis_ "in various ways" — different sages have described it in different hymns and in different ways; the word for "sung" can mean songs or descriptions `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Brahma-sūtra_ — the same book as the _Vedānta-sūtra_. Śrīla Prabhupāda generally preferred "_Vedānta-sūtra_"; traditional commentators, such as Madhvācārya, would typically call it the _Brahma-sūtra_ `04:00`.
- _Pada_ — a unit of the _Brahma-sūtra_'s construction, like a chapter; the book has _adhyāyas_ and _padas_ `04:00`.
- The verse's word for cause and effect — logic, on which the _Brahma-sūtra_ is largely based `04:00`.
- _Chandas_ — can refer to the way Vedic hymns are recited, or to the _Chāndogya_ `06:00`.

#### Why two names for one book [04:00]

- It is called _Vedānta-sūtra_ because it establishes Vedānta as the conclusion of the six systems of philosophy.
- It is called _Brahma-sūtra_ because it presents Brahman as the ultimate reality.

#### How the Vedānta-sūtra argues [04:00 – 06:00]

- It starts with scripture and proceeds step by step to show that Brahman is the ultimate reality and Vedānta the highest of all schools of thought current at the time.
- Orthodox schools accept Vedic authority — _sāṅkhya_, _yoga_, _vaiśeṣika_, _mīmāṁsā_ and _vedānta_ among them; heterodox schools reject it — Buddhism, Jainism and the Cārvāka school.
- It establishes the import of scripture both by quoting parallel scriptural verses and by logical inference.

#### Kṛṣṇa sets a standard by citing authority [06:00 – 08:00]

- What Kṛṣṇa speaks is never concocted — He is the ultimate authority — yet He sets a standard by saying His teaching is what great sages have spoken earlier.
- The Vedic hymns generally focus on _karma-kāṇḍa_ and say less about the ultimate reality, which the Upaniṣads discuss more `06:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda cites the _Vedānta-sūtra_ and Parāśara (Vyāsa's father), who wrote that we are all indestructible; the teacher skips the technical _sūtras_ Śrīla Prabhupāda then quotes `08:00`.
- One has to search for this truth in the _Vedānta-sūtra_ (_Brahma-sūtra_) `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even Kṛṣṇa, the ultimate authority, presents His teaching as confirmed by the sages and the logic of the Vedānta-sūtra — setting the standard that spiritual knowledge must rest on scripture, tradition and sound reasoning. [04:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.6 – 13.7 [08:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.6 -->

<!-- vedabase:13.7 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now describes the field: its twenty-four constituent elements (13.6) and the transformations it undergoes (13.7) — a condensed description of _sāṅkhya_ `08:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mahā-bhūtas_ — the gigantic elements: earth, water, fire, air and ether `08:00 – 10:00`.
- _Ahaṅkāra_ — false ego; _buddhi_ — intelligence; the unmanifested (_pradhāna_) `10:00`.
- _Vikāra_ — transformation, change `10:00 – 12:00`.
- _Icchā_ and _dveṣa_ — attachment (desire) and aversion; with them come _sukha_ and _duḥkha_, happiness and distress `12:00`.
- _Cetanā_ — consciousness; here, consciousness projected into matter `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### The twenty-four elements [10:00]

- First line: the five gross elements, false ego and intelligence, then the unmanifested — eight.
- Second group: the eleven senses — five knowledge-acquiring senses, five working senses and the mind — making nineteen.
- Then the five sense objects — form, touch, taste, smell and sound — making twenty-four.
- Kṛṣṇa first described the knower briefly (_ātmā_ and _paramātmā_); now He describes what the field is made of `10:00`.

#### Desire, aversion, happiness and distress [12:00]

- When we get what we desire, we experience happiness; when we get what we dislike, distress.
- Simple example: if the only vegetable dish in a meal is one I dislike, my mouth turns sour — and life brings many bigger things we dislike.

#### The movie-theatre analogy [12:00 – 16:00]

- The analogy recurs through the chapter: the person watching a movie is like the knower of the field; the screen, projector, acoustics and lighting that create the audiovisual experience are like the field.
- When the hero and heroine embrace, the viewer feels desire and happiness; when the hero is beaten or the villain chases the heroine, aversion and distress.
- These changes happen within the illusory framework of the projection, yet they affect the viewer — so they are transformations of the field.

#### Why consciousness is listed as a transformation [14:00 – 16:00]

- Consciousness comes from the soul, not matter — the theme of Śrīla Prabhupāda's conversation book _Life Comes From Life_ (with Svarūpa Dāmodara Mahārāja and other disciples).
- Here the verse means consciousness projected within matter — consciousness experiencing a virtual or "movie" reality.
- Spectators could look anywhere, but their attention and consciousness are projected into the screen; what happens there has nothing to do with their real lives outside.
- Likewise our real life is in relationship with Kṛṣṇa; we have forgotten it and identify with a temporary illusion — a body, family, nationality — and experience emotions accordingly. That consciousness is a transformation of the field, unrelated to the soul's original pure consciousness `16:00`.
- Convictions are also products of the field — e.g. an Indian may think Pakistanis are evil, and Pakistanis may think Indians are evil `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### Why only a summary of sāṅkhya [18:00 – 22:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda notes that only a summary is given; _sāṅkhya_ is itself a complex, developed philosophy, and its theistic version is taught in the Third Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_ by Lord Kapila `18:00`.
- Theistic _sāṅkhya_: analysing the twenty-four elements should lead from matter to spirit, and within spirit to the Supreme Personality of Godhead `18:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's purpose is not an exhaustive analysis but Arjuna's decision on the battlefield — made on the understanding that he is part of Kṛṣṇa and best acts in harmony with Him by learning to love and serve Him, with _Bhagavān_ as the highest goal and _bhakti_ as the best means. Even _sāṅkhya_ analysis leads there `18:00 – 20:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The field — body, mind and even our present emotions and convictions — is a projection we watch like a movie; analysing it shows it is not our real life, and theistic sāṅkhya leads from matter to the Supreme Person. [12:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa lists the twenty items of knowledge and concludes that all this is knowledge and everything else ignorance. The teacher notes this is the only place in the whole Gītā where Śrīla Prabhupāda gives one combined purport for five verses — logically sound, since all five discuss one theme, _jñāna_ `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Amānitvam_ — humility: not being proud even when we have reason to be proud `22:00`.
- _Adambhitvam_ — pridelessness: not claiming to be what we are not; claiming to be a billionaire when one is not is hypocrisy `22:00`.
- _Ahiṁsā_ — nonviolence `26:00`, `42:00 – 44:00`.
- _Kṣānti_ — tolerance `44:00`.
- _Ācāryopāsanam_ — accepting and worshipping a spiritual master `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### The five-verse purport [20:00]

- Two verses are often combined, three sometimes; 6.20–6.23 is a four-verse set (really three and a half verses).
- 13.8–13.12 is the only five-verse combined purport, because the five verses share one theme.

#### Humility: why those who aim to look big are small [22:00 – 26:00]

- People want to look big in the world's eyes and speak a lot about themselves — and end up looking small.
- They become miserable, afflicted by inadequacy and insecurity, dependent on others' praise and recognition, with no inner shelter or self-esteem.
- "One who thinks he knows doesn't know, and one who knows he doesn't know, knows." A devotee never claims to know Kṛṣṇa entirely `24:00`.
- Arjuna tells Kṛṣṇa, "You alone know Yourself"; and Kṛṣṇa says His opulences are unlimited. So we should not be proud even of spiritual knowledge, what to speak of material knowledge `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### Knowledge as virtue, not information [26:00 – 30:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda discusses these twenty items in the _Īśopaniṣad_'s _vidyā_ / _avidyā_ section (mantras 9–11), connecting _vidyā_ with these virtues `26:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa defines knowledge in terms of virtues, not items of information `28:00`.
- An engineer may build excellent houses, bridges, cars or planes, yet the education says nothing about character `28:00`.
- Humanities touch the finer aspects of the human being; sciences deal with controlling nature; but today's education focuses on earning. Education's purpose is character development `28:00 – 30:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda clarifies that these items are not the interaction of the field, as less intelligent people think; they are the means of getting out of the entanglement of the field's elements `30:00`.

#### Bhakti within jñāna [32:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport says the most important item is unalloyed devotional service to Kṛṣṇa.
- From the _jñānīs'_ perspective _bhakti_ is one element, but even _jñānīs_ must practise _bhakti_ to gain realisation and liberation; _bhakti_ is present in all processes.
- These qualities, developed in _jñāna_ practice, are also helpful on the path of devotion.

#### Symptom, deeper problem, solution [32:00 – 38:00]

- Medicine: stomach pain is a perceivable symptom; scans reveal the underlying problem (e.g. appendix); then comes treatment such as surgery.
- Iceberg: a small part shows above water; the giant body below sank the Titanic.
- A symptom like difficulty swallowing may reveal leukaemia; then comes therapy and cure.
- Applied:
  - Surface problem — birth, old age, disease and death; birth is painful too, since the conscious being is trapped in the womb `36:00`.
  - Deeper problem — consciousness misdirected from spirit to matter, producing bad qualities; the twenty virtues are absent `36:00 – 38:00`.
  - Solution — redirecting consciousness to spirit by practising _bhakti_. A doctor listing low blood counts is not enough; the patient needs a practical prescription `38:00`.
- A devotee has all the good qualities of the demigods `38:00`.

#### Accept respect, don't expect it [40:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's "classic" definition: humility means not being anxious for the satisfaction of being honoured by others.
- Devotees do receive honour — people even offer obeisances — and they accept it as part of Vaiṣṇava etiquette and reciprocation of affection.
- But a devotee does not expect or demand respect; the concern is how best to serve Kṛṣṇa in every situation.

#### These qualities are the test of progress [40:00 – 42:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda gives a balanced view:
  - Practising _bhakti_ will develop these qualities; nothing extra is needed.
  - Yet one should check whether they are developing — they are the test of advancement.
- A patient taking iron for a low red-cell count should watch whether the count rises; if not, some other complication may exist. Likewise, if devotion is not producing results, we may be committing offences.

#### Nonviolence and tolerance [42:00 – 46:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's elevated definition: nonviolence means trying one's best to distribute real knowledge so people can become enlightened and leave material entanglement `42:00 – 44:00`.
- The soul has evolved through species to the human form, where liberation is possible; not helping it take that opportunity is violence `44:00`.
- A doctor who does not give a known cure is culpable for negligence, even without stealing an organ; similarly, withholding spiritual knowledge obstructs people's recovery `44:00`.
- Tolerance is persistence: not accepting everything inflicted on us, but not letting small things — an insult, daily mishaps — distract us from our ultimate spiritual purpose `44:00`.

#### Simplicity and the spiritual master's blessing [46:00 – 48:00]

- Simplicity and truthfulness: a devotee is not unnecessarily diplomatic or political `46:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda acknowledges that the bona fide spiritual master's blessing can advance a disciple even without the disciple following the regulative principles, and that the principles become easier for one who has served the spiritual master without reservation `46:00`.
- The principles are important but not all-important; attraction to Kṛṣṇa, developed through association with devotees, is the propellant on the spiritual path `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Pessimism or realism? [48:00 – 52:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda writes that unless we have a pessimistic view of material life, considering birth, death, old age and disease, there is no impetus for advancement `48:00`.
- Half-empty versus half-full is a matter of outlook; but handing a thirsty person a fully empty glass is not "negativity" — it is reality. Denying reality is not optimism; it is stupidity `48:00`.
- A serious disease must be faced; denying it only makes it worse. Accepting it lets us seek treatment `50:00`.
- Birth, old age, disease and death are inevitable only at the material level; at the spiritual level they are avoidable `50:00`.
- Any pessimism in Vedic philosophy is initial, not final — like a doctor's initial seriousness, followed by the optimism of a cure `52:00`.

#### A broader problem of evil [52:00 – 54:00]

- Western philosophy asks: if God is good, why do babies die of diseases and innocents in earthquakes and tsunamis?
- Vedic philosophy asks more broadly: why does anybody die at all? Untimely death jolts us, but all death is suffering.
- Rather than questioning specific problems within the natural system, question the system itself; Vedānta explains how the temporary comes from the eternal through the transformation of the Absolute Truth's energies `54:00`.
- With _jñāna-cakṣu_ a devotee sees material existence itself as the fundamental misery, rather than getting carried away by lesser or greater miseries within it `54:00`.

#### Affection without attachment [54:00 – 59:24]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda says detachment from children, wife and home does not mean having no feeling; they are natural objects of affection `54:00`.
- A householder must care for family members emotionally, not only financially and physically `54:00 – 56:00`.
- Bhīma and the lotus `56:00`:
  - In exile near Badrī, Draupadī saw a thousand-petalled lotus floating in the river and asked Bhīma for more.
  - Bhīma climbed the Himalayas and fought the Yakṣas whose garden the flower came from.
  - Not a hen-pecked husband: Draupadī had been dishonoured in the Kuru assembly, was in exile, and Arjuna — who had a special place in her heart — had gone to the heavens; Bhīma wanted to please her.
- Vertical (with Kṛṣṇa) and horizontal (with loved ones) relationships should strengthen each other: love for devotee family members fuels shared devotion, and love for Kṛṣṇa brings self-mastery, sensitivity and maturity that make us better family members `56:00 – 58:00`.
- If family members are not devotees, internalise humility, tolerance and sensitivity and inspire them — not by being preachy or bossy `58:00`.
- Only in extreme, hostile situations is extreme action needed — like Prahlāda, who respected Hiraṇyakaśipu yet continued his devotion `58:00`.
- A devotee should be affectionate but not attached; the difference is continued next class `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge, for Kṛṣṇa, is character: humility that accepts but does not expect respect, nonviolence that shares spiritual knowledge, tolerance as persistence, realism about suffering, and affection without attachment — all flowing from devotion and serving as the test of real progress. [22:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
