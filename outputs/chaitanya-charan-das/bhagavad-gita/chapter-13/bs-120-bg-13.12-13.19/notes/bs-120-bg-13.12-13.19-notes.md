## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 13.12 [00:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.12 -->

**Context.** The teacher completes Śrīla Prabhupāda's combined purport on the twenty items of knowledge (13.8–13.12) — affection versus attachment, socialisation and humility — and then explains Kṛṣṇa's conclusion that all this is knowledge and everything else ignorance `00:00 – 16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Affection — a natural emotional human connection, vital for relationships to exist `00:00`.
- Detachment — absence of excessive emotional entanglement in material things `02:00`.
- Irresponsibility — evasiveness; unwillingness to shoulder a burden that is actually one's own `02:00`.
- _Amānitvam_ — humility, the beginning of knowledge; its antithesis is thinking oneself God `10:00 – 12:00`.
- _Jñāna_ — here, the knowledge that enables spiritual advancement and liberation from material existence `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Caring for the whole person [00:00 – 02:00]

- We relate to people as complete persons — we cannot say "I love your soul but don't care for your body."
- On Śrīla Prabhupāda's Vyāsa-pūjā in Māyāpur, amid thousands of devotees glorifying him, he noticed one devotee limping, called him forward and prescribed a homemade remedy for the leg.
- He was also concerned when he heard devotees in Māyāpur were not getting proper _prasāda_.
- Care covers body, mind and soul: people should feel valued, cared for and cherished — not only told what is right and wrong. The mission's success depends on inspiring devotees to practise lifelong `02:00`.

#### Detachment is not irresponsibility [02:00 – 06:00]

- When book distribution was being emphasised in the earlier temple-centred ISKCON, mothers were told distributing books was more important than caring for their babies; a mother wrote to Śrīla Prabhupāda, who replied strongly that the children are gifts of God and must be cared for `02:00 – 04:00`.
- He avoided both extremes: mothers should neither neglect home responsibilities for missionary work nor be barred from missionary work `04:00`.
- The same applies to husbands: detachment does not mean neglecting family members' physical, emotional or spiritual needs `04:00`.
- The dividing line is not always easy to discern — sometimes we drift left, sometimes right — but conceptually, affection is not only acceptable but essential `04:00`.
- The _Bhāgavatam_'s Third Canto, in the section on Devahūti and Kardama, describes the wife as a husband's protection like a fortress `06:00`.

#### Attachment and extreme situations [06:00]

- Only when a horizontal relationship becomes an insurmountable obstacle to the vertical one may extreme action be needed — but we should not assume it insurmountable from the start.
- Practise devotion without unnecessarily disturbing others, including family; in largely democratic societies, communicate one's chosen principles without imposing them and without giving them up.
- Attachment is illustrated by Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Duryodhana: unable to say no, one lets — even supports — a loved one going on the wrong track.

#### Spiritualising the home and socialising [08:00 – 10:00]

- Chanting, deity worship, discussing the Gītā — the potent limbs of devotional service — and taking _prasāda_ spiritualise the home `08:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda writes that a devotee has no taste for unnecessary sporting, cinema-going or social functions; we have limited time and need priorities `08:00`.
- "Hanging out" — gossiping, drinking together — rarely leads to deep, satisfying relationships `08:00`.
- Devotion is not against socialisation — Śrīla Prabhupāda founded a _society_ — but socialising should be Kṛṣṇa-centred among devotees and, with others, at least not harmful to Kṛṣṇa consciousness; infection is always possible `10:00`.
- Understanding that only spiritual knowledge lasts inspires a serious search for the philosophical truth `10:00`.

#### Humility, full circle [10:00 – 12:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport ends where it began: thinking oneself God is most puffed up, though the living entity is always "kicked" by nature's laws — so the beginning of knowledge is humility.
- Besides self-styled god-men, many who want to be "number one" are also claiming to be God in a way.
- A devotee uses talents in Kṛṣṇa's service and may come into public attention as a service, but does not seek glorification or think "I am the greatest."

#### Why everything else is "ignorance" [12:00 – 16:00]

- Physics, chemistry and economics have validity and utility in their own sphere `12:00`.
- Vedic culture did not neglect material skills: within _varṇāśrama_ artisans, _kṣatriyas_ and _brāhmaṇas_ trained the next generation, and succession was carefully maintained `14:00`.
- Vedic architecture, music, dance and literature were high-class; the teacher notes the _Mahābhārata_ is by far the world's longest poem, many times longer than the _Iliad_ and _Odyssey_ combined — not work for lazy people `14:00`.
- Such skills were mostly connected with God and _dharma_, which is good; but "everything else is ignorance" means none of it can, by itself, bring liberation from material existence — it only makes things relatively better within it, temporarily `14:00 – 16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge means virtues — affection without attachment, responsibility without entanglement, humility instead of wanting to be God; all other learning, however valid, is "ignorance" in that it cannot liberate us from material existence. [00:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.13 [16:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.13 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa clearly marks the transition: what was told before was knowledge; now He will explain the knowable, by knowing which one tastes the eternal `16:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jñeya_ — the object of knowledge; ultimately the _ātmā_ and the Supreme Lord (the _paramātmā_ being one manifestation of the Supreme Lord) `18:00`.
- _Anādi_ — beginningless `18:00`.
- _Mat-param_ — subordinate to Me `18:00`.
- The verse's pair of terms that Śrīla Prabhupāda renders "cause and effect" — the words can mean temporary or eternal, among other things `18:00`.
- _Amṛtam_ — here not heavenly nectar but immortality: the nectar of devotion, then pure devotion, tasted eternally in the spiritual world `24:00`.

#### Outward, inward, upward [18:00 – 24:00]

- Looking outward — we see matter; this gives rise to materialism, relying on direct perception (_pratyakṣa_) and leading to naturalistic conclusions that matter is supreme `20:00`.
- Looking inward — valid, but without guidance we cannot see beyond the mind, a pseudo-"I" that makes its emotions seem ours; the inner territory is mostly guesswork and inference (_anumāna_) `20:00 – 22:00`.
- Looking upward — waiting for descending revelation, which gives direct knowledge from Kṛṣṇa and helps us make sense of what we observe externally and internally; more important than the other two `22:00`.
- This is a rough division; people mix the approaches, not always with good results `22:00`.

#### Different readings of 13.13–13.17 [24:00]

- Different _ācāryas_ take different positions on these verses: each may refer to the _ātmā_, Brahman or the _paramātmā_; one must consult the _ācāryas'_ commentaries.
- The _ātmā_ is one unit of consciousness; Brahman is like an ocean of consciousness, all-pervading.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda takes 13.13's Brahman as referring to the _jīva_ — the living entity is always the servant of the Lord, with no other position `28:00`, `32:00`.

#### Beyond the cycle of cause and effect [24:00 – 28:00]

- Chicken or egg? Pure logic regresses endlessly; the origin of both is the Absolute Truth, which is beyond material cause and effect `26:00`.
- Scientists speak of "causal completeness": all material phenomena follow material laws, so there is no room for God to act — a dropped stone simply falls `26:00`.
- In one sense they are correct: Kṛṣṇa has made the world so that He need not intervene constantly; His descent is extremely rare — once in a day of Brahmā `26:00 – 28:00`.
- Material causes and effects have their own cycle, but spiritual reality exists beyond it `28:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda quotes Upaniṣadic verses describing the Lord as master of all fields and lord of the modes; knowing that Absolute Truth liberates one from birth and death `28:00`.

#### Emotional closure at death [30:00]

- In Vedic tradition, people visit someone about to die to seek blessings and forgiveness, so that negative feelings do not block anyone's connection with the Absolute Truth.
- With forgiveness given and received, the dying person can turn the mind peacefully toward what comes next — for a devotee, Kṛṣṇa.
- Material distractions never end, but some are major and some minor: we should neither dismiss all as minor nor obsess over all as major; attend to each according to how much it distracts from serving Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The knowable is the beginningless Absolute Truth beyond material cause and effect; it is reached not by looking only outward or inward but upward to revelation, and knowing it gives the nectar of eternal devotion. [18:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.14 [32:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.14 -->

**Context.** The following verses describe the all-pervading aspect of the Absolute Truth: everywhere are His hands and legs, eyes, heads and faces, and ears `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- All-pervading — the Supersoul exists pervading everything `32:00 – 34:00`.

#### Why this is the Paramātmā [32:00 – 34:00]

- All-pervasiveness might suggest the impersonal Brahman, but Brahman is not generally described with face, hands and legs.
- From our philosophical understanding it is easy: the _paramātmā_ is all-pervading, so this describes the _paramātmā_ as _jñeya_.
- Since He is everywhere, observes, does and knows everything, knowing Him should not be difficult `34:00`.

#### The insider's perspective [34:00 – 36:00]

- The verse is less a direct definition than a transmission of an intuitive, inspirational understanding — the insider's view.
- People want the "inside story": a survivor of the 26/11 attack inside the Taj Hotel in Mumbai can tell what it was like from inside.
- We lack a pure devotee's devotion and so cannot see as he does; Kṛṣṇa reports the vision of an advanced spiritualist.

#### Advaita Ācārya's dream [36:00]

- Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, glorifying His devotees, described Advaita Ācārya as such a great Vaiṣṇava that he explained any Vedic text in terms of pure devotional service.
- Yet he could not explain this verse that way — until the Lord appeared in his dream and revealed that it refers to the _paramātmā_ aspect.
- For us, Śrīla Prabhupāda makes it simple; for Advaita Ācārya it was a brilliant insight given by the Lord's grace.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda also quotes the _Brahma-saṁhitā_: the Lord resides in Goloka yet is the indwelling Supersoul of all beings — a position the _jīva_ does not have `36:00 – 38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
"Hands and legs everywhere" describes the all-pervading Supersoul — an insider's vision of advanced devotees, so subtle that even Advaita Ācārya received its meaning by the Lord's grace. [32:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.15 [38:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.15 -->

**Context.** The Absolute Truth is the source of all senses yet has no senses; detached, yet maintainer of all; beyond the modes, yet their master `38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Inconceivable — not that we can never understand, but that we cannot understand at our present level of consciousness; we will understand more and more, though never completely `40:00`.

#### Three sets of contradictions [38:00 – 40:00]

- Source of all senses, yet without senses.
- Maintainer of all, yet detached from all.
- Lord of the modes, yet beyond the modes.
- The Absolute Truth is a reservoir of contradictory attributes integrated into one — possible by His inconceivable potency.
- The _Īśopaniṣad_ also spoke of the Absolute Truth walking and not walking, far away yet near, inside and outside everything `38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord harmonises opposites — sense-source yet senseless, maintainer yet detached, master of the modes yet beyond them; "inconceivable" means beyond our present consciousness, not forever unknowable. [38:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.16 [40:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.16 -->

**Context.** The Truth is inside and outside all beings, moving and non-moving; being very subtle, it cannot be known by material senses; it is far away yet near `40:00 – 42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Very subtle — the reason we cannot properly know Him `40:00`.

#### Why we cannot see Him [40:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda explains that due to materially contaminated senses we cannot see His form; therefore impersonalists, still materially affected, cannot understand the Personality of Godhead.

#### Far and near [42:00 – 44:00]

- The verse that promised to describe the knowable now calls it far and near.
- For non-devotees Kṛṣṇa is far away; for devotees He is close.
- Another understanding: _Bhagavān_ is far away while the _paramātmā_ is close — the Supersoul inside as Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, outside as Mahā-Viṣṇu or Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu.
- Or simply: these attributes reveal the glory of the Supreme Being; both perspectives are true.
- This is a chapter in which Śrīla Prabhupāda quotes the Upaniṣads extensively, because they contain parallel passages `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The same Lord is far from those who turn away and near to devotees; our contaminated senses, not His absence, keep Him hidden. [40:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.17 [44:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.17 -->

**Context.** The Supersoul appears divided though never divided; He is the maintainer of all, and also devours and develops all `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Iva_ — "as if," for comparing; contrasted with _eva_ — "certainly," for emphasis. The verse says He is "as if" divided `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### One Supersoul, many hearts [44:00 – 46:00]

- The Supersoul is in your heart, my heart, every heart — not many Supersouls, but one in many manifestations.
- The moon reflected in a dozen puddles after rain is one moon, yet the reflections lack its full potency — they cannot illuminate the same way.
- The example is material and imperfect: the Supersoul expands and still remains fully the same being.

#### Creation, maintenance and destruction through time [46:00 – 50:00]

- Creation, maintenance and destruction are the three aspects of time; the Absolute Truth is transcendental to these changes, which are agencies of time `46:00`.
- We are instruments; our actions are not final `46:00 – 48:00`:
  - A _Bhāgavatam_ verse describes a wallet dropped on the road — sometimes still there days later or returned by a local boy; yet money in one's pocket may be stolen. If the Lord wills, it is protected.
  - A fertile couple may still not have a child — the husband and wife are not the only doers; God creates through them.
- Remembering the Lord as the ultimate doer, we need not maximise the pain of life's cycles but can view them philosophically `48:00`.
- Parīkṣit Mahārāja saw his curse as Kṛṣṇa coming to him, giving him the opportunity to take shelter in the form of hearing the _Bhāgavatam_ — seeing even death as a form of the Absolute Truth `48:00 – 50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The one Supersoul only seems divided among hearts, and through time He creates, maintains and destroys — so we are instruments, and even loss or death can be seen as His approach. [44:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.18 [50:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.18 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa is the light in all luminous objects, beyond darkness, and situated in the heart of all beings; He is knowledge, the object of knowledge and the goal of knowledge `50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Knowledge, object of knowledge, goal of knowledge — three distinct things `50:00`.
- "Situated in the heart of all" — clearly the Supersoul; Brahman is all-pervading, not specifically in the heart `50:00`.

#### Object vs goal of knowledge [50:00 – 52:00]

- A doctor examining a patient: the object of knowledge is the patient's body, studied through scans; the goal is to cure the patient or improve his health.
- One could write a PhD on a wire — colour, composition, thickness, current capacity — and it may be somewhat useful, but it is not transcendental knowledge that liberates `52:00`.
- For us, the object of knowledge is the _ātmā_, the _paramātmā_ and their loving relationship; understanding this brings liberation `52:00`.

#### Studying the grass but not its designer [52:00 – 54:00]

- On a morning walk, Śrīla Prabhupāda asked a scientist about current scientific knowledge of the soul; told there was none, he said that knowledge is useless — without knowing who you are, how can you know how life should be pursued?
- As Śrīla Prabhupāda left for class, the scientist pointed to grass: "We have two big volumes explaining how grass grows." Śrīla Prabhupāda replied that grass grew even without their books.
- "If God didn't want us to study grass, why did He put it there?" — Śrīla Prabhupāda: you study the grass but not the person who put it there; that is fragmented knowledge, not integrated understanding.
- For science the knowable is matter; for us it is the Supreme Lord, the _ātmā_ and their relationship, and the goal is to know Kṛṣṇa more, love Him more, and be eternally liberated to serve Him `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge must be measured by its goal: studying matter without its designer is fragmented, while knowing the soul, the Supersoul in the heart and their loving relationship leads to liberation and love for Kṛṣṇa. [50:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.19 [54:00 – 57:38]

<!-- vedabase:13.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes the section: He has summarised the field, knowledge and the knowable — the knower having been included, though treated briefly — and says only His devotees can understand this and thereby attain His nature `54:00 – 56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- "My devotee" — the one who can understand; others cannot `56:00`.
- Attaining "My nature" — attaining one's eternal spiritual nature, the spiritual level of existence; becoming liberated `56:00`.

#### Bhakti in the heart of the jñāna section [56:00]

- This knowledge is understood by _bhakti_ — not merely by mental or intellectual speculation.
- _Bhakti_ appears twice in this chapter's _jñāna_ discussion: among the items of knowledge (13.8–13.12), and now as the qualification for understanding.
- Even one practising _jñāna_ must include _bhakti_; if one cultivates _bhakti_, _jñāna_ will come — not necessarily spontaneously, but through steady study and consistent practice.
- The next class will discuss _puruṣa_ and _prakṛti_ `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Right in the heart of the knowledge section, Kṛṣṇa says only His devotees truly understand the field, knowledge and the knowable — so bhakti is the key even to jñāna. [54:00 – 57:38]
</Callout>
