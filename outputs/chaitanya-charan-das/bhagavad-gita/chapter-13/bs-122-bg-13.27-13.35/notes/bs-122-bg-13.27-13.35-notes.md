## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 13.27 [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.27 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to know that whatever comes into existence — moving or non-moving, even the smallest thing — comes by the contact of the field (_kṣetra_) and the knower of the field (_kṣetrajña_) `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kṣetra_ — the field; e.g. the colours, paints and canvas of a painting `02:00`.
- _Kṣetrajña_ — the knower of the field; e.g. the painter `02:00`.
- _Jñāna-cakṣu_ — seeing with the eyes of knowledge, which brings detachment `02:00`.

#### Reducing diversity to fundamentals [02:00]

- The variety of this world can be reduced to fundamentals, and that capacity gives a more enlightened view.
- A beautiful painting is a combination of field (colour, paint, canvas) and knower (the painter); a big building is likewise such a combination.

#### Seeing more deeply [02:00 – 04:00]

- Philosophical vision lets us see things more deeply, more seriously and ultimately more lovingly — seeing lovingly comes when we see Kṛṣṇa.
- Before that, we see more dispassionately: towards material things we want detachment, towards spiritual things attachment.
- When so many things seem captivating, seeing that they are "nothing but that combination" keeps us from getting so worked up.
- Beyond this is the Supreme, the controller of everything, who comes a little later in the section `04:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Seeing every captivating thing as merely a combination of matter and spirit is the first step of philosophical vision: it frees us from infatuation with the world and prepares us to see more lovingly. [02:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.28 [04:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.28 -->

**Context.** Having established that existence is made of the field and its knower, Kṛṣṇa describes the result: equal vision — seeing the transcendental within all perishable bodies `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Parameśvara_ — the _ācāryas_ explain it differently; Śrīla Prabhupāda says it can refer both to the Supreme Lord and to the _jīva_. _Parama_ need not mean "supreme"; it can mean transcendental — an _īśvara_ who is not material, though currently in material consciousness `04:00 – 06:00`.
- Perishable and imperishable — the bodies are destructible; the souls within are imperishable `06:00`.
- "One who sees thus truly sees" — a phrase Kṛṣṇa uses several times; in 5.5 it describes seeing the common purpose of _sāṅkhya_ and _yoga_ beyond superficial differences `06:00`.

#### Relevance on the battlefield [06:00]

- Arjuna was concerned about killing his elders, especially Droṇa and Bhīṣma.
- Kṛṣṇa says: see that they are not the bodies but the souls; they are in perishable bodies but are themselves imperishable.

#### Knowledge removes prejudice [08:00 – 10:00]

- A great result of knowledge is the capacity to go beyond superficial biases, prejudices and emotionally induced misconceptions `08:00`.
- Blanket statements that equate an entire nationality, religious community or ethnic group with a negative trait — and the jokes made about such groups — are characteristic of the bodily misconception `08:00`.
- Bodily differences exist and may bring cultural and sociological differences that must be dealt with appropriately; the teacher does not deny them, but we also have to see the commonality — every person is a soul `08:00`.
- Dynastic feuds: one family member is killed, and sons, grandsons and great-grandsons go on fighting long after the original feud has become irrelevant — all based on bodily conceptions `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### Discrimination within devotee circles [10:00 – 16:00]

- We laugh at these "simple bodily conceptions," yet we have our own: "this is my guru's disciple, that is another guru's disciple" `10:00`.
- Our relationship with the spiritual master is spiritual, but we must check our consciousness: am I glorifying my guru so that I can feel great? `10:00`.
- Divisions should create greater cohesion, not dissension `10:00`:
  - _Varṇāśrama_ was meant as a social arrangement like an extended family; within the _varṇas_ were _jātis_, e.g. different kinds of _brāhmaṇas_ `10:00 – 12:00`.
  - Belonging to a community is positive if it brings a sense of a bigger family, connection and caring; it is negative if it leads to conflict and power struggles `12:00`.
- A devotee's defining identity should be the connection with Kṛṣṇa — not an identity defined in opposition to others. Defining a religion merely by opposition to another is a very superficial understanding `12:00`.
- "My guru is an Indian guru, yours is not" is a foolish idea: the guru's transcendence rests on his connection with the transcendental _paramparā_ and the message he delivers `12:00`.
- Equally, one could draw the opposite inference from the fact that none of the first eleven gurus Śrīla Prabhupāda appointed were Indian; we can make any inference we want, but Prabhupāda never followed such a quota system — whoever knows Kṛṣṇa can be a spiritual master `14:00`.
- The same applies to devotees generally: the body affects behaviour and attitudes, which need not be denied, but we must not pigeonhole people by their designations — spirituality is meant to bring us out of pigeonholes, not deeper into them `14:00 – 16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
True seeing perceives the imperishable soul and the Lord within every perishable body; such equal vision dissolves prejudice, feuds and even devotional sectarianism, because our defining identity is our connection with Kṛṣṇa. [04:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.29 [16:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.29 -->

**Context.** The next verse describes the benefit of equal vision: one who sees the Lord equally situated everywhere does not degrade himself by the mind and approaches the transcendental destination `16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Īśvara_ — interpreted by some as the individual soul and by others as the Supersoul `16:00`. Śrīla Prabhupāda's reading as the soul is explained: the soul is the master of the body — the body acts at least partially by the soul's will, and the soul goes on to another body when this one is destroyed `16:00`.
- Equally situated — the _īśvara_ is in my heart, in that person's heart, in everyone's heart `20:00`.
- Destructive mentality (purport) — acting in a self-defeating way that destroys oneself, rather than acting so as to become purified, elevated and liberated `28:00`.

#### Material vision degrades [16:00 – 20:00]

- It is material vision that makes us hold grudges, create vendettas, and leads to lust, anger, greed, pride and illusion, which degrade the soul `18:00`.
- Without equal vision: "this person is wealthy — I want that money; this one is poor and useless" — and so we neglect and disrespect people `18:00`.
- A man sees a woman as an attractive object, lust develops and is acted upon, leading to karmic entanglement — it all starts from the inability to see beyond matter to spirit `18:00`.
- The focus of this verse is vision and its effect on our destination `18:00`.

#### Seeing the Lord's hand in others' actions [20:00 – 22:00]

- When the son of a sage cursed Parīkṣit Mahārāja, Parīkṣit said it was by the Supreme Lord's will — though the boy was simply giving in to uncontrolled anger. The Lord is so expert that He can use someone else's anger to elevate and instruct us `20:00`.
- How _yogamāyā_ and _mahāmāyā_ work is complex `20:00`.
- In the _Bhāgavatam_, Vidura is verbally castigated by Duryodhana — called the son of a _śūdra_, a traitor biting the hand that fed him `20:00 – 22:00`:
  - Duryodhana acted from his own greed and envy — that is true.
  - But Vidura did not have to respond in _mahāmāyā_; seeing with spiritual vision, he responded in _yogamāyā_ and saw the situation as the Lord's arrangement to disentangle him.
  - He knew a war was coming, did not want to fight for the Kauravas, had tried his best to counsel, and when insulted he simply departed — taking the opportunity to go closer to Kṛṣṇa `22:00`.

#### Devotional reinterpretation [22:00 – 28:00]

- Devotional reinterpretation of events is vital for spiritual advancement `22:00`.
- The teacher's example: Jesus preached in a limited area with few followers, was betrayed by Judas and crucified — a devastating defeat for followers who expected a conquering messiah. Afterwards they understood it as a victory by which they had been redeemed, became more devoted and more inspired to share his message, and what could have ended as a small whimper became one of the world's largest religions `22:00 – 26:00`.
- This does not mean anyone can interpret reality however they like: events will happen that we don't always understand, but devotees can always ask, "What can I do in this situation that takes me closer to Kṛṣṇa?" `26:00`.
- Often we let small things become very big because we see them negatively `26:00`.
- Sometimes terrible things happen to devotee communities: temples demolished, devotees accused of being a cult, arrested, and in the former Soviet Union even tortured. The Lord may allow such things through others' greed, arrogance and lust, yet each is also an opportunity to go deeper with Kṛṣṇa `26:00 – 28:00`.
- To the extent we pray for this vision we receive it, and the more we have it, the more we advance `28:00`.

#### Staying on the road [28:00]

- By Kṛṣṇa's and Śrīla Prabhupāda's mercy we have a proper path; we only need to move along it.
- If we take a U-turn or go off the road and fall, it is our mind that makes us do so; if we stay steady, the mind cannot do that, and we move on.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Seeing the Lord equally present in every heart — even behind those who insult or persecute us — keeps the mind from dragging us into grudges and lust, and turns every event into an opportunity to move closer to Kṛṣṇa. [16:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.30 [30:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.30 -->

**Context.** One who sees that all activities are performed by material nature, and that the self is not the doer, truly sees `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- "One who sees thus truly sees" — the phrase of 13.28 returns, now split over the third and fourth lines of the verse `30:00`.
- Non-doer — material nature is the doer of things; the soul is the non-doer `30:00`.

#### The opposite of 3.27 [30:00 – 32:00]

- 3.27, the more famous verse, describes the deluded soul who thinks "I am the doer."
- Here Kṛṣṇa describes the enlightened person who sees properly: not "I am the doer," but material nature acts.

#### Why material nature, not the Lord? [32:00]

- Kṛṣṇa has said elsewhere (9.10) that all movements of _prakṛti_ happen under His supervision — that is definitely true.
- But here He describes a _jñānī_ whose conception is evolving: the vision moves from matter towards spirit. The _jñānīs_ focus less on God as controller of the world and more on going beyond this world to transcendental spirit.
- So they see matter acting by its own patterns, and therefore see no need to become attached, captivated or deluded by it.

#### Attractive features come from prakṛti [32:00 – 36:00]

- People attract us by singing beautifully, speaking eloquently, managing efficiently, or by looks, grace and poise `32:00`.
- These features come ultimately from _prakṛti_, through past _karma_ and learned behaviour. The teacher does not deny personal endeavour: an eloquent speaker may have practised for long hours, and credit should be given `32:00 – 34:00`.
- Generic features show the point: no human sings as sweetly as some birds, no record-setting sprinter is as fast as a leopard or cheetah, and our eyes lack the piercing vision of an eagle or hawk — all these attributes come from material nature `34:00`.
- Seeing others with spiritual vision means one is not devastated when the world seems to fall apart, because one perceives and connects with a reality beyond it `34:00 – 36:00`.

#### Responsibility and forgiveness [36:00 – 38:00]

- If someone acts with hostility or lust towards us, we defend ourselves; such a person is definitely responsible and must be punished — as a king would, and as Lord Rāma did to Rāvaṇa `36:00`.
- Yet one also recognises that the modes induce lust and the person has become a puppet of it — not a helpless puppet, since people choose to become lusty, greedy or angry `36:00`.
- Forgiveness is important for ourselves, because unforgiven resentment burns us; recognising the principles by which material nature acts helps us forgive. If we play with fire, we get burnt `36:00`.
- The teacher stresses the example is not meant to condone any crime, only to show the difference between materialistic and spiritual vision `38:00`.

#### Seeing our own conditioning [38:00 – 40:00]

- When lust overcomes us, thinking "I am a lusty person, I'll never control myself, I'm so fallen" means identifying with our material nature `38:00`.
- Actually I am the soul, whose spiritual nature is to love and serve Kṛṣṇa. Lust, greed and the like are like an infection — superficial; at my core I am pure, and the impure periphery can be removed, certainly with Kṛṣṇa's mercy `38:00`.
- This does not make us irresponsible: "my father was angry" justifies neither his anger nor mine; genes and influences may flow through the blood, but I have the capacity to choose `38:00 – 40:00`.
- Recognising material nature lets us distance ourselves: the more we identify emotionally, the further we are dragged; without identification, its movements get no further fuel `40:00`.
- We do not neglect the body — eating, resting, sleeping — but we do not obsess over these as the goal of life `40:00`.
- Because of desires one is put into difficult circumstances; seeing things as the workings of material nature, one gives up those desires `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Seeing material nature as the doer lets us forgive others without condoning wrong, and lets us face our own lust or anger as a removable infection rather than our identity — distancing ourselves from conditioning while still owning our choices. [30:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.31 [42:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.31 -->

**Context.** The focus shifts further from matter to spirit: one who sees that living entities have different natures yet are ultimately one, because all are spirit, attains the Brahman situation — a transcendental position `42:00`.

#### One substance, many appearances [42:00 – 44:00]

- The teacher refers to the Bhagavad-gītā picture of the Supersoul present in various bodies — in the meditating _yogī_'s heart, in a flying bird's heart, in the hearts of people approaching the _yogī_ — with the soul also present in each.
- From one Supreme Being everything has expanded: the Supersoul expansions, and the material and spiritual energies.
- The instruction: see beyond the diversity of appearances to the constancy of substance.
- The diversity itself has come about by the sanction of the Supreme Lord, according to the desires and _karma_ of the living beings `44:00`.

#### Not getting captivated by dualities [44:00]

- Seeing someone as a demigod, a human being, a dog or a cat is material vision, not actual vision.
- Don't get captivated by the world's dualities; see beyond them to the higher reality.
- The next verse introduces two significant words illustrated in 13.33 and 13.34, so 13.32 is in that sense the root of both.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Behind the diverse natures and species stands one spiritual substance expanded from the one Supreme; seeing that constancy beneath the variety lifts one to the Brahman platform. [42:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.32 [44:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.32 -->

**Context.** The soul is transcendental and imperishable; though in the body, it neither acts nor becomes entangled `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paramātmā_ — here, the transcendental soul `46:00`.
- Imperishable and beginningless — no ending and no beginning; in that sense beyond time, beyond space and beyond the modes `46:00`.

#### The flow of the section [46:00]

- The teacher recaps: 13.27 — everything is matter and spirit; then see that spirit is common everywhere, and the mind will not degrade you (13.29); see the oneness of spirit beyond the diversity of forms; the soul is not the doer.
- Now the question: if the soul is so completely different from the varieties of this world, how does it interact with them? That is explained in the next two verses.

#### Not entangled because not acting selfishly [48:00]

- Because it is transcendental — beyond space, time and modes — the _ātmā_ does not become entangled.
- One is not entangled because one is not doing action with selfish intention for its fruit.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul is beginningless and beyond time, space and the modes; though in the body, it is not the doer and is not entangled — the root idea that the next two verses illustrate. [46:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.33 [48:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.33 -->

**Context.** The first illustration — how the soul is not entangled: the all-pervading sky (_ākāśa_) is not contaminated by what moves through it `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ākāśa_ — not easily translated here; it can mean the sky or ether, as discussed with 9.6, where the wind moves within _ākāśa_ but does not go beyond it `48:00 – 50:00`.

#### The sky and the dirt [48:00 – 50:00]

- Sometimes wind full of dirt flies through the sky, and the sky temporarily appears contaminated; then everything falls away and the sky remains as it is.
- Either reading works: the grosser elements cannot contaminate ether, or the vast sky is not permanently affected by the wind and dirt flowing through it.

#### The soul in any body [50:00]

- The soul may be in an elephant's body, a human body or a child's body; each body has its own program by which it behaves in certain predefined ways, though within that there is some freedom.
- The point is not the body's relative freedom but that the soul is not affected — even in the body of a fox or a monkey, considered relatively low creatures, the soul remains transcendental, as the sky is unaffected.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Like the vast sky that dust storms only seem to stain, the soul passes through every kind of body — high or low — without ever being contaminated by it. [48:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.34 [52:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.34 -->

**Context.** The second illustration — how the transcendental soul nonetheless affects matter: as the sun illumines the whole world, the soul illumines the body with consciousness `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### The mind–body question [52:00 – 54:00]

- The sky is detached and does not affect what is in it, but spirit clearly affects matter: as the teacher speaks, the six changes — growth, maintenance, reproduction and so on — take place in the body.
- If soul and matter are entirely disconnected, how do they act together? This is a major question in Western philosophy; the teacher says the inability to answer it led to the exile of the soul from mainstream Western intellectual circles.
- The Vedic explanation: the connection is mediated by the Supersoul, who controls both matter and spirit and enables the soul's desires to be transmitted and appropriate changes to be made in matter `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### The sun and the earth [54:00]

- The sun is far from the earth, yet at sunrise flowers blossom, birds chirp and vegetation comes alive.
- The sun is not responsible for whether good crops or weeds grow — that depends on which seeds were sown.
- Likewise, the soul illumines the body with consciousness, and that consciousness causes activity in the world.
- Vedic culture understood that the sun is not the only luminous source and that higher planets also illumine; the verse's "world" refers to our known world — a contextual example to illustrate a specific point.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda emphasises that consciousness is not a product of combinations of matter; it is a symptom of the living entity.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul, though untouched by matter, enlivens the body with consciousness as the distant sun enlivens the earth; consciousness is the soul's symptom, not a product of matter. [52:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 13.35 [54:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:13.35 -->

**Context.** The last verse puts it all together: one who understands the difference between matter and spirit, and how spirit can be liberated from matter, attains the supreme destination `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Putting the chapter together [56:00]

- The process of liberation was briefly discussed in 13.25–13.26 and will be discussed again later.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda gives the purport of the whole chapter: one should know the distinction between the body, the owner of the body and the Supersoul, and devotional service is the best way to know this; the Gītā's flow of thought leads to that conclusion.
- Since the modes have been described as what causes bondage, the next chapter takes up the three modes.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The chapter's knowledge culminates in seeing the distinction between body, soul and Supersoul — and Śrīla Prabhupāda concludes that devotional service is the best way to attain that vision and the supreme destination. [56:00]
</Callout>
