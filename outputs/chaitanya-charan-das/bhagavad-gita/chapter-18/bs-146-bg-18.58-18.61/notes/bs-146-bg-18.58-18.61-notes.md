## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 18.58 [00:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.58 -->

**Context.** The teacher calls this one of the most directly indicative verses in the Gītā for the phrase "Kṛṣṇa consciousness": people sometimes say the term sounds modern rather than Vedic, but here Kṛṣṇa says, in effect, become conscious of Me and by My mercy you will cross all obstacles; out of pride, if you do not hear Me, you will be lost `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prasāda_ — mercy; "by My mercy" `00:00`, `04:00`.
- "Cross over" — to float over; compared with Kṛṣṇa's similar wording about crossing over by surrender in 7.14 and His being the deliverer from the ocean of material existence `04:00`.
- _Ahaṅkāra_ — pride; here "I am great — big enough to solve any problem, I don't need anyone's help" `02:00`, `20:00`.
- "You will be lost" — the result of not hearing Kṛṣṇa out of pride `02:00`, `24:00`.
- _Whirlpool_ (from the purport) — a part of a river or ocean where once one goes in one is sucked down; similarly desires, agitations and frustrations suck one into the cycle of birth and death `26:00`.

#### Instructing by contrasting [02:00 – 04:00]

- 18.58 contrasts what happens with and without Kṛṣṇa consciousness — "with Kṛṣṇa we flourish, without Kṛṣṇa we perish."
- Kṛṣṇa has used this method repeatedly:
  - 3.31–3.32 and 4.39–4.40 — one result if you have faith in Me, another if you do not;
  - 9.25 — depending on whom we choose to worship, we get the corresponding result.
- The teacher's conclusion: the Gītā is not so much a book of commandments as a book of choices and consequences.

#### Obstacles come to all; the response differs [04:00 – 10:00]

- Crossing obstacles does not mean a magic wand removes them when one becomes a devotee; the material world is a place of obstacles for devotees and non-devotees alike.
- The difference is in attitude and consciousness — in understanding what purpose problems serve.
- Why problems hurt: as souls we are _ānandamaya_, but misidentifying with the body, we limit our enjoying nature to the material level, where our plans for enjoyment meet obstacles `06:00`.
- For a non-devotee, material enjoyment is the only enjoyment, so obstructed enjoyment ends in frustration `08:00`:
  - expressed externally — violence, beating or shouting at others;
  - expressed internally — self-pity, inferiority complex, even suicide;
  - other outlets the teacher names — alcoholism, depression.
- For a devotee, problems lead to a sequence of three `08:00 – 10:00`:
  1. **Realization** — accepting as reality what scripture says: this world is a place of misery, and "I am experiencing it now."
  2. **Renunciation** — not frustration, but giving up the enjoying and controlling mentality: "I cannot be the enjoyer or controller, but I can be the servant," still planning to do the best service possible.
  3. **Devotion** — the service attitude brings peace and, in the long run, pleasure; "Kṛṣṇa is always my master, He will never leave me," and attraction to Kṛṣṇa increases.

#### Transcending problems: the skyscraper and the airplane [10:00 – 12:00]

- Transcending problems means problems are there, but we do not let them dominate our consciousness.
- Standing next to a skyscraper, I am tiny and it is giant; seen from an airplane, the skyscraper is small.
- Alone, we feel puny before a problem; connected with Kṛṣṇa, we remain tiny before Him but not before the problem, because the problem is not big for Kṛṣṇa, and He is with us.

#### The demigods' prayers and Prahlāda's prayers [12:00 – 16:00]

- In the Seventh Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_, the demigods' prayers to Lord Nṛsiṁha (end of chapter 8) purified them but did not pacify the Lord; Prahlāda's prayers (chapter 9) did.
- Indra's prayer: Hiraṇyakaśipu's power and cruelty had dominated our hearts so that we could not think of You or worship You; now that You have killed him, our hearts will be free to serve You again.
- Prahlāda, by contrast, lived in the same house as Hiraṇyakaśipu — whom the teacher calls a "mega terrorist" terrorising the whole universe — and was tormented, threatened, insulted and nearly assassinated, yet stayed determined, because Viṣṇu remained fixed in his consciousness and was never displaced by fear of Hiraṇyakaśipu.
- The teacher identifies this as the difference between mixed devotion and pure devotion.
- If problems trouble us too much, it indicates we are not properly sheltered in Kṛṣṇa; the more we take shelter, the less problems shake us and the better we deal with them `16:00`.
- "Don't tell Kṛṣṇa how big our problems are; tell the problems how big Kṛṣṇa is" `16:00`.

#### Perplexed but never discouraged [16:00 – 20:00]

- Quoting a Prabhupāda purport, the teacher says a devotee may be perplexed but is never discouraged:
  - **Perplexed** — indecisive; not knowing how to serve.
  - **Discouraged** — not feeling like serving.
- Prabhupāda's own life as illustration:
  - Just as his mission seemed to be picking up in India, a conspiracy practically threw him out of the place meant to be his international headquarters in Jhansi.
  - He used intelligence: Indians were not taking it seriously, so he went to America; there people seemed satisfied with daily pleasures, so he went to those rejecting them and seeking something higher — the hippies.
  - In the Bowery, a roommate went mad on drugs and attacked him; he had to leave immediately to save his life, and on the street, among senseless hippies and bums, he realised he was as homeless as they were.
  - He had realistic hope based on "Kṛṣṇa is my protector," so he kept moving forward — e.g. calling someone from his programme to find another place to stay.
- A devotee always understands that Kṛṣṇa is the well-wisher and that the best thing in any situation is to serve Kṛṣṇa with all one's senses, resources and wealth `20:00`.

#### Pride means hearing the mind [20:00]

- Not hearing Kṛṣṇa does not leave us hearing no one — we then hear the mind, which is already working to drag us down and make life miserable.
- We therefore desperately need protection from being dragged down by the mind.

#### Don't make Kṛṣṇa consciousness conditional [22:00 – 26:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is not a harsh God; He gives as much facility as possible. A devotee facing severe problems may weaken in practice and start mixing ideas from various people `20:00 – 22:00`.
- The more conscious of Kṛṣṇa we are, the stronger our vertical relationship becomes; that gives tolerance, patience and persistence, so we can act calmly and intelligently in material life too `22:00 – 24:00`.
- Without that, we are like a small boat on an impossibly vast ocean with giant killer waves from all directions — we may survive a few moments but eventually succumb `24:00`.
- Vital difference for steadiness: not "when my life becomes problem-free I will become conscious of Kṛṣṇa," but "when I become conscious of Kṛṣṇa, my life becomes problem-free" — in the sense of transcending problems `24:00`.
- The second half of the verse — being lost — raises a question: many godless people seem to live quite normally; Kṛṣṇa answers it in the next two verses `24:00 – 26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Problems come to devotee and non-devotee alike; Kṛṣṇa's mercy lets the devotee cross them by turning suffering into realization, renunciation and devotion, while pride leaves one hearing only the mind and lost. Don't tell Kṛṣṇa how big the problem is — tell the problem how big Kṛṣṇa is. [04:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.59 – 18.60 [26:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.59 -->

<!-- vedabase:18.60 -->

**Context.** These two verses elaborate how one is "lost" by taking shelter of _ahaṅkāra_ instead of Kṛṣṇa. The teacher recites both and discusses them together `26:00 – 28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ahaṅkāra_ — "I am powerful, I am strong, I know what to do and I'll do it"; Arjuna's resolve not to fight is falsely directed determination `26:00`.
- _Svabhāva_ — one's nature, by which one is conditioned, almost bound, to do certain kinds of work `28:00`.
- _Vāsanā_ — passion or desire induced by lust, anger, greed, pride and illusion; this must be regulated `30:00`.
- _Vṛtti_ (as the teacher uses it) — innate psychophysical tendencies: _brāhmaṇa_, _kṣatriya_, _vaiśya_ or _śūdra_ nature `30:00`.
- _Artha_ — not just money but holistic prosperity, though it primarily expresses itself as money `32:00`.

#### Arjuna would fight anyway [26:00 – 30:00]

- From the purport: Arjuna was a military man born of _kṣatriya_ nature, so he was going to fight, whether by Kṛṣṇa's will or by his own nature.
- If Arjuna prematurely became a renunciate and went to the forest to beg alms, and someone refused him and said "go away," he would become enraged, draw his sword and attack — for a _kṣatriya_, being refused like that is almost impossibly insulting.
- The teacher links this to the enormous insult the Pāṇḍavas felt at being dispossessed and at their wife being dishonoured.
- Point: we all have to act according to our nature; if one refuses now, later one will be forced to act helplessly.

#### Svabhāva is not vāsanā [30:00 – 32:00]

- Kṛṣṇa speaks of _svabhāva_, not _vāsanā_.
- _Vāsanā_ must be regulated — e.g. the natural attraction between man and woman is regulated by marriage, not simply acted upon; 3.36–3.43 is a whole section on conquering lust.
- _Svabhāva_ refers to one's innate nature (_vṛtti_); acting according to it is indispensable.

#### Direct, don't reject or repress [32:00 – 34:00]

- The _kṣatriya_ protects, fights, rules and takes taxes (whereas the _brāhmaṇa_ accepts charity); his ruling must follow _rāja-dharma_, given in scripture and reiterated by Bhīṣma to Yudhiṣṭhira in the Śānti and Anuśāsana Parvas of the _Mahābhārata_.
- Regulation directs one's _svabhāva_; it does not reject or repress it.
- Example: a person of _vaiśya_ nature — industrious, producing _artha_ — told to live as a _brāhmaṇa_ would be miserable; while studying scripture he would be thinking how much money the book would fetch.
- We need to act according to our nature if we want to become purified.

#### Then why speak the Gītā? [34:00 – 36:00]

- If Arjuna would fight sooner or later anyway, why did Kṛṣṇa speak the whole Gītā? So that he would fight at the proper time, place and circumstance, in the proper consciousness.
- A soldier who shoots an enemy at the front may get a medal; the same soldier who shoots a neighbour in a petty quarrel at home may be hanged. The nature to fight must be regulated by higher authority — governmental rules for a soldier, scriptural principles for a _kṣatriya_.
- A _kṣatriya_ fighting a _dharma-yuddha_ becomes elevated; that is the purpose of the Gītā's instruction to Arjuna `36:00`.
- From the purport: everyone acts under a particular combination of the modes, but anyone who voluntarily engages under the Supreme Lord's direction becomes glorious `36:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We cannot escape our nature — refuse it now and it will force us later — so the Gītā's aim is not to reject or repress our svabhāva but to direct it by higher authority, in the right time, place, circumstance and consciousness, so that it purifies and elevates us. [28:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.61 [36:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.61 -->

**Context.** If we must all act according to nature, is nature supreme? No — above nature is the _īśvara_, the Supreme Lord, situated in the region of the heart of all living beings `36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Īśvara_ — controller; here the Supersoul `36:00`.
- "In the region of the heart" — not simply "in the heart" `36:00 – 38:00`.
- Wandering / causing to wander — the verse's key verb; the same root as the verse Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu spoke to the Gosvāmīs about souls wandering throughout the universe `40:00`, `50:00`.
- _Yantra_ — machine; the body, made of _māyā_ `42:00`.
- _Ārūḍha_ — situated on, mounted on; Kṛṣṇa uses the word in 6.4 for stages of yoga, and Viṣṇu is said to be mounted on Garuḍa `42:00`.
- _Māyā_ — can mean illusion, or material nature in general `42:00`.

#### "In the region of the heart" [36:00 – 40:00]

- "Heart" has two senses:
  - the biological heart that pumps blood;
  - the metaphorical heart, the seat of emotions — Cupid's arrow does not make a hole in the biological heart.
- The soul and Supersoul sit next to each other in the region of the heart.
- Heart transplant analogy: like a king on a throne — if a leg of the throne breaks, attendants bring another, the king steps aside, and then sits on the new one. The soul is not bound to the heart; it moves aside, the new heart is placed, and the person functions normally `38:00`.

#### Wandering, not travelling [40:00 – 42:00]

- Travelling is generally purposeful (e.g. going to Mayapur for a _yātrā_); wandering is aimless — going wherever a mood leads.
- The soul's journey in material existence is largely wandering: circumstances inject desires, and those desires shape where the soul goes.
- Born in Punjab, the soul thinks parathas are very nice; born in South India, dosa — such desires are circumstantial and have nothing to do with the soul's intrinsic nature.

#### The body as a machine [42:00 – 46:00]

- A _yantra_ is not conscious in itself; it is made of components and works by particular laws — e.g. pillars collapse under too much weight, and legs or back sprain under too heavy a load.
- Science calls the universe entirely mechanistic; the teacher says the universe is not, but Kṛṣṇa does call the body a machine — one that interacts with a supervising "Supreme Mechanic," as in 9.10.
- Like a young person given a bike who rides fast and loud just to turn heads, the soul wanders on the body for pleasure, here and there.

#### The video-game analogy [46:00 – 48:00]

- A player affects the virtual world only through the game's mechanism — buttons, levers, cursor — moving a policeman to catch a thief.
- The arrangement is designed by the game's developer, whose intelligence sets up the whole system; the player identifies with the character.
- Similarly, Kṛṣṇa as _īśvara_ oversees the world, which is like a virtual reality.
- The teacher mentions _Maya: The World as Virtual Reality_ by Dr. Richard Thompson (Sadāpūta Prabhu), a mathematician and disciple of Śrīla Prabhupāda, which correlates virtual reality with _māyā_ to shed light on puzzles of modern science `48:00`.
- Within the game each has an allotted role — the thief cannot suddenly become the police; the _kṣatriya_ acts as a _kṣatriya_, the _brāhmaṇa_ as a _brāhmaṇa_ — and acting properly in that role purifies us `48:00`.

#### Desire, sanction, execution [50:00 – 56:00]

- Does the Lord cause our wanderings, overriding free will? The teacher explains: the soul desires, the Lord sanctions, material nature executes.
- Neurosurgery experiment (as the teacher narrates it):
  - A Nobel laureate neurosurgeon stimulated the motor area of a patient's brain; the hand rose, and the patient said "you raised my hand, I didn't"; when the stimulus stopped and the hand fell, "you put it down."
  - Asked to raise his hand himself, the patient said "I raised my hand."
  - Stimulating other brain areas never produced the feeling "I am the activator."
  - Such neurosurgeons concluded that the brain is like a computer whose user is outside it — "extra-cerebral."
- Printer analogy: whether I press print or someone else does, the initiator is different from the printer head and from the machine moving it `52:00 – 54:00`.
- Science sees only the execution; it misses the soul's desiring and the Supersoul's sanctioning, since it largely restricts itself to the study of matter `56:00`.

#### "Directs the wanderings" [56:00 – 60:00]

- The Lord "causing to wander" means He sanctions desires and provides the facility for the soul to move according to them — like the institution that sets up a casino or game, within which players win or lose.
- Prabhupāda translates the word as "causes to wander" in the word-for-word, but "directs the wanderings" in the translation — like a film director, invisible yet very important.
- Living entities are not mere puppets; they have free will, but the overall framework is supervised: in a human body one cannot fly like a bird or bark like a dog; the soul in a dog's body cannot suddenly chirp.
- Summary of the flow: 18.59–18.60 — we are controlled to some extent by nature; 18.61 — we and nature are controlled by the Lord; 18.62 (next class) — therefore surrender to Him and attain auspiciousness `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul wanders on the body-machine driven by circumstantial desires, but it is not alone: the Lord in the region of the heart sanctions those desires and directs the overall framework. Since both we and our nature are under His supervision, the sensible response is surrender to Him. [36:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
