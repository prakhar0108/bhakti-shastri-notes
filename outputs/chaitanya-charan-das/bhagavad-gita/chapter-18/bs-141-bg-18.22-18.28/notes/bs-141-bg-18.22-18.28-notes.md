## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 18.22 [00:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.22 -->

**Context.** "Knowledge in ignorance" seems a paradox, since knowledge and ignorance are considered opposites. Kṛṣṇa describes it as knowledge attached to one kind of work as if it were everything, without regard to truth, and very meagre `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ekasmin_ / _kṛtsna-vat_ — taking one thing to be everything: considering one part of reality to be the totality of reality `00:00`, `02:00`.
- _Kārye saktam_ — becoming attached to one activity as if it were everything `00:00`.
- _Ahaitukam_ — normally used positively for causeless, unmotivated, pure devotion; here it means senseless — having no rational or intelligent basis `00:00 – 02:00`.
- _Atattvārtha-vat_ — without any true meaning or true value `02:00`.
- _Alpam_ — meagre: dealing with only part of reality, not complete knowledge `02:00`.

#### A generic characteristic, not a list [02:00]

- Kṛṣṇa does not call specific things knowledge in ignorance; He gives the generic characteristic.
- Depending on time, place and circumstance, different schools of thought can be classified as goodness, passion or ignorance.

#### Materialism as a case in point [02:00 – 06:00]

- Materialism is widespread and often considered progressive — supposedly giving up old-fashioned, pre-scientific, mythology-based religious dogmas.
- But it essentially assumes one part of reality — matter — to be the totality. We are spirit-based creatures; matter is our outer covering, not our inner substance.
- Many materialist thinkers have their own logic and intellectual brilliance, but their vision is unidimensional: they reduce a poly-dimensional reality — matter, spirit, and the Supreme Lord who is the source of both — to one aspect.
- Reductionism example: a mother's love for her child is explained as nothing but a secretion of chemicals in the brain `06:00`.
- No one has demonstrated how matter leads to the emergence of consciousness to a reasonable degree of satisfaction, yet this is assumed as the default way of looking at things `06:00`.

#### Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura on material knowledge [06:00 – 10:00]

- The teacher cites a song of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura describing all material learning as an opulence of illusion (_māyāra vaibhava_):
  - it makes the living being like a donkey — very strong, working hard, but with intellectual faculties not used at a level much higher than animals';
  - it is an obstacle (_bādhā_) to worshipping Kṛṣṇa, because one thinks material knowledge holds the solution to all problems and feels no need for God.
- _Māyā-vaibhava_ is like becoming a billionaire in a dream — how long will it last? `08:00`.
- Hard work itself is not bad, but it should be for a meaningful purpose, not for something without real value `06:00`.

#### Science, God, and Pascal [08:00 – 10:00]

- Modern intellectual history is largely Western, and European thought spread over the world.
- From the conflict of Galileo and Copernicus with the Church over a geocentric or heliocentric universe, many came to think that science has all the answers and does not need God; enamoured by material knowledge, they gave up God `10:00`.
- The more people are intoxicated by material knowledge, the less need for God they feel.
- But if one keeps acquiring knowledge without being infatuated by it — as Pascal said, a little science takes a person away from God, but immersion in science brings one back; various scientists have said similar things `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge in ignorance takes one fragment of reality — such as matter — to be the whole, and so, however brilliant, becomes an obstacle to worshipping Kṛṣṇa; Kṛṣṇa's thrust is: don't live in ignorance, live in goodness. [02:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.23 [12:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.23 -->

**Context.** Action in goodness is regulated action, performed without attachment, without love or hatred, and without desire for fruitive results `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Niyatam_ — prescribed by scripture `12:00`.
- _Saṅga-rahitam_ — done without attachment `12:00`.
- _Arāga-dveṣataḥ kṛtam_ — done without attachment or aversion `12:00`.
- _Aphala-prepsunā_ — without the motivation of the fruit of the result `12:00`.

#### A consistent theme of the Gītā [12:00]

- To the extent we are attached to the fruits of work, we cannot think about the transcendental reality that exists beyond action and fruit.
- Kṛṣṇa wants to raise our consciousness to spiritual reality; that is why He repeatedly says not to be attached to material fruits — they bind us in material existence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Action in goodness is scriptural duty done without attachment, aversion or hankering for results — freeing consciousness to rise to spiritual reality. [12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.24 [12:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.24 -->

**Context.** Action performed with great effort by one seeking to gratify his desires, enacted from a sense of false ego, is action in passion `12:00 – 14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kāmepsunā_ — work done with worldly desire, longing for the fruits `14:00`.
- _Sāhaṅkāreṇa_ — with arrogance, thinking "I am the doer"; contrasted with 18.17, where one without false ego is not bound `14:00`.
- _Bahulāyāsam_ — done with great trouble, severe labour `14:00`.
- _Ugra-karma_ — intense work, which can be passionate or ignorant depending on the activity and consciousness `14:00`.

#### Churning the milk ocean [14:00 – 16:00]

- In the pastime of churning the milk ocean in the _Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_, the demons and demigods both wanted to hold the head of the serpent Vāsuki, thinking it the more honourable end.
- The Lord saw the demons' stubbornness and let them hold the head.
- When Vāsuki was pulled as the churning rope, poisonous flames came from his mouth and scorched the demons.
- Prabhupāda writes in the purport that the demons worked very hard without profit even to themselves.

#### Never satisfied [16:00]

- In passion one feels driven to do more and more and is never satisfied: "I want something more, I want something more."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Action in passion is great labour driven by desire and the ego of doership — like the demons churning at the serpent's fiery head, working hard and gaining nothing, never satisfied. [14:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.25 [16:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.25 -->

**Context.** Action performed in illusion, disregarding scriptural injunctions and future bondage, and causing destruction and violence, is action in ignorance `16:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Anubandha_ — that which causes bondage; all work causes bondage, but work in ignorance is an especially excruciating binding, like addiction, very difficult to break `18:00`.
- _Kṣaya_ — destruction, generally with respect to things; e.g. someone angrily punching a table and cracking it, or throwing things and breaking them `18:00`.
- _Hiṁsā_ — violence, generally against people; its opposite _ahiṁsā_ is non-violence `18:00`.
- _Anapekṣya_ — acting recklessly, self-destructively; the teacher contrasts it with _anapekṣa_ among the qualities of the devotee in the twelfth chapter `20:00`.
- _Pauruṣa_ — harshness, especially in speech `20:00`.
- _Moha_ — illusion; a common word for describing the mode of ignorance `22:00`.

#### Terrorism and vandalism [18:00]

- Terrorism, vandalism and the like are actions in ignorance — violent against people or destructive toward things, and very self-defeating.

#### Harsh speech mistaken for cleverness [20:00 – 22:00]

- Harshness means being monstrously insensitive: using foul language, speaking insults as if it were intelligent or clever.
- Some make parodies and caricatures, imitating mockingly, to "prove" something is wrong.
- Rather than constructive criticism, they do destructive criticism — the goal is not to improve another's idea but to destroy the idea and the person's morale.
- In some circles, the more subtly one can taunt, the cleverer one is considered. It may require cleverness to come up with taunts, but speaking to hurt others and delighting in that hurt is _tāmasic_ `22:00`.

#### The devotee's contrasting mood [22:00]

- A pure devotee is ready to take on suffering so that others become free from suffering — unhappy at others' unhappiness and eager to relieve it, even taking anxiety upon himself.
- This is tragically perverted in those who think that by causing pain to others they will get pleasure.
- Prabhupāda's translation conveys a self-willed attitude: "Whatever I feel like doing I will do, because it's my life." The teacher links this harshness with the demoniac qualities described in 16.4.

#### Moha throughout the chapter [22:00 – 24:00]

- At the very start of the chapter, renunciation done out of _moha_ was called renunciation in ignorance (18.7); the same word marks action in ignorance here.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Action in ignorance binds like an addiction: reckless, destructive of things, violent toward people and harsh in speech — the very opposite of the devotee who suffers so that others may be relieved. [18:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.26 [24:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.26 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now describes the three types of doer in 18.26–28. The doer in goodness is free from material attachment and false ego, enthusiastic and resolute, and unchanged by success or failure `24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mukta-saṅgaḥ_ — not attached to the activity or its fruits `24:00`.
- _Anahaṁ-vādī_ — not considering oneself the doer; understanding that success comes from multiple factors, not "I alone did it" `24:00`.
- _Dhṛti_ — determination; _utsāha_ — enthusiasm; _samanvitaḥ_ — endowed with both `24:00`.
- _Siddhy-asiddhyor nirvikāraḥ_ — _vikāra_ means change; _nirvikāra_ means no change — whether success or failure comes, the person remains the same and moves forward `28:00`.

#### Enthusiasm and determination together [24:00 – 26:00]

- The teacher recalls the same words from the discussion of the _Upadeśāmṛta_ (factors favourable to devotion).
- On a long journey — say a 30- or 50-hour drive to Kolkata — one needs enthusiasm to embark at all, and determination to sustain that enthusiasm.

#### Goodness is not life in slow motion [26:00 – 28:00]

- Some think goodness means doing everything slowly — speaking, walking, working slowly. Slowness is not the defining characteristic of goodness.
- The defining characteristic is **contemplation before action**: one thinks, reasons, then decides.
- In passion there is action first and thinking afterwards — "why did I do that?" — leading to lamentation and regretful actions.
- If one is well organised and has clear principles, contemplation is quick and clear; a person in goodness can work fast too.
- Externally both may be fast, but the person in passion is in bondage and illusion, not weighing the consequences of action; the person in goodness weighs them `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The doer in goodness acts without attachment or ego, combining enthusiasm with determination, thinking before acting, and remaining steady in success and failure. [24:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.27 [28:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.27 -->

**Context.** The doer in passion is attached to work and its fruits, greedy, envious, and moved by joy and sorrow `28:00 – 30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Rāgī_ — one filled with attachment (as _kāmī_ is one filled with _kāma_) `28:00`.
- _Karma-phala-prepsuḥ_ — very attached to quickly obtaining the fruits of one's work `28:00`.
- _Lubdhaḥ_ — greedy `28:00`.
- _Hiṁsātmakaḥ_ — one whose very essence is violence; Prabhupāda's purport says such a person is envious of others, since generally we are violent toward those we envy — "he is better situated than me; I'll bring him down" `30:00`, `38:00`.
- _Harṣa-śokānvitaḥ_ — subject to jubilation when the result comes and dejection when it does not `30:00`.

#### Process versus product [30:00 – 32:00]

- Compare with the doer in goodness: there it was enthusiasm and determination; here it is joy and sorrow — results of action.
- In passion the focus is not on the action but on its consequence — a short-sighted attitude that leads to underperformance and entanglement.
- If, while giving a class, I keep thinking "Will people like it? Who will praise me?" — that is stupidity; I must zoom in and focus only on the class to be productive.
- Focus on the action comes from enthusiasm and determination (18.26): commitment to the process rather than being carried away by positive or negative results `32:00`.

#### The weightlifter and the student [32:00 – 34:00]

- At a weightlifting competition people focus on the record — how many pounds more than before. But a true champion lifted that weight because of long-term, rigorous, unflinching training.
- Students who think only of marks become jubilant or morose with the result; some memorise just before the exam and get good marks, but that does not lead to mastery or to developing the mind's capacity to learn.
- Focus on the process happens in goodness; in passion everything is external — how many marks, not how much one studied; how much weight was lifted, not how much exercise was done `34:00`.

#### Karma and kartā can differ [34:00 – 38:00]

- _Karma_ refers to specific activities; _kartā_ to the person doing them. A person in goodness may have to do passionate activity, and vice versa.
- Teaching is broadly an activity in goodness — understanding concepts, sharing them, others learning — but the teacher's motive determines the doer's mode:
  - some teach for prestige, to display "I know so much";
  - some delight in showing how foolish the students are, speaking so confusingly that no one understands — has that increased the students' learning or desire to learn?
  - some analyse the subject deeply without concern for the audience, speaking loftily;
  - the best teachers want to educate: they understand the audience's level, start there and help them move upward `36:00`.
- If one teaches with arrogance — "I am so great, let me exhibit my learning" — the _karma_ may be in goodness but the _kartā_ is in passion `38:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa gives both analyses so that over time we can choose actions in goodness and also become doers in goodness `38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The doer in passion fixes on results — greedy, envious, swinging between jubilation and dejection — and so underperforms; the remedy is to commit to the process, and even a good activity done for display makes the doer passionate. [30:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.28 [38:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.28 -->

**Context.** A very graphic description by Kṛṣṇa of the doer in ignorance: one who acts against scripture, is materialistic, obstinate, deceitful, expert in insulting others, lazy, morose and procrastinating `38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ayuktaḥ_ — _yukta_ means well situated, well connected — here, according to scripture; _ayukta_ means not according to scripture `38:00 – 40:00`.
- _Prākṛtaḥ_ — materialistic, from _prakṛti_; the term _prākṛta-sahajiyā_ describes people who seem devotional but are materialistic, taking devotion cheaply — their concepts and aspirations lie within matter, with only a religious garb `40:00`.
- _Stabdhaḥ_ — obstinate: once they decide to do something, even if wrong, they don't give it up `40:00`.
- _Śaṭhaḥ_ — deceitful: concerned only with one's own good without considering others' good `46:00`.
- _Naiṣkṛtikaḥ_ — Prabhupāda translates it as "expert in insulting others" — "a very nice way of putting it" `46:00 – 48:00`.
- _Alasaḥ_ — lazy `48:00`.
- _Dīrgha-sūtrī_ — procrastinating `48:00`.
- _Viṣādī_ — morose `48:00 – 50:00`.

#### Determination versus obstinacy [40:00 – 44:00]

- They seem similar but differ:
  - **Determination** — "this is good; even if I don't succeed, I'll continue."
  - **Obstinacy** — continuing something undesirable even after one's own experience and others' advice say to give it up.
- Dhruva Mahārāja as a five-year-old child went to the forest despite Nārada Muni discouraging him — we call that determination.
- In the _Mahābhārata_, Nārada Muni also told Duryodhana not to wage war against the Pāṇḍavas, that he would be destroyed — yet Duryodhana went on his suicidal course. We call that obstinacy `42:00`.
- The difference: determination is for something good, in harmony with scriptural principles; obstinacy goes against scripture, seeks selfish enjoyment without considering consequences for others or even long-term consequences for oneself, and rejects good advice `42:00`.
- Rāvaṇa is another example: many, including those close to him and the Lord's messengers Hanumān and Aṅgada, counselled him, yet he never gave up `44:00`.

#### Deceitfulness in modern guise [44:00 – 46:00]

- Some try to copyright what already exists — a yoga teacher trying to copyright a sequence of postures so nobody can do it without paying.
- Herbs grown for millennia by farmers who know nothing of trademarks — suddenly a big company says, "if you grow this crop any more, you'll be penalised, even arrested."
- "Tween" advertising targets children aged around 9–11, who lack the capacity to discriminate: favourite characters from cartoons, movies and video games are linked to expensive brands, and the children become trapped `46:00`.

#### Expert in insulting [48:00]

- If harsh words are meant to convey truth and illuminate another person, that is good; but some simply delight in hurting — very undesirable.

#### Lazy and procrastinating — and Prabhupāda's contrast [48:00]

- The lazy person does nothing; the procrastinator delays for a year what could be done in an hour, needing to be followed up again and again — "Have you done it? Have you done it?"
- Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura gave Śrīla Prabhupāda one instruction at their first meeting — "You are an educated man; why don't you preach Lord Caitanya's message all over the world?" — and once more in a letter just before departing: "I am confident you can turn yourself into a very good preacher in the English language."
- He did no follow-up at all; Prabhupāda was self-motivated — the inspiration came from his spiritual master, but the motivation was his own. _Dīrgha-sūtrī_ is the exact opposite: prodded, pushed and reminded, and still not ready.

#### Morose: selfishness of thought [50:00 – 52:00]

- We usually think of selfishness in terms of things — a big brother wanting both toys. But selfishness can also be at the level of thoughts: wanting everyone's attention on oneself.
- Two ways to attract attention:
  - "I've achieved something so wonderful; I'm so great, so powerful";
  - "I'm so miserable; everything has gone wrong for me" — wallowing in a "pity pot."
- Hypochondriacs pretend to be physically sick so that others will give them attention; some become "mentally sick," constantly thinking of themselves negatively `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### Self-pity is not humility [52:00 – 54:00]

- Self-pity is not humility; it is self-centred: one thinks of others only to the extent they give attention to oneself.
- Humility is never self-centred; it is Kṛṣṇa-centred. We may be cleverer, wealthier or better-looking than others, which could make us proud, but with Kṛṣṇa as the focus of consciousness, however great we are, compared to Kṛṣṇa we remain small — and so we stay humble rather than becoming proud.
- In this way one can move up in spiritual life by keeping Kṛṣṇa at the centre.

#### Wallowing and its cost [54:00]

- To the extent we give up tendencies to lethargy, procrastination and wallowing in self-pity — like a pig wallowing in dirty water, which only becomes dirtier — we can become happy and productive.
- Many psychological problems that bring people to psychiatrists arise because their whole life is in the mode of ignorance; ignorant actions bring consequences such as mental dejection and depression.
- Some mental problems are due to brain damage and similar causes, but in many cases people can choose to stop living in ignorance and move to goodness; then many problems decrease and one can live more peacefully and productively.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The doer in ignorance is unscriptural, obstinate, deceitful, insulting, lazy, procrastinating and wallowing in self-pity; by choosing goodness and keeping Kṛṣṇa at the centre — the root of real humility — one becomes peaceful and productive. [38:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

- No audience Q&A appears in the captions. The teacher raises and answers a few questions himself:
  - **How can knowledge be "in ignorance"?** It is knowledge that takes one part of reality for the whole `00:00 – 02:00`.
