## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 18.62 [00:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.62 -->

**Context.** Since the Lord in the heart controls both nature and the soul, Kṛṣṇa now tells Arjuna to surrender completely to that Lord, and by His mercy attain supreme peace and an eternal abode `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sarva-bhāvena_ — completely; with all the emotions of the heart, offering complete love to Kṛṣṇa. The teacher connects the same phrase with the fifteenth chapter, where Kṛṣṇa speaks of one who knows Him as the Supreme Person `02:00`.
- _Tat-prasādāt_ — by the mercy of that Being situated in everyone's heart; by it one overcomes obstacles `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Parāṁ śāntim_ — supreme peace `04:00`.
- _Śāśvataṁ sthānam_ — an eternal resting place, a terminal destination `04:00`.

#### An eternal destination [04:00]

- As long as we are in material nature we cannot have an eternal destination; every destination here is temporary.
- To reach that destination we must take shelter of the Supreme Lord, because He is the master and controller of material nature and can take us beyond its reach.

#### Peace of mind and peace of heart [04:00 – 08:00]

- **Peace of mind** — at the mental level: going to a scenic place with a calm atmosphere and soothing wind, feeling relaxed.
- **Peace of heart** — the peace that comes from loving and being loved; there may be no enjoyment available, yet there is great satisfaction.
- The teacher cites the Bible's "peace that passeth all understanding," attained by knowing, surrendering to and loving God:
  - "Passes understanding" means this peace cannot be experienced at the material level, which thinks of peace only as the absence of external and internal anxiety and agitation.
  - The supreme peace may even look like anxiety or agitation, yet it is peace because the heart rests in enjoyment — what we long for even when we don't realise it.

#### Arjuna gets both [06:00 – 10:00]

- On the battlefield Arjuna had to choose between _śānti_ (peace) and _sthāna_ (a place — a kingdom).
- Acting independently, without surrender, he would get only one; surrendering to Kṛṣṇa, he gets both, in the best possible way — supreme peace and an eternal abode.
- Once we return to Kṛṣṇa and love Him, nothing can disrupt or destroy that life of love `08:00`.
- Material places are not permanent `08:00`:
  - Nowadays people hardly think of kingdoms; they think of a flat of so many square feet, a house or a bungalow.
  - Even a kingdom is temporary: someone may rise up, the king may be imprisoned or exiled, and lose power with humiliation after reigning in glory.
- The supreme reality: we are eternally meant to love Kṛṣṇa and rejoice in that love `08:00`.

#### Serving according to one's nature [10:00 – 16:00]

- Surrender will be discussed later; the important point here is that we have to serve Kṛṣṇa according to our nature `10:00`.
- Chapter 18's progression: gradually from _karma-kāṇḍa_ and _mokṣa_ to the higher level — the Supersoul — and then surrender to the Supreme Lord `10:00`.
- How do we discover our nature? `10:00 – 12:00`
  - Everyone has a nature, but it need not be only one kind; over generations, unions across different _varṇas_ have produced people with a mixture of natures, one of which is predominant.
  - Some activities we don't mind doing; others we can hardly stand and would avoid if we could. Doing disliked activities requires expending strength.
  - We need some activities we like; they give strength and inspiration and make spiritual life joyful even at our level.
- As one advances, one is less controlled by material nature, and can do whatever one wants for Kṛṣṇa's sake with joy `12:00`.
- In Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, every _gopa_ and _gopī_ has an individual nature and serves according to it to please Kṛṣṇa — spiritual individuality. Here, however, the topic is material nature and material individuality, which must be purified and elevated `12:00 – 14:00`.
- Material nature is purified by engaging it properly in service — not by rejection or repression, but by expression and direction `14:00`:
  - **Rejection** — imagining "I don't have this nature."
  - **Expression and direction** — expressing it as a Vaiṣṇava, asking how best one can serve Kṛṣṇa according to time, place, circumstance and one's _svabhāva_.
- Primary focus: "I am a soul with an eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa; I am going to serve Him" — how I serve will vary with time and place `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Since the Lord in the heart controls both nature and the soul, surrender to Him wholeheartedly — then, by His mercy, one gets both the peace of a loving heart and an eternal abode, and serves Him according to one's own nature, directed rather than repressed. [02:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.63 [16:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.63 -->

**Context.** Having described surrender, Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to deliberate on all this thoroughly and then act as he wishes `16:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Deliberate fully_ — think seriously and deeply; "don't leave any stone unturned" `16:00`.
- _Do as you wish_ — as is your desire, so you can act `18:00`.
- _Durlabha_ — rare; the human form of life is rare and valuable `20:00`.
- _Artha_ / _paramārtha_ — the human form is truly meaningful because it can give the supreme goal `20:00`.

#### A book of choices, not commandments [18:00]

- Here again the Gītā is not a book of commandments; it gives choices — Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to decide for himself.

#### Not depriving ourselves in the name of freedom [20:00 – 24:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's guidance does not deprive us of freedom; it assures that we do not deprive ourselves in the name of freedom.
- The thousand-rupee note: a child who says "I want to be free" may use a ₹1000 note for just four rupees' worth of something — and in the name of freedom deprive himself or herself.
- The human form is rare and truly valuable; it can give the supreme goal. This body is temporary and can fall at any time `20:00 – 22:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's analysis is meant to guide Arjuna to the best action — not to take away freedom, but to say: stop depriving yourself in the name of freedom, and get the best bargain out of life `22:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa wants us to choose, and choose intelligently, but ultimately it is our free will `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa leaves the final choice to Arjuna, but His guidance exists so that we don't squander the rare human form — like a child spending a thousand-rupee note on four rupees' worth — in the name of freedom. [18:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.64 [24:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.64 -->

**Context.** Seeing Arjuna contemplating seriously and unable to decide, Kṛṣṇa's heart overflows with compassion: "You don't have to keep thinking — I know you want what is best, and what I want for you is the best; I will guide you" `24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Most confidential_ and _supreme word_ — two superlatives Kṛṣṇa combines here to glorify what He is about to say `30:00 – 32:00`.
- _Very dear to Me_ — the intimacy of the relationship `26:00`, `34:00`.
- _For your benefit_ — Kṛṣṇa speaks like a good doctor, for Arjuna's welfare `30:00`.

#### How long will you contemplate? [24:00]

- Arjuna could contemplate as long as he wants — but the living entity has been trying to think out what will give happiness lifetime after lifetime, and always reaches false conclusions.
- So Kṛṣṇa simplifies and gives the knowledge Himself.

#### Not a paid teacher [26:00]

- What Kṛṣṇa speaks is a very intimate expression of His heart.
- He is not like a teacher in an institution where teaching is a paid profession, who has some relationship with students but is not deeply concerned about them.
- Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna, in effect, "you are very strongly loved by Me — in fact I am in love with you."

#### Determined to forget, yet still loved [26:00 – 30:00]

- We are determined to forget Kṛṣṇa; the teacher says modern progress actually means inventing newer ways to forget Him.
- Examples:
  - In the past, entertainment such as dance — e.g. _Bharatanāṭyam_ on Kṛṣṇa's pastimes — was connected with Kṛṣṇa.
  - Now there are over a hundred TV channels, yet knowledge about God is very rarely available; then the internet and video games — ever newer ways of forgetting.
- We have directed our intelligence away from Kṛṣṇa, but by turning toward Him we can learn to love Him; He, in His determination, never leaves us, lifetime after lifetime — forever waiting `28:00`.
- In the _Gopī-gīta_, the _gopīs_ praise Kṛṣṇa as doing great good to everyone — a welfare worker for all `28:00 – 30:00`.

#### A double superlative [30:00 – 34:00]

- In 9.1–9.2 Kṛṣṇa calls His teaching the most confidential knowledge — the chapter's title speaks of royal, confidential knowledge — and the chapter is clearly about _bhakti_: _ananya_ devotion (9.22), offering a leaf, flower, fruit and water (9.26), and 9.34.
- In 15.20 He again calls His teaching most confidential.
- Here He doubly glorifies it — "most confidential of all" and "supreme word" — and nowhere else in the Gītā is such a concentrated combination of superlatives used.
- Together with the expression of intimacy ("very dear to Me," "for your benefit"), this signals that what follows is extremely special and exalted `34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa does not lecture like a paid teacher: out of love for Arjuna — and for us who keep inventing ways to forget Him — He prefaces His final teaching with the Gītā's strongest superlatives, signalling that what follows is the heart of the book. [24:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.65 [34:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.65 -->

**Context.** The most confidential instruction: always think of Me, become My devotee, worship Me and offer obeisances to Me — and you will certainly come to Me `34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- The four instructions `34:00 – 36:00`:
  - _Think of Me always_ — offer your mind.
  - _Become My devotee_ — offer your heart.
  - _Worship Me_.
  - _Offer obeisances to Me_ — with the body.
- _Satyam_ — truth; "I am speaking the truth to you" `38:00`.
- _Dear to Me_ — Kṛṣṇa's love behind the promise `42:00`.

#### 9.34 and 18.65: two sides of one relationship [36:00 – 38:00]

- The first two lines are similar to 9.34; together the four instructions mean offering our entire existence to Kṛṣṇa.
- A relationship between A and B succeeds through what each is ready to offer.
- In 9.34 the onus is on the devotee — engage yourself completely, wholly devoted to Me, and you will come to Me.
- In 18.65 Kṛṣṇa states what He brings to the relationship — His promise and His love.

#### Why "truly"? [38:00]

- Kṛṣṇa emphasises truth because the claim is extraordinary: by such simple things one can attain the supreme perfection of going back to the spiritual world.
- In 9.31 Kṛṣṇa told Arjuna to declare to the world that His devotee never perishes; here Kṛṣṇa Himself makes the declaration to everyone.

#### A public promise — honour and heart [40:00 – 42:00]

- An informal promise is not taken seriously; a promise made before witnesses, or publicly, is far more serious.
- Example: someone whose relative is lost announces publicly, now even on social media, a reward for information; then paying becomes a matter of honour — otherwise, what is the value of their promise?
- Kṛṣṇa's proclamation is a matter of honour, but even more a matter of the heart: "I love you, therefore I will take you back."
- Kṛṣṇa is baring His heart so that we can offer our hearts to Him and turn towards Him.
- The teacher calls this the supreme sweetness of the Gītā: an analytical book with a profoundly moving devotional conclusion `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
In 9.34 Kṛṣṇa spoke of what the devotee gives; in 18.65 He reveals what He gives — a public, truthful promise backed by His heart: "you are dear to Me, and you will certainly come to Me." [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.66 [42:00 – 62:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.66 -->

**Context.** A question arises: "You tell me to do this, but there are so many other things one can do — what exactly should I do?" Kṛṣṇa answers in one of the most quoted and famous verses of the Gītā `42:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sarva-dharma_ — all varieties of _dharma_, to be given up apart from the _dharma_ of surrender to Kṛṣṇa `44:00`.
- _Mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja_ — surrender to Me alone: not just surrender of emotion, but undistracted, undiluted, wholehearted emotion `44:00`.
- _Para-dharma_ — pure devotional relationship with Kṛṣṇa, developed by the nine activities of devotion such as hearing and chanting `48:00`.
- _Apara-dharma_ — various duties: to the family, saints, country, humanity `48:00 – 50:00`.
- _Kṣatriya-dharma_ and _kula-dharma_ — duty as a warrior and duty to the family `48:00`.

#### Why "Me alone" [44:00 – 46:00]

- Love is a matter of the heart; when devotion is directed to many people and things, it cannot be wholehearted.
- Even if we respect the demigods, they are within this world; their blessings cannot take us beyond it or grant pure love, which is the passport to the spiritual world.

#### The Mahābhārata background [46:00 – 50:00]

- Arjuna did not become a renunciate after hearing this; he continued as a _kṣatriya_.
- The _parvas_ as the teacher lists them: Sabhā Parva (the dice game and Draupadī), the exile of the Pāṇḍavas, Virāṭa Parva (incognito), Udyoga Parva (fifth) and Bhīṣma Parva (sixth), in which the Gītā appears.
- In the Udyoga Parva, while the Pāṇḍavas debate whether to fight, a _brāhmaṇa_ goes as peace messenger, Dhṛtarāṣṭra sends Sañjaya for peace, and then Kṛṣṇa Himself goes.
- The underlying conflict is between _kṣatriya-dharma_ and _kula-dharma_: should I protect my family members whatever the cost, or fight whatever the cost? `48:00 – 50:00`
- We come into this world alone and leave alone; relationships become meaningful when they help us develop our devotional relationship `48:00`.
- The Gītā resolves this conflict philosophically, not just pragmatically `50:00`.

#### Surrender is not giving up all duty [52:00 – 56:00]

- Suppose someone thinks surrender means giving up job and family and renouncing everything for Kṛṣṇa — what after that? One still has a _dharma_, e.g. _brahmacārī_ or _sannyāsa_ duties.
- So "give up all _dharmas_" does not mean becoming irreligious; it means accepting the _dharma_ that helps one surrender to Kṛṣṇa alone, and giving up those that do not.
- Example: relatives and society may object when young men become _brahmacārīs_ — "the country spent so much on your engineering seat; India is losing an engineer" `52:00`.
  - The teacher's answer: what the country did for me came through resources that come from Kṛṣṇa, so it is Kṛṣṇa who did it through the country `54:00`.
  - Serving Kṛṣṇa therefore also serves the country — and such devotees carry the country's spiritual heritage, which is itself a great service `54:00`.
- For Arjuna, _kula-dharma_ was unfavourable and _kṣatriya-dharma_ favourable; at the end of the Gītā he acts as a _kṣatriya_ externally so that he can practise the _para-dharma_, _bhakti-yoga_ — a _bhakta_ practising _kṣatriya-dharma_ `56:00`.
- Likewise one may be an engineer, doctor, housewife, temple president or accountant — all in Kṛṣṇa's service; we accept the roles that help us surrender `56:00`.

#### The six aspects of surrender [56:00 – 60:00]

- The teacher cites the Vaiṣṇava scriptures (also elaborated by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura): surrender has six aspects:
  1. Accepting what is favourable for devotion.
  2. Rejecting what is unfavourable.
  3. Faith that Kṛṣṇa will protect.
  4. Accepting Him as maintainer.
  5. Full self-surrender — my whole existence depends on Kṛṣṇa.
  6. Humility — without Kṛṣṇa I am worthless.
- The first two are actions; the remaining are emotions `56:00`.
- The emotions cannot be produced by a mental adjustment; faith grows as we see Kṛṣṇa protecting us over time — "analog growth, not a digital switch," not like switching on a light `58:00`.
- To foster that gradual growth, the actions are important: choosing what is favourable for _bhakti_ `58:00`.
- Surrender is not just passive `58:00 – 60:00`:
  - Draupadī raising her hands while her sari was being pulled, and Kṛṣṇa supplying unlimited cloth, is an expression of surrender for moments of extreme danger when nothing else can be done.
  - But that is not what she did throughout her life; at other times surrender means intelligent choices about the proper action.

#### "I will free you from all reactions" [60:00 – 62:00]

- Arjuna's remaining worry: how can I attack my relatives and venerable elders like Bhīṣma, and Droṇa, my martial teacher? There will be sinful reactions.
- Kṛṣṇa answers: even if there are reactions, I will protect you from them. For Kṛṣṇa's sake one sometimes gives up a lower moral principle to adopt a higher one, and Kṛṣṇa assures protection.
- Contrast with 7.28: there Kṛṣṇa said that to become fixed in devotion, one must end sinful reactions by acting piously and consistently; here He says, even if your sins have not ended, just come to Me and I will free you.

#### The mother and the helpless child [62:00]

- A mother trains her baby hard to respond to nature's call by going to the washroom; but when the child becomes helpless and cries "Mama, I don't know what to do," the mother's love comes out and she cleans the child.
- Likewise, in 7.28 Kṛṣṇa says "first clean yourself, then come to Me," but in 18.66 He says "don't worry, I will protect you — just come to Me."
- 18.66 reveals Kṛṣṇa's love for us and invites us to love Him — the Gītā's message of love, a call to love and a revolution of love; this will be discussed further in the next class.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
"Give up all dharmas" does not mean abandoning duty but keeping only the duties that help one surrender to Kṛṣṇa alone; and to one who does, Kṛṣṇa — like a mother cleaning a helpless child — promises freedom from all reactions without first demanding perfect purity. [44:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
