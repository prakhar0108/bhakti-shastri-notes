## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 18.72 [02:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.72 -->

**Context.** Until now Arjuna has questioned and Kṛṣṇa answered; now Kṛṣṇa, like a concerned teacher, questions Arjuna to check his understanding: have you heard with one-pointed attention, and have your ignorance and illusion been dispelled? `02:00 – 04:00`

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ekāgra_ — one-pointed attention; in the stages of _samādhi_ discussed in chapter 6, it is the penultimate stage of concentration, or more generally concentrated hearing `06:00`.
- _Ajñāna_ — ignorance: not knowing `06:00`.
- _Moha_ — illusion: understanding wrongly `06:00`.
- _Dhanañjaya_ — conqueror of wealth; "have you conquered this wealth too — the wealth of wisdom?" `06:00 – 08:00`.

#### "Are you able to hear me?" [04:00 – 06:00]

- In a turbulent pre-battle setting, a student may not be attentive — imagine holding a Bhakti Shastri class in a noisy marketplace.
- On a _yātrā_, a speaker often asks "am I audible?" to check the hearing facilities. Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are close, so audibility is not the issue — concentration is.
- Kṛṣṇa's expertise: by His presence and presentation He transforms a battlefield into a classroom and reveals knowledge practically never revealed before or after.

#### Two problems: not knowing and knowing wrongly [06:00]

- If I am sick and don't know the treatment, I stay sick; worse, if I take the wrong medicine, complications may follow.
- Both ignorance and misconception must be removed.

#### A compassionate, not commercial, teacher [08:00]

- The _ācāryas_ explain the implication: if Arjuna has not understood, Kṛṣṇa is ready to speak again whatever he missed — even the whole Gītā.
- By contrast, some teachers today explain unclearly in school and then say "come to my private coaching classes and pay extra" — teaching as a business.
- For Kṛṣṇa it is a compassionate enterprise, concerned only with our well-being.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa ends as a concerned teacher, asking whether Arjuna heard attentively and whether both ignorance and illusion are gone — ready to repeat the whole Gītā if needed, out of compassion rather than commerce. [04:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.73 [08:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.73 -->

**Context.** Arjuna last spoke in 18.1; these are his final words in the conversation: his illusion is destroyed and his memory regained by Kṛṣṇa's mercy, and he will act according to Kṛṣṇa's word `08:00 – 10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Moha_ destroyed and memory restored — the memory that "I am the _ātmā_, primarily a devotee, meant to serve" `10:00`.
- _Prasāda_ — mercy; the word's third occurrence in this chapter, after 18.58 and 18.62, because _bhakti_ is ultimately a path of mercy `10:00`.
- _Acyuta_ — one who never falls; Arjuna's first and last address to Kṛṣṇa `12:00`.
- Doubts gone — Arjuna is now firmly situated, no longer trembling outwardly or shaking inwardly `12:00`.

#### A path of mercy [10:00]

- The Gītā gives much analysis, but in its conclusion describes itself as a path of grace; by Kṛṣṇa's mercy what is hard to understand or do becomes easy.

#### Acyuta, first and last [12:00]

- In chapter 1 Arjuna said "O Acyuta, place my chariot between the two armies"; now he uses the same name.
- Kṛṣṇa never falls and never fails His devotees. At the start He served Arjuna as charioteer; in love, the devotee wants to serve the Lord and the Lord wants to serve the devotee.
- Now as spiritual master too, He is infallible: "I asked for enlightenment and You gave it by Your mercy."

#### A call for action, not applause [14:00]

- The end of the Gītā is not an abstract intellectual exercise or elocution performance; it is a call for transformation and practical change.
- A doctor explains the X-rays and prescribes medicine; the patient should not reply "very good talk — you're a good speaker," but "I'll do what you say."
- Similarly, after hearing from Kṛṣṇa, Śrīla Prabhupāda and devotees, real appreciation is shown not in compliments but in implementation.

#### "I will do Your will" [16:00]

- The teacher calls this the pinnacle of surrender.
- In the Bible, the night before the crucifixion, Jesus foresaw danger and prayed "let the cup pass" — a cup of poison, as Socrates was given hemlock — and then "Thy will be done, not mine."
- That is surrender: accepting God's will as He does it through various agents.
- Arjuna goes further: not only "let Your will be done," but "I will do Your will" — taking responsibility to implement it.

#### Ask "how now", not "why this" [16:00 – 18:00]

- When things go wrong, we can protest "why this?" — which means "I had a plan for enjoyment and now I can't enjoy."
- Or we can ask "how now? — how can I best serve Kṛṣṇa here?" and find a way forward.
- Being an enjoyer is disempowering: it leaves us dependent on many circumstances outside our control.
- Being a servant is empowering: we can serve in every situation — even sick on our deathbed we can remember, speak about and glorify Kṛṣṇa.
- So surrender is not passive or weakening; it is strengthening and empowering.

#### From the purport [18:00 – 20:00]

- _Moha_ is what opposes knowledge: the living entity is a servant but thinks he is a master.
- Kṛṣṇa consciousness means acting according to Kṛṣṇa's order — a matter of consciousness expressed through action: thinking how we can serve Kṛṣṇa and moving closer to Him through that service.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's last words go beyond "Thy will be done" to "I will do Your will": the Gītā's real appreciation is implementation, and the servant's question "how can I serve now?" empowers where the enjoyer's "why this?" disempowers. [10:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.74 [20:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.74 -->

**Context.** The "camera" moves back from Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna to Sañjaya and Dhṛtarāṣṭra: Sañjaya says he has heard this wonderful conversation between the two great souls, and his hair is standing on end `20:00 – 24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mahātmā_ — great soul; used by Sañjaya for both Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna `22:00`.
- _Adbhutam_ — wonderful, extraordinary, amazing `24:00`.
- _Roma-harṣa_ — hair standing on end, from fear or from ecstasy `24:00`.

#### Nested conversations [20:00 – 22:00]

- The Gītā is part of the _Mahābhārata_, which has several levels of nesting:
  - Outermost — the narration to Janamejaya, son of Parīkṣit Mahārāja and great-grandson of Arjuna (as the teacher states it).
  - Within it — Sañjaya narrating the events of Kurukṣetra to Dhṛtarāṣṭra.
  - Within that, in the Bhīṣma Parva — Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna.
- The Gītā began with Dhṛtarāṣṭra's question in 1.1 — what did they do on the battlefield? — and Sañjaya now concludes: the first thing that happened was this beautiful conversation `22:00`.

#### Two mahātmās [22:00 – 24:00]

- Kṛṣṇa uses _mahātmā_ for His devotees, e.g. 9.13 and 7.19; in chapter 11 the word refers to Kṛṣṇa.
- Kṛṣṇa is the great soul as the Supersoul present everywhere.
- Arjuna is a great soul because he understood the message of devotion and surrendered to the Lord's will: when the small soul surrenders to the will of the big Soul, the small soul becomes big in influence.

#### Hair standing on end — twice [24:00]

- At the Gītā's start, Arjuna's hair stood on end out of fear — not just of dying, but of being part or cause of a disaster killing so many.
- Here Sañjaya's hair stands on end out of ecstasy, as the next verses show.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Gītā's narration pulls back to Sañjaya, who calls both Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna mahātmās — a small soul becomes great by surrendering to the great Soul — and whose hair now stands on end from ecstasy, not the fear with which the Gītā began. [20:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.75 [26:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.75 -->

**Context.** Sañjaya explains how he heard: by the mercy of Vyāsa, he heard this most confidential yoga directly from Kṛṣṇa, the master of all mysticism `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vyāsa-prasāda_ — the mercy of Vyāsa `26:00`.
- Most confidential knowledge, supreme instruction — the same words Kṛṣṇa used in 18.64 `28:00`.
- _Yogeśvara_ — the Lord of all mystic powers `30:00`.
- _Sākṣāt_ — directly, live `30:00`.

#### Background: Vyāsa's gift [26:00 – 28:00]

- Before the war Vyāsa had tried to counsel Duryodhana, who was incorrigible, and offered Dhṛtarāṣṭra eyes to see the battle.
- Dhṛtarāṣṭra replied: "I never saw my sons' lives — what is the use of seeing their deaths?" — knowing in his heart they would die; but he wanted to hear.
- So Vyāsa gave special vision to Sañjaya, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's secretary.
- Dhṛtarāṣṭra respected Vidura for his wisdom, though Vidura, born in a _śūdra_ family, never became king; when Vidura left, Sañjaya, earlier subordinate to him, also sometimes counselled Dhṛtarāṣṭra.

#### Vision and comprehension [28:00]

- Kurukṣetra and Hastināpura were far apart; by a "mystic television" Sañjaya could see.
- But even standing beside Arjuna, one would not necessarily understand; Vyāsa's mercy gave not just vision and audibility but comprehension.

#### How rare this is [30:00 – 32:00]

- Historians praise a great pre-war speech by a head of state; someone who says "I was there, I heard it live" is specially fortunate.
- In cosmic time scales, Kṛṣṇa's appearances are rare; within them He mostly performs _līlā_ and only occasionally gives systematic philosophy. Lord Rāma gives instructions in places, but nothing directly comparable to the Gītā.
- Rarer still is a devotee able to hear it.
- Sañjaya's words are more than "thank you" — sometimes we say thanks without valuing what we received; Sañjaya says, "how fortunate I am" `32:00`.

#### Hearing through paramparā [32:00]

- From the purport: without coming through the disciplic succession, one cannot hear Kṛṣṇa; knowledge outside it is imperfect.
- As Sañjaya understood by his spiritual master Vyāsa's mercy, we understand by our spiritual master's mercy; so we are grateful to Śrīla Prabhupāda, our spiritual master and the Vaiṣṇavas.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sañjaya heard the Gītā live from Yogeśvara Kṛṣṇa only by Vyāsa's mercy, which gave him not just sight but comprehension — just as we understand Kṛṣṇa only through the mercy of the disciplic succession, which calls for gratitude, not mere thanks. [26:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.76 – 18.77 [34:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.76 -->

<!-- vedabase:18.77 -->

**Context.** Sañjaya remembers this holy, wonderful conversation and Kṛṣṇa's wonderful universal form again and again; he is greatly astonished and rejoices at every moment. The teacher discusses the two verses together, since the same sentiment repeats `34:00 – 36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Puṇyam_ — purifying, auspicious, sacred; paired with "wonderful" (used earlier two verses before) `34:00`.
- Remembering again and again `34:00`.
- Rejoicing — to delight, be jubilant `36:00`.
- _Viśvarūpa_ — the universal form Sañjaya remembers in the second verse `34:00 – 36:00`.

#### Thrill at every moment [34:00 – 36:00]

- From the purport: as one becomes increasingly enlightened in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, one enjoys life with a thrill, not just for some time but at every moment.
- The two verses share similar opening and closing lines; the essence is delight and jubilation on hearing Kṛṣṇa's message.
- Nārada Muni, as a boy in his previous life, had Kṛṣṇa's _darśana_, which thrilled him so that each of his senses felt independently enlivened; that effect can come by remembrance too, especially when remembering the message along with the form `36:00`.

#### Same activities, ever-fresh person [36:00 – 40:00]

- People seek newness — travel, gadgets, new relationships, houses, even spouses — but at the bodily level all pleasure boils down to eating, sleeping, mating and defending, under a superficially new covering.
- In _bhakti_ we seem to repeat the same activities — the same holy names, the same duties — yet get freshness and joy, because of the newness not of the activity but of the person we connect with: Kṛṣṇa is ever fresh and keeps revealing His attractiveness.
- A devotee could relish one _darśana_ of Kṛṣṇa for all eternity `40:00`.

#### Why Dhṛtarāṣṭra did not change [40:00 – 44:00]

- The same message Kṛṣṇa spoke to Arjuna, Sañjaya repeated to Dhṛtarāṣṭra; Arjuna changed, Dhṛtarāṣṭra did not, still hoping the Kauravas would win.
- Arjuna was submissive; Dhṛtarāṣṭra may have been inquisitive but was attached and biased. 4.34 asks for both inquiry and submission `40:00`.
- He was attached to Duryodhana and to remaining king. Earlier, when an envoy sent by Kṛṣṇa warned him, he said the words were true, like nectar, but he could not accept them — like lightning on a dark night, illuminating for a moment before darkness returns `42:00`.
- Even while attached, we can apply the Gītā step by step within the freedom our attachments leave; bondage then decreases until we are free to serve Kṛṣṇa `42:00`.

#### Sañjaya did not fail [42:00 – 48:00]

- Did Sañjaya fail, like a teacher whose student doesn't understand? No — he is elated and thrilled, because by receptively receiving and transmitting the message he is in contact with Kṛṣṇa.
- So in sharing Kṛṣṇa's message, even if people don't appreciate or change, we are purified and thrilled; there is no failure in Kṛṣṇa consciousness `44:00`.
- At the material level service may fail: Abhimanyu fought the Kauravas and lost his life; but Vyāsa consoled Yudhiṣṭhira — we are still in the world of temptation, while Abhimanyu, so young, has gone beyond it to the eternal world; lament for ourselves, not him `44:00 – 46:00`.
- The goal of service is not productivity or popularity but purity `46:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda, from his initiation around 1932–33 until going to America in 1965–66, had practically no preaching success for over thirty years, yet everyone found him cheerful and satisfied; people were attracted: "he has nothing, yet he has something much more than I have" `46:00`.
- Focusing too much on external results can dishearten us, so that we may not even be available when Kṛṣṇa wants to give us the inner result — purification and a richer, sweeter contact with Him `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sañjaya rejoices at every remembrance even though his listener did not change — showing that the thrill of Kṛṣṇa consciousness comes from contact with the ever-fresh Kṛṣṇa, and that service aims at purity, not productivity or popularity, so it never truly fails. [34:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.78 [48:00 – 66:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.78 -->

**Context.** The last verse: wherever there is Kṛṣṇa, the master of all mystics, and wherever there is Arjuna, the supreme archer, there will be opulence, victory, extraordinary power and morality — this is Sañjaya's opinion `48:00`, `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yogeśvara_ — master of all mystic powers; all mystic powers gained on the path of yoga come from Kṛṣṇa `48:00`.
- Arjuna with his bow raised — the significant words of the verse, per the teacher `48:00`.
- _Śrī_ — opulence; can also refer to the goddess of fortune `56:00`.
- _Vijaya_ — victory `54:00`.
- _Bhūti_ — extraordinary power; _vibhūti_ is used in chapter 10 for Kṛṣṇa's extraordinary power `54:00`.
- Morality — the fourth result, which the teacher calls supreme morality `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Arjuna picks up his bow [48:00 – 52:00]

- At the end of chapter 1, Arjuna, his mind overwhelmed with grief, put aside his bow and arrows; now, after hearing the Gītā, he has picked them up — ready to do Kṛṣṇa's will.
- The bow is historical — Arjuna's famous Gāṇḍīva — but also represents the living entity's determination to serve Kṛṣṇa. For a book-distributing devotee, it may be picking up the books again after people refuse them `50:00`.
- How Arjuna lost it: he wanted to see the opposing strength; by that seeing (_pratyakṣa_) and his own mental speculation (_anumāna_), his determination was lost `50:00`.
- How he regained it: by hearing Kṛṣṇa's message (_śabda_) `52:00`.

#### Losing and restoring determination [52:00 – 54:00]

- We lose determination the same way: "others seem to be enjoying, I'm following regulative principles and chanting — what am I doing?"; "who has seen Kṛṣṇa or the spiritual world?"
- In association and by hearing Kṛṣṇa's message, we see that others' happiness is a facade — old age, disease, death and miseries are everywhere — so lasting happiness must exist at a different, spiritual level, revealed in the Gītā; directing our consciousness there is real intelligence.
- Determination lost by _pratyakṣa_ and _anumāna_ is restored by _śabda_ — so regular hearing is vital.
- When hearing becomes irregular, devotional life becomes a burden: chanting is heavy, service becomes an internal battle with the mind, without enthusiasm `54:00`.
- As food gives physical energy, hearing gives spiritual energy — not something mystical like a current through the head, but the desire and determination to serve Kṛṣṇa that comes from understanding how much He loves us `54:00`.

#### Sañjaya's prophecy [54:00 – 58:00]

- Sañjaya, now enlightened and attracted to Kṛṣṇa, gives his opinion: where there is the miracle-working Lord and the determined devotee, there will be opulence, victory, extraordinary power and morality.
- This also answers the implication of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's opening question — who won? — there is no hope for your sons; the Pāṇḍavas will win `56:00`.
- Opulence: the goddess of fortune will be where her Lord is. When the Kaurava army faced reversals and Duryodhana chastised his senior commanders, Droṇa replied that they could only endeavour; the result depends on destiny, and the Lord of destiny sits on Arjuna's chariot `56:00`.
- Power: Duryodhana may count Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa, Śalya, Karṇa, Aśvatthāmā and his own powerful brothers, but all their power cannot compare with Kṛṣṇa's supreme power `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Supreme morality [58:00]

- Some say the Pāṇḍavas used immorality — Bhīṣma shot from behind Śikhaṇḍī, Droṇa killed while meditating, Karṇa shot while lifting his chariot wheel.
- The teacher answers: those warriors had participated in or consented to far greater unfairness, and with the cunning one may be cunning.
- The Pāṇḍavas could have won without such strategies; their willingness to use them shows they were ready to sacrifice morality for devotion — and that commitment to pure devotion, to the Lord's will, is the supreme morality.

#### Prabhupāda's closing purport [58:00 – 60:00]

- The teacher highlights a few points of Prabhupāda's "classic" purport:
  - The Gītā is the last word in all morality and religion.
  - The Absolute Truth is realised in three aspects — Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān.
  - The most confidential instruction is surrender to Kṛṣṇa.
  - A summary of the five _tattvas_.
  - By proper use of independence, the living entity comes under Kṛṣṇa's direct order and attains his normal condition in the pleasure-giving potency.
- That is the purpose of the Gītā and of Prabhupāda's movement: to restore us to our normal position, under the shelter of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, in loving service to Kṛṣṇa `60:00`.

#### The Gītā's journey in review [60:00 – 66:00]

- The Gītā begins with Arjuna asking "what is my _dharma_?" (2.7) `60:00`.
- Chapters 2–6: don't renounce action, act without attachment (the dialectic of chapters 3 and 5); chapter 6 — renunciation and yoga when mature, but the topmost yogīs are _bhaktas_ `60:00`.
- Middle six chapters, on _bhakti_ directly `60:00 – 62:00`:
  - 7 — how to practise _bhakti_; the four kinds of people who surrender; Kṛṣṇa's position.
  - 8 — compares _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ and _bhakti-yoga_ and establishes _bhakti_'s supremacy.
  - 9 — _bhakti_ sweetly, revealing Kṛṣṇa's heart intimately.
  - 10 — a concentrated essence of _bhakti_; how to remember Kṛṣṇa in the material world.
  - 11 — the universal form, showing Kṛṣṇa's supremacy.
  - 12 — meditation on the personal form is far better than on the impersonal.
- Last six chapters, _jñāna_ leading to _bhakti_ `62:00 – 64:00`:
  - 13 — _prakṛti_, _puruṣa_ and related concepts, understood by the devotee; 13.22 — the soul lodged in matter by the desire to enjoy.
  - 14 — the modes that water the tree of material existence; going beyond them by devotion (14.26), and Kṛṣṇa as the basis of Brahman (14.27).
  - 15 — the tree of material existence and Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Person to whom we should surrender.
  - 16 — the upper and lower extremes: divine and demoniac natures.
  - 17 — the intermediate: those with faith but not in scripture.
  - 18 — a summary: action without attachment better than giving up action; the five factors of action; analysis of knowledge, intelligence, happiness and so on; _varṇāśrama_ duties as worship; _jñāna-yoga_ from 18.49; _bhakti-yoga_ from 18.54–18.55; from 18.56–18.57 _bhakti_ at whatever level; going beyond _svabhāva_ by surrendering to its Lord; the most confidential knowledge; the _phala-śruti_; and Sañjaya's ecstatic declaration `64:00`.
- The journey culminates clearly in Kṛṣṇa as the most glorious goal and pure devotion as the most glorious means; "As It Is" means these conclusions are made crystal clear throughout the Gītā's flow, as Śrīla Prabhupāda forcefully presented for all humanity `64:00 – 66:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna picking up his bow is the Gītā's real ending: determination lost to perception and speculation is restored by hearing Kṛṣṇa's word. Where the Lord and such a determined devotee unite, opulence, victory, power and the supreme morality — pure devotion — are assured. [48:00 – 66:00]
</Callout>
