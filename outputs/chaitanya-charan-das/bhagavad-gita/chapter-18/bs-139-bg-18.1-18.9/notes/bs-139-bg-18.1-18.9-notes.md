## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 18.1 [04:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.1 -->

**Context.** Arjuna asks to understand the truth of renunciation, using two words — _sannyāsa_ and _tyāga_ — and asking to understand their difference `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sannyāsa_ — the renounced order of life `04:00`.
- _Tyāga_ — renunciation itself `04:00`.
- _Pṛthak_ — "the difference": Arjuna wants to understand how the two differ `04:00`.
- _Mahā-bāhu_ — the mighty-armed one; one of three names of the Lord used in the verse `04:00`.
- _Hṛṣīkeśa_ — the Lord of the senses; the senses are formidable and need to be controlled `04:00`.

#### Three names, one capacity [04:00]

- The verse uses three names of the Lord: the mighty-armed one, the killer of a demon (named in the verse), and the Lord of the senses.
- All three indicate the Lord's capacity to destroy the demon of doubt.

#### Why Arjuna still asks questions [06:00]

- By the tenth chapter, when Arjuna glorifies the supreme position of the Lord, his illusion is already gone; he has understood the essential message of the Gītā and is expressing it himself.
- His subsequent questions are, as the Gītā itself indicates, for the benefit of others — for the maintenance of the world — giving insight into how a person can live properly according to scripture.
- Arjuna takes broad concepts known in the Vedic context but not always understood holistically, and Kṛṣṇa gives him that holistic understanding.

#### The real question [08:00]

- Arjuna's essential question remains: should I fight or not fight? Should I renounce only in spirit, or renounce in deed also?

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's question about _sannyāsa_ and _tyāga_ is really his original dilemma in conceptual form — whether to renounce in spirit or in deed — and he asks it for the benefit of all who want to live by scripture. [04:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.2 [08:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.2 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa responds by stating what the wise declare: _sannyāsa_ is giving up one kind of work, and _tyāga_ is giving up the fruits of all work `08:00 – 12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma_ — here, fruitive activity performed according to scriptural direction; such activity is not sinful, and in that sense it is good `08:00`.
- _Nitya-karma_ — standard prescribed duties to be done daily and lifelong; for example, the daily worship a _brāhmaṇa_ performs `08:00 – 10:00`.
- _Kāmya-karma_ — religious activity done to fulfil a particular desire; for example, a king performing a sacrifice to get a son `10:00`.
- _Tyāgī_ — one who does work but without attachment `12:00`.

#### What the sannyāsī gives up [10:00]

- The _sannyāsī_ gives up _kāmya-karma_ — he does not continue the sacrifices of householders, and does not strive for the fulfilment of material desires.
- One wants to give up material desires themselves, but at least the striving for their fulfilment must be given up.

#### Renunciation of attachment, not of action [10:00 – 12:00]

- Kṛṣṇa defines renunciation not merely as renunciation of action but as renunciation of attachment in action.
- The difference:
  - one gives up a particular kind of work (the _sannyāsī_ gives up _kāmya-karma_);
  - the other does work but without attachment (the _tyāgī_).
- Those with the spirit of _tyāga_ may even perform desire-oriented rites, but not for material desires — for purification, realisation and ultimately liberation.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa locates renunciation in the heart: what must be given up is not action as such but attachment to its fruits. [10:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.3 [12:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa presents the opinions of two groups: some say all work should be given up as faulty; others say acts of sacrifice, charity and penance should never be given up `12:00`, `16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Eke_ / _apare_ — "one kind of people" and "another kind of people" `12:00`.
- _Manīṣiṇaḥ_ — thinkers: _manas_ is the mind and _īśa_ the controller; these are not ordinary people but thoughtful people `12:00`.

#### Respecting views one disagrees with [12:00 – 16:00]

- Kṛṣṇa does not call those who say work should be renounced foolish; he calls them thinkers — though he will not support their conclusion.
- People often think "my opinion alone is right": the totalitarian boss's "my way or the highway"; in religious circles, "my God is the only true God; if you don't believe you'll go to hell — and we'll help you get there faster."
- The Gītā does take an assertive stand, but taking a stand does not mean throttling — choking — all opposing voices.
- Being respectful does not mean agreeing: intelligent people may still be deficient in their understanding, and that deficiency must be acknowledged if we seriously seek the conclusive truth.
- A person whose opinion differs from scripture need not be foolish; he may be a serious seeker who has not yet come to appreciate scriptural wisdom. Respect serious thinking wherever it is found `16:00`.

#### The same spirit in the fifth chapter [16:00]

- When comparing _karma-yoga_ and _jñāna-yoga_ (renunciation of work), Kṛṣṇa first says both are conducive to liberation, and only then says which is better.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
By calling those he will contradict "thinkers," Kṛṣṇa models firm conviction without contempt — a conclusive stand can coexist with respect for sincere opposing views. [12:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.4 [18:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.4 -->

**Context.** After quoting the two schools, Kṛṣṇa says: now hear My conclusion about renunciation, which is of three kinds `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Niścaya_ — certainty; the conclusion in a subject where different people hold different opinions `18:00`.
- _Puruṣa-vyāghra_ — tiger among men; the teacher notes this is the only place where Arjuna is addressed by this name `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### The three modes as a recurring reference point [18:00]

- Kṛṣṇa explained the three modes in the fourteenth chapter and has returned to them repeatedly — in the seventeenth chapter and now in the eighteenth.
- Here he applies them to _tyāga_, and later to other elements: each is of three types, in goodness, passion and ignorance.

#### Tiger among men [20:00]

- In the Vedic context, for a king or _kṣatriya_ to be compared to a tiger is a great honour — as powerful, ferocious and fearless as a tiger.
- Even when someone is in a subordinate position, like Arjuna as a student, his qualifications can be recognised and appreciated; throughout the Gītā Kṛṣṇa addresses Arjuna by glorifying epithets, and Arjuna likewise glorifies Kṛṣṇa.
- Just as a tiger cannot be overcome by small animals in the jungle, a tiger among men will not be overcome by small, animal-like misconceptions — Arjuna can conquer all misconceptions and understand the conclusive truth.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa prefaces His conclusion by honouring Arjuna as a tiger among men — confidence that Arjuna can overcome every petty misconception — and by announcing that renunciation itself comes in three modes. [18:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.5 [22:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.5 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa's definite opinion: acts of sacrifice, charity and penance are not to be given up; they must be performed, for they purify even the great souls `22:00`, `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tyājya_ — that which can or should be given up; these activities are not `22:00`.
- _Pāvanāni manīṣiṇām_ — they purify even the thinkers; the same word _manīṣiṇaḥ_ from 18.3 is used again `22:00`.
- _Yajña_, _dāna_, _tapas_ — the three parts of how prescribed _karma_ is generally performed `24:00`.

#### No one goes beyond purification [22:00]

- Nobody goes beyond the power of these practices.
- In _bhakti_, one may not require the ritualistic rites of the Vedas, but _bhakti_ is not only the emotion of the heart — it also has expression as an art: folding hands, offering obeisances, offering _ārati_. These are our rituals.
- Even advanced devotees perform them, because they keep purifying; no seeker can say "I'm so pure now, I don't need this."

#### Ekādaśī: same act, different purpose [24:00]

- Devotees observe _Ekādaśī_ to go closer to Kṛṣṇa: minimising bodily activities like eating, and if possible sleeping, to have more time for _bhakti_.
- Others observe it for material benefits; books glorifying particular _Ekādaśīs_ describe the benefit of each. They are accepted and may inspire people to observe seriously, but they are not the primary recommendation for a devotee.
- A devotee focuses on the supreme benefit — love for Kṛṣṇa — and does whatever is required for that.

#### Marriage as a sacrifice that regulates the mind [26:00 – 30:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport gives the example of the marriage ceremony, meant to regulate the mind so that it becomes peaceful for spiritual advancement.
- Without experience of worldly pleasures, the mind can become hyperactive, fantasising, restless.
- Within a dharmic framework, gratification brings satisfaction of that hankering and the realisation that it is not as great as the mind made it out to be.
- Today people compare their spouses with the most good-looking people shown in the media and think, "with that person I'd be satisfied" — so the mind never becomes regulated `26:00 – 28:00`.
- Taking marriage seriously means moving from fantasy to reality: "things could have been worse, could have been better; this is what I have, let me accept it and move forward." This does not mean accepting abusive situations `28:00`.
- The goal of a peaceful mind is spiritual advancement: chanting also calms the mind, but a constantly agitated mind prevents focus on Kṛṣṇa `28:00 – 30:00`.

#### Prabhupāda conducting marriages [30:00]

- Prabhupāda wrote that _sannyāsīs_ are not meant to do this, yet he himself arranged and conducted his disciples' marriages: he was the only spiritual master then and they depended on him for everything.
- A _sannyāsī_ is not to associate with the opposite sex, but marriage is not forbidden for everyone — there is another _āśrama_ in which those disciples could make the best advancement.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sacrifice, charity and penance are never to be abandoned because they keep purifying even the most advanced — whether as a devotee's daily rituals or as marriage that calms the mind for spiritual life. [22:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.6 [30:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.6 -->

**Context.** All these activities should be performed without attachment and without expectation of result, as a matter of duty — this is Kṛṣṇa's final, definite opinion `30:00 – 32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kartavyāni_ — understanding "this is what I should be doing," one's duty `30:00`.
- _Niścitam matam_ — "My definite opinion"; Kṛṣṇa gives a conclusive understanding, having earlier promised "this is My conclusion" `30:00 – 32:00`.

#### Work with detachment [32:00]

- Do what is required, understanding it to be one's duty; work with detachment and do not get entangled.
- Prabhupāda writes that everything that leads to Kṛṣṇa consciousness should be encouraged.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's settled conclusion is simple: perform prescribed duties as duties, giving up attachment and expectation of results. [30:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.7 [32:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.7 -->

**Context.** Giving up prescribed duties that are not worthy of being given up, out of illusion, is renunciation in the mode of ignorance `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Moha_ — illusion; renunciation done out of illusion is _tāmasic_ `32:00`, `36:00`.
- Three words Kṛṣṇa uses to characterise the modes `36:00`:
  - _prakāśa_ (illumination) — goodness;
  - _pravṛtti_ (action) — passion;
  - _moha_ (illusion) — ignorance.

#### Renunciation is not automatically good [32:00 – 34:00]

- Renunciation is glorified in Vedic scriptures; even great kings respect saintly people.
- But the Gītā goes beyond superficial analysis: renunciation itself can be in the three modes, and renunciation in ignorance leads to no elevation.
- Renunciation is ultimately meant to facilitate devotion and liberation.

#### The hippies' renunciation [34:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda would say the hippies had done the first part — giving up — but not the second part: following the Gītā.
- They had given up duties to nation, family, religion, education and culture, and many lived in terrible conditions; the places in New York and San Francisco where Prabhupāda first went had become places of great degradation.
- Whatever sparks of spiritual hankering they had initially were lost as they went deeper into drugs; their only regulative principle was to break all regulative principles.
- Merely giving up activity is not the renunciation glorified in Vedic literature; it is _tāmasic_ renunciation.

#### Illumination or illusion? [36:00]

- In a pious culture, people assume a renounced person must have some great wisdom or enlightenment behind the renunciation.
- Kṛṣṇa underscores that renunciation may come from illusion, not illumination: rejecting one's duties and responsibilities is _tāmasic_, and He does not recommend it at all.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Renunciation can spring from illusion rather than illumination; abandoning prescribed duty without a higher engagement — as the hippies did — is renunciation in ignorance and leads nowhere. [32:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.8 [38:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.8 -->

**Context.** One who gives up prescribed duties as troublesome, out of fear of bodily discomfort, renounces in the mode of passion and does not obtain the fruit of renunciation `38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kāya-kleśa_ — trouble for the body; "this involves too much trouble, so I won't do it" `38:00`.
- _Tyāga-phala_ — the fruit of renunciation: renunciation is not itself the goal; it should lead to liberation `38:00`.
- _Kāma_ and _krodha_ — the desire for sense gratification and the anger or frustration when desires are not fulfilled; both arise from passion, as Kṛṣṇa says in 3.37 `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### Evasion, not renunciation [38:00 – 40:00]

- Here renunciation comes not from illusion but from evasion — not doing what one is expected to do.
- Examples:
  - A boy whose love affair fails decides, "now I'll become a _brahmacārī_."
  - Someone in a demanding job of 12–16 hours thinks, "why work like this? I'll just go to the temple, chant, dance and be happy."
- Frustration may give an initial spark toward renunciation, but it will not last.

#### Why frustration belongs to passion [40:00 – 44:00]

- We must philosophically understand that this world is a place of suffering — but that understanding comes from goodness or the cultivation of goodness.
- Both the craving for sense gratification and frustration in it are characteristics of passion.
- A person in passion is not convinced that this world is miserable; he only thinks _my_ world is miserable — "if I move from a small city to a big city, from India to America, I'll be happy."
- "The grass is always greener on the other side" — like a horse grazing by a river, seeing greener grass across it.
- Situations can sometimes be preferable, but that must be decided by calm deliberation, not impulse `44:00`.

#### Māyā keeps us hopefully frustrated [44:00 – 46:00]

- Māyā is so cunning that she never makes us hopelessly frustrated; she keeps us hopefully frustrated — suffering after suffering, then just as we decide to leave, some pleasure, then suffering again.
- Is there happiness in this world? There is enough happiness to make us forget it is a miserable place.

#### The alcoholic and his hangover [46:00 – 50:00]

- A drunk lying in the gutter tells a friend, "who is suffering? I'm enjoying!" — that is his illusion.
- If alcohol gave no exhilaration, no one would drink; the addict ignores the trouble before and after — money, time, disgrace, hangovers, vomiting, babbling, long-term health problems — because the spike of pleasure "seems worth it."
- The frustration during a hangover is temporary; when the next temptation comes he drinks again. Becoming sober — free from addiction — requires serious thinking, acceptance, determination, sometimes counselling or a de-addiction retreat; and taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa's holy name can act very powerfully.
- Likewise, frustration with material life alone is not enough to reject it. One must pin one's hope on Kṛṣṇa; turning the heart from the world to Kṛṣṇa requires philosophical education and devotional purification `50:00`.

#### Lessons for joining an āśrama [50:00 – 54:00]

- In the movement's early days in the West, people who wanted to join immediately were allowed to; over time it was recognised that those joining as _brahmacārīs_ need to continue.
- Now they should prove their commitment by practising seriously for several years, observed by senior devotees; those studying are best advised to complete their education — dedication should be sustained, not impulsive `52:00`.
- In India and other traditional cultures sons are expected to care for their parents in old age; that responsibility cannot be abandoned. Working for some time also removes illusions about how they could have enjoyed material life `52:00`.
- Householders cannot become irresponsible in the name of transcendence — going on pilgrimages is good, but not so many that one shirks responsibilities `54:00`.
- Avoid both extremes — neglecting spiritual life for material life, or material life for spiritual life; learn the balance through guidance from seniors and one's own experience.
- Renunciation is not so cheap: one qualifies for it through positive spiritual attachment to Kṛṣṇa, not mere frustration `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Giving up duty because it is troublesome is passion, not renunciation: frustration may spark a turn, but only philosophical conviction and devotional attachment to Kṛṣṇa can sustain it. [38:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.9 [54:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.9 -->

**Context.** When one performs prescribed duty because it ought to be done, giving up material association and attachment to the fruit, that renunciation is in the mode of goodness `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Renunciation in goodness — action in renunciation: not renunciation _of_ action but renunciation _in_ action, doing action in a renounced spirit `56:00`.

#### How Kṛṣṇa answered Arjuna [56:00]

- Arjuna asked Kṛṣṇa to explain _sannyāsa_ and _tyāga_, but Kṛṣṇa does not go into a technical, hair-splitting analysis of the two terms.
- In 18.7 He uses both words in the same verse, and in His answer uses them interchangeably.
- Instead, He takes up the broad principle of the three modes and answers Arjuna's question through it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Renunciation in goodness is doing one's duty because it should be done, free from attachment — renunciation in action rather than of action, which is Kṛṣṇa's real answer to Arjuna. [54:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
