## Continuity & Recap from Preceding Class

- The first section of the chapter (to 18.9) showed that action with detachment — action in the mood of renunciation — is far better than renunciation of action `00:00`.
- Renunciation in passion — giving up duty because it is troublesome to the body (_kāya-kleśa_) — does not bring the fruit of renunciation `04:00`.

#### Bhakti as the synthesis of karma and jñāna [00:00 – 02:00]

- Here _karma_ means not _karma-yoga_ but the ritualistic, fruitive section of the Vedas.
  - **_Karma_** — positive: natural, because we all want to be active; negative: most actions cause bondage.
  - **_Jñāna_** — positive: liberating, since one avoids activity; negative: inactivity is unnatural.
- The teacher cites the European thinker Hegel: a thesis, an antithesis, and a synthesis that integrates the virtues of both while removing their blemishes.
- _Bhakti_ is that synthesis: acting out of devotion is natural, and because the action is motivated by the longing to serve the Lord rather than one's own desires, it is liberating, not binding `02:00`.

#### Not all jñānīs are māyāvādīs [02:00]

- The teacher cites a commentator's classification of _jñānīs_:
  1. those who do not practise _bhakti_ at all, only analysis — they fall back;
  2. those who practise _bhakti_ but offend the Lord's form — they also fall back;
  3. those who practise _bhakti_ to attain _sāyujya_ (merging) — referred to in the twelfth chapter and again in 18.54–55;
  4. those who practise _bhakti_ and by the association of devotees attain _prema_ — the topmost _jñānīs_.
- So the word _jñānī_ should not always be equated with _māyāvādīs_.

#### Renounce out of super-ambition [02:00 – 04:00]

- The goal of renunciation is purification and illumination. Misery can be an initial spur but not a sustainer; for that, philosophical conviction and devotional attraction are needed.
- Renounce not out of lack of ambition but out of super-ambition; not because what the world offers is too tough, but because it is too little.
- In material life the pleasure is not worth the trouble; in spiritual life the happiness is worth whatever trouble is required.

#### Neither torment nor pamper the body [04:00]

- In 17.5–6 Kṛṣṇa calls those who torment the body out of illusion foolish, even demoniac.
- The other extreme is pampering the body — giving up even prescribed duties if they cause trouble.
- Focus instead on what brings purification, elevation and liberation; accept bodily trouble when duty requires it. Prabhupāda's standard examples: taking bath in the morning, or a housewife cooking in summer.

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 18.10 [06:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.10 -->

**Context.** Having described renunciation in the three modes (18.7–18.9), Kṛṣṇa now explains how renunciation in goodness leads to liberation: the intelligent renouncer, situated in goodness, neither hates inauspicious work nor is attached to auspicious work `04:00 – 06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kuśala_ / _akuśala_ — _kuśala_ can mean expert, or that which brings good results; some work is pleasant to us and some unpleasant `06:00`.
- _Tyāgī_ — the renouncer, who is neither attached nor averse `06:00`.
- _Sattva-samāviṣṭa_ — absorbed in goodness `06:00`.
- _Medhāvī_ — truly intelligent `06:00`.
- _Chinna-saṁśaya_ — one whose doubts are cut, having understood that the art of work is to work with detachment `06:00`.

#### The material need not monopolise the emotional [06:00]

- Some work we dislike but do because it is duty; some we like, but we don't simply keep doing it and forget the higher reality of life. Either way, the material does not dominate one's conceptions.
- Such a person's intelligence has understood that the material does not have to have a monopoly on the emotional — our emotions need not be restricted to material things.

#### Doubts that disappear [06:00 – 08:00]

- Without this art, one has many doubts: "If I do this, will I get enjoyment? If I get enjoyment, will I also get bondage? Should I renounce all work?"
- For one who acts in _karma-yoga_ with detachment, all these doubts go away.
- Kṛṣṇa is speaking of _karma-yoga_ that will mature into _bhakti-yoga_ later in the chapter; Prabhupāda's purports explain everything from the perspective of that conclusion `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The renouncer in goodness neither runs from unpleasant duties nor clings to pleasant ones; having learnt to work with detachment, his doubts about enjoyment and bondage are cut. [06:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.11 [08:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.11 -->

**Context.** It is impossible for an embodied being to give up all activities; therefore one who gives up the fruits of action is the true renouncer `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Na śakyam_ — "it is not possible" `08:00`.
- _Aśeṣataḥ_ — completely, with nothing left; Kṛṣṇa uses this word several times, most prominently in the seventh chapter when promising to speak knowledge in full `08:00`.
- _Karma-phala-tyāgī_ — one who gives up the fruits of work; such a person is the true renunciate `08:00`.

#### Why giving up all action is impossible [08:00 – 10:00]

- Someone may argue: to avoid bondage, simply avoid work. Kṛṣṇa says that is not possible.
- As long as we are embodied, the body itself requires activity for survival.
- Science shows many involuntary activities go on inside the body — breathing, digestion, circulation — which we cannot stop.
- We must also eat, earn and protect ourselves; giving up all action would make survival impossible.
- Moreover, it is our very nature to be active — whether embodied or liberated, the soul is active. Kṛṣṇa made the same point in the third chapter: no one can stay without action even for a moment `10:00`.
- Therefore one should act in a way that does not cause bondage.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Since both the body and the soul are by nature active, renunciation cannot mean stopping action; it means giving up the fruits of action. [08:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.12 [10:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.12 -->

**Context.** For one who is not renounced, the threefold fruits of action — desirable, undesirable and mixed — accrue after death; but those in the renounced order do not have such results to suffer or enjoy `10:00 – 12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Atyāgī_ — one who is not renounced, i.e. who is attached `12:00`.
- _Iṣṭa_ — good results from good _karma_ `12:00`.
- _Aniṣṭa_ — bad results from bad _karma_ `12:00`.
- _Miśra_ — mixed results from actions that combine good and bad `12:00`.
- _Pretya_ — after death `12:00`.

#### Two contrasts [12:00 – 14:00]

- **First contrast (18.11):** between one who tries to give up all action and one who acts with detachment. Both seek liberation, but giving up all action is practically impossible; acting without attachment leads to liberation.
- **Second contrast (18.12):** a broader one — between one who acts with attachment and one who acts without attachment. The first experiences bondage; the second experiences liberation.
- Kṛṣṇa concludes that the _sannyāsī_ will not become bound even slightly `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The fruits of action — good, bad and mixed — bind only the attached; the one who acts without attachment stays free from them and attains liberation. [12:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.13 – 18.14 [14:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.13 -->

<!-- vedabase:18.14 -->

**Context.** The second section of the chapter begins: the knowledge that keeps a _karma-yogī_ free from bondage. Kṛṣṇa explains, in terms of _sāṅkhya_, the five factors involved in bringing any action to its fruit `14:00`, `16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mahā-bāhu_ — Kṛṣṇa addresses Arjuna this way for the second time in this chapter `14:00`.
- _Sāṅkhya_ — analysis of material elements into material factors; one of the six systems of Vedic philosophy `14:00 – 16:00`.
- The five factors `16:00 – 22:00`:
  - _Adhiṣṭhāna_ — the place of action; Prabhupāda's translation gives "the body"; some commentators take it as the place or arena of action.
  - _Kartā_ — the doer.
  - _Karaṇa_ — the instruments of action, i.e. the senses.
  - _Ceṣṭā_ — the various endeavours.
  - _Daiva_ — destiny, the will of the Lord; specifically, the Supersoul, since material actions are being discussed.

#### Sāṅkhya as the stem, Vedānta as the flower [14:00 – 16:00]

- Prabhupāda explains: _sāṅkhya_ is the stock of knowledge, and Vedānta is the final stock of knowledge accepted by all leading _ācāryas_.
- The teacher plays on the word "stock": like the stem that supports a flower, _sāṅkhya_ supports, and the fully blossomed flower is Vedānta.
- They are connected, not opposed: _sāṅkhya_'s metaphysical division of matter is an integral part of Vedānta, but Vedānta goes beyond matter to spirit. Kṛṣṇa does not reject _sāṅkhya_; He uses its conceptual analysis here `16:00`.
- Prabhupāda explains that the ultimate control is vested in the Supersoul `14:00`.

#### The batsman hitting a six [16:00 – 22:00]

- **Doer** — the batsman has to be alive; a dead batsman cannot hit.
- **Instruments** — if both hands are in plaster, he cannot even hold the bat.
- **Endeavour** — against a top-class bowler, just swinging the bat doesn't work; talent becomes achievement only through serious practice.
- **Destiny** — every sports person knows destiny plays a role:
  - cricketers keep superstitions — a lucky handkerchief or bandana, or one who closed every commode lid in the dressing room before batting;
  - the particular link may be superstitious, but performers know peak performance is not just a matter of diligence; these are attempts to reach out to a higher providence `18:00 – 20:00`;
  - a champion batsman can suddenly be clean bowled and go "out of form" — nobody wants that, yet it happens `20:00`.
- "Fortune favours the brave" is true, but a wise person still acknowledges a higher factor `20:00`.
- **Place / body** — the whole body must be fit and the specific organ functional (the batsman's hands, an orator's speaking organ); the arena matters too — a bad pitch makes performance harder `22:00`.

#### Why Kṛṣṇa lists the factors [22:00]

- A wise person acknowledges all these factors and therefore does not arrogate credit to himself when it is not due.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every action needs a place, a doer, instruments, endeavour and the sanction of the Supersoul; seeing this, the wise do not take credit for results that depend on far more than themselves. [16:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.15 [22:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.15 -->

**Context.** Whatever right or wrong action a person performs by body, mind or speech is caused by these five factors `22:00 – 24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śarīra_, _manas_, _vāk_ — body, mind and speech: the three channels of action `22:00`.
- Right and wrong — Prabhupāda's purport says these are determined by whether an action is according to scripture `24:00`.

#### The pervasiveness of the five factors [24:00]

- Our activities sometimes give good results and sometimes bad.
- Whether bodily, mental or verbal, auspicious or inauspicious — all involve these five factors.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
No action — of body, mind or speech, good or bad — falls outside the five factors; scripture decides which actions are right. [22:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.16 [24:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.16 -->

**Context.** One who thinks himself the only doer, not considering the five factors, is not intelligent and cannot see things as they are `24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Akṛta-buddhitvāt_ — the intelligence is not functioning properly `24:00`.
- _Durmati_ — misdirected consciousness `24:00`.
- _Na paśyati_ — such a person is not a seer `24:00`.

#### The doer, but not the only doer [26:00]

- People find "we are not the doers" hard to understand: "If I did not speak, people would not hear; if a scientist had not invented it, the gadget would not exist; if the general had not led well, the war would not be won."
- One _is_ a doer — the illusion is to think one is the _only_ doer.
- Even an intelligent general who makes the right decisions needs many other factors to go right for success.
- Consciousness obsessed with ego cannot understand what is happening.

#### Acknowledge the Supersoul [26:00 – 28:00]

- As Prabhupāda mentions, we must acknowledge the role of the Supersoul in whatever happens.
- Focusing only on material causes, or only on oneself at the level of ego, gives no proper understanding; the final cause is the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The error is not in seeing oneself as a doer but in seeing oneself as the only doer; real vision acknowledges the many factors and above all the Supersoul. [24:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.17 [28:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.17 -->

**Context.** Taking the converse perspective, Kṛṣṇa says one who is not motivated by false ego and whose intelligence is not entangled does not kill even if he kills, and is not bound `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ahaṅkṛta-bhāva_ — false ego, the mentality of being the doer `28:00`.
- _Buddhi na lipyate_ — the intelligence is not entangled `28:00`.

#### Arjuna's dilemma addressed [28:00]

- Killing is considered a very severe crime, and in one sense what a _kṣatriya_ does is killing; this refers directly to Arjuna's dilemma — should I fight, should I kill?
- Kṛṣṇa says that without false ego, one will not be bound and will not actually be killing.

#### Not a licence for violence [28:00 – 32:00]

- Can a murderer drop bombs and say "I'm not the doer"? Then the police will say, "We'll hang you — and we're also not the doers." Anyone could slap another and say the same.
- Renouncing doership is not a simple verbal claim; we are the choosers and the doers, and we are responsible for our actions `30:00`.
- Even terrorists know they cannot act independently — so many things must go right (from their perspective) for a plot to succeed.
- The verse assumes action according to scripture, as part of prescribed duty, done with detachment; whimsical, arbitrary action certainly causes bondage and cannot be rationalised by this verse.
- Prabhupāda's example: a soldier who kills under the command of a superior officer is not judged, but one who kills on his own account is judged by a court of law `32:00`.

#### Not silence, not violence, but transcendence [40:00]

- The Gītā's message is neither silence nor violence but transcendence.
- This is the only reference to war in the last six chapters after 11.32–34, and it is indirect: Kṛṣṇa does not say "fight," only that even one who fights and kills will not be bound.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prescribed duty done without false ego does not bind — even a warrior's — but the verse is no licence for whimsical violence: we remain responsible choosers, and the Gītā's message is transcendence. [28:00 – 32:00, 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.18 [32:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.18 -->

**Context.** Knowledge, the object of knowledge and the knower are the factors that motivate action; the instruments, the act and the doer are its constituents. The teacher calls this a very important verse for understanding the flow of the next twenty verses `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Codanā_ — impetus or inspiration for action `32:00`.
- _Jñānam_ — knowledge itself; _jñeyam_ — the object of knowledge; _parijñātā_ — the knower `32:00 – 34:00`.
- _Karaṇam_ — the instrument; _karma_ — the action; _kartā_ — the doer `32:00`.

#### Sightseeing and studying [34:00 – 36:00]

- A person goes into nature to look at birds, flowers, clouds and mountains: he is the knower, observation is the knowledge, and the goal of that knowledge may be satisfaction or a deeper appreciation of nature — ultimately happiness.
- College students may study engineering not because they love it but for employment, and through employment, enjoyment; so knowledge and the objective of knowledge can be different.
- To become an eloquent speaker: the person, the voice (_karaṇa_) and the act of speaking — repeated fifty or a hundred times — are the components of action `36:00`.

#### Jīva, jagat, jagadīśa [36:00 – 38:00]

- The same analysis can be seen through three basic factors: _jīva_ (the soul), _jagat_ (the material world) and _jagadīśa_ (the Lord — not only of the material world but of the _jīvas_ too, of all existence). All three begin with "ja," an easy way to remember them.
- Prabhupāda refers to them as the enjoyed, the enjoyer and consciousness in his title for chapter 13; the Lord is the supreme enjoyer.

#### The jīva desires, the īśvara sanctions, prakṛti executes [38:00]

- A batsman decides to hit a six (the _jīva_ desires), but he may miss completely and be bowled; for success the _īśvara_ must sanction, and then material nature executes.
- Mapping this onto the five factors:
  - _jīva_ — the _kartā_;
  - _daiva_ — the _īśvara_;
  - _adhiṣṭhāna_ and _karaṇa_ — _prakṛti_ (the body and its senses);
  - _ceṣṭā_ — a combination of _jīva_ and _prakṛti_: the _jīva_ endeavours through the body, as a singer becomes expert by laborious practice with the throat `38:00 – 40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Action has three motivating factors — knowledge, its object and the knower — and three constituents — instrument, act and doer; behind them all, the jīva desires, the Lord sanctions and nature executes. [32:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.19 [40:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa reduces the six elements of 18.18 to three — knowledge, action and doer — and asks Arjuna to hear how each is of three kinds according to the modes `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### Why six become three [42:00 – 44:00]

- **Knower and doer merge** — the same person who learns about singing (knower) then sings (doer).
- **Knowledge and action are kept** — knowledge relates to the senses of perception; action relates to the working senses.
- **Object of knowledge and instruments are left out** — they are practically dead matter, made of certain elements in certain modes; their consciousness cannot be transformed. Kṛṣṇa's analysis is transformational, so He focuses on what can be transformed `44:00`.

#### The chocolate example [44:00 – 46:00]

- A child hears from friends, mother and brother that chocolate is delicious — knowledge.
- "I want chocolate" — he asks his parents — action.
- He goes and gets it — the doer.
- Knowledge, action and doer together comprise the unfolding of an action.

#### The road ahead [44:00 – 46:00]

- Knowledge in three modes: 18.20–22; action: 18.23–25; doer: 18.26–28.
- After a transitional verse, Kṛṣṇa will analyse _buddhi_ (understanding — seeing things in proper perspective, at the beginning of implementing action), _dhṛti_ (determination to continue acting) and _sukha_ (the happiness we ultimately want, discussed in 18.36–39), each in the three modes.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa analyses only knowledge, action and doer because they can be transformed; the matter-based elements simply are as they are. [42:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.20 [46:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.20 -->

**Context.** Knowledge by which one undivided spiritual nature is seen in all living entities, though they are divided into innumerable forms, is knowledge in goodness. Prabhupāda's purport notes that after worship in the modes (chapter 17), Kṛṣṇa now describes types of knowledge `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Unity beneath diversity [48:00]

- At the material level we see differences — male and female, Indian and American, young and old, good-looking and not.
- Knowledge in goodness sees the oneness of spirit beneath that diversity.
- It is not necessarily complete spiritual knowledge; it can be more or less a kind of impersonalism.

#### Reaching it by inference: Socrates [48:00 – 50:00]

- One need not necessarily refer to scripture to reach knowledge in goodness; by living in goodness one can infer these truths.
- Prabhupāda speaks of Socrates, who was not in any disciplic line but had metaphysical knowledge of the soul, was fearless at death because of his understanding of the soul's immortality, and in his dialogues consoled a friend with arguments for the soul's immortality.
- He did not know Kṛṣṇa, but he did understand the soul.

#### Spiritual oneness vs. humanist oneness [50:00 – 52:00]

- People say "all Indians are one," "all human beings are one family" — but the basis of such oneness is still the bodily conception: others' bodies are similar to mine, so I have special affection for this "human animal" over a dog, cat or cow.
- Knowledge in goodness sees the universality of spirit below the diversity of form — a common spirit in all living beings.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge in goodness sees one spiritual nature beneath all bodily diversity — a deeper unity than any oneness based on similar bodies. [48:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.21 [52:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.21 -->

**Context.** Knowledge by which one sees a different type of living entity in every different body is knowledge in passion `52:00`.

#### Mistaking outer form for inner nature [52:00]

- Knowledge in passion takes one's external form to be one's essential nature: a tree, an animal and a human look different, so their inner natures must differ.
- The teacher mentions thinkers, including Aristotle, who described different kinds of souls — vegetable, animal and human — taking "soul" merely as whatever gives life.

#### Same soul, different covering [52:00 – 54:00]

- The Vedic understanding: all living beings have the same kind of soul; the difference is in the extent of covering.
  - In a plant body the soul is covered much more;
  - in an animal body, less;
  - in a human body, the least.
- The difference is in covering, not in essence or substance; when the covering ends, one can see the commonality of all beings.
- The view that plant and animal souls come into existence and die with the body is superficial. The teacher adds that he is not criticising any tradition — only pointing out schools of thought that, however evolved in other ways, are _rājasic_ in this analysis of the soul `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge in passion infers different essences from different forms; knowledge in goodness sees the same soul under different degrees of covering. [52:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
