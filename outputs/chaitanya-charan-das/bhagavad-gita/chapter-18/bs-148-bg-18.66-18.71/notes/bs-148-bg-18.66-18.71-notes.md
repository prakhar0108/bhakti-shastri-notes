## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 18.66 [00:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.66 -->

**Context.** Continuing from the last class, the teacher explains "Me alone" through a commentary by an _ācārya_, addresses whether the verse is exclusivist, shows its examples in the _Bhāgavatam_, and explains "do not fear" `00:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sādhana_ — the means; _sādhya_ — the goal `00:00`.
- _Ekam_ — "alone," applying both to "Me" and to "surrender" `00:00`.
- _Dharma_ — here, religious formularies or externals associated with religion `04:00`.
- _Apara-dharma_, _adharma_, _para-dharma_ — lower religious duty, irreligion, and the supreme _dharma_ of devotion `08:00`.
- _Mā śucaḥ_ — do not fear; give up pseudo-security `14:00`.

#### Supreme goal, only means [00:00 – 02:00]

- Explaining a commentary by Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, the teacher says "alone" qualifies two things:
  - **Me alone** — Kṛṣṇa is the only object of worship; give up all other goals, such as _mokṣa_, worship of the demigods and worship of the Supersoul.
  - **Surrender alone** — surrender to Kṛṣṇa, i.e. _bhakti_, is the only means; give up all other means.
- So Kṛṣṇa is the supreme _sādhya_ and _bhakti_ is the only _sādhana_.

#### Inclusive, not exclusivist [02:00 – 04:00]

- The Vedic culture is a multi-level form of worship: different goals and means for people at different levels, all ultimately meant to lead to pure, unalloyed devotion to the one Supreme Lord.
- Exclusivist would mean "this is the only way; all other ways are false." Kṛṣṇa does not say that: He calls the other paths _dharmas_ — religious, virtuous and elevating, but lower, and not leading to the fastest or highest elevation.
- Kṛṣṇa Himself acknowledges different paths and destinations, e.g. 9.25 — demigod worshippers go to the demigods, ancestor worshippers to the ancestors; and reaching heaven is itself no small thing `04:00`.
- Inclusive does not mean everything is equal: other paths are potent in their own way, but not as potent as _bhakti_ `04:00`.

#### A universalist declaration [04:00]

- Far from exclusivist, the verse is universalist: _dharma_ here can mean religious externals, and Kṛṣṇa says not to get caught up in them but to focus on the inner essence — surrender to Him.

#### Bhāgavatam examples [04:00 – 12:00]

- **The _yājñika brāhmaṇas_' wives** `04:00 – 06:00`:
  - A wife's _dharma_ is to assist her husband, all the more when he is virtuous and performing sacrifice, which needs both husband and wife.
  - When Kṛṣṇa's friends asked for food and the _brāhmaṇas_ neglected them, the wives gave up both duties — to their husbands and to the joint sacrifice — and went to Kṛṣṇa.
- **Bali Mahārāja** rejected the instruction of his teacher, Śukrācārya, who was like a spiritual master for him `06:00`.
- **The _gopīs_**, in the highest degree `06:00 – 10:00`:
  - In traditional culture a woman's chastity, and even her good name, are considered most valuable; for a woman to go to another man at night is a scandal.
  - When Kṛṣṇa plays His flute, the _gopīs_ give up everything — their duties as wives and even to their children, which is extremely important; no one can take a mother's place.
  - It may seem immoral, but the women are the most exalted devotees and the man is the Supreme Lord.
  - An ordinary woman going to a man goes from _apara-dharma_ to _adharma_ and is condemned; the _gopīs_ give up _apara-dharma_ for _para-dharma_.
  - In the _rāsa-līlā_, when Kṛṣṇa assumes the form of Viṣṇu, the searching _gopīs_ are disinterested and simply ask Him where Kṛṣṇa is — "Me alone" `12:00`.
- The lesson: for Kṛṣṇa's sake we must be ready to give up not only the bad of this world (sinful activities) but, if required, even its good (pious activities), because both are at the material level while Kṛṣṇa is transcendental `10:00`.

#### Externals serve the essence [10:00 – 12:00]

- Surrender to Kṛṣṇa is the supreme virtue; religious externals are important because they enable us to surrender to Him.
- The teacher cites a famous _Viṣṇu Purāṇa_ verse: the purpose of all rules and regulations is to always remember Kṛṣṇa and never forget Him.
- This does not make externals unimportant — "I won't chant, I'll just focus on the essence" is wrong; externals do help us reach the essence. Here _dharma_ refers more to material religiosity, such as sacrifices, to be given up if it does not help us take shelter of Kṛṣṇa.
- From the purport: we should become attracted to Kṛṣṇa's beautiful form, which is the supreme perfection of life; among the various transcendentalists — _karma-yogī_, empiric philosopher, mystic — the pure devotee is the best `12:00`.

#### Do not fear: giving up pseudo-security [12:00 – 18:00]

- Surrender can involve sacrifice, so we fear; in the world we cling to things that give a sense of security — status, job, relationships `12:00 – 14:00`.
- Even an alcoholic gets a pseudo-security from a familiar routine repeated again and again, though it brings much suffering and little, unstable pleasure `14:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa says: give up pseudo-security and accept the real security of surrender. To protect us, a person must be both strong and caring; Kṛṣṇa is supremely strong and supremely loving `14:00`.
- In the Second Canto, Śukadeva Gosvāmī begins by describing how people rely on body, children, spouse and relatives as their "army" of protectors, yet though they see these perish, they still cling to them — "seeing but not seeing" `16:00`.
  - E.g. some rely on strong muscles, or learn karate or self-defence.
- Such pseudo-security actually causes uncertainty, because we are never sure it will protect us; Kṛṣṇa says, "do not think I will not protect you — I definitely will" `16:00 – 18:00`.
- What we give up is like a drop of water in a desert; the security Kṛṣṇa offers is like an ocean `18:00`.

#### Surrender is dynamic [18:00 – 20:00]

- Shelter is taken primarily by accepting what is favourable and rejecting what is unfavourable; surrender is not passive.
- Kṛṣṇa has given us intelligence and wants us to use it in His service: what is favourable for my _bhakti_ here? what should I not do?
- Śrīla Prabhupāda was always surrendered yet always dynamic — should I write books, start a magazine, start an institution, go to America? — devising new strategies to serve.
- Sometimes surrender means a new project or service; sometimes persevering in the same service despite difficulties.
- We decide by being sincere — when we tell Kṛṣṇa "I want to serve You, please guide me," He gives the intelligence for how to act — and also by consulting senior devotees.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa alone is the goal and surrender alone is the means — not by condemning other paths, but by placing them as lower steps. Surrender asks us to drop pseudo-securities, a drop of water in the desert, for the ocean of Kṛṣṇa's protection, and to serve dynamically with the intelligence He gives. [00:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.67 [20:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.67 -->

**Context.** Having more or less completed the Gītā, Kṛṣṇa now gives its _phala-śruti_ — the glory of the book in terms of hearing and speaking it. He begins with the confidentiality of this knowledge: whom not to tell `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Phala-śruti_ — given at the end of a Vedic book: the benefit of hearing it, its glory `20:00`.
- Four kinds of people not to be told `20:00 – 22:00`:
  - those who do not perform austerity (_tapas_);
  - those who are not devotees — _bhakta_ refers to the attitude of devotion;
  - those not engaged in service — _śuśrūṣā_ refers to the activity of service;
  - those who are envious of Kṛṣṇa.

#### Then who is left? [22:00]

- Practically everyone has some envy of Kṛṣṇa; how many are engaged in His service or doing austerity? So what does the verse mean?
- Kṛṣṇa is referring to the most confidential knowledge (18.63–18.64); people understand according to their _adhikāra_ (eligibility).

#### The father's treasure [22:00 – 26:00]

- A father keeps very rare jewels deep in his treasury and does not tell even his children, because he wants them to grow up first, so they will not squander the treasure.
- Children absorbed in playing marbles while the father has a multi-million-dollar business get only a limited allowance until they become responsible.
- Similarly souls are not grown up: caught in petty, trivial worldly things, envious, not serving. Kṛṣṇa's greatest blessing is Himself — when He reveals His beauty, we are attracted, choose to love Him and enter supreme happiness.
- When one child pleases the father very much, he may take that child into the inner treasury and show the jewels — "this is what I meant for you; don't tell your brothers and sisters yet" — not because it isn't meant for them, but because they are not mature enough `26:00`.

#### Spoken in public, yet confidential [26:00 – 28:00]

- The Gītā was spoken on a battlefield with soldiers on both sides — so Kṛṣṇa wants it given to everyone, yet wants people to grow up to understand it.
- "Don't throw pearls before swine": a pig interested only in stool sees no value in pearls. Kṛṣṇa's love is the supreme jewel; the spiritually immature will devalue it, reject it and commit offences.
- E.g. the envious may hear "surrender to Me alone" and think Kṛṣṇa is arrogant; but He is supremely sweet, and transcendental love is beyond our worldly concepts `28:00`.
- Restriction and revelation are not deprivation but stimulation to grow; as the soul grows, Kṛṣṇa wants to reveal everything to everyone `28:00`.
- From the purport: without understanding Kṛṣṇa from authority, one should not try to comment on the Gītā `30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Gītā's most confidential knowledge is withheld from the envious and unserving not to deprive them but, like a father's hidden jewels, to wait until they grow up enough to value it rather than squander or offend it. [22:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.68 [30:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.68 -->

**Context.** After saying whom not to tell, Kṛṣṇa says how He wants the knowledge shared — through proper channels: whoever explains this supremely confidential knowledge to His devotees will attain pure devotion and certainly come back to Him `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paramaṁ guhyam_ — supremely confidential `30:00`.
- _Parā bhakti_ — transcendental, pure devotion `30:00 – 32:00`.
- _Sādhaka_ — a practitioner moving towards pure devotion `32:00`.

#### Parā bhakti by sharing [30:00 – 32:00]

- Kṛṣṇa uses the phrase _parā bhakti_ in one other significant place, 18.54: one gets it after coming to the _brahma_ platform and becoming completely equipoised towards everything material.
- Here He guarantees it simply for sharing this knowledge of _bhakti_: we do the supreme good to others, attain pure devotional service, and without doubt come back to Him.

#### Preaching is part of sādhana [32:00 – 34:00]

- As Prabhupāda taught, preaching is not just outreach but part of our _sādhana_; we are not pure yet, but by speaking about Kṛṣṇa we please Him, and He blesses us with advancement.
- Don't hesitate thinking "I don't have many realisations": compared with the general Kali-yuga level, a devotee with a few years in Kṛṣṇa consciousness has tremendous knowledge; as our understanding deepens, we share more deeply. We have to begin somewhere.

#### "To My devotees" — according to level [34:00 – 38:00]

- "Devotee" here includes upcoming devotees — the devotionally minded.
- Tell someone who doesn't even accept God exists that God is a blue-complexioned cowherd boy with a peacock feather, and they'll call it mythology; first explain the existence of God and the soul step by step.
- Prabhupāda gave people the experience of Kṛṣṇa through the holy names, focused on basics in classes, and for devotees gave the _Bhāgavatam_, _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ and _Kṛṣṇa_ book.
- Even new people are potential devotees, with dormant attraction to Kṛṣṇa; attracting them is valuable service.
- So this is not a restriction but a precaution: present according to people's level `36:00`.
- E.g. Kṛṣṇa's intimate _rāsa-līlā_ pastime, where the _gopīs_ demonstrate 18.66, is easily misunderstood and has been called immoral; countering that requires systematic education — you can't teach triple integral calculus to a kindergarten student `36:00 – 38:00`.
- Prabhupāda gave the highest goal — love for Kṛṣṇa in Goloka — but systematically, beginning with basic concepts `38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa promises pure devotion and return to Him to anyone who shares His message with the devotionally minded — so preaching is part of our own sādhana, done step by step according to each person's level, not postponed until we feel advanced. [30:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.69 [38:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.69 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says further how dear such sharers are: among all human beings no one is dearer to Him, and in the future no one will ever be dearer `38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Manuṣya_ — human being; in Vedic usage inclusive of men and women, as _nara_ also is in its generic sense `38:00`.
- _Bhavitā_ — in the future `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### Not a sentimental declaration [38:00 – 40:00]

- Sometimes people, overcome by sentiment, say "I love you so much, I'll do anything for you" — and the next moment refuse a request.
- Kṛṣṇa's "even in the future there will be no one dearer" is a guarantee for all time.

#### Kṛṣṇa's eagerness [40:00]

- This shows how eager Kṛṣṇa is for the lost souls to return and how grateful He feels to those who help.
- The same Kṛṣṇa as Caitanya Mahāprabhu, in Kāśī, said He had come to distribute the fruits of love of God, but the impersonalists there were not interested; "I can't carry so many fruits alone — just accept them, and help Me distribute them."

#### Many "bests"; the army [40:00 – 44:00]

- "No one more dear" means there can be many equally dear; in the spiritual realm there are many bests.
- We should not think preachers are dearer than devotees who cook, make garlands or do Deity worship; everyone is dear to Kṛṣṇa.
- Army analogy: not only front-line soldiers but cooks, doctors, intelligence, technology and spies make up the army; all serve the nation, all sacrifice. Those on the front, facing the first bullets, are specially glorious, but it is all teamwork.
- No one should claim privilege or become proud of a specific service; what Kṛṣṇa ultimately sees is how much service attitude I have developed `42:00`.

#### A broad definition of preaching [42:00 – 48:00]

- Preaching is not only sitting on an _āsana_ and speaking; broadly, it means inspiring souls to come towards Kṛṣṇa.
- The teacher quotes Saint Francis's saying, in effect: preach all day, and when required, use a few words `44:00`.
- "People don't care how much we know until they know how much we care" — our actions and care for others, as Kṛṣṇa's lost children, are also preaching `44:00`.
- Deeper preaching removes obstacles on people's path to Kṛṣṇa `44:00 – 46:00`:
  - **Inspiring example** — "these devotees are really good people; I want to become like them."
  - **Removing misconceptions** — Prabhupāda said refuting atheism and evolution brings spiritual advancement; misconceptions form a wall between the soul and Kṛṣṇa, and many of his purports break that wall down.
  - **Social support** — counselling, marriage bureaus and other support systems, so devotees need not go away from Kṛṣṇa to meet material needs `46:00 – 48:00`.
- Preaching is helping everyone come closer to Kṛṣṇa from wherever they are — including helping those who already know Him go deeper `48:00`.
- Those who directly share Kṛṣṇa's message have a special position, but it should make them feel the gravity of their responsibility, not pride `48:00`.

#### Śrīla Prabhupāda's example [48:00 – 50:00]

- In old age, without money, contacts or institutional support, Prabhupāda went alone to America, risking his life and enduring heart attacks, and shared Kṛṣṇa's message.
- All day he spoke about Kṛṣṇa to those who came to meet him, and all night to those who did not — by writing books.
- By his mercy we have _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_, understand its devotional essence, and can share it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
No one is or will ever be dearer to Kṛṣṇa than those who help bring His lost souls back — yet preaching is broad teamwork, from speaking to example, removing misconceptions and social support, and it should breed a sense of responsibility, never pride. [38:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.70 [50:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.70 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now gives a "descending ladder of mercy": if sharing seems too complicated, at least study this sacred conversation — such study is worship of Him with the intelligence `50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dharmyaṁ saṁvādam_ — a conversation about _dharma_ `50:00`.
- _Āvayoḥ_ — between us (Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna) `50:00`.
- _Jñāna-yajña_ — worship by knowledge, with the intelligence `50:00 – 52:00`.
- _Mati_ — opinion; "this is My opinion" `52:00`.

#### Why call it a conversation on dharma? [50:00]

- The conclusion of the conversation is to give up all _dharmas_ — so why is it "dharmic"? Because there is _apara-dharma_ and _para-dharma_: it discusses various levels of _dharma_ and which is supreme.

#### Study as worship [52:00 – 56:00]

- Some think the Gītā is "too philosophical — we are _rasika_ devotees, we'll relish pastimes in the _Bhāgavatam_ and _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_; we don't want to be _jñānīs_." But the Gītā is for devotees.
- Worship is usually offered with a lamp and incense; Kṛṣṇa says it can also be offered with subtle paraphernalia — the intelligence. Studying the Gītā is a limb of _bhakti_, an integral part of devotion.
- People have different opinions, but Kṛṣṇa's is the supreme opinion because He is the Supreme Lord `52:00`.
- The study must have a devotional disposition: scholars who come with a preconceived notion, e.g. that impersonal Brahman is highest, and want to prove it from the Gītā, are not worshipping Kṛṣṇa; in 3.32 Kṛṣṇa calls those envious of Him bewildered in all knowledge, their knowledge lost because they are misdirected `52:00 – 54:00`.
- Studying the Gītā, as in this Bhakti Shastri course, is a very central limb of _bhakti_ `54:00`:
  - **Explicitly** — understanding how much Kṛṣṇa loves us increases our love for Him.
  - **Implicitly** — the study itself is service: worshipping Him with the intelligence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Studying the Gītā with a devotional disposition is not dry jñāna but jñāna-yajña — worship of Kṛṣṇa with the intelligence, as real as offering a lamp — which both reveals His love and deepens ours. [50:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.71 [56:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.71 -->

**Context.** One more step down the ladder: if you can't study, at least hear — one who hears with faith and without envy is freed and attains the auspicious planets of the pious `56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śraddhāvān_ — with faith; _anasūyaḥ_ — without envy `56:00`.
- _Śubhān lokān_ — auspicious planets, attained by those of pious deeds `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### The rope of increasing mercy [56:00]

- The sequence: 18.68–18.69 share the message; 18.70 if you can't, study it; 18.71 if you can't scrutinise it, at least hear it.
- Someone has fallen into a river or well; a rope is thrown: "hold it and climb out." — "I can't." — "Just hold on, I'll pull you out." — "I can't." — so the rescuer makes a lasso: "just put this around you and be still; I'll pull you out."
- So Kṛṣṇa offers ever-increasing mercy, showing how eager He is for our elevation.

#### Which auspicious planets? [58:00]

- The _ācāryas_ explain this is not just heaven, which can be reached by any form of yoga, but elevated abodes like Dhruvaloka — almost like the spiritual world within the material world, where Dhruva, a pure devotee, resides and Lord Viṣṇu is present.
- There, in the association of Dhruva Mahārāja and other exalted devotees, one advances and is ultimately liberated.
- Kṛṣṇa offers multiple levels of worship and encourages us somehow or other to connect with His message and turn back to Him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even one who cannot share or study the Gītā can simply hear it with faith and without envy — Kṛṣṇa's final lasso of mercy — and be lifted to the association of pure devotees and ultimately liberated. [56:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
