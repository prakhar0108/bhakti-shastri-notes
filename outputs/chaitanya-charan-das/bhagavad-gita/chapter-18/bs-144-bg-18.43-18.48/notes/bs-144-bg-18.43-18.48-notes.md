## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 18.43 [00:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.43 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa lists the qualities of the _kṣatriya_, born of his nature `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śauryam_ — heroism, courage, chivalry; readiness to take great risks for adventurous deeds `00:00`.
- _Tejas_ — power `04:00`.
- _Dhṛti_ — determination `06:00`.
- _Dākṣyam_ — expertise, especially in military strategy `10:00 – 12:00`.
- Not fleeing from battle `02:00`, `12:00`.
- _Dāna_ — charity `12:00`.
- _Īśvara-bhāva_ — leadership: the mentality and ability of a controller `08:00`.

#### Different sources of stimulation [02:00 – 04:00]

- Everyone needs some excitement, stimulation and satisfaction: intellectuals get it from new books and concepts; _kṣatriyas_ from adventure.
- These are not inherently right or wrong; they can be made right or wrong.
- The soul delights in ever-new spiritual stimulation with Kṛṣṇa — seeing, remembering, touching, serving Him — the supreme satisfaction. But until we reach that level we cannot suddenly rely only on devotional stimulation; we need satisfaction according to our propensities.

#### Heroism within dharma [04:00]

- _Kṣatriyas_ get their self-worth by fighting evil-doers and protecting the innocent.
- The "macho hero beating the bad guys" in films is attractive; it is not necessarily materialistic if the protection follows the principles of _dharma_ — essential, since this world has evil people.

#### Power and determination [04:00 – 08:00]

- _Kṣatriya_ bows were very heavy, needing great strength to discharge arrows over long distances with penetrating force; physical strength matters less with modern weapons, but still matters in direct combat.
- Determination is needed by all, but _kṣatriyas_ must not just do things but get things done.
- A saying: _brāhmaṇas_ deal with ideas, _kṣatriyas_ with people, _vaiśyas_ with animals, _śūdras_ with things — not a watertight division, but the thrust of each occupation `06:00`.

#### Īśvara-bhāva in the Lord's service [08:00 – 10:00]

- Normally "I am the controller, I am the enjoyer" is what brings and keeps us in the material world, yet Kṛṣṇa presents _īśvara-bhāva_ positively here.
- Functionally, any team of ten or five thousand, any kingdom, needs a controller to direct and supervise.
- It is not the desire to have others dance at one's beck and call; it is understanding that the Supreme Lord is the _parameśvara_, and to get His will done He needs competent agents — _īśvaras_ in the service of the Supreme _Īśvara_.
- Heroism is not wrong as long as it is not independent of Kṛṣṇa's supreme heroism `10:00`.

#### The Hanumān spirit [10:00]

- In the _Rāmāyaṇa_, Lord Rāma is the supreme hero, but Hanumān is also an extraordinary hero.
- The Sundara-kāṇḍa, considered the most beautiful section, is almost entirely about Hanumān's exploits; Rāma is present in the hearts of Hanumān and Sītā.
- Devotees speak of not just being human but being "Hanumān" — loving, serving and acting wonderfully in the Lord's service. _Kṣatriyas_ are like that.

#### Not fleeing and charity [12:00 – 14:00]

- _Kṣatriyas_ go to war either to win or to die, confident that dying in a righteous battle brings heaven; they do not run away.
- Charity was discussed in the 17th chapter (in the three modes) and the 16th (as a godly quality); it pertains especially to householders and _kṣatriyas_, who collect taxes, build the treasury and give to those in need.
- These qualities are seen in the lives of Arjuna, Parīkṣit and even in the way Kṛṣṇa lived `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The kṣatriya's heroism, power and leadership are not ego when used within dharma in the Lord's service — like Hanumān, an īśvara serving the Supreme Īśvara. [00:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.44 [14:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.44 -->

**Context.** The first two lines give the qualities of the _vaiśya_; the last two, those of the _śūdra_ `14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kṛṣi_ — agriculture `14:00`.
- _Go-rakṣya_ — cow protection `14:00`, `16:00`.
- _Vāṇijya_ — trade or business, taking a proper but not disproportionate profit `14:00`, `26:00`.
- _Paricaryā_ — serving others; the role of the _śūdras_ `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### The social body [14:00 – 16:00]

- _Śūdras_ are the feet, _vaiśyas_ the stomach, _kṣatriyas_ the arms, _brāhmaṇas_ the head.
- Every contribution is significant and, when done as work for the Supreme Lord (discussed later), helps one move closer to Kṛṣṇa.
- _Varṇāśrama_ arranges material cooperation and facilitates spiritual advancement.

#### Cow protection, not cattle-rearing [16:00 – 18:00]

- Some translators rendered _go-rakṣya_ as "cattle rearing"; Prabhupāda insisted on "cow protection," since rearing often implies eventual slaughter, never done in Vedic culture.
- A money-minded mentality naturally drops unprofitable investments; if it dominates consciousness, everything is seen only in monetary terms.
- To prevent this, _vaiśyas_ are ordained to do some activities that do not seem financially viable — caring for a cow even after she stops giving milk, rather than selling her to a butcher.
- The cow is like a mother (_go-mātā_); as we care for elderly parents, we care for elderly cows.

#### Answering the skeptics [18:00 – 24:00]

- Some skeptics say that if India does not eat its cows, the cows will eat India, and use the phrase "holy cow" for irrational attachment (the teacher's example: evolution as the holy cow of materialistic science).
- In Vedic culture cows are holy because they are dear to Kṛṣṇa. Buffaloes may give more milk, but Kṛṣṇa is Gopāla; as a person He has His own sweet, independent choices — a particular feather, the flute, the cows `20:00`.
- Even financially, in an agrarian society the cow benefits many: cow urine as medicine; cow dung as manure and, above all, as fuel `20:00 – 22:00`.
- Fossil fuels cause environmental harm in extraction, transport and burning, while cow-dung fuel is already widely used for cooking in rural areas `22:00`.
- India was prosperous for millennia; invaders from Alexander onward came because it was wealthy. Plundered cities recovered within years or decades because the cities were centres, not sources, of prosperity — the sources were the villages, cow protection and agriculture `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### Principles for the whole world [24:00 – 26:00]

- The Gītā's principles apply everywhere — all over the world and all of existence; India is discussed because, in human historical memory, it is where Vedic culture has been present for millennia.
- Today agriculture is trivialised and other occupations glamorised, though it feeds everyone; it should be given its due importance.

#### Vaiśya culture and brahminical culture [26:00]

- Jīva Gosvāmī's _Gopāla-campū_, describing the life of Vraja's residents (transcendental), also gives glimpses of _vaiśya_ culture: God-centred, respectful of _brāhmaṇas_, diligent — even Kṛṣṇa herded the cows.
- "Brahminical culture" means a culture guided by the _brāhmaṇas_ as sources of moral and spiritual authority — not that only _brāhmaṇas_ participate.

#### Śūdras and appropriateness [28:00]

- Many occupations fall under _vaiśya_ and _śūdra_ activity; the _śūdra_'s basic role is assisting the other _varṇas_.
- _Śūdra_ is not a value judgement; the issue is not right or wrong but appropriateness according to individual nature.

#### Do women have their own varṇa? [28:00 – 30:00]

- Some think a woman simply has her husband's _varṇa_; logically this does not hold — husband and wife are separate souls with different pasts and natures; either may be more brahminical.
- The aim is not labelling but "customising professions according to predilections."
- Marrying within the same _varṇa_ was encouraged for greater compatibility, but the woman is not an appendage automatically sharing the man's _varṇa_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Vaiśyas and śūdras are vital parts of the social body; cow protection keeps money from dominating consciousness, and varṇa is about customising work to individual nature, not ranking or labelling people. [14:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.45 [30:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.45 -->

**Context.** Being absorbed in one's own work — as described for the four _varṇas_ in the previous three verses — a human being can attain perfection; Kṛṣṇa asks Arjuna to hear how `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Abhirata_ — engaged, absorbed `30:00`.
- _Sva-karma_ — one's own work `30:00`.
- _Siddhi_ / _saṁsiddhi_ — perfection `30:00`.

#### Three kinds of varṇāśrama [32:00 – 36:00]

The teacher cites Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's scripturally based explanation:

- **_Vaidika_ (Vedic) _varṇāśrama_** — based on birth, but birth generally matched nature, since parents of a certain nature attract a similar soul as their child. Even so, scriptures show that one with a different nature should be engaged accordingly; the focus was qualities, not birth `32:00`.
  - Satyakāma Jābāli, asked by his teacher Gautama who his father was, honestly reported his mother's answer that she did not know; the teacher accepted him as a _brāhmaṇa_ because of his truthfulness `32:00 – 34:00`.
- **_Āsura_ _varṇāśrama_** — what has prevailed in India for a long time: bodily identification reduces _varṇa_ to birth; the higher castes take privilege and exploit the lower `34:00`.
  - This led to rebellion; the spread of Buddhism and Jainism culturally offered the lower castes an escape from domination; political propaganda and quota allocations also stem from this history.
  - This exploitation is not original Vedic culture but its degradation `36:00`.
- **_Daiva_ _varṇāśrama_** — everyone is meant to serve Kṛṣṇa according to individual nature `36:00`.
  - In Vedic _varṇāśrama_ progress is linear: _śūdra_ → _vaiśya_ → _kṣatriya_ → _brāhmaṇa_ → liberation over lifetimes.
  - In _daiva varṇāśrama_ progress is vertical: whatever one's _varṇa_, serious devotion to Kṛṣṇa can bring liberation directly.
  - Kṛṣṇa is speaking here of _daiva varṇāśrama_ — not the birth-based form, and certainly not the demoniac one.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's varṇāśrama is daiva — based on qualities and directed to Him — in which anyone, absorbed in their own nature-based work with devotion, can rise directly to perfection. [30:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.46 [36:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.46 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the process: by one's own work, worship that Supreme Being from whom all beings emanate and by whom all is pervaded, and thus attain perfection `36:00 – 40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pravṛtti_ — can mean living beings or all of existence that emanates from the Lord `38:00`.
- _Hṛṣīkeśa_ — master of the senses, used in Prabhupāda's purport `40:00`.

#### "Work is worship"? [38:00]

- This is the closest the Gītā comes to the popular idea that work is worship, but it never says work is automatically worship.
- It says: by your work, worship Him — translate work into worship through devotion.
- We spend time worshipping the Lord, develop spiritual consciousness through that service, and carry it into our daily work; that is how work becomes worship.

#### Third person, not yet bhakti-yoga [38:00]

- Kṛṣṇa still refers to "that Supreme Lord" in the third person, because He has not yet come directly to the level of _bhakti-yoga_.
- He has already said He is the source of everything, especially in 10.8.

#### Source and sustainer [40:00]

- Like a parent who is the source of the children and extends himself to provide for them, the Lord is source and sustainer; the child's duty is to cooperate.
- The system Kṛṣṇa describes is based on reuniting with our Supreme Father by spiritualising our work and offering it to Him.
- Prabhupāda's purport: everyone should think he is engaged in his occupation by Hṛṣīkeśa — it is Kṛṣṇa who engages us; "this is the role Kṛṣṇa has given me in His plan, and I do my part."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Work is not automatically worship; it becomes worship when, through devotion, we offer it to the Lord who is the source and sustainer of all, seeing our role as one He has given us in His plan. [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.47 [40:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.47 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa addresses "problematic prescribed duties": one's own duty, even if imperfectly performed, is better than another's duty performed perfectly; acting according to one's nature, one does not incur reaction `40:00 – 42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sva-dharma_ — one's own prescribed duty; _para-dharma_ — another's `42:00`.
- _Viguṇa_ — faulty, imperfectly performed `42:00`.
- _Kilbiṣa_ — karmic reaction, bondage `42:00`.

#### Arjuna's concern [42:00]

- Executing our responsibilities responsibly, we progress spiritually toward Kṛṣṇa.
- Arjuna thinks violence, especially against relatives, is undesirable. Tolerance and forgiveness are _brāhmaṇa_ qualities; for a _kṣatriya_, running from battle is against his nature — an indirect reference to Arjuna's situation. Even if one's duty appears faulty, one is purified by doing it.

#### Customised medicine [42:00 – 44:00]

- In a hospital, each patient gets medicine for their affliction; one cannot take another's medicine because it seems more palatable.
- The _kṣatriya_'s work may involve killing, but violence is sometimes needed to maintain law and order, and should be accepted.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Like customised medicine, one's own duty — even if it seems unpalatable or imperfect — is what purifies us; Arjuna cannot adopt the brāhmaṇa's forgiveness to escape a kṣatriya's duty. [42:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.48 [44:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.48 -->

**Context.** Work born of one's nature should not be given up even if it has faults, for all endeavours are covered by fault, as fire is by smoke `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Saha-jam_ — born with; one's natural work `44:00`.
- _Dhūma_ — smoke; _agni_ — fire `44:00`.

#### Fire and smoke [44:00 – 46:00]

- Fire is auspicious — used in sacrifice, cooking and warmth; smoke is undesirable — it obstructs, irritates the nose and is not the mouth of Agni.
- Wherever there is fire there is smoke; one handles the fire expertly so the smoke clears and the fire blazes, tolerating the smoke while it remains.
- Similarly, every duty has palatable and unpalatable sides; recognise the unpalatable and tolerate it.
- A _brāhmaṇa_ may perform sacrifices involving killing; done under scriptural direction, he is not bound and can still be elevated.

#### Toward jñāna-yoga [46:00]

- This whole section teaches acting according to _dharma_ to be elevated; from the next verse the discussion transitions to _jñāna-yoga_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every duty carries some fault, as fire carries smoke; we tolerate the smoke and tend the fire, doing our work under scriptural direction so that it elevates rather than binds. [44:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>
