## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 18.29 [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.29 -->

**Context.** A transition verse: Kṛṣṇa announces that He will now describe the differences of _buddhi_ and _dhṛti_ according to the modes, and asks Arjuna to hear `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bheda_ — differentiation, analysis, categorisation `00:00`.
- _Aśeṣa_ — can mean "with nothing left," exhaustively, and also "in detail" `02:00`.
- _Buddhi_ — translated as intelligence; distinct from knowledge `02:00`.
- _Dhṛti_ — determination `04:00`.

#### Why intelligence and determination are singled out [02:00 – 04:00]

- Kṛṣṇa first spoke of action in terms of knowledge, action and doer — what we perceive, how we decide to act, and the person acting.
- Apart from these, _buddhi_ and _dhṛti_ are also involved whenever we act.
- Happiness (_sukha_) will be marked separately in another transition verse, the 36th `02:00`.

#### Knowledge versus intelligence [02:00 – 04:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda explains (in the tenth-chapter purport and elsewhere) that knowledge is often theoretical, whereas intelligence means seeing things in their proper perspective.
- Comparing the two: knowledge is more about perception; intelligence is more about action — how I should act, what I should and should not do.
- Determination is the consistency and persistence to keep acting once I know how I should act.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Successful action needs both intelligence, which sees what should and should not be done, and determination, which persists in doing it — and both are coloured by the modes. [02:00 – 04:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.30 [04:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.30 -->

**Context.** Intelligence in the mode of goodness: knowing what is to be done and not done, what is to be feared and not feared, and what binds and what liberates `04:00 – 10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pravṛtti_ and _nivṛtti_ — more general inclinations, toward or away from action `04:00`.
- _Kārya_ and _akārya_ — more specific: actions I should do and should not do `04:00 – 06:00`. The 16th chapter (16.7) says the demoniac do not know what is to be done and not done `04:00`.
- _Bhaya_ and _abhaya_ — what should be feared and what should not be feared `06:00`.
- _Bandha_ and _mokṣa_ — the cause of bondage and the cause of liberation `06:00`, `10:00`.

#### Healthy fear and unnecessary fear [06:00 – 10:00]

- Healthy fear: a child must fear fire, a knife or a live electric wire, or it may wound itself.
- Unnecessary fear, to be overcome as part of growing up: fear of going to school, of going to college and living away from parents.
- Some fear practising spiritual life because of the unknown; one must use intelligence to see what is right, but eventually take a leap of faith.
- Being unduly fearful and being unduly reckless are both undesirable — either way one loses opportunities for growth.

#### "Fools rush in where angels fear to tread" [08:00 – 10:00]

- Saintly, wise people fear to go into sinful activities because they see how entangling and destructive these can be; foolish people rush in, thinking them enjoyable.
- Film scenes called "bold" are not boldness but blindness — blindness to the purpose and value of life, and so to consequences.
- Discrimination means asking: this may feel good, but what will its long-term consequence be? If counterproductive, I should not get involved.

#### How goodness in intelligence is cultivated [10:00]

- To a large extent _sāttvic_ intelligence comes by study of scripture — not merely theoretical, but transformational study in the association of devotees whose values have been transformed by it.
- Through such study consciousness rises from the lower modes to goodness and ultimately beyond goodness to transcendence.

#### The illumined doorway [10:00 – 12:00]

- Kṛṣṇa described goodness in 14.11 in terms of the "gates" of the body — the senses.
- A doorway marks a transition (a different floor, traffic, vehicles outside); at a point of transition illumination is especially needed, or one may fall down a step one did not see.
- The senses are the doorway between the inner and outer world; if not illumined, we make wrong choices, take self-destructive decisions and torment ourselves.

#### Knowledge in goodness versus intelligence in goodness [12:00 – 14:00]

- Knowledge in goodness (18.20) sees beyond external differences — male, female, Indian, Pakistani, American, European — to the undivided, imperishable spiritual nature: seeing beyond matter to spirit.
- Intelligence in goodness is about understanding what should and should not be done.
- Hence, as Kṛṣṇa analyses them, knowledge relates more to perception and intelligence more to action.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Intelligence in goodness is illumined discrimination: it knows what to do and avoid, what to fear and not fear, and what binds and what frees — and it grows through transformational study of scripture among devotees. [04:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.31 [14:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.31 -->

**Context.** Intelligence in the mode of passion cannot distinguish properly between _dharma_ and _adharma_, between what should and should not be done `14:00 – 16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dharma_ / _adharma_ — more general principles; _kārya_ / _akārya_ — what we should and should not do `16:00`.
- _Yathāvat_ — acting correctly, "as it is"; the teacher connects it with the Hindi title of Prabhupāda's _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_. _Ayathāvat_ — not properly `16:00`.

#### Vibhīṣaṇa is not a traitor [16:00 – 18:00]

- Many consider Vibhīṣaṇa a betrayer of his brother Rāvaṇa; actually Rāvaṇa was the betrayer — of truth, _dharma_, virtue, all living beings, and ultimately God.
- We need the right frame of reference: all of us are meant to be loyal to the Supreme Lord; only then can we return to Him.
- Loyalty to the Lord does not mean giving up worldly loyalties, but harmonising them under that priority — as Kṛṣṇa says, give up all varieties of religion and surrender unto Me `18:00`.
- Followers of Rāvaṇa who call him good and Vibhīṣaṇa bad show a perversity of scriptural values `18:00`.

#### Lower principles made higher [18:00 – 20:00]

- Contaminated knowledge makes the short term (pleasure in this world or in heaven) seem more important than the long term (liberation).
- Considering _karma-kāṇḍa_ more important than _bhakti_ is such a mistake, arising from not recognising the ultimate reality: we are meant to return to Kṛṣṇa, the only way to real happiness, and all actions should be chosen by that priority.
- With incomplete knowledge, a lower principle is made higher and a higher one neglected.

#### Emphasis on externals [20:00]

- As in 18.21, passion sees different bodies and thinks they represent different inner natures.
- Too much emphasis on externals hides the internals: the soul, its awareness of spiritual reality, and its relationship with Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Intelligence in passion has knowledge, but it is contaminated: without the frame of loyalty to the Supreme Lord it misjudges dharma, raises short-term goals above the ultimate one, and fixes on externals. [14:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.32 [22:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.32 -->

**Context.** Intelligence covered by ignorance goes in the direction opposite to reality: it takes _adharma_ to be _dharma_ and _dharma_ to be _adharma_ `22:00`.

#### God as ordinary, the ordinary as God [22:00 – 24:00]

- A striking example: people consider God an ordinary person and an ordinary person God.
- When Kṛṣṇa descends, people reduce Him to an extraordinary man — someone with magical powers, or a skilled diplomat.
- Conversely they raise their own glory, or that of a "pet guru" whom they make into God.
- Prabhupāda called this a double insanity: one person claiming to be God, and the greater insanity of others accepting it `24:00`.

#### Modes act on different aspects of a person [24:00 – 28:00]

- Intelligence in ignorance does not always mean a fully _tāmasic_ lifestyle.
- People who largely follow regulative principles (no meat-eating, intoxication, gambling, illicit sex) and some religion are relatively in goodness, though not transcendental; their efforts at their level are to be appreciated.
- A completely unregulated person may be in ignorance overall, yet show traits we associate with goodness — e.g. a chain-smoking champion researcher.
- The influence of the modes is complex: one mode may influence one aspect of a person and not another.
- Addictions such as drinking are dangerous and spread ignorance to others; the remedy is serious spiritual practice to rise to goodness and ultimately transcend.
- We need not whitewash anyone: being a devotee does not automatically free one from the modes; we become free through study and practice `28:00`.

#### Three levels of ignorance [28:00 – 30:00]

- Being in ignorance is bad — e.g. not knowing that God exists.
- Being in ignorance and claiming to be in knowledge is worse — e.g. claiming, without proof, that consciousness emerges from matter and calling it the scientific, respectable view.
- Being in ignorance, claiming it as knowledge, and calling knowledge ignorance is worst — e.g. "religion is the opium of the masses," "God is a crutch for the weak-minded."

#### Vena and the king as a route to God [30:00 – 32:00]

- In the Fourth Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_, Vena was like a terrorist ruler who plundered his kingdom, stopped worship of the Lord and ordered the _brāhmaṇas_ to worship him instead.
- The concept of _nara-deva_ — the king as God in human form — means the king is a representative who reminds us of God and guides us to worship Him.
- The king is meant to be a route (r-o-u-t-e) to God, not a replacement; likewise a guru cannot say "I am God, worship me instead."
- When Vena chastised the _brāhmaṇas_, they cursed him and he was reduced to ashes.

#### The impostor in the Mahābhārata [32:00 – 34:00]

- After the king's coronation following the war, a _rākṣasa_ named Cārvāka, disguised as a _brāhmaṇa_, claimed to speak for all the _brāhmaṇas_ and blamed the king's greed for the bloodshed, widows and orphans, telling him to go to the forest.
- The king, shocked, humbly agreed; the real _brāhmaṇas_ protested that they had not chosen him, and by the power of their glance and sound the impostor fell dead.

#### Correction as welfare [34:00]

- The _brāhmaṇas_ gave the king freedom to rule responsibly, but when he diverted worship to himself, it had to be corrected — sometimes by shock treatment.
- When they curse they are not cruel; they desire the ultimate welfare of that person.
- With proper understanding one can rise even from ignorance to transcendence; without it one goes downward to destruction.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Intelligence in ignorance inverts reality — making God ordinary and the ordinary into God; the remedy is not to whitewash ourselves but to free ourselves from the modes through study and practice. [22:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.33 [36:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.33 -->

**Context.** Determination in goodness: that by which one sustains the activities of the mind, life air and senses through unbreakable _yoga_ `36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dhṛti_ — that by which one accepts and perseveres in one's acceptance `36:00`.
- _Avyabhicāriṇī_ — unbreakable, uninterrupted; not unfaithful, i.e. faithful devotion `36:00 – 38:00`.
- _Yoga_ — connecting ourselves to spiritual reality in a way that is not easily shaken `38:00`.

#### The same word as in the fourteenth chapter [36:00 – 38:00]

- Kṛṣṇa used this word earlier when explaining how to transcend the modes: practising unbreakable devotional service, one transcends them (14.26) and comes to the _brahman_ platform.
- _Bhakti_ is not only a means to transcendence; even in transcendence one continues practising it, as indicated in 18.54.
- Here _avyabhicāriṇī_ need not always mean _parā_ (pure, spiritual) devotion; faithful, steady practice itself elevates one.

#### Steadiness over years [38:00]

- Chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa one or two rounds for a day or two is not difficult; chanting 16 rounds every day, year after year, for decades is not easy.
- The determination that accepts that challenge is determination in goodness.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Determination in goodness is faithful, unbroken steadiness in connecting with spiritual reality — like chanting sixteen rounds daily for decades — and such steady practice itself elevates and ultimately transcends the modes. [36:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.34 [40:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.34 -->

**Context.** Determination in passion holds fast to _dharma_, _artha_ and _kāma_ with strong attachment to results `40:00 – 42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Phalākāṅkṣī_ — very obsessed with getting the result `42:00`.

#### Religion for prosperity [40:00 – 42:00]

- Of the four _puruṣārthas_, most people with some dharmic inclination pursue not liberation (_mokṣa_) but material prosperity, while not wanting to become _adharmic_; in modern times many pursue only _artha_ and _kāma_.
- Persevering in one's practice for the sake of _dharma_, _artha_ and _kāma_ is determination in passion.
- Passion is not necessarily sinful: done within the framework of _dharma_, such activities are not considered sinful.
- Some perform rituals or vows thinking they will get a sense gratification not obtained before; this is not a very elevated form of religion `42:00`.
- Contrast: determination in goodness leads to _yoga_, connection with transcendental reality.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Determination in passion may be religious and not sinful, yet it perseveres only for prosperity and enjoyment, obsessed with results rather than connection with the Lord. [40:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 18.35 [42:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:18.35 -->

**Context.** Determination in ignorance cannot give up dreaming, fear, lamentation, moroseness and illusion — five negative characteristics `42:00`, `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Svapna_ — dreaming; here excessive sleep and a dreamy mentality disconnected from reality `42:00 – 44:00`.
- _Bhaya_ — fear `44:00`.
- _Śoka_ — lamentation `48:00 – 50:00`.
- _Viṣāda_ — moroseness `50:00`.
- _Mada_ — intoxication or illusion `52:00`.

#### Dreaming as disconnection from reality [42:00 – 44:00]

- Laziness usually leads to sleeping more than necessary, which deepens ignorance; sleeping more often brings more dreams.
- The word's emphasis is a mentality: as a dreamer is disconnected from reality, so is the excessively lazy, sleepy person.

#### Healthy and baseless fear [44:00 – 48:00]

- When fear is meant positively, the context shows it — e.g. by fear of the Lord the wind moves. That is not terror but harmonisation born of respect: Indra, Candra, Vāyu, Agni and Sūrya understand the Lord's cosmic plan and play their part.
- Having been given a part shows a basic trust, which we can honour or betray.
- Baseless fear: "False Evidence Appearing Real." Most present anxieties do not matter after a while, yet stress, anxiety and hypertension arise from worrying about what is not worth worrying about.
- Devotees should be warriors, not worriers: with Kṛṣṇa's help, deal with problems instead of thinking aimlessly.

#### Why fear needs "determination" [48:00]

- Fear is distressing, even devastating, yet we hold on to it inordinately.
- Without understanding who I am and what to pursue, small things become too important and seem like great dangers.

#### Grieving versus lamenting [48:00 – 50:00]

- Kṛṣṇa says "do not lament" in 18.66 and elsewhere; lamentation is called a _śūdra_ mentality and considered _tāmasic_.
- Losing a loved one is painful, and where there is love there is grief; but to keep living in pain is more painful than necessary.
- Grieving is not bad; lamenting means never coming out of it, never learning or growing from it — even when everyone urges one to move on. When life gives a raw deal, accept it and move forward.

#### Perverse determination [52:00 – 56:00]

- Holding on to things not worth holding, imagining them a treasure, is determination in ignorance.
- Some sleep 12 or 15 hours and resist all urging; persisting in laziness despite disincentives and others' derision is a perverse determination.
- Addiction: nobody starts out wanting to be an addict; a little enjoyment grows until one feels "possessed and pursued" — internally a craving, externally temptations everywhere — minds inflamed by unfulfilled desires.
- The teacher calls it obstinacy, stubbornness, insolence: told to do right, one still does wrong `56:00`.
- "Things" here include mindsets — too dreamy, too gloomy — not only external actions or possessions `56:00`.

#### The way out [56:00]

- It is very difficult to overcome such intelligence except by cultivating pure devotional service.
- If we find these traits in ourselves, we can introspect ("I am acting impulsively"), but more importantly we focus on pleasing Kṛṣṇa, who assures in 10.10 that He will give the intelligence by which we can come to Him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Determination in ignorance is stubborn attachment to dreaminess, baseless fear, endless lamentation, gloom and illusion; the way out is less self-analysis than sincerely pleasing Kṛṣṇa, who gives the intelligence to return to Him. [42:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
