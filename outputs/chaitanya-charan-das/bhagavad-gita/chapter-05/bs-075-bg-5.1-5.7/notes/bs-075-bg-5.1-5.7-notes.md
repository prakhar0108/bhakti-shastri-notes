## Continuity & Recap from Preceding Class

- The fourth chapter, on transcendental knowledge, is sandwiched between two chapters Prabhupāda titles _karma-yoga_ (chapter 3) and "_Karma-yoga_ — Action in Kṛṣṇa Consciousness" (chapter 5) `00:00`.
  - Conventionally the fourth is called _jñāna-sannyāsa-yoga_ and the fifth _karma-sannyāsa-yoga_ — but this means renunciation _in_ karma, not renunciation _of_ karma.
  - The knowledge of how to do that was given in chapter 4; how to practise it is given in chapter 5.
- Chapter 4 ended with "rise and fight"; but in 4.39 Kṛṣṇa spoke of peace and in 4.41 of renouncing _karma_, and fighting was never mentioned in the whole chapter — so Arjuna doubts whether Kṛṣṇa really wants him to fight `00:00 – 02:00`.
- Arjuna's question here is similar to that in chapter 3; similar chapters (3 and 5, 7 and 9) revisit themes at a deeper level `02:00`.

#### Overview of the fifth chapter [02:00 – 04:00]

- **5.1 – 5.6** — _karma-yoga_ is equal to, but easier than, _jñāna-yoga_ (renunciation).
- **5.7 – 5.16** — by fixing the mind on the _ātmā_ (not on sense objects), the _karma-yogī_ remains free from further bondage.
- **5.17 – 5.29** — by fixing the mind on the Supersoul (_Paramātmā_), the _karma-yogī_ attains liberation.
  - 5.27 – 5.28 recommend _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ (elaborated in chapter 6), not _jñāna-yoga_; 5.29 speaks of _bhakti-yoga_ (elaborated in chapters 7 – 12).
- The recovery analogy: recovering from sickness has two parts — stopping the infection from spreading, and regaining strength. Knowledge of the soul stops further bondage; knowledge of the Supersoul brings liberation `04:00`.

### Bhagavad-gita 5.1 [04:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.1 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says: "You recommend renunciation of work, and then again work with devotion; please tell me definitely which of the two is more beneficial" `04:00 – 06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śreyaḥ_ — that which is beneficial in the long term; Arjuna has persistently asked Kṛṣṇa for this `06:00`.
- _Brūhi_ — "please tell me" `06:00`.
- Definitely — now Arjuna asks to be told with absolute, conclusive definiteness `06:00`.

#### The source of the doubt [06:00]

- Arjuna refers to Kṛṣṇa's recommendation of renouncing _karma_ (4.41) and the command to be situated in _yoga_ and rise and fight (4.42), and asks which of the two is better.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's doubt is a natural one — peace and renunciation of karma on one side, "rise and fight" on the other — and he now demands a conclusive answer about what is truly beneficial. [00:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.2 [06:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.2 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa replies that both renunciation of work and work in devotion lead to the highest long-term benefit, but of the two, _karma-yoga_ is better `06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Niḥśreyasa_ — long-term benefit; both paths lead to it `06:00`.
- Attachment — the cause of bondage; to the extent there is no attachment, there is no bondage. Both paths prevent attachment, but _karma-yoga_ is easier `06:00 – 08:00`.
- Sense gratification vs. fruitive activity — directly enjoying through the senses (eating, touching) vs. activity that leads later to sense gratification, such as ritual _karma-kāṇḍa_ `12:00`.
- _Deha-yoga_ — the "yoga," i.e. contact or bondage, of the soul with the body `14:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's focus: staying free from bondage [08:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport focuses less on the comparative merit of the two paths and more on the point that staying free from karmic bondage is fundamentally important.
- He quotes King Ṛṣabhadeva's teachings from the fifth canto of the _Bhāgavatam_ (5.5.4 – 6), which explain how the conditioned soul becomes bound.

#### Ṛṣabhadeva's diagnosis [08:00 – 14:00]

- One who acts for sense gratification is like one intoxicated or mad; such action is not good because the soul gets another body `08:00`.
- The body is temporary and miserable: it gives insignificant pleasure (a few moments) and significant pain (hours, months, years) `10:00`.
- As long as one is ignorant — without inquiry into the soul — one's activity will fail; without spiritual activity, by default we do material activity, and the mind remains absorbed in fruitive work `10:00 – 12:00`.
- **Car and driver** — distinct; the driver can leave the car. Yet for all practical purposes the soul has become identified with the body `12:00`.
- **Welding** — the enjoying mentality welds and melds the soul to the body, as heated metals are fused; in identity the soul is always separate, but experientially it is hard to feel `12:00`.
- One remains under the control of fruitive mentality, in ignorance of spiritual reality, until one develops love for Lord Vāsudeva; then the enjoying mentality goes, and with it the bondage of soul to body `14:00`.

#### Knowledge alone is not enough [14:00 – 16:00]

- Prabhupāda's significant statement: knowledge that one is not the body but spirit soul is not sufficient for liberation; one has to act in the status of spirit soul — that is, devotional service.
- Renouncing what can be used in Kṛṣṇa's service is false renunciation; real renunciation is giving up the enjoying mentality and using things in Kṛṣṇa's service.

#### The only thing to renounce [16:00 – 18:00]

- Nothing belongs to us — even our body is Kṛṣṇa's; so the only thing to renounce is the illusion that we have anything to renounce — the illusion of "mine."
- Free of that, we understand: "I am Kṛṣṇa's, and Kṛṣṇa is mine — what else do I need?"
- We need not renounce action or possession, but the misconception that we are the enjoyers; we are cooperators — when Kṛṣṇa enjoys, we enjoy as subordinate enjoyers `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Both paths can free us, but the real issue is bondage, caused by the enjoying mentality; we need not renounce action or possessions — only the illusion that anything is ours — and act as spirit souls in Kṛṣṇa's service. [08:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.3 [18:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains why _karma-yoga_ is better: one who neither hates nor desires the fruits of activity is known to be always renounced; free from duality, he easily overcomes material bondage `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Constant renunciate — one who renounces in action, not of action `18:00`.
- Hanker — "I want that, I want that"; resent — "Why did I get this? I don't want this" `18:00`.
- Free from duality — not caught between hankering and resenting `18:00`.
- Happily / easily — freedom from bondage comes easily because activity is natural `18:00`.

#### Why renunciation in action is easier [18:00 – 20:00]

- At this stage Kṛṣṇa speaks of detachment through knowledge of the soul; later, through _bhakti_, attachment and aversion go away when we are attached to Kṛṣṇa and want to please Him `18:00`.
- Activity is natural and inactivity unnatural, so it is easier to act and stay free from bondage than to be inactive and seek freedom `18:00 – 20:00`.
- Externally, inactivity seems easy — just sit; internally it is very difficult, because passion in the body pushes us to go here, see this, do that, round and round `20:00`.
- Externally, activity seems difficult; internally it is easy — according to our conditioned nature and also our eternal nature as souls active in service to Kṛṣṇa `20:00`.

#### The devotee is liberated even here [20:00]

- Prabhupāda: the devotee is always liberated, because he acts for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure, and so has no duality in mind.
- A devotee does not think "this is good, this is bad" in material terms, but "how can whatever comes be used in Kṛṣṇa's service?"
- The form of service may change, but the identity "I am Kṛṣṇa's servant" can be sustained in all situations — so the devotee is liberated from circumstances.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Renunciation lives in the heart, not in inactivity: one who neither hankers nor resents is a constant renunciate while acting — and since activity is our nature, this path is far easier than forced inaction. [18:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.4 – 5.5 [22:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.4 -->

<!-- vedabase:5.5 -->

**Context.** Only the childish and less intelligent say that analytical renunciation (_sāṅkhya_) and _karma-yoga_ are different; one properly situated in either gets the fruit of both. The destination reached by one is reached by the other too, and one who sees them as one truly sees. The teacher explains both verses together `22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sāṅkhya_ — here the process of _jñāna_, of introspection and inaction; i.e. renunciation of work (as in 5.2) `22:00`.
- _Yoga_ — the process involving action, i.e. _karma-yoga_ `22:00`.
- Childish / ignorant — those who think the two are separate; the wise do not `22:00`.
- "One who sees thus truly sees" — a phrase that recurs in the Gītā; the _Bhāgavatam_ also has a similar concluding verse `28:00`.

#### Diversity in paths, uniformity in goal [24:00]

- These verses in no way support the idea that all paths lead to the same goal — that is not a Gītā teaching, nor even a logical one, as discussed in 4.11.
- Kṛṣṇa is speaking of two specific paths — introspective inaction (_jñāna-yoga_) and action (_karma-yoga_) — whose goal is freedom from bondage.
- Both free one only when done with detachment; detachment while acting is easier than detachment while giving up all activity, which is unnatural.

#### Taking the discussion deeper than chapter 3 [26:00 – 28:00]

- Conventionally there is a hierarchy: _karma-yoga_, then _jñāna-yoga_, then _bhakti-yoga_.
- In chapter 3 Kṛṣṇa let the notion stand that _jñāna-yoga_ is higher: "You are qualified for _karma-yoga_; even if you think you are qualified for _jñāna-yoga_, practise _karma-yoga_ to set an example."
- Here He goes deeper: the destination attained by _jñāna-yoga_ can also be attained by _karma-yoga_ — both aim at liberation.

#### Seeing with knowledge [28:00 – 32:00]

- **Chest X-ray** — to a layperson it is a black film with an image; a doctor sees advanced tuberculosis with lesions throughout the lungs. The same thing, seen differently `28:00`.
- What we can know depends on what we know: telling an illiterate villager that damage to the medulla oblongata can be fatal means nothing until it is connected to something known, like the brain `28:00 – 30:00`.
- Quoting stand-alone verses shows some piety, but understanding the Gītā's flow and integrity requires systematic study `30:00`.
- Externally the silent renunciate seems far more advanced than the person engaged in the world; when we see the common destination beyond the diversity of process, we truly see `30:00 – 32:00`.

#### The ultimate goal: Lord Viṣṇu [32:00]

- Prabhupāda: the real purpose of philosophical research is to find the ultimate goal of life — which, as the seventh canto explains, is to know, love and serve Lord Viṣṇu.
- The _Bhāgavatam_ establishes Kṛṣṇa as the original Supreme Personality of Godhead, but then uses the names Kṛṣṇa and Viṣṇu as identical and interchangeable — e.g. at the end of the _rāsa_ pastimes, where only Kṛṣṇa appears, it speaks of hearing the pastimes of Lord Viṣṇu — without hair-splitting analysis.

#### Watering the root [34:00 – 36:00]

- Watering the leaves makes a tree look watered but does not nourish it.
- One person digs carefully around the roots to find where they go — and then must still water the root. Another, in faith from authority, simply waters the root from the start. Both nourish the tree, but the second is easier.
- Application: the body is the tree; the soul is the root; the Supersoul is the root of all roots — Kṛṣṇa calls Himself the imperishable seed of all existence in chapter 7. After the analytical process of _sāṅkhya_ one must still act at the level of the soul — devotional service.

#### Karma-yoga vs. bhakti-yoga [36:00]

- The _karma-yogī_ may not know what the Absolute Truth is: "I am the soul, not the body; let me act in the mood of sacrifice and not pander to the body."
- The _bhakti-yogī_ knows: "I am part of Kṛṣṇa, the Absolute Truth; I work in the mood of divine sacrifice, for the Lord's pleasure."
- Prabhupāda concludes: superficially one process involves detachment and the other attachment, but detachment from matter and attachment to Kṛṣṇa are one and the same; one who sees this sees things as they are.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Analytical renunciation and karma-yoga share one goal — freedom from bondage — but acting with detachment is easier, just as watering the root directly is easier than first digging to find it; the one who sees this unity beyond outward appearances truly sees. [22:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.6 [36:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.6 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes this part: renunciation without _yoga_ brings distress, while the sage engaged in _yoga_ attains the Absolute without delay `36:00 – 40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mahā-bāho_ — "mighty-armed," used again in this chapter `38:00`.
- Distress — renouncing action without _yoga_ is troublesome, unsustainable, unnatural `38:00`.
- Not after a long time — the yogī attains it soon `38:00`.
- _Brahman_ — a generic word for attaining the Absolute Truth `40:00`.

#### Neither here nor there [38:00 – 40:00]

- Without _yoga_ — without connection to the Absolute Truth — one is detached from matter but gets no satisfaction, and cannot act, since any material activity causes bondage; detachment from matter then means inactivity.
- It is difficult to stay inactive and without pleasure; not experiencing higher happiness, one is coming out of matter but not entering spirit — and so is lost.
- Therefore be engaged in _yoga_, and one soon attains liberation.

#### Prabhupāda on the Māyāvāda sannyāsīs [40:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport contrasts Māyāvāda and Vaiṣṇava _sannyāsīs_: the former, engaged in speculation, cannot realise the Lord's transcendental service; their studies become tedious and, tired of dry speculation, they sometimes take shelter of the _Bhāgavatam_ without proper understanding.

#### Bhāgavatam and the impersonalist tradition [40:00 – 44:00]

- As the teacher recounts, Śaṅkarācārya did not comment on the _Bhāgavatam_ — a profound philosophy concluding in personal theistic devotion; Rāmānujācārya, primarily responding to Śaṅkara, also did not, though he quoted similar concepts; Madhvācārya began commenting on it `40:00`.
- The teacher gives Śaṅkara as 7th century, Rāmānuja 10th – 11th, Madhva 11th – 12th, followed by a _bhakti_ renaissance in medieval Indian history `40:00 – 42:00`.
- Against _bhakti_, _māyāvāda_ began to seem stale: "I am Brahman" may relate to the ego but does not satisfy the heart; and since one must renounce the world even to begin, the path is very demanding and does not reach the ultimate destination. Impersonalists could neither sustain themselves nor hold people's attention `42:00`.
- On the word "cult" — in Prabhupāda's time it simply meant a group; today it connotes isolated, brainwashed people, so the teacher avoids it, though Prabhupāda used it `42:00`.
- Impersonalists too began practising _bhakti_ and commenting on the _Bhāgavatam_ — a prominent scholar in their line wrote extensive works, with dozens of pages on the first verse alone — but they cannot enter its mystery: they interpret Kṛṣṇa's pastimes with the _gopīs_ as symbolic, feel embarrassed by them, and study the _Bhāgavatam_ to transcend Bhagavān and attain Brahman, which is quite offensive `44:00`.

#### Affiliation vs. inclination [44:00 – 48:00]

- Some with genuine _bhakti_ join impersonalist organisations because they are prominent and respectable; they may be _māyāvādīs_ by affiliation but not by inclination `44:00 – 46:00`.
- Śrīdhara Svāmī was like this — the seminal _Bhāgavatam_ commentator whose commentary is available, glorified by Caitanya Mahāprabhu; externally of the impersonalist _sampradāya_, but an exalted devotee. Because of that affiliation some statements seem to support _māyāvāda_ `46:00`.
- When a scholar tried to counter and correct Śrīdhara Svāmī's commentary as inconsistent, Mahāprabhu, understanding Śrīdhara's heart, did not approve and chastised him quite strongly `46:00 – 48:00`.
- Jīva Gosvāmī in his _Sandarbhas_ uses Śrīdhara Svāmī as the standard reference, quoting his "_ṭīkā_" without naming him where it supports personalism; where Śrīdhara gives impersonal explanations, Jīva does not mention him, but says "according to an imaginary interpretation some say this — the actual understanding is this," with logical and scriptural basis `48:00`.
- So we should not assume that those who are _māyāvādīs_ by affiliation are so by inclination; given proper understanding, they may turn to _bhakti-yoga_ `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Renouncing matter without connecting to the Absolute leaves one neither here nor there; engaged in yoga, one attains the goal soon — and we should distinguish people's affiliation from their heart's inclination. [38:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.7 [48:00 – 57:39]

<!-- vedabase:5.7 -->

**Context.** One who works in devotion, who is a pure soul, who controls mind and senses, and who is dear to everyone and everyone dear to him — though always working, is never entangled `48:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Purified soul — one who is pure `50:00`.
- Self-controlled — the second _ātmā_ refers to the mind; one who controls the mind `50:00`.
- One who has conquered the senses `50:00`.
- Compassionate to all — as Prabhupāda translates, dear to everyone and everyone dear to him; others' _ātmā_ becomes like one's own `54:00`.
- Not bound — even while acting `50:00`.

#### The story of Priyavrata [50:00 – 54:00]

- In the fifth canto of the _Bhāgavatam_: the line of rulers descending from Svāyambhuva Manu (described in the fourth canto with Dhruva Mahārāja's pastimes) comes to an end, and Priyavrata is asked to become king `50:00`.
- He had gone to the forest as a _brahmacārī_, under Nārada Muni's guidance, planning to take _sannyāsa_ and be liberated; he was extremely reluctant: "I have saved myself from entanglement; I don't want to become entangled" `50:00`.
- Svāyambhuva Manu, seeing the need for a competent administrator, urged him; Nārada urged non-entanglement. Both are _mahājanas_ — what to do? `52:00`
- Brahmā himself came and told Priyavrata: don't resent the order the Lord has given you; doing what He wants is the meaning of being a servant `52:00`.
- Brahmā's teaching: one who is attached, even in the forest, lives with "six wives" — the five senses and the mind — and remains a bound hypocrite contemplating sense objects; one who is self-controlled and detached is not bound even at home with wives. Since the service is required, accept it `52:00`.
- Priyavrata accepted it as the Lord's will. Brahmā, the spiritual superior of both Manu and Nārada, gave the decision and both accepted: Manu did not gloat, and Nārada accepted his superior's will `52:00 – 54:00`.
- Point: one who is purified and has controlled mind and senses stays liberated even at home, in family, in a palace, even as a king `54:00`.

#### Dear to all [54:00 – 56:00]

- The deep emotional connection with others almost feels like oneness — but this does not mean all become one impersonally; more in 6.31 – 6.32 `54:00`.
- Such a person has no selfish motive, wants nothing from anyone, and simply wants to serve `56:00`.
- A test of relationships `56:00`:
  - Selfish — "What can this person do for me?"
  - Selfless — "What can I do for this person?"
  - Material — "What can this person give me for sense enjoyment?"
  - Spiritual — "How can this person bless me with advancement in Kṛṣṇa consciousness?" — by respectful, attentive, submissive hearing, questions and association.
- A devotee is a well-wisher of all living beings — selfless and concerned for others' welfare — and though acting, is not bound `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Purity and control of mind and senses — not the forest — decide bondage: like Priyavrata, a self-controlled devotee can rule a kingdom unbound, and his selfless care for others makes him dear to all. [50:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
