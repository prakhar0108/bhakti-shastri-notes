## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 5.15 [02:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.15 -->

**Context.** A doubt arises from 5.14: if nature makes me act — say I am born a _śūdra_ and act like one — is the Supreme Lord responsible? Kṛṣṇa answers that the Lord does not accept anyone's sinful or pious activities; living entities are bewildered because their knowledge is covered by ignorance `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- The covering word in the verse — used by Kṛṣṇa earlier for lust covering knowledge, in response to Arjuna's question in 3.36 (discussed around 3.39) `02:00`.
- _Ajñāna_ — ignorance; it can be analysed in different ways: as lust, as arrogance, as the various _anarthas_, or as the three modes `06:00`.
- _Anartha_ — that which has no _artha_ — no value or meaning `06:00`.
- Conditioned soul — the soul covered by the body and the mind `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Īśa_ / _anīśa_ — controller / non-controller; the living entity is not in control `16:00`.

#### The impartial judge [04:00]

- When one surrenders to Kṛṣṇa, the Lord takes away even pious and sinful reactions — but this chapter has not yet come to _bhakti_.
- Here Kṛṣṇa is contrasting the consciousness of a _karma-yogī_ with that of a _karmī_; in neither is the focus Kṛṣṇa, so the Lord is not directly involved.
- Like a judge, who does not accept the criminal's punishment or the victim's compensation, but acts on the merits of the case.

#### Self-defeating and self-destructive action [06:00]

- Deluded by the covering, the soul acts in ways that are:
  - Self-defeating — trying to advance spiritually but making wrong choices.
  - Self-destructive — acting so as to lose the human body and go to lower species; such a person is called the killer of the soul.
- At root, the soul forgets "I am a soul, part of Kṛṣṇa, meant to serve Him" and thinks "I am meant to enjoy" — male or female, Indian or American, fair or dark: all misconceptions based on the body.

#### Dovetail some urges, curtail others [08:00 – 12:00]

- Three parties are involved: the soul, the Supreme Lord and material nature. Nature pushes us, but we are not helpless.
- Within our natural urges:
  - Propensities of one's nature — to be dovetailed in Kṛṣṇa's service.
  - Lust, anger, greed, pride and illusion — to be curtailed.
- At the pure, liberated level, one rejects identification with all _varṇas_ and _āśramas_ — "I am simply a servant of the servant of the Lord of the _gopīs_." At the _sādhaka_ level we accept that in principle but act according to our bodily nature `08:00 – 10:00`.
- Example of a _brāhmaṇa_: good memory and eloquence can make him infatuated and elated; lacking them can make him dejected — "I'm good for nothing." Both are illusion. Whatever faculties we have, we use them in Kṛṣṇa's service `10:00`.
- Everyone in the _varṇāśrama_ system must regulate lust, though the extent of its expression varies — a _kṣatriya_ may have many wives, palaces and wealth within his duties `10:00 – 12:00`.
- Bondage comes when we identify with our bodily ways of behaving as our essence; when we remember "I am a soul meant to love and serve Kṛṣṇa," there is no bondage `12:00`.

#### The "circumstantial" body [12:00 – 14:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that the embodied soul identifies with the circumstantial material body and becomes subject to temporary misery and happiness.
- From eternity's viewpoint, the body itself is circumstantial: desires arose in certain circumstances, we became attached, developed a thought process at death, and accordingly got this body.
- We are not victims — we cooperated and chose desires, so we are partially responsible — but nothing intrinsic connects us with the body.

#### The Lord facilitates, we desire [14:00 – 16:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes a verse saying the Lord makes the living entity do good or go to lower places. Does the Lord engage us, or do we? Both: nothing happens without His sanction, but it is we who desire, and He facilitates accordingly.
- Sometimes Prabhupāda quoted verses whose references devotees could not find; his editors left such verses without references `16:00`.

#### The helpless soldier [16:00 – 20:00]

- We are like a soldier on a battlefield whose weapons have fallen, right in the line of fire — any moment a "karma bullet" may strike in the form of calamities.
- We may say "I have determination, I have free will" — and we should deal with problems according to capacity — but capacity is limited. One may travel the globe for a cure for a terminal disease and still have to accept the outcome.
- Without understanding "I am not the body," that is crushing — like a candle blown out by one blow of destiny. Understanding that we are greater than our body and our life greater than this lifetime enriches our conception of existence `18:00`.
- When there is no hope in material life, we need spiritual life, or we have no hope at all.
- Prabhupāda's phrase: the embodied soul, by his "immemorial desire" to avoid Kṛṣṇa consciousness, causes his own bewilderment — immemorial meaning we cannot know when or how it started `18:00`.
- Balancing this, he quotes the _Vedānta-sūtra_: the Lord takes no side, neither hating nor liking anyone. He may appear to make someone wealthy or poor, but it is according to their own karma `20:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord is an impartial judge who sanctions according to our own desires and karma; our bewilderment comes from our own long-standing desire to avoid Kṛṣṇa, which covers knowledge and makes us identify with a merely circumstantial body. [04:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.16 [20:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.16 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa tells how one becomes free from this ignorance: when knowledge arises, it illuminates everything like the sun `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ātma-jñāna_ — knowledge of oneself as the soul; this verse marks the transition from it to knowledge of the Supreme `22:00`.
- The Supreme (_param_) — Kṛṣṇa does not yet say what it is; He will reveal at the end of the chapter, in 5.29, that it is Himself `24:00`.

#### Torch and sun [22:00 – 24:00]

- As the _karma-yogī_ keeps practising with detachment, knowledge awakens: one understands not only "I am not the body, I am the soul," but also that the Supreme Lord is present in the heart, is one's greatest well-wisher, and should be the object of devotion and sacrifice and the goal of life.
- In _sādhana-bhakti_, scriptural knowledge and intelligence are like a torch in darkness — we see a few steps and keep moving. As we advance, the sun of spiritual wisdom rises and we see in all directions, far ahead.
- Our inner world becomes comprehensible: "if I do this my conditionings increase; if I do that I advance."
- Once the Absolute Truth is revealed, it becomes the focus of practice — the focus shifts from one's own identity to one's connection with the Absolute.

#### Meeting the representative of God [24:00 – 28:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that one can know God and one's relationship with Him only by meeting the representative of God.
- The soul is a spark of consciousness whose knowledge is covered; contact with a soul who has knowledge kindles it, as one candle is lit by another lit candle or a match.
- Socrates understood "I am not the body" — hence his fearlessness, consoling a friend with reasons for the soul's immortality. One can understand the _ātmā_, but understanding the _paramātmā_ is nearly impossible without connection to a spiritual master.
- Outer illumination activates our inner flame; as it blazes, it eventually becomes like a sun, and we see the Supreme Lord in our own heart `28:00`.

#### Oneness is a sign of darkness [28:00]

- Some say seeing oneness is a sign of illumination. Prabhupāda says oneness is a sign of darkness: at night everything looks one; in daylight everything is seen in its real identity.
- Entering a dark room one sees only darkness — "oneness." When the light comes on: a chair, a table, a friend, a picture of Kṛṣṇa.
- Stages the teacher describes:
  - Material darkness — seeing variety, all for my enjoyment, with no connection to Kṛṣṇa.
  - Impersonal — "nothing is for my enjoyment; everything is illusion"; all is one.
  - Spiritual illumination — variety in existence and unity in purpose: everything is meant for Kṛṣṇa's service.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Practice with detachment awakens knowledge that reveals not only the self but the Supreme in the heart; that light is kindled by a representative of God and shows real variety united in Kṛṣṇa's service, not a dark oneness. [22:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.17 [30:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.17 -->

**Context.** Having understood the Absolute Truth, one makes it the focus of meditation: fixing four inner faculties on it, one is cleansed of all contamination and goes on the path of no return `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- The four faculties `30:00 – 40:00`:
  - _Buddhi_ — intelligence.
  - _Ātmā_ — here, the mind, i.e. the emotions.
  - _Niṣṭhā_ — faith.
  - _Parāyaṇa_ — making the Absolute the goal.
- Why _ātmā_ means mind here: it can mean body, mind, soul or intelligence. The soul cannot be one of its own faculties; the body is unlikely since only inner faculties are listed; intelligence is already named — so it is the mind. It will mean the mind in 6.5 – 6.6 too `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Sādhya_ and _sādhana_ — that which is to be achieved, and the means; e.g. going to Māyāpur (_sādhya_) by car, train or plane (_sādhana_) `46:00`.
- Identity — here meaning sameness, as in identical twins, not "who are you" `50:00`.

#### Four lanes of consciousness [30:00 – 32:00]

- Our consciousness moves in four lanes; if they head in four directions we cannot move steadily. In 2.41 Kṛṣṇa spoke of becoming one-pointed — fixing all four on the Absolute is how.

#### Intelligence [32:00 – 34:00]

- Spiritual life begins with intelligence: by study and contemplation we understand there must be a higher happiness, "I am the soul," the world has a designer.
- Intelligence lets us doubt the world's promises of pleasure. It comes from scripture: Kṛṣṇa is the Absolute Truth, the ultimate enjoyer and source of enjoyment; whatever attracts in this world is like sparks of the whole.
- Philosophical understanding initiates spiritual life and keeps it on steady footing.

#### Mind — emotional involvement [34:00 – 38:00]

- Students study physics or maths for marks, not out of emotional interest — while studying they think of the party after exams. Intelligence alone is not enough.
- Example: a patient told by allopathy that his cancer has no cure tries Ayurveda; he and his relatives study the treatment intelligently and are emotionally invested in getting well, yet he lacks faith — "I don't think it's going to work" `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Faith [38:00]

- People pray at temples or donate not from faith but to avoid trouble — a residual, fear-based trace of faith.
- In chanting: intellectually we understand the holy name is Kṛṣṇa Himself, non-different from Him; emotionally we feel "Kṛṣṇa Himself is coming — let me receive Him enthusiastically with the affection of my heart." Kṛṣṇa asks us to fix not only the intellectual and emotional faculties but faith as well.

#### Goal [40:00 – 44:00]

- At the _karma-kāṇḍa_ level the Lord is not the goal: people go to "Visa Gaṇeśa" temples to remove the obstacle to going to America, or to Bālājī to remove obstacles to happiness. Faith and emotion may be there, but the goal is "I want to be here."
- Purūravā, infatuated with Urvaśī, worshipped Lord Viṣṇu to get her back; even with direct _darśana_ he did not ask for purification or pure devotion — only for his wish. _Darśana_ will have its effect, but it takes time if one is too attached `40:00 – 42:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa should not be just the object of meditation to do better in life, but the goal of life.
- Spirituality is often used as stress relief — fine as a start — but it is meant to be the complete distress remover. One meaning of "Kṛṣṇa" is one who ends repeated birth and death `42:00 – 44:00`.
- "The goal of spiritual life is to make spiritual life our goal" `44:00`.

#### Cleansing and the scattered lanes [44:00 – 46:00]

- Spiritual knowledge is meant to be transformational, not theoretical; investing all four faculties washes away all contamination.
- Example of scattered lanes: we like the philosophy, but when India wins a cricket match the mind is caught there; we have faith in Kṛṣṇa, but "life here isn't so bad"; we want the spiritual world, but later — "right now I want to make it big here."

#### Evolution of sādhya and sādhana [46:00 – 50:00]

- Stages of the goal:
  - Temporary only — house, spouse, job, fame.
  - Temporary and eternal — "something here, and something beyond" — where most of us probably are.
  - Eternal only — ready to give up this world for the eternal destination; one may still act in the world, but the heart's goal is fixed there.
  - Within the eternal, the conception evolves — finally "I want the Supreme Lord, _Bhagavān_; Kṛṣṇa is my all."
- Corresponding means: most people today do only _vikarma_ — no conception of piety; _karma-kāṇḍa_ uses _dharma_ to get _artha_ and _kāma_; with an eternal goal, _yoga_ enters, with the _paramātmā_ as the conception of the goal `48:00`.

#### Simultaneous identity and individuality [50:00 – 52:00]

- Prabhupāda: a Kṛṣṇa conscious person understands the simultaneous identity and individuality in Kṛṣṇa and, equipped with this knowledge, makes steady progress toward liberation.
- We are one with Kṛṣṇa in quality, yet the Lord is an individual and so are we. The one Absolute Truth manifests in many forms, and the parts and parcels are also His.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Consciousness runs in four lanes — intelligence, emotion, faith and goal; when all four are fixed on Kṛṣṇa, knowledge stops being theoretical and becomes transformational, cleansing the heart and leading to the path of no return. [30:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.18 [52:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.18 -->

**Context.** When all faculties are fixed on Kṛṣṇa, how does one see the world? The humble sage, by true knowledge, sees with equal vision a learned and gentle _brāhmaṇa_, a cow, an elephant, a dog and a dog-eater. Prabhupāda quoted this verse often `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- The verse's word glossed as humility or gentleness `52:00`.
- Equal vision — seeing both the _ātmā_ and the _paramātmā_ in all `54:00`.

#### Externally diverse, internally equal [52:00 – 54:00]

- In conventional Vedic culture the differences among these are very significant, and people think separation must be kept to maintain purity.
- Kṛṣṇa accepts separation in the sense that everyone should stick to their duties and gradually be purified — but that should not mean rejecting others. See diversity externally, similarity internally.
- The three animals represent the three modes: the cow goodness, the elephant passion, the dog ignorance (and the _brāhmaṇa_ is in goodness). Whatever mode one is in, one should be seen with spiritual vision — all are souls, accompanied by the Supreme Lord `54:00`.
- Prabhupāda: these bodily differences are meaningless from the viewpoint of a learned transcendentalist.

#### Equal vision, appropriate reciprocation [56:00]

- Equal vision does not mean equal reciprocation. One can embrace a cow, not a tiger — embrace a tiger and you will need a brace afterwards, if you survive at all.

#### A vision for a fractured world [56:00 – 60:00]

- The world is caught in prejudice and sectarian conflict; people want something practical from the Gītā. These verses can be presented to those concerned about conflict: if we are all souls connected with Kṛṣṇa — one family — conflicts decrease substantially.
- This unifies not only human beings but all living beings.
- In ISKCON, devotees from different parts of the world chant and dance together in _kīrtana_.
- At a big _paṇḍāl_ programme in Mumbai, Prabhupāda arranged on one of the last days a wedding of two disciples — the boy from America, the girl from Australia, going to preach in a third country in Europe — and said, "This is the real United Nations" `58:00`.
- A prophecy spoke of people from Russia, Germany, England, America and elsewhere chanting the holy names in Navadvīpa; Prabhupāda fulfilled it to some extent. The more we internalise and share this vision, the more equality, unity, peace and harmony manifest `58:00 – 60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The sage sees every being — across all three modes — as a soul accompanied by the Supersoul; this equal vision, joined with appropriate dealings, is the Gītā's practical answer to a world fractured by prejudice. [52:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
