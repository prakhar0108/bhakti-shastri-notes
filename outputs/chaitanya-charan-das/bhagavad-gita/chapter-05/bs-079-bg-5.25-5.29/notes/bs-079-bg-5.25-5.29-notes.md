## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 5.25 [00:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.25 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes who attains the spiritual platform (_brahma-nirvāṇa_) and adds a striking qualification: such people are engaged in the welfare of all living beings `00:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Brahman_ — the spiritual platform `00:00`.
- _Ṛṣi_ — words like _ṛṣi_ and _muni_ each have specific meanings, but here, following the previous verse, the word means one who turns inward; Śrīla Prabhupāda writes "those who are active within" `00:00 – 02:00`.
- Freedom from contaminations — the impurities of the heart are destroyed; Kṛṣṇa has used the same idea earlier, that by knowledge one becomes free from all contaminations `02:00`.
- Cutting duality — the related words for "duality" all mean "this is good, this is bad"; such a person cuts through it `02:00`.
- Self-controlled — one who is controlled at the level of the soul and focused on realising the soul `02:00`.
- Engaged in the welfare of all living beings `02:00 – 04:00`.

#### Spiritualists are not self-centred [04:00 – 08:00]

- Many people feel that those who pursue spiritual realisation are self-centred, concerned only with their own purification and liberation; Kṛṣṇa says that is not the case.
- Kṛṣṇa uses the same description again in the twelfth chapter:
  - **12.3–12.5** — even the impersonalists, who meditate on the formless _Brahman_, must be engaged in the well-being of all; they too eventually attain Kṛṣṇa, but by a very difficult process.
  - **12.13** — the devotees are non-envious toward all living beings and are their well-wishing friends.
- Two kinds of compassion `06:00`:
  - **The devotee's** — personal: all living beings are parts and children of Kṛṣṇa, one family; how can our beloved Lord be happy if His other parts are suffering? So devotees help them come back home.
  - **The impersonalist's** — not personally based, because the ultimate reality is not personal; it takes the form of not discriminating ("I like this person, I don't like that one") and having equal vision and a benevolent attitude toward everyone.
- Either way, both personalist and impersonalist spiritualists are expected to engage in the welfare of all `06:00`.

#### Why purity is the qualification for helping [06:00 – 12:00]

- Helping seems simple — give food to the hungry, clothes to the needy; these are noble and compassionate acts `08:00`.
- But society's problems are not so simple: poverty is fuelled by a socio-economic complex of greed and exploitation among the haves and unrestrained indulgence among everyone, including the have-nots — sometimes money given to a beggar is used for smoking or drinking instead of food `08:00`.
- The chain of reasoning `08:00`:
  1. Greed and unregulated indulgence must be curbed.
  2. That happens only by attaining higher spiritual happiness.
  3. Without higher happiness, one cannot give up lower happiness.
  4. Without giving up lower happiness, an exploitative mentality comes up sooner or later.
  5. Even with genuine compassion, one who does not know the treatment cannot cure the patient.
- People are "sick" with lust, anger, greed and illusion; those free from these contaminations, and who know how to care for others, can offer the best help `08:00 – 10:00`.
- This does not ban others from helping: anyone concerned and selfless enough to help should be appreciated — but help only at the level of the body cannot be complete, holistic or sustainable `10:00`.
- Prabhupāda: one engaged only in ministering to the physical welfare of the body cannot factually help anyone; the real cause of difficulty is forgetfulness of one's relationship with the Supreme Lord, and that forgetfulness is what the _ṛṣi_ removes `10:00 – 12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Turning inward does not make a spiritualist selfish: only one freed from lust, anger, greed and illusion can address the real cause of suffering — forgetfulness of Kṛṣṇa — and so offer complete, sustainable help to all. [04:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.26 [12:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.26 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa continues to speak of _brahma-nirvāṇa_, naming what is required for it: freedom from lust and anger, and control of the mind `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kāma_ and _krodha_ — lust and anger, the inner enemies `12:00`.
- Liberated / thoroughly liberated — the teacher notes the intensified form of "liberated" in the verse `12:00`.
- Control over the mind — the teacher renders the term as "mind" or "consciousness" `20:00`.
- "Assured in the near future" — liberation comes soon, not after a long time `20:00 – 22:00`.
- _Kāmānuja_ — a name for anger: "the younger brother of lust" `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### Lust and anger as a pair [12:00 – 14:00]

- Kṛṣṇa has coupled _kāma_ and _krodha_ before:
  - **3.36–3.37** — Arjuna asks what impels us to do wrong; Kṛṣṇa replies, lust and anger.
  - **2.62–2.63** — desire becomes strong, becomes lust; obstructed lust becomes anger.
  - **5.22–5.23** — understand sense pleasure leads to misery; therefore tolerate the urges.

#### Drowning in the puddle of anger [14:00 – 16:00]

- Many people can control _kāma_ but cannot control _krodha_. A scriptural statement the teacher cites says the _jñānīs_ sometimes cross the ocean of lust and then drown in the puddle of anger.
- Why: when lust is only suppressed, not purified, there is a constant battle of wills — "I want it / no, I won't do it" — which creates inner hardness and harshness. Because that battle is self-centred, the suppressed frustration eventually erupts.
- The devotee's focus is different: "I want to love, serve and please Kṛṣṇa, therefore I will do this and not that." The desire to please Kṛṣṇa crowds out both the desire to enjoy and the desire to renounce.
- If we as devotees are motivated by the desire to renounce rather than the desire to serve, a volcano-like eruption of anger is likely: "I'm giving up so much, and still so many things are going wrong" — and that lava can break our relationships `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### A sage's fall to lust, then to anger [16:00]

- The teacher narrates a scriptural account of a sage doing meditation:
  - First, he was seduced by a celestial woman, forgot who he was and what he was doing, and lived with her.
  - Determined not to fall again, he resumed austerity. When a second celestial woman came, he understood she had been sent by Indra to plot his fall, felt a rush of wrath, and cursed her — and by that too he lost his power.
- Lesson: both _kāma_ and _krodha_ direct our emotions away from Kṛṣṇa, and both are obstacles.

#### Kāmānuja: anger follows lust [16:00 – 18:00]

- _Anuja_ means younger brother — just as Lakṣmaṇa is called _Rāmānuja_, the younger brother of Rāma.
- So anger is called _kāmānuja_: wherever lust comes, sooner or later anger will follow.
- To be advanced, one must be free from both.

#### Substitution versus purification [18:00 – 20:00]

- If one is free from lust but not from anger, desire is not being redirected to Kṛṣṇa; it is only shifting from one material object to another within the material arena.
- Example: in "substitution therapy," people giving up alcohol are told to take chocolates instead — and some become addicted to chocolates. It may be the lesser of two evils, but it is still an evil.
- Devotees seek purification: replacing the material with the spiritual — really a _reversion_, since as souls we are originally meant to delight at the spiritual level. We revert by _sādhana_ `20:00`.

#### Control must be internal [20:00]

- Lust and anger are primarily internal; controlling them externally — not indulging, not venting — is good, but does not purify.
- As long as the _anarthas_ remain at the mental level, they keep prodding, pushing, punching and pinching us to act them out physically.
- That is why remembering Kṛṣṇa is so important: it redirects the heart's desires from the material to the spiritual.

#### Why Kṛṣṇa speaks generically here [20:00 – 24:00]

- "Soon" is another place, like **4.39**, where Kṛṣṇa promises a quick result `20:00 – 22:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa does not refer to Himself much here because He is building Arjuna's thought-flow toward _bhakti-yoga_ rather than speaking of it directly `22:00`.
- "Give up outer pleasures, pursue inner pleasures" is a generic principle for _karma-yogīs_, _jñāna-yogīs_ and _bhakti-yogīs_; only the method of sense control differs `22:00`.
- Prabhupāda goes so far as to say that impersonalists, and even schools outside the Vedic fold such as the Buddhists, agree that entanglement in material desire causes suffering. The method of giving it up depends on one's conception of the goal; when the goal is a loving and lovable Person whom we can serve even with our senses, becoming free from material desire becomes much easier `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### Delighting in the Lord's lotus feet (SB 4.22.39) [24:00 – 28:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes a _Bhāgavatam_ verse from the Pṛthu Mahārāja section, which concludes that one should worship Lord Vāsudeva, and gives the reason: by delighting in the Lord's lotus feet, devotees uproot the deep-rooted impressions that impel us toward fruitive activity `24:00`, `26:00`.
- Delight in _bhakti_ is not forbidden — some devotees think renunciation means we should not feel happiness:
  - We should not chant and dance _only_ for our pleasure, but we are not meant to feel nothing.
  - Dance gracefully, not sensuously or obnoxiously. In the early days on the Lower East Side, a young woman — under the influence of drugs, without bad intention — danced provocatively in the _kīrtana_; Prabhupāda had her spoken to and dressed properly, and she continued participating `24:00 – 26:00`.
  - The test of devotion: continuing even when we don't feel happiness — "some days chanting feels so tiring, so boring, but I continue, because I know this is the way I will experience happiness and advance" `26:00`.

#### Fill the mind, don't empty it [26:00 – 32:00]

- The other way — trying to empty the mind ("don't think of that, don't think of that") — cannot succeed `26:00 – 28:00`.
- **The waves on the shore** — trying to keep a beach dry by throwing water back; the waves keep coming. Waves of sensual desire come from outside (sense objects) and from inside (memories of past indulgence) `28:00`.
- **The glass of air** — a glass is by default full of air; we can pump out a vacuum, but at the slightest exposure air rushes back. Fill the glass with water and there is no room for air. So fill the mind with devotional desires, impressions and thoughts — with Kṛṣṇa `28:00 – 30:00`.
- **"No time for lust"** — after a large Delhi _paṇḍāl_ program, Prabhupāda asked his disciples why people were rushing to offer him respects. When they could not answer, he said it was because he was free from lust — then added, "not that I have no lust, but that I have no time for lust." "No lust" may take long; "no time for lust" is possible for us by busying ourselves in fulfilling devotional service. The craving then stays in the background and gradually goes out `30:00 – 32:00`.
- Thinking too much "I will not do it" keeps the mind on the object rather than on Kṛṣṇa, and that contemplation kindles desire against our will `32:00`.

#### The Lord meditates on us too [32:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes a verse: just as fish, tortoises and birds maintain their offspring by vision, meditation and touch, so does the Lord `32:00`.
  - Birds sit on their eggs and hatch them by touch; fish look upon their offspring; tortoises meditate.
  - Meditation is not observable by science; there may be specific kinds of fish and tortoises that do this, just as not all lotuses are as fragrant, nor all birds called swans able to separate milk from water `32:00 – 34:00`.
  - In such examples, focus on the principle, not the factuality — our perception is limited and relative `34:00`.
- The principle: offspring grow by the parent's benevolent care. Just as we meditate on the Lord, the Lord is meditating on us — always in our heart and thinking of our welfare `34:00`.
- **Doctor and patient** — a doctor cannot do much for a patient who refuses treatment; when we start thinking of Kṛṣṇa, we cooperate, and He helps us more and more. We are not alone in the struggle — Kṛṣṇa is also striving to help us fix our mind on Him `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Brahma-nirvāṇa_: _Brahman_ is Kṛṣṇa, and _nirvāṇa_ is liberation from material existence `36:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Liberation requires freedom from both lust and anger, which comes only when desire is redirected to Kṛṣṇa — not by suppressing or emptying the mind but by filling it with Him, while He, like a caring parent, is meditating on our welfare. [12:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.27 – 5.28 [36:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:5.27 -->

<!-- vedabase:5.28 -->

**Context.** In these two verses Kṛṣṇa gives a brief explanation of how one can attain liberation by _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_, which Chapter 6 will elaborate `36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- External sense objects — though the word normally means touch, here it refers generally to the contact of the senses with sense objects, as in 5.22 `40:00`.
- Fixing the eyes between the eyebrows `40:00`.
- Regulating the breath `40:00`.
- Desire, fear and anger — the three things the yogī becomes free from `40:00 – 42:00`.
- "Always liberated" — one in this stage is as good as liberated already `44:00`.

#### Where these verses fit: the chapter's logic [36:00 – 38:00]

- Arjuna's mind held two options — action (_karma-yoga_) and renunciation (_jñāna-yoga_). Kṛṣṇa has shown they need not be opposite: they can be integrated, not fragmented.
- Can _karma-yoga_ alone lead to liberation? Yes. One can stay in a liberated state while working; later, as one progresses through the _varṇāśrama_ stages, one may renounce the world — taking up _jñāna-yoga_, _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ or full-time _bhakti-yoga_.
- For that stage of renunciation, Kṛṣṇa offers _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ here (two verses), elaborated in Chapter 6 (47 verses, among the Gītā's longer chapters) `38:00`.
- In 5.29 He speaks of _bhakti-yoga_, elaborated in Chapters 7–12 `38:00`.
- Overall progression: Chapters 1–5 establish that for Arjuna action is better than renunciation; when he later renounces, he may take up _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ or _bhakti-yoga_ — and _bhakti-yoga_ can be practised continuously without changing the process `38:00`.

#### External and internal control [40:00]

- **5.27 — physical control:**
  - Keep external sense objects external; don't focus on them.
  - Fix the eyes between the eyebrows.
  - Regulate the breath.
- **5.28 — internal control:**
  - Bring the senses, mind and intelligence under control.
  - Be focused on liberation — "nothing in this material world can make me happy; I want to go beyond."

#### Free from desire, fear and anger [40:00 – 44:00]

- Desire can be equated with _kāma_; _kāma_ and _krodha_ were discussed in 5.26. Fear is the new element `40:00 – 42:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa names these three together regularly — in **4.10** (obstacles to taking shelter of Him) and before that in **2.56** `42:00`.
- The three obstacles:
  - **Attachment / desire** — "I want to enjoy this world."
  - **Anger** — "Who can stop me from enjoying? I'll get over the obstacles." Renouncing out of frustration or hatred for this world keeps one's thoughts fixed on it; renunciation must be based on attraction to the other world, or it cannot be sustained.
  - **Fear** — "I don't know what's there in the spiritual realm, let me stay where I am"; or "there are relationships and complexities there; let me merge into some void" `42:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's surprising declaration: one who shuts out sense objects, fixes the mind inward and is free from these three is always liberated — as good as liberated here itself `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Prabhupāda notes that the eyes should be kept open, not closed, lest one fall asleep; this will be explained in the next chapter, and he makes clear that Kṛṣṇa consciousness is better than _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Aṣṭāṅga-yoga combines outer control (shutting out sense objects, fixing the gaze, regulating the breath) with inner control of senses, mind and intelligence; one free from desire, fear and anger is already liberated — and renunciation must grow from attraction to the spiritual, not frustration with the material. [40:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 5.29 [44:00 – 61:42]

<!-- vedabase:5.29 -->

**Context.** The last verse of the chapter, often quoted: one who understands Kṛṣṇa as the enjoyer of all sacrifices and austerities, the proprietor of everything and the well-wisher of all living beings attains peace `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bhoktā_ — the enjoyer `44:00`, `54:00`.
- _Sarva-loka-maheśvara_ — the proprietor and controller of all planets and places `44:00`, `54:00`.
- _Suhṛt_ — the well-wisher; it can also mean one situated in the heart `44:00`, `56:00`.
- _Śānti_ — peace `44:00`, `54:00`.

#### The peace formula [44:00 – 46:00]

- Prabhupāda made pamphlets of the "peace formula" from this verse and distributed them: accept Kṛṣṇa as the proprietor, the enjoyer and the well-wisher.
- People generally think peace needs negotiations, conferences and settling grievances. That matters at the material level, but there peace will always be elusive and unstable, because some will always be exploited.
- Real peace grows from within: internally satisfied people radiate peace outward. As long as people depend on external satisfaction — and externals are bound to be disrupted — they cannot be peaceful. Inner peace is the only sustainable building block for outer peace `46:00`.

#### Making peace with God [46:00 – 48:00]

- Before peace in the world, we must make peace with God. "Who's fighting with God?" — but when we want to enjoy separately from Kṛṣṇa, we are in a sense waging war against Him, subconsciously trying to usurp His position.
- He is the supreme enjoyer; we are meant to be co-enjoyers with Him, not independent enjoyers.
- As independent enjoyers our enjoyment is limited by our finite capacity; connected with Him, it becomes unlimited, because His capacity to enjoy is unlimited.

#### Three directions of resentment [48:00 – 52:00]

- When our plans don't work out, resentment can go:
  - **Outwards** — "because of that person…" → hatefulness and vengefulness, which certainly hurt us even if we never hurt them.
  - **Inwards** — "I'm useless, I bungle everything" → inferiority complex, depression, even suicidal tendencies.
  - **Upwards** — "Why does God do things like this?" → a wall between us and God, so we cannot accept His help `48:00 – 50:00`.
- We all need help, because the world sends obstacles and reversals; that help comes through knowledge and faith that Kṛṣṇa is always my well-wisher `50:00`.
- **Surgeon's knife** — a patient may not immediately see how the knife helps; it seems to cause only pain. Over time we will understand `50:00`.
- When we accept Kṛṣṇa as the well-wisher of all — and so mine too — resentment breaks down; we act in the best possible way, are purified, and peace deepens `50:00 – 52:00`.
- "Send resentment on retirement": what's happened has happened and can't be changed, but the present is still with me; Kṛṣṇa is there to guide, help and empower me `52:00`.
- This is not merely to be accepted on faith: if we act accordingly, we will see in our own lives how things that seemed terribly wrong worked for our good `52:00`.

#### Possessiveness and war [52:00 – 54:00]

- Most wars are over wealth, property and power — all forms of possessiveness.
- Hitler wanted to be emperor of Europe and then to conquer Russia and Asia — in a tiny way wanting to be the _maheśvara_, controller of all places.
- When we understand that all we need for happiness is to serve Kṛṣṇa, we need not usurp His position as controller or enjoyer; as His servant we find far deeper satisfaction.
- The _śānti_ here is not mainly the absence of outer war — outer wars may continue — but the end of inner war, fuelled by resentment toward things, people and ultimately God `54:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa as the goal of all paths [54:00 – 58:00]

- The verse is a conclusion showing Kṛṣṇa as the ultimate goal of the various paths:
  - **Enjoyer of sacrifices** — the goal of _karma-yogīs_ and _karma-kāṇḍīs_, who perform _yajña_ (Chapter 3) `54:00`.
  - **Enjoyer of austerities** — the goal of the _jñānīs_, who perform austerities to become detached `54:00 – 56:00`.
  - **Proprietor of all worlds** — even the planets of the demigods, which their worshippers aspire to (9.25), are His; they are secondary proprietors, He the ultimate one `56:00`.
  - **_Suhṛt_, situated in the heart** — the goal of _aṣṭāṅga-yogīs_, who turn inward to perceive the Supersoul `56:00`.
- So He is the goal of all living beings — the point of 4.11, that all people are on His path — stated here in a subtle, indicative way `56:00`.

#### The "bare facts" and Kṛṣṇa consciousness [58:00 – 61:42]

- Prabhupāda's long, classic purport: the "bare facts" — unadorned, unvarnished — are that the Lord is the master of material nature and the conditioned souls are under its stringent rules; without understanding this, peace is impossible individually or collectively `58:00`.
- The _sense_ of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is not just activities (chanting, studying the Gītā, associating with devotees — all important) but "Kṛṣṇa is the master and I am His servant" `58:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa consciousness arouses spiritual existence even while one is under the jurisdiction of matter `58:00`:
  - In _jñāna-yoga_ there are two distinct steps — act in the world, then disconnect from it to connect with the spiritual.
  - In _bhakti-yoga_ one may stay in the world but invoke spiritual reality by devotional intention, acting in a regulated, devotional, dedicated way — "that's what makes it so exciting" `58:00 – 60:00`.
- The gradual _aṣṭāṅga_ process only prefaces perfection in devotional service: at the end of it, in _samādhi_, one becomes conscious of Kṛṣṇa; one who takes up Kṛṣṇa consciousness directly achieves the same perfection more easily `60:00`.

#### Chapter 5 in summary [60:00]

- **5.1–5.6** — _karma-yoga_ and _jñāna-yoga_ lead to the same result, but _karma-yoga_ is easier.
- **5.7–5.16** — by knowing oneself to be the _ātmā_, one stays free from bondage.
- **5.17–5.29** — by fixing the mind on the _Paramātmā_, one attains liberation.
- Message to Arjuna: practise _karma-yoga_, with no doubt that acting in the world can bring liberation; the next chapter describes what to do when one eventually renounces.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Peace begins with making peace with God: accepting Kṛṣṇa as the enjoyer, the proprietor and our well-wisher dissolves resentment and possessiveness, and reveals Him as the goal of every path. [44:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

- No separate audience Q&A appears in the captions. The teacher raises and answers a few questions himself:
  - Are spiritualists self-centred? No — even impersonalists must work for the welfare of all, and devotees are friends of all `04:00 – 06:00`.
