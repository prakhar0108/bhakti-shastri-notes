## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 10.12 – 10.13 [04:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.12 -->

<!-- vedabase:10.13 -->

**Context.** Having heard the four central verses, Arjuna declares Kṛṣṇa to be the Supreme and adds that great sages also declare this — and now Kṛṣṇa Himself is saying it `04:00 – 16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Param dhāma_ — the supreme abode or shelter: the ultimate destination where the soul actually wants to go and where it becomes happy `06:00`.
- _Pavitram paramam_ — the purest of all; in Him no impurity can enter `08:00`.
- Arjuna's further names for Kṛṣṇa in 10.12 — the eternal, original person — are, the teacher notes, very clearly personal `08:00`.

#### The supreme shelter versus fallible shelters [06:00]

- We all hope for shelter in some person or thing — a house, a job, our dreams.
- Such shelters are fallible; Kṛṣṇa is the supreme shelter, and our heart's deepest aspiration is ultimately for Him.

#### Is Arjuna simply flattering Kṛṣṇa? [12:00 – 14:00]

- Friends sometimes exaggerate to please each other — "you are the greatest speaker in the whole world" — whether or not it is true.
- But this is not friendly chit-chat. From 2.11 up to 10.11 Kṛṣṇa has been giving a very deep philosophical discourse; seen in that context, Arjuna's words are most unlikely to be an insubstantial exaggeration.
- **Cricket analogy:** if a friend says "you are the world's best batsman," it means little; if the world's best commentators and experts say so, the statement carries weight, because they are authorities in that subject. Likewise Arjuna cites great authorities who confirm Kṛṣṇa's position.
- Arjuna himself had heard such great personalities speak about Kṛṣṇa on many occasions described in the _Mahābhārata_ `14:00`.

#### "Now You Yourself are telling me" [16:00]

- How does Kṛṣṇa's own statement add to the authority of the sages? If Kṛṣṇa is the Absolute, then what He says while giving a philosophical explanation of reality has to be taken as absolute.
- Kṛṣṇa is the ultimate authority because He is the Absolute Truth. Through this discourse Arjuna has come to understand that Kṛṣṇa is the Absolute, and he now explains this in his own words.

#### Why some still miss the point [18:00]

- Unfortunately some people, proud of academic education, mistakenly take Kṛṣṇa to be an ordinary person and remain obstinate in that view.

#### Scripture and experience together [20:00 – 26:00]

- Through various conversations Śrīla Prabhupāda drives home that both scriptural evidence and personal experience are important; when combined, conviction becomes deep.
- **Scripture alone** leads to endless debate: anyone who wants to claim something can draw a quotation from somewhere. In one exchange of letters, a professor of Hinduism kept answering, "this mantra actually means this, that one actually means that."
  - **Court analogy:** different lawyers present arguments from different perspectives, but the decision is made by an authority — the judge.
  - Prabhupāda replied that there is no process as potent as chanting the holy name, citing the verse that says "the holy name" three times; the exchange was later published as a booklet and also appears in _The Science of Self-Realization_ `22:00 – 24:00`.
  - If we only quote scripture without trying out a process, we stay on the mental platform and reach no conclusive understanding `24:00 – 26:00`.
- **Experience alone** is also unreliable: someone may go to a retreat where a _bābā_ is made into God, in a nice natural setting with sweet music in the background, feel very good, and accept that person on experience alone. That is sentimental experience `26:00`.
- The experience that counts is experience on the path of _bhakti-yoga_ — the process of devotional service by which we connect with God.
- So scripture plus personal experience in _bhakti_ is a very important way of deepening conviction `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's declaration is not a friend's flattery but the conclusion of a deep discourse, backed by recognised authorities and by Kṛṣṇa Himself; our own conviction likewise deepens when scriptural authority is joined with personal experience in devotional service. [12:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.14 [26:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.14 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says he accepts as true everything Kṛṣṇa has told him, and acknowledges that neither the demigods nor the demons can understand Kṛṣṇa's personality `26:00 – 28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vyakti_ — Kṛṣṇa's personality, His nature as revealed to Arjuna `28:00`.

#### Two points in one verse [28:00]

- "My dear Lord, I accept what You say."
- "It is not easy to understand You."
- The connection: when we accept, we suspend judgment about matters we cannot yet understand, recognising that some things require a higher vantage point to be understood. If even the demigods cannot understand Kṛṣṇa, what to speak of me?

#### Putting the cart before the horse [30:00 – 32:00]

- An approach the teacher criticises: "I have my own set of conclusions, and I will accept a teacher only if he agrees with them."
- **Doctor–patient analogy:** it is like a patient who will take medicine only after finding a doctor who agrees with the patient's own diagnosis. The patient has to agree with the doctor, not the doctor with the patient — otherwise the whole thing is turned upside down.
- The teacher allows that some organisations may interpret things improperly, so people do need to be cautious; but ultimately the student accepts the teacher, not the other way round.

#### How to identify the Gītā's real conclusion [32:00 – 36:00]

- **The teacher's concluding statement:** in a conversation between teacher and student, look at what the teacher concludes. In the Gītā this is 18.66 — "whatever I have told you, set it aside; this is what I want you to do."
- **Whether the teacher corrects the student:** Arjuna calls Kṛṣṇa the Supreme here. Kṛṣṇa does not correct him by saying "do not think I am the Supreme"; He accepts Arjuna's understanding as correct.
- **The student's action:** after the Gītā, Arjuna does not take up impersonal speculation; he acts in devotional service. We can also look at what happens afterwards in the _Mahābhārata_.
- So if we let the Gītā speak for itself, it is very clear that Kṛṣṇa, and devotional service to Him, is the highest.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Real acceptance means taking Kṛṣṇa's words as given while humbly admitting He is beyond full comprehension — not shopping for a teacher who confirms our own conclusions; the Gītā's teacher, His concluding instruction and His student's actions all point to devotion to Kṛṣṇa. [28:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.15 [36:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.15 -->

**Context.** Arjuna addresses Kṛṣṇa as the one who alone knows Himself by His own potency, and with a series of names that establish Him as the Absolute `36:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Puruṣottama_ — the Supreme Person `36:00`.
- _Bhūta-bhāvana_ — the source of all living beings `36:00`.
- _Bhūteśa_ — the controller of all living beings `36:00`.
- _Deva-deva_ — the God of all gods `36:00 – 38:00`.
- _Jagat-pati_ — the master of the whole universe `38:00`.

#### Source and controller [36:00 – 38:00]

- A father may be the source of his children, yet the children may go out of his control. Kṛṣṇa is not like that: He is both the source of all beings and their controller.
- The demigods are controllers with some power over various departments of the universe, but Kṛṣṇa is God even for the gods:
  - when demigods give blessings, the power to bless comes from Him;
  - when the demons become extremely powerful, the demigods go to Viṣṇu for help.
- Each of these names is used by Arjuna to demonstrate and emphasise that Kṛṣṇa is the Absolute Truth `38:00`.

#### Where the section turns [38:00]

- Verses 12 to 15 express Arjuna's understanding and acceptance; from here onward Arjuna makes a request.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Unlike a father whose children may slip out of control, Kṛṣṇa is both the source and the controller of all beings, and even the demigods' powers come from Him — which is why Arjuna's names for Him all point to the Absolute Truth. [36:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.16 – 10.17 [40:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.16 -->

<!-- vedabase:10.17 -->

**Context.** Arjuna asks Kṛṣṇa to speak about His divine opulences, by which He pervades the worlds, and asks how he should constantly think of Kṛṣṇa `40:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vibhūti_ — Kṛṣṇa's opulences `40:00`.
- _Yogī_ — usually a seeker who practises yoga, but here Arjuna addresses Kṛṣṇa as _yogī_ `42:00`.
- _Yoga_ — connection `44:00`.

#### Arjuna asks on behalf of others [40:00]

- Arjuna is thinking about how people will understand Kṛṣṇa's glory and be able to meditate on Him.
- People look around and see how extraordinary the world is; if they learn Kṛṣṇa's opulences, they can see what they meet in the world with Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

#### Why Kṛṣṇa is called "yogī" here [42:00 – 44:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is not seeking any realisation through yoga; He is the one who helps us get realisation.
- He is also mysteriously connected with the whole universe, both matter and spirit.
- Elsewhere Kṛṣṇa is called _yogeśvara_ and _mahā-yogeśvara_ — Sañjaya uses _yogeśvara_ at the conclusion of the Gītā in 18.78 — showing that He is not an ordinary yogī but the master of all yogīs.
- We are trying to connect with the Absolute Truth; the Absolute Truth is also connected with us and with everything that exists. Understanding that connection, revealed by Kṛṣṇa, is also _yoga_ — from Kṛṣṇa's side `44:00`.

#### Constant remembrance [44:00]

- Arjuna asks how he can constantly think of Kṛṣṇa. When we have love for Kṛṣṇa, remembrance comes naturally; the opulences give a way to connect even material things with Kṛṣṇa, since this part of the verse refers to material nature.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's request is for everyone: since Kṛṣṇa's transcendental nature is hard to know, His opulences in this world give ordinary people a way to connect what they see with Him and so remember Him constantly. [40:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.18 [46:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.18 -->

**Context.** Arjuna asks Kṛṣṇa to describe His opulences again in detail, because he is never satiated by hearing them `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vistareṇa_ — in detail `46:00`.
- _Ātma-yoga_ — Kṛṣṇa's own glory `46:00`.

#### Great books are read again and again [48:00]

- Classic literature has multiple layers of meaning, and people want to re-read it carefully, because every sentence is meaningful.
- The greatest book is the _Bhagavad-gītā_. Every time we read it we can get new meanings — not only because we are discovering them, but because the Gītā is a manifestation of Kṛṣṇa, His self-revelation, and Kṛṣṇa reveals new meanings.
- **Shakespeare and Hamlet:** Shakespeare is not present in _Hamlet_; whatever artistry, poetry and imagery a material work has, credit is due and people may enjoy it. But Kṛṣṇa's words are extraordinary — He is present in them.

#### Taste that keeps growing [50:00]

- Along with the depth of meaning, there are many levels of meaning: we connect one point with another and start developing a taste for _kṛṣṇa-kathā_, and that taste goes on and on.
- By gradual purification the taste becomes stronger and stronger.
- The _Purāṇas_, histories that relate the Lord's pastimes, remain ever fresh to read, because it is Kṛṣṇa Himself manifesting through those pastimes and giving us the opportunity to relish His glory `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### External newness versus internal newness [52:00 – 54:00]

- People want something new — a new dress, watch, cell phone, computer, house. Yet internally all these new things offer the same old experiences: the pleasures of eating, sleeping and so on.
- For a devotee, the external activities may stay the same — seeing the same deity's beauty, chanting the same _mahā-mantra_ — but internally they become new, because love for Kṛṣṇa awakens.
- Kṛṣṇa is unlimited, and our original love for Him is also unlimited.
- **The unopened letter:** judging spiritual life only from outside is like assessing a letter without opening it. When we connect emotionally with Kṛṣṇa, the same activities give richer and richer experiences `54:00`.

#### Recovery, not discovery [56:00]

- Kṛṣṇa consciousness is not the discovery of a new concept but the recovery of an old map — the map showing the path to Kṛṣṇa. We get the map, then move along it by studying His words.
- **Childhood friend:** meeting a childhood friend after a long separation brings joy; Kṛṣṇa is our longest-lost friend, so the joy of reconnecting with Him is the greatest — and since He is unlimited, the joy of relishing His glory is also unlimited.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Hearing about Kṛṣṇa never satiates because His words are His self-revelation: the same verses, deity and holy name keep yielding new meaning and fresh taste as love awakens — the joy of recovering our longest-lost friend. [48:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
