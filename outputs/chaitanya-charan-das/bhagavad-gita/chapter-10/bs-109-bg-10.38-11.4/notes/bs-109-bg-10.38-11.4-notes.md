## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 10.38 [00:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.38 -->

**Context.** The last group of named opulences: among means of correction Kṛṣṇa is punishment, among those who seek victory He is morality, among secrets He is silence, and among the wise He is wisdom `00:00 – 08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Daṇḍa_ — to suppress, to control, to bring someone on the right track; punishment `00:00`.
- _Nīti_ — morality; ethical means `02:00`.
- _Guhya_ — secret `06:00`.
- _Jñāna_ — wisdom; what distinguishes the wise `08:00`.

#### Punishment as the last resort [00:00 – 02:00]

- There are many ways of bringing people on the right track: good advice, counsel, warnings.
- When these do not work, the last resort is punishment; that is why all cultures have had jails or other places of punishment and reformation.
- Kṛṣṇa does not mean He wants to punish everyone. The verse highlights what is most powerful and influential among various manifestations — punishment is the most forceful way of making a point when no other way works.

#### Three kinds of nīti [02:00 – 06:00]

- A common saying in Western literature holds that in war everything is fair — whatever it takes to win. Kṛṣṇa does not recommend that attitude: seek victory, but by virtuous means.
- The teacher describes three kinds of _nīti_:
  - **Bṛhaspati-nīti** — Bṛhaspati is the priest and counsellor of the _devas_; strive for good ends by good means.
  - **Śukra-nīti** — as long as the ends are good, the means do not matter; bad means can be used for good ends.
  - **Kaṇika-nīti** — care neither for ends nor means; strive only for one's own protection, pleasure and power.
- Kaṇika was a _brāhmaṇa_ friend of Śakuni. When Dhṛtarāṣṭra felt pangs of conscience about the devious tactics used against the Pāṇḍavas, Śakuni brought Kaṇika, who advised that survival and prosperity are all-important and whatever is required may be done. This is very bad and must be avoided.

#### Kṛṣṇa's own practice in the Mahābhārata [04:00 – 06:00]

- Kṛṣṇa first tries His best to avoid war, ready to accept just five villages for the Pāṇḍavas; the Kauravas refuse.
- When the Kauravas use devious tricks even on the battlefield, Kṛṣṇa also uses such means; some principal Kaurava warriors are killed by what seem devious means — an application of _śukra-nīti_.
- Yudhiṣṭhira's victory was important for the sake of _dharma_; how it is achieved may vary with time, place and circumstance.
- Seemingly unethical means for ethical ends are exceptional, not the standard — especially when the enemy uses them and there is no alternative. The best is _bṛhaspati-nīti_; the power of those who win by virtuous means is Kṛṣṇa's _vibhūti_.

#### Silence and wisdom [06:00 – 08:00]

- Many people enjoy not knowing a secret but telling others they know it — "I'll tell only you, don't tell anyone" — and through a chain of such tellings the secret becomes public news.
- The best way to keep a secret is to stay silent.
- What distinguishes the wise is their wisdom; a person known to be wise who suddenly speaks foolishly loses value. That attracting wisdom is Kṛṣṇa's opulence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's opulence shows itself in whatever is most effective — the force of punishment, the power of victory won by virtuous means, the silence that keeps a secret, the wisdom that marks the wise — and the ideal He upholds is good ends pursued by good means. [00:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.39 [08:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.39 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa begins the conclusion of the section; the last four verses are summary verses `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bīja_ — the seed of all living beings `08:00`.

#### Source and sustainer [08:00 – 10:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is the seed of all living beings, and without Him nothing can exist.
- That He is the source and sustainer of everything was discussed in the seventh and ninth chapters; He reiterates it here.
- When Kṛṣṇa says "among fish I am the shark," it does not mean only the shark comes from Him, or that He has special affection for the shark alone. The shark attracts attention by its speed, power and ferocity, and that attracting power is His manifestation.
- Lest one restrict Kṛṣṇa to specific manifestations and miss His connection with everything, He clarifies: not only is He the source of everything, He is also the sustainer of everything.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The named vibhūtis are not a limit on Kṛṣṇa's presence; He is the seed and sustainer of every being, and the special opulences simply make that connection easier to see. [08:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.40 [10:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.40 -->

**Context.** There is no end to Kṛṣṇa's divine opulences; what He has spoken is only a sample — repeating what He said at the start of this section `10:00 – 12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vibhūti_ — Kṛṣṇa's divine opulence, immensely vast `10:00`.
- _Jñāna-cakṣu_ — eyes of knowledge, needed to see an attraction as Kṛṣṇa's opulence `18:00`.

#### Cultural examples, universal principle [12:00 – 14:00]

- Kṛṣṇa gives a sample to help us understand a broad principle: in this world, whatever attracts us — according to our culture, time, place and circumstance — is a manifestation of His opulence.
- The cultural can be specific; the spiritual is universal and can manifest in different cultural forms.
- Kṛṣṇa gives His examples as per the cultural and cosmological understanding of His time. Those beings — Indra, Garuḍa and others — still exist and remain part of the cosmology, but they may not be part of our present conceptual framework because we have been educated differently.
- As we keep studying the Vedic scriptures, our framework gradually changes and we can relate to them.

#### Examples must make the unfamiliar familiar [14:00 – 18:00]

- The purpose of an example is to make an unfamiliar concept familiar. If the example itself is unfamiliar, the work doubles — first we must understand the example, then the concept.
- Remote people who have never heard of tennis would gain nothing from "he has a fighting spirit like Nadal."
- Śrīla Prabhupāda gave examples people understood:
  - A fire sacrifice was exotic to Western audiences, so he compared _yajña_ to paying tax to the universal government.
  - Saying the ecstasy of Vaikuṇṭha is a thousand times _brahmānanda_ explains one unfamiliar concept with another. Once, asked about the ecstasy of going to Vaikuṇṭha, he said it is like floating in an ocean of LSD — and the audience said, "Yes, I want to go there!"
- Is this a mundane interpretation of the spiritual? A material interpretation is offensive when it is _reductive_ — when the spiritual is reduced to the material and no higher reality is admitted:
  - "The Deity is just a stone representation."
  - "Chanting feels good only because certain chemicals fire in the brain." That may be part of the process, but beyond the brain and body the soul is being activated and its natural joyfulness aroused.
- The important point is to be relevant, and Śrīla Prabhupāda was remarkably relevant `18:00`.

#### Seeing Kṛṣṇa in what attracts people today [18:00 – 20:00]

- A famous musician attracting millions, sports stars, business tycoons — their opulence is Kṛṣṇa manifesting through them.
- One could dismiss it all as _māyā_, but that is where people's consciousness is.
- Had Arjuna adopted a dismissive attitude, there would be no need for the _vibhūti-yoga_ chapter; and Kṛṣṇa did not answer "matter is illusion, forget it" — He gave examples by which Arjuna could remember Him.

#### Prabhupāda and Charlie Chaplin [20:00 – 22:00]

- On a plane, a Charlie Chaplin movie was showing and Śrīla Prabhupāda laughed at the jokes. Devotees, assuming anything not explicitly connected with Kṛṣṇa is _māyā_, were bewildered.
- Prabhupāda explained that Chaplin's humour is a small, fragmental manifestation of Kṛṣṇa's capacity for humour; humour is original and a spark of Kṛṣṇa's qualities.
- Three ways of seeing:
  - **Enjoyer's view** — this humour is nice; laugh and forget your sufferings ("you have to laugh to stop from crying").
  - **Renouncer's view** — it is all illusion; this laughter is part of the illusion that perpetuates suffering.
  - **Devotee's view** — see the connection between the thing and Kṛṣṇa.
- This does not mean we go and watch Chaplin movies to see Kṛṣṇa's _vibhūti_; that is not the only way.

#### Krishna consciousness everywhere [22:00 – 26:00]

- Going to the temple, seeing the Deities, reading Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, chanting, associating with devotees and going on pilgrimage are direct, explicitly devotional, primarily recommended and vital.
- But Kṛṣṇa consciousness should not be pigeonholed only into these; its jurisdiction is everywhere, as Prabhupāda said in the seventh chapter.
- Politicians, sports stars and movie stars are constantly in the news; we need not delight in them, nor reject all of it as mundane gossip. When we are exposed to them, we try to see the "Kṛṣṇa factor": their capacity to attract attention ultimately comes from Kṛṣṇa.
- We often have to illustrate eternal principles with contextual examples. The Gītā's examples are factual and part of the Vedic cosmology, but not what people are familiar with now; we can give examples from the familiar to bring people closer to Kṛṣṇa.
- A brilliant student who answers every question attracts others; that intelligence is Kṛṣṇa's manifestation. If this student can attract so many, how much more can Kṛṣṇa — and how much joy will there be in a relationship with Him?
- The whole process of _bhakti-yoga_ is not rejection but connection, and this chapter reveals that connection of matter with spirit beautifully `24:00 – 26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's opulences are endless and His list is only a sample; the principle is that whatever attracts people — in any culture or age — is His opulence, so a devotee neither indulges in nor dismisses it, but connects it to Kṛṣṇa. [10:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.41 [26:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.41 -->

**Context.** In the last-but-one verse of the chapter, Kṛṣṇa says that whatever is glorious, beautiful and opulent is but a spark of His splendour `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śrīmat_ — that which is filled with beauty, as in _Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_: the book about Bhagavān whose beauty is the intimate love of the Lord with His devotees `26:00`.
- _Aṁśa_ — a spark, a fragment `26:00`.

#### From infinite longings to longing for the infinite [26:00 – 28:00]

- If we are attracted only to the sparks, they give a little pleasure that soon ends, leaving us with nothing.
- Instead, let the sparks remind us of the whole, and redirect our attraction to the whole.
- Attraction to sparks separate from the whole produces infinite longings — "I want this, this is attractive, that is attractive" — one thing after another forever.
- A devotee chooses instead to long for the infinite, in whom all the attractive features scattered among different people are present in one place.
- This is the essential message of the chapter: since we are anyway attracted to things of this world, rise from infinite longings to longing for the infinite, and through gradual purification redirect consciousness to Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every splendour in the world is only a spark of Kṛṣṇa's; let the spark point to the fire — rise from infinite longings to longing for the infinite. [26:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.42 [28:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.42 -->

**Context.** What is the need for all this description of opulence? Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna to understand one point: by a single fragment of Himself He pervades and supports this entire universe `28:00 – 30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bahunā_ — many; "I could speak of many such things, but what is the use of knowing all of them?" `28:00`.
- _Kṛtsnam_ — complete, entire; the word appears several times in the Gītā — in the fourth chapter (one who performs all activities with proper understanding stays unbound) and the seventh chapter (after describing His material and spiritual energies, BG 7.4 – 7.6, Kṛṣṇa says He is the source and final shelter of the whole world) `28:00 – 30:00`.
- _Eka-aṁśa_ — one fraction, one fragment `30:00`.

#### The fragment that pervades: Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu [30:00 – 36:00]

- The verse has multiple meanings; one specific meaning is that Kṛṣṇa pervades the universe through the Supersoul.
- Using a diagram based on the _Bhāgavatam_ and further elaborated in later literature, the teacher traces Kṛṣṇa's expansions:
  - Kṛṣṇa, the cause of all causes → Balarāma → a first set of four expansions.
  - From these comes Nārāyaṇa, and from Nārāyaṇa a second set of four expansions — all in the spiritual world, all eternal.
- Beyond them are the _puruṣa-avatāras_, who interact with material nature without being contaminated by it:
  - **Mahā-Viṣṇu (Kāraṇodakaśāyī)** — the Supersoul of all universes, from whom everything comes.
  - **Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu** — expands from Mahā-Viṣṇu and is present in each universe.
  - **Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu** — the _Paramātmā_, present above Brahmaloka on the ocean of milk and also in the heart of all living beings and in every atom; this is the all-pervading manifestation.
- Looking back from Kṛṣṇa down through all these expansions, Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu is just one expansion — this is how one fragment pervades the whole world.

#### Kṛṣṇa as avatārī and as avatāra [36:00 – 38:00]

- The common Hindu idea is that Viṣṇu has ten incarnations, of whom Kṛṣṇa is one. This is not entirely incorrect, but it must be understood in context.
- **Kṛṣṇa the avatārī** — eternally present in the spiritual world, manifesting sweetness (_mādhurya_) more than majesty, the source of all manifestations of Viṣṇu.
- From Kṛṣṇa, through a chain, Viṣṇu manifests (with Brahmā and Śiva connected with material existence). Viṣṇu is in charge of maintaining this world.
- When danger arises, the _devas_ pray to Viṣṇu, who promises that an incarnation will come; _avatāra_ means one who descends from the spiritual world to the material world.

#### Viṣṇu as gateway, not source [38:00 – 40:00]

- From the perspective of this world, the prayers went to Viṣṇu and then an _avatāra_ came, so people think Viṣṇu is the source and Kṛṣṇa His incarnation — true from a limited perspective.
- Rather, Viṣṇu is a gateway: prayers go up through Him, and from among the many _avatāras_ in the spiritual world the one suited to the required pastime descends.
- In the perspective of complete reality, beyond Viṣṇu is Kṛṣṇa, eternally manifesting sixty-four qualities, whereas Viṣṇu forms manifest sixty.

#### Kṛṣṇa's four special sweetnesses [38:00 – 40:00]

- **Rūpa-mādhurya** — the sweetness of His threefold-bending form, not present in any other incarnation.
- **Līlā-mādhurya** — very intimate pastimes in _mādhurya-rasa_, _sakhya-rasa_ and others, not so present in other forms.
- **Veṇu-mādhurya** — with His flute He conquers the hearts of His devotees and calls them.
- The sweetness of His devotees — so great that even Lakṣmī, the goddess of fortune, aspires for what the _gopīs_, simple cowherd girls, receive, and wishes to enter the _rāsa-līlā_.

#### Not minimising Viṣṇu [40:00 – 42:00]

- Being attracted to Kṛṣṇa does not mean looking down on Viṣṇu: Viṣṇu is exalted, and worshipping Him also takes one back to the spiritual world. Otherwise we would unnecessarily deride Viṣṇu's devotees.
- From the point of view of _rasa_ there is a difference, and we should recognise it.
- Prabhupāda's purport: some say all gods are the same and any may be worshipped, but Kṛṣṇa makes clear that everything else manifests only a spark of His splendour; worshipping the spark and worshipping the whole are not the same.
- Prabhupāda also quotes that even the formidable power of the sun comes from a part of Kṛṣṇa's energy — therefore Kṛṣṇa is to be worshipped.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Detailed knowledge of every opulence is unnecessary: one fragment of Kṛṣṇa — the Supersoul — pervades the whole universe, which shows that Kṛṣṇa is the source of all, and that worshipping the whole is not the same as worshipping a spark. [28:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.1 [42:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.1 -->

**Context.** Having heard that Kṛṣṇa pervades all existence, Arjuna will ask how; the eleventh chapter mainly concerns the universal form (_virāṭ-rūpa_) `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Moha_ — illusion `48:00`.
- _Vigata_ — gone away `48:00`.

#### Overview of Chapter 11 [42:00 – 46:00]

- With fifty-five verses it is the third-longest chapter of the Gītā, after the eighteenth (seventy-eight) and the second (seventy-two).
- BG 11.1 – 11.4 — Arjuna's request to see the universal form.
- BG 11.5 – 11.8 — Kṛṣṇa's description of the universal form: what Arjuna will see is so extraordinary that he needs context, so description precedes vision.
- BG 11.9 – 11.14 — Sañjaya's description. Sañjaya appears in chapters one, two and eighteen, and only here in between: the narration steps back from the Kṛṣṇa–Arjuna conversation (itself reported by Sañjaya, and within the larger frame of Vaiśampāyana speaking to Janamejaya) to describe what is being seen.
- BG 11.15 – 11.31 — Arjuna's description, the most detailed account of the _virāṭ-rūpa_, which turns into a prayer and includes the _kāla-rūpa_; overwhelmed, Arjuna asks, "Who are You?"
- The following verses — Kṛṣṇa's instruction, whose essence is "become My instrument."
- BG 11.35 – 11.46 — Arjuna's prayers: apology, seeking forgiveness, and seeking the vision of the four-armed form.
- BG 11.47 – 11.55 — pure devotion alone grants all visions — the universal, four-armed and two-armed forms.
- Both chapters ten and eleven reveal God in matter: the tenth through His _vibhūtis_, the eleventh through the _virāṭ_; the eleventh culminates in pure devotion `46:00`.

#### Arjuna's acknowledgement [46:00 – 50:00]

- Out of great mercy Kṛṣṇa has spoken confidential spiritual knowledge — chapters nine and ten, which focus on Kṛṣṇa's glories leading to the glory of pure devotional service.
- "By these words my illusion has gone." Arjuna will say the same at the end of the Gītā (BG 18.73), when Kṛṣṇa asks whether he has heard attentively (BG 18.72).
- This shows that by the time of the _catuḥ-ślokī_ in chapter ten, Arjuna had accepted Kṛṣṇa's glorious position (as he stated in calling Him the Supreme Brahman) and was established in true understanding; his further questions are for the benefit of others.
- A teacher wants to know whether the student has understood; here the student tells the teacher, "Yes, my illusion is gone, and You have been very merciful to me."
- When Kṛṣṇa speaks out of kindness, that speech removes our illusion.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna confirms that Kṛṣṇa's merciful words have removed his illusion; from here his questions serve others, and the eleventh chapter will move from the cosmic vision of God to pure devotion as the means of seeing Him in every form. [42:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.2 [50:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.2 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says he has heard in detail about the creation and destruction of all beings, and about Kṛṣṇa's imperishable glories `50:00 – 52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kamala-patrākṣa_ — "O lotus-eyed one," an intimate form of address usually found in devotional prayers about the Lord's beauty `50:00`.
- _Avyaya_ — imperishable; the nature of Kṛṣṇa's glory `52:00`.

#### The lotus-eyed and the blazing-eyed [50:00]

- Arjuna has addressed Kṛṣṇa by many names; this one is surprising and sweet.
- Soon the _virāṭ-rūpa_ will appear with eyes so blazing one cannot look at them — the same God is supremely attractive in one manifestation and intolerably fearsome in another.
- The whole flow of the Gītā takes us towards an intimate understanding of God, and this address reveals Arjuna's intimate relationship with Kṛṣṇa.

#### What Arjuna has heard [52:00 – 54:00]

- Creation and destruction were discussed in chapters seven to nine:
  - BG 7.6 — everything comes from Kṛṣṇa, is sustained by Him and returns to Him.
  - BG 8.17 – 8.19 — the day and night of Brahmā; BG 8.20 — the spiritual world.
  - BG 9.6 – 9.9 — the cycle of creation and destruction, with Kṛṣṇa remaining its transcendental controller; BG 9.10 also.
- Arjuna is setting up a context for his request: the destructible material world on one side, and Kṛṣṇa's indestructible position and imperishable glories on the other.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna holds together Kṛṣṇa's intimate sweetness ("lotus-eyed one") and His imperishable cosmic glory; the Gītā moves us toward intimacy with the very God who controls creation and destruction. [50:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.3 [54:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:11.3 -->

**Context.** "As You have described Yourself, so You are" — and yet Arjuna desires to see Kṛṣṇa's divine form `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Puruṣottama_ — Supreme Person `54:00`.
- _Rūpam aiśvaram_ — the opulent form by which Kṛṣṇa pervades existence `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### "As You have spoken, so You are" [54:00]

- The teacher calls this "Bhagavad-gītā As It Is" in a nutshell: there is no higher reality beyond Kṛṣṇa; as He has told, so He actually is.
- In one sense this repeats Arjuna's earlier acceptance, in chapter ten, of everything Kṛṣṇa says.

#### From yoga-aiśvara to rūpa-aiśvara [54:00 – 56:00]

- Kṛṣṇa has manifested His intimate form; He has described the knowledge of how He pervades and controls existence. Arjuna now wants to see more.
- In the previous verse he called Him lotus-eyed — "You are my intimate Lord" — but he also knows His position as the Supreme Person.
- In BG 9.5 Kṛṣṇa spoke of His _yoga aiśvara_ — His opulence manifest through creation, by which we can know and connect with Him.
- Arjuna now asks to see the _rūpa aiśvara_ — the form by which Kṛṣṇa, with just one spark of Himself, pervades and sustains all existence: the _virāṭ-rūpa_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna fully accepts Kṛṣṇa as He describes Himself, and from that acceptance — not from doubt — he asks to see the form by which Kṛṣṇa pervades the universe. [54:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 11.4 [56:00 – 61:36]

<!-- vedabase:11.4 -->

**Context.** Arjuna phrases his bold request humbly: if Kṛṣṇa thinks it possible for him to see the form, then please reveal it `56:00 – 58:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yogeśvara_ — Lord of mystic power; Arjuna knows it is possible for Kṛṣṇa because He is _yogeśvara_ `56:00`.
- _Darśana_ — seeing, which happens only when the Lord reveals Himself `56:00`.

#### Humility begs and waits [58:00]

- The question is not whether Kṛṣṇa can show the form, but whether Arjuna can see it.
- Pride demands and complains: the proud think they already deserve something and, not getting it, demand "Why is it not coming?" and then complain.
- Humility begs and waits: "This is what I desire, but I am not qualified; whenever I become qualified, You can reveal it to me."

#### "If you think I am qualified" [58:00 – 60:00]

- In the early days of the movement, especially for Western devotees, Kṛṣṇa consciousness was a radical lifestyle change; there was euphoria in the 1970s that the whole world would soon chant Hare Kṛṣṇa — and the outreach brought remarkable results.
- There was also an expectation of becoming advanced very quickly. One devotee told Śrīla Prabhupāda he had strictly followed the four principles and chanted sixteen rounds for six full months and still had not had _darśana_ of Kṛṣṇa.
- Prabhupāda became grave, opened the Gītā to this verse and had him read it — twice — until he understood: "if You think I am qualified."
- Prabhupāda explained that our qualification is not decided by us but by Kṛṣṇa.

#### Desire without demand [60:00 – 61:36]

- Desiring advancement and _darśana_ is good; we would be grateful and ecstatic to receive it.
- But a devotee desires without demanding; service means we do not make demands.
- Because it is a personal relationship, desire is not wrong — only the demand is inappropriate from the point of view of _bhakti_.
- How Kṛṣṇa reveals His form will be discussed in the next class `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna asks to see the universal form only "if You think I am qualified" — the devotee's mood: desire Kṛṣṇa's reciprocation, but leave the judgement of qualification to Him, because humility begs and waits while pride demands and complains. [56:00 – 61:36]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

- No separate audience Q&A appears in the captions. The teacher raises and answers questions himself:
  - Does "among fish I am the shark" mean only the shark comes from Kṛṣṇa? No — its attracting power is His manifestation; He is the source and sustainer of all `10:00`.
  - Is comparing Vaikuṇṭha to an "ocean of LSD" an offensive material interpretation? No — a material interpretation is offensive only when it is reductive, reducing the spiritual to the material `16:00 – 18:00`.
  - Is Kṛṣṇa an incarnation of Viṣṇu or the source of Viṣṇu? From this world's limited perspective one may say the former; in complete reality Kṛṣṇa is the _avatārī_ and Viṣṇu the gateway through whom _avatāras_ descend `36:00 – 40:00`.
