## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 10.19 [00:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa agrees to tell Arjuna of His divine opulences. Before going through the list, the teacher addresses how to understand Kṛṣṇa speaking like this about Himself and what the chapter's approach is `00:00 – 10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vibhūti_ — _bhūti_ is existence and the prefix _vi_ means special: a special existence. Wood lying on the ground is a _bhūta_, an existing thing, but not a _vibhūti_; a table that is an artistic masterpiece, with intricate carving and polishing, draws attention. So _vibhūtis_ are those things in the material manifestation that have a special attractiveness `06:00`.
- _Vibhūti-yoga_ — the Sanskrit name of the chapter; _yoga_ means connection, so _vibhūti-yoga_ is when a _vibhūti_ connects us with Kṛṣṇa `08:00`.
- _Brahma-bhūta_ — spiritual existence; the platform on which a person is joyful and free from hankering and lamentation `06:00`.

#### Is this arrogance? [02:00 – 04:00]

- If a person said "I am the best of this, I am the best of that," we would call him arrogant. How do we understand Kṛṣṇa speaking like this?
- Look at the context: actions speak louder than words. Kṛṣṇa, out of love, is serving as Arjuna's chariot driver, and here He is simply answering Arjuna's question.
- He is giving everyone an opportunity to redirect attraction from the things of this world to the Lord, who is their source and whose attractiveness is manifest fragmentally in them.
- **Doctor's diagnosis:** when a doctor says a patient urgently needs surgery, he is not speaking out of ego; he is stating the reality the patient's condition requires. Likewise Kṛṣṇa speaks about Himself because our condition requires it.

#### The spark and the whole fire [04:00 – 06:00]

- One may doubt: "If I surrender to Kṛṣṇa, will He really make me happy? Should I not enjoy these things too?"
- The conclusion of the chapter (10.41): whatever is splendid is only a spark of Kṛṣṇa's splendour. We need not run in all directions for sparks of light when the whole fire is available — the whole can give far more than any spark.
- To the extent we are attracted to the things of this world without seeing their connection with God, we are diseased: our desires are misdirected from God to the world, and that is the cause of suffering.
- Redirecting desire is the whole process of healing, and redirection requires philosophical understanding.

#### When attraction becomes yoga [08:00]

- A magnificent building or an eloquent speaker attracts us; if we perceive the Kṛṣṇa connection, the attraction becomes _yoga_.
- A movie star may be extremely good-looking, and that beauty is a _vibhūti_; but if it does not remind people of Kṛṣṇa, it does not lead to _yoga_.
- Whatever attracts — power, strength, beauty, intelligence — reflects a spark of Kṛṣṇa's splendour. Perceiving the connection from the _vibhūti_ to Kṛṣṇa is what makes it _yoga_.

#### Different ways to approach spiritual reality [10:00 – 20:00]

1. **Vedānta — "not this, not this"** `10:00 – 12:00`
   - Vedānta has personalist and impersonalist interpretations, but it focuses on the radical difference between matter and spirit.
   - "Am I this body? This handsome form? No, no." By saying no, one checks matter and moves toward spirit; renunciation of the world is considered very important because matter is temporary, illusory and a cause of bondage.
   - The Gītā also distinguishes matter and spirit in the second chapter; it integrates various strands of Vedic thought.
2. **The way of sound** `12:00 – 14:00`
   - The Lord is manifest in sound — in the mantra. When we chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa _mahā-mantra_ or hear _kṛṣṇa-kathā_, the sound of the holy name connects us with Kṛṣṇa, who exists on the transcendental level.
   - Such sound vibrations raise us from the lower modes toward goodness and transcendence.
3. **Tantra — right-hand and left-hand** `14:00 – 18:00`
   - Right-hand: deity worship. The deity is made of matter, beautifully decorated and prayerfully worshipped; people become attracted. Yet the deity is Kṛṣṇa — matter is spiritualised by invoking the Lord.
   - So matter can be a means to spirit: we use our senses and body to serve, to cook for the deity, to dance for the deity. The purpose is to raise consciousness from the material to the spiritual level. This is _sāttvika_ worship.
   - Left-hand: witchcraft and black magic — for example, someone who wants to hurt another goes to a _tāntrika_. Some accounts may be exaggeration or fabrication, but there are people with such powers.
   - The teacher adds that sex can be a means of approaching God only when used within a _dhārmika_ framework, where the performance of _dharma_ gradually elevates; the idea that indulgence itself takes one to spirit is not supported in scripture `18:00`.
4. **The way of the opulences** `18:00 – 20:00`
   - Most people just become attracted to attractive things. In _vibhūti-yoga_ we look at the source of the attractiveness and move from that particular thing to Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa lists His opulences not out of ego but as a doctor states a diagnosis: our desires are misdirected to sparks of His splendour, and seeing each attractive thing's connection to Him — vibhūti-yoga — redirects desire to the whole fire. [02:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.20 [20:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.20 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa addresses Arjuna as Guḍākeśa and says He is the _ātmā_ in all beings, and the beginning, middle and end of all beings `20:00 – 28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Guḍākeśa_ — one of its meanings: _guḍa_ is jaggery, and the name indicates one who gives sweetness, like jaggery, even to Brahmā and Śiva `20:00`.
- _Ātmā_ — here the Supersoul, not the individual soul `22:00`.

#### The sweetness of Guḍākeśa [20:00 – 22:00]

- The love between the Lord and His devotee is so endearing that it attracts even great personalities like Brahmā and Śiva.
- Such reciprocation is possible when the devotee has devotion: the Lord always has love for the devotee, but the sincerity and intensity of the devotee's love from the heart makes loving pastimes possible.
- Because of the sincerity and intensity of Arjuna's devotion, Kṛṣṇa became his chariot driver. One who makes such a sweet manifestation of the Lord's love possible gives sweetness even to Brahmā and Śiva.

#### Which ātmā? [22:00]

- The individual soul is always present in one body only; one soul is in your body and another in mine — there is no sense in saying one soul sits in all bodies.
- So when Kṛṣṇa says He is the _ātmā_ in all, the word here refers to the Supersoul.

#### Beginning, middle and end [24:00 – 28:00]

- **Beginning:** at the very beginning there is only Mahā-Viṣṇu, the beginning of everything. Even for a specific life, the way male and female parts come together and a fully developed child eventually emerges and grows up into a person like you and me is extraordinary — possible only by God's arrangement `24:00`.
- **Middle:** the body has voluntary systems — if I sit crookedly and develop pain, that is my responsibility — and certain systems not under my control, which are essential for my capacity to act. Who maintains these? Ultimately Kṛṣṇa `26:00`.
- **End:** death. When death happens it is final, and that too indicates the power of God `26:00`.
- The teacher notes that the phrase has multiple meanings, and also connects it with aspects of the Lord such as Brahmā and Viṣṇu `26:00 – 28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is present in every heart as the Supersoul and stands behind the beginning, maintenance and end of each life — and the intensity of a devotee's love, like Arjuna's, draws Him into pastimes sweet even to Brahmā and Śiva. [20:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.21 [28:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.21 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa begins naming the most prominent member of each group — among the Ādityas He is Viṣṇu, and among the stars He is the moon `28:00 – 34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nakṣatra_ — in the Vedic conception, heavenly bodies in general; different from the modern astronomical "star" `36:00`.
- _Śaśī_ — the moon `34:00`.

#### Persons behind impersonal forces [30:00 – 32:00]

- What we see as impersonal forces — winds and their direction, for instance — are controlled by persons.
- **Billiards analogy:** on a billiards table the stick moves and the balls move; looking only at the table, one sees sticks moving balls, but outside the table there is an expert person holding the stick. Likewise demigods are in charge of different natural forces.
- Among the beings in charge of the winds, Kṛṣṇa names the most powerful `32:00`.

#### The one among the many [32:00 – 34:00]

- In one sentence: a _vibhūti_ is the one among the many that connects us with the One beyond the many.
- We may not know these names, but we have such names from our own context too — in India, for example, a cricketer may be called a "god" of cricket.
- But the spark is not the whole; we need to move from the spark to the whole by seeing the connection.

#### The moon among the stars [34:00 – 40:00]

- There is a difference of nomenclature: in modern science stars are sources of light and planets only reflect light, while _nakṣatra_ refers to heavenly bodies in general.
- In one place it is said the moon gives light, and Prabhupāda in some places also says the sun's light is reflected by the moon. Similarly the demigods reflect attractiveness that comes from Kṛṣṇa; their qualities are not independent `36:00`.
- **Kinds of fire and light:** there are seven kinds of fire with different natures; similarly there are different kinds of light and energy. At one level the moon reflects the sun's light, but the moon is not just a barren object — it is a higher planet, with Somadeva in charge, and it may give a different kind of light that we cannot perceive `38:00`.
- The simple point is based on observation: among the visible heavenly objects the most attractive is the moon — magnificent and beautiful, especially the full moon on a clear night. Its capacity to attract comes from Kṛṣṇa, so we move our attention from the moon to Kṛṣṇa `38:00 – 40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Behind impersonal forces stand persons, and behind them Kṛṣṇa; the moon's beauty on a clear night is a reflection of His attractiveness, so each "one among the many" should carry our attention to the One beyond the many. [30:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.22 [40:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.22 -->

**Context.** Among the Vedas Kṛṣṇa is the Sāma Veda, among the demigods Indra, among the senses the mind, and in living beings He is consciousness `40:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Manas_ — the mind, grouped together with the senses `42:00`.
- _Cetanā_ — consciousness, which comes from the _ātmā_ `44:00`.

#### The Vedas and Indra [40:00 – 42:00]

- The different Vedas serve specific purposes, with some overlap — the Ṛg Veda, for example, to invoke the demigods. This is a broad classification.
- The Sāma Veda is marked by glorification, with beautiful verses.
- Indra is the most powerful of the gods.

#### The mind among the senses [42:00 – 44:00]

- The mind is grouped with the senses; 15.7 also speaks of the senses including the mind, by which the living entity struggles in material existence.
- The five senses are centred around the mind, and the mind has a god-like power.
- **Heaven and hell:** as a poet put it, the mind can make a heaven of hell and a hell of heaven. The mind can completely distort our perception of the world — a scary kind of power. Understanding this, we see that even this power is a small fraction of God's power `44:00`.

#### Consciousness makes a person attractive [44:00]

- A person with a total poker face, or someone fast asleep, is not very attractive.
- What makes a person attractive is consciousness: the more alive, conscious, reciprocal and vibrant a person is, the more capacity they have to attract.
- Consciousness is the foundation of everything.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even the most intimate powers we have — the mind that can make heaven of hell, and the consciousness that makes a person attractive — are fragments of Kṛṣṇa's power, to be recognised as His. [42:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.23 [46:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.23 -->

**Context.** Among the eleven Rudras Kṛṣṇa is Śaṅkara, and He names the foremost of other groups `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### The only reference to Śiva [46:00 – 48:00]

- There are eleven Rudras, and among them is Śaṅkara.
- Apart from this one reference — and the eleventh chapter, where the demigods are seen in the universal form — there is no reference to Śiva in the whole _Bhagavad-gītā_.
- The one with the trident and a snake on his head is a person in the service of Kṛṣṇa. Here it is simply said that among them the most attractive is Śaṅkara, and his power comes from Kṛṣṇa; if we are attracted to him, we can be attracted to Kṛṣṇa `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Śaṅkara, the most attractive of the Rudras, is mentioned in the Gītā as a vibhūti whose power comes from Kṛṣṇa — a servant of Kṛṣṇa through whom attraction can lead to Kṛṣṇa. [46:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.24 [50:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.24 -->

**Context.** Among priests Kṛṣṇa is Bṛhaspati, among generals Skanda, and among bodies of water the ocean `50:00`.

#### Three prominent ones [50:00]

- **Bṛhaspati** — highly respected among the priests.
- **Skanda** — Kārtikeya, the son of Śiva and Pārvatī and commander of the demigods. There are many commanders, but he led the army of the gods to victory against the demons, so he is very powerful.
- **The ocean** — the biggest body of water; lakes and rivers become small in front of the vastness of the ocean.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
In each field — priesthood, military command, bodies of water — the one whose greatness overshadows the rest is a pointer to Kṛṣṇa's greatness. [50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.25 [50:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.25 -->

**Context.** Among the great sages Kṛṣṇa is Bhṛgu, among vibrations the one transcendental syllable, and among sacrifices the chanting of the holy names `50:00 – 54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ṛṣi_ — sages who are not preserving a particular dynasty but are focused on attaining transcendence and the Absolute Truth `52:00`.
- _Oṁ_ — considered very special and powerful; a manifestation of Kṛṣṇa `52:00`.
- _Japa-yajña_ — the sacrifice of chanting `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### Sacrifice culminates in chanting [52:00 – 54:00]

- This develops the thought of the third and fourth chapters: a specific activity, the fire sacrifice, gives the principle of sacrifice for the Lord's sake.
- Once that principle is established, Kṛṣṇa explains various kinds of sacrifice: we can offer honey, ghee and so on — but the most difficult thing to sacrifice is our consciousness.
- This is a very significant verse for the glory of the holy name: it substantiates directly from the Gītā itself that chanting, and _saṅkīrtana_, is also a _yajña_ `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Among all sacrifices Kṛṣṇa names chanting as Himself — the Gītā's own evidence that japa and saṅkīrtana are yajña, offering not just ghee or honey but our very consciousness. [52:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.26 [54:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.26 -->

**Context.** Among trees Kṛṣṇa is the _aśvattha_, and among perfected beings the sage Kapila; the teacher also mentions a personality who once fought with Arjuna `54:00 – 60:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Aśvattha_ — a tree like the banyan, very difficult to break down because it has so many branches `54:00`.
- _Siddhas_ — perfected beings `56:00`.

#### Kapila among the perfected [56:00 – 60:00]

- Among the perfected beings Kapila-deva is special and extremely powerful; his teachings are in the Third Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_ `56:00`.
- After giving guidance to his mother, Kapila leaves and goes to a particular place, performing austerity as an example for the world; he is fully realised `58:00`.
- He is an incarnation, known as the son of Devahūti. In the West, a teaching of a son is usually identified by the father's name; here the mother is named. The son instructing the mother is extraordinary, and this particular set of teachings is given to her `58:00`.
- His teaching differs from atheistic Sāṅkhya, a material analysis that does not speak much about God: the Sāṅkhya of the _Bhāgavatam_ is devotional and culminates in pure devotion. This Kapila is different from the other Kapila `58:00 – 60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kapila, the son who taught his mother Devahūti a Sāṅkhya that culminates in pure devotion, is Kṛṣṇa's opulence among perfected beings — perfection is shown in realisation that leads to devotion. [56:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
