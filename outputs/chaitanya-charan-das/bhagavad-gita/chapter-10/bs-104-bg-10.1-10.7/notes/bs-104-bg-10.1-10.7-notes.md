## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 10.1 [06:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.1 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa opens the chapter on a very intimate, personal note: He will speak again, because Arjuna is dear to Him and for Arjuna's benefit `06:00 – 08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Anxious to be Kṛṣṇa conscious_ — Prabhupāda's phrase in the purport: discourses in the society of devotees can take place only among those really eager to be in Kṛṣṇa consciousness `10:00`.

#### Not a professor, but a friend [06:00 – 08:00]

- A college professor may teach a subject just as a job, without much care for the students.
- Kṛṣṇa is not speaking the Gītā like that: He speaks because "I love you and I want your benefit."
- The more one hears about the Supreme, the more one becomes fixed in devotional service `08:00`.

#### Where do we put our anxiety? [10:00]

- We usually reserve anxiety for material things: Will I get a good job? A good spouse? Will my health be fine?
- How often are we anxious about being Kṛṣṇa conscious — about chanting our rounds or hearing the holy name?
- Anxiety for Kṛṣṇa consciousness can be at various levels:
  - **Fear** — "If I am not Kṛṣṇa conscious, I will continue in the cycle of birth and death."
  - **Desire** — "Hearing about Kṛṣṇa gives so much happiness; I want that."
  - **Duty** — "I have taken initiation; I have given my life to this."
  - **Love** — one does not want to forget the Beloved and is constantly anxious to remember Him.
- Kṛṣṇa is not an abstract object of thought but the object of our service; when we love someone we constantly think, "How can I please my Beloved?" `10:00 – 12:00`

#### Mutual enlightenment, upliftment and joy [12:00]

- Others can also participate in such discussions, but the Kṛṣṇa consciousness invoked will not be very deep.
- Among anxious devotees there is mutual enlightenment, mutual upliftment and mutual joy — discussed later in this chapter — of a different scale and quality from an academic discussion of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa speaks not as a detached professor but as a loving friend; the discussion of His glories becomes deep only among devotees who are genuinely anxious to be Kṛṣṇa conscious — anxious with the anxiety we usually reserve for material things. [06:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.2 [14:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.2 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa reveals what He is going to speak about: no one — not even the demigods or great sages — knows His origin, because He is the cause of all causes `14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- One word in this verse describes, in a single term, how Kṛṣṇa is the source in every respect — a verse where Kṛṣṇa's glory is spoken of through multiple qualities in one word `14:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa is the original person [16:00]

- The verse demonstrates that Kṛṣṇa is the original person.
- Even those considered powerful and worshipped in this world are not comparable with Kṛṣṇa; Prabhupāda quotes the _Bhāgavatam_ on the same point.

#### Conceptions of God that stop at power [18:00]

- We can compare this with the _Paramātmā_ and _Brahman_ conceptions; the primary conceptions of God in the world's traditions are of some being that controls the world, some organising power, or a pervading spirit.
- In the Old Testament, God reveals Himself through extraordinary displays of power — such as the bush that burns and is never consumed — but not in person.
- Even in ordinary speech, an unexplained disaster is "an act of God": God is perceived primarily as power.
- To know Kṛṣṇa as the person behind all this, we need His mercy, manifesting through His devotees and through the process of _bhakti-yoga_ `20:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even the demigods and sages do not fully know Kṛṣṇa, because He is the source of everything; most conceptions of God stop at power, and the person behind that power is known only by His mercy through His devotees and bhakti. [14:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.3 [20:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.3 -->

**Context.** One who knows Kṛṣṇa as unborn, beginningless and the Supreme Lord of all the worlds is undeluded and freed from all sinful reactions `20:00 – 22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Asammūḍha_ — undeluded; one free from delusion `22:00`.
- _Impurity_ — essentially, misdirection toward worldly objects. Sinful thoughts are impure, but even thoughts that are not sinful are impure if they are misdirected `22:00`.

#### Beyond time and in control of everyone [22:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's position: He is beyond time and the controller of everyone.
- **Death row:** all people in this world are under a death sentence; we don't know when we will die, but we know we will.
- Against this backdrop Kṛṣṇa speaks of knowing Him, who is beyond time — and that knowing is not just theoretical but transformational.

#### Hearing about Kṛṣṇa itself purifies [22:00 – 24:00]

- Coming to know Kṛṣṇa purifies, and even just hearing about Him is purifying.
- Prabhupāda contextualises "unborn" with the _Bhāgavatam_'s passage on Kṛṣṇa's birth `24:00`.

#### Waking up, not improving the dream [26:00]

- In material existence the primary goal should not be to create better situations; it is to wake up, because real life is after we wake up.
- The very idea that something separated from Kṛṣṇa is good is itself _māyā_; the _Bhāgavatam_ makes the same point.
- _Māyā_ means thinking something is valuable when it is not connected with Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowing Kṛṣṇa as unborn and supreme is transformational knowledge: it frees us from delusion — the idea that anything apart from Him is valuable — and purifies a heart whose impurity is simply misdirection toward the world. [22:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.4 – 10.5 [28:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.4 -->

<!-- vedabase:10.5 -->

**Context.** Having said that no one knows Him and that those who do are freed, Kṛṣṇa now elaborates how He is the origin of everything — even the abstract qualities seen in living beings, which He lists across these two verses taken together `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Buddhi_ (intelligence) — the power to analyse things in their proper perspective: their interactions and priorities, e.g. "when I have to do certain things, this is more important" `30:00`.
- _Jñāna_ (knowledge) — understanding what is spirit and what is matter; understanding things in their constitution or composition `30:00`.
- _Ahiṁsā_ (non-violence) — not doing anything that puts others in misery or confusion `38:00`.

#### Recap of the flow so far [28:00]

- 10.1: "I tell you this supreme knowledge."
- 10.2: even the demigods and sages don't know Me.
- 10.3: those who come to know Me become free.
- Now Kṛṣṇa describes how He is the origin of everything in this world — not only things (living and non-living) at the physical level, but also qualities and concepts at the subtle level.

#### Conceptual spectacles [32:00]

- Even without physical spectacles we all wear conceptual spectacles — the conditioning we bring into this world and acquire while living in it.
- If I wear pink glasses I see everything pink; if my glasses are tinted, I see everything tinted.

#### Truthfulness: pleasing, but strong when needed [32:00 – 34:00]

- Truth should be spoken for others' benefit, and should not be misrepresented.
- One should speak what is pleasing to others, and if it is necessary to speak what is not pleasing, be ready to do that too.
- Prabhupāda spoke strongly when it was necessary, yet also tried to use soft words.
- The real point is to speak words that take a person toward the Absolute Truth.

#### Dualities come from Kṛṣṇa's arrangement [36:00]

- Even fear and fearlessness come from Kṛṣṇa — nothing exists outside His jurisdiction.
- He has made the overall arrangement within which the dualities function, and what comes to us specifically comes because of our particular karmic reactions.
- Kṛṣṇa wants to give us a supreme stability beyond dualities, which we reach when we take shelter of Him.

#### Misuse of thinking power [36:00 – 38:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that one should not spend one's time pondering over earning money — that is the misuse of thinking power.
- Earning is an important part of life, and the necessary thought and time should go into a livelihood; but being obsessed with it is a characteristic of demoniac people.
- It should not be the default thought; the prime necessity of life is to revive our connection with Kṛṣṇa.

#### The best non-violence [38:00]

- Misery is psychological and physical; confusion is intellectual — misleading people is also a form of violence.
- Prabhupāda explains that the best form of non-violence is to share Kṛṣṇa consciousness: activities for the future spiritual happiness of people in general are called non-violence.
- Thus whatever we find, good or bad, has its origin in Kṛṣṇa `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every quality — intelligence, knowledge, truthfulness, even fear and fearlessness — comes from Kṛṣṇa; using these gifts rightly means seeing through Kṛṣṇa's arrangement, not obsessing over money, and offering the highest non-violence: sharing Kṛṣṇa consciousness. [28:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.6 [40:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.6 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains that the great sages, the progenitors before them and the Manus come from His mind, and all the living beings of the universe descend from them `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### The genealogy of the universe [42:00]

- The teacher recounts that Rudra's progeny could not help populate the universe, so Rudra was asked to go and perform austerity instead.
- From the seven great sages and the Manus come the living beings of the universe; the teacher notes that dynasties such as the one in which Lord Rāma appeared are also traced this way.

#### Going back to the real roots [42:00 – 44:00]

- Kṛṣṇa explains the genealogy from a transcendental perspective of origins.
- People may proudly say, "I know my ancestors twenty generations back; my ancestor was a king of South India."
- "Going back to the roots" is valuable, but our original roots are not material — they are spiritual. Going back all the way leads to Kṛṣṇa, who lives at the spiritual level of reality.
- What Kṛṣṇa stated abstractly ("I am the source of everything") He now explains in more detail; He is giving the principle of _vibhūti-tattva_ `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every genealogy in the universe — sages, Manus and dynasties — traces back to Kṛṣṇa; our real roots are not material but spiritual, so the deepest "return to our roots" is returning to Him. [42:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.7 [46:00 – 57:24]

<!-- vedabase:10.7 -->

**Context.** One who is factually convinced of Kṛṣṇa's opulence and mystic power engages in unalloyed devotional service, without doubt `46:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vibhūti_ — Kṛṣṇa's power and glory, so great that even the demigods and great sages do not know Him `48:00`.
- _Yoga_ — used in both its meanings `46:00 – 48:00`:
  - Kṛṣṇa revealing His glory in a way that facilitates our connection with Him;
  - our spiritual practice striving for that connection — meditation, contemplation, action or devotional service (_jñāna-_, _dhyāna-_, _karma-_ or _bhakti-yoga_).
- _Unalloyed (without deviation)_ — fixing oneself wholeheartedly without considering any alternative `48:00`.

#### Knowledge that fixes the mind [48:00 – 50:00]

- Kṛṣṇa reveals His glory so that our attraction to Him increases.
- As we understand more and more about Him, the mind becomes fixed, and nothing distracts us from Kṛṣṇa.

#### Beyond freedom from sin [50:00]

- Earlier Kṛṣṇa said that one who knows His position is freed from sins; here He says something more.
- Freedom from sin is not the perfection of life; the perfection is spontaneous devotion to Kṛṣṇa without any doubts.

#### The personal source is the summit [52:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport: perfection is knowledge of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- _Māyāvādīs_ think the personal idea is not the summit — a ladder to the impersonal Brahman. Kṛṣṇa says no: know Me in this way, as the transcendental personal source of everything and everyone, and fix the mind on Me.
- The greatness of God is elaborated in this chapter and will be seen again in the eleventh `54:00`.

#### God on the menu card [54:00]

- Understanding Kṛṣṇa is the best thing for us intellectually, emotionally and spiritually.
- Many people know little about God; they go to a temple, or hold some concept of God, or are simply caught up in material things — but at least God is one alternative on their "menu card": "Let me try God."
- Kṛṣṇa consciousness is much more than trying God out.

#### Jñāna that increases bhakti [56:00]

- This knowledge is required to increase one's interest in the loving devotional service of the Lord.
- Usually, _jñāna_ that does not lead to _bhakti_ refers to impersonal knowledge; but knowledge about Kṛṣṇa increases _bhakti_, because understanding how great and wonderful He is naturally attracts us to Him.
- So one should not neglect this knowledge.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowing Kṛṣṇa's opulence is not dry speculation: it fixes the mind on Him without alternatives, and its perfection is not just freedom from sin but spontaneous, undoubting devotion — jñāna about Kṛṣṇa increases bhakti. [46:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
