## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 10.27 [02:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.27 -->

**Context.** Among horses Kṛṣṇa is the horse born from the churning of the milk ocean, among lordly elephants Airāvata, and among human beings the king `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Aśva_ — horse; the horse sacrifice (_aśvamedha_) was performed in the past `02:00`.
- _Gajendra_ — lordly elephants, the kings of elephants. It can be a proper noun — the specific elephant Gajendra caught by a crocodile in the Eighth Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_ — or a common noun, a generic describer of a kind of animal, as here `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Narādhipa_ — the ruler, the king among human beings `04:00`.

#### The extraordinary horse and Airāvata [02:00 – 04:00]

- Among many wonderful horses, the greatest is the horse that appeared during the churning of the milk ocean; so extraordinary and captivating that Indra took him.
- Some devotees may think the cow is the best of animals — and it is, in the sense of being dear to Kṛṣṇa — but the most attractive and beautiful animal is the horse: it can fight in war, ride majestically and carry people.
- Airāvata, Indra's carrier, is the greatest among the kingly elephants; very powerful, he strikes terror in Indra's opponents. As Indra is king of the gods, Airāvata is king among elephants.

#### The king's power — representative, not substitute [04:00 – 06:00]

- The king has enormous power compared with others; that power attracts everyone and is a manifestation of Kṛṣṇa's power. Whether it is used for good or bad is a different point.
- Not all kings are godly. In the Fourth Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_, Vena abused his power, telling the brāhmaṇas to stop worshipping Viṣṇu and worship him instead, since he represented the Supreme Lord — a distorted argument.
- The king is worthy of worship _because_ he represents God; he does not substitute for God. He is meant to guide people toward God. A king who replaces God needs to be defeated and even destroyed — which the brāhmaṇas did by cursing `06:00`.

#### Every opulence comes from Kṛṣṇa, but not every opulence leads to Him [06:00 – 10:00]

- _Vibhūtis_ can be divine or demoniac. A king may be a tyrant ruthlessly killing people: European history records two great population shocks — the Black Plague and the attack of Genghis Khan, who left a trail of corpses wherever he went.
- That power is a _vibhūti_ given by God according to the person's past karma, but the person chose to abuse it, bringing harm to others and to himself in this or the next life.
- **Two singers:** a devotee sings _kīrtana_ nicely; a mundane musician sings sexual songs melodiously. The musical talent in both is Kṛṣṇa's opulence, but the devotee's singing reminds us of Kṛṣṇa and brings us closer, while the other arouses material emotions that take us away from the spiritual reality of life `08:00`.
- **Two speakers:** Hitler would spend hours before a mirror studying each facial expression and modulation of his tone; by his power of speech in national broadcasts he perverted the thoughts of practically an entire nation. That power of speech was Kṛṣṇa's opulence, misused `10:00`.

#### We choose what attracts us [10:00 – 12:00]

- We are conscious beings, so we must be attracted to something — but our free will decides which opulences we become attracted to.
- Attraction grows with exposure: hearing a materialist's well-sung music repeatedly increases attraction to it; hearing devotional music sung by a devotee increases attraction to that.
- Likewise with literature: well-written books by materialists impress us, while devotee authors who make profound points eloquently are also Kṛṣṇa's opulence and attract us toward Him.
- Prabhupāda explains in the purport how the king should use his power for virtue and to take people toward the ultimate good `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every power — a king's authority, a singer's voice, an orator's eloquence — comes from Kṛṣṇa, but not all of it leads to Him; since we must be attracted, we should deliberately expose ourselves to the opulences that draw us toward Kṛṣṇa. [04:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.28 [12:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.28 -->

**Context.** Among weapons Kṛṣṇa is the thunderbolt, among cows the desire-fulfilling cow, among causes of procreation Kandarpa, and among serpents Vāsuki `12:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Āyudha_ — weapons; _vajra_ — the thunderbolt, Indra's weapon `12:00`.
- Desire-fulfilling cows that give as much milk as one desires `12:00`.
- _Kandarpa_ — the god of love; _prajana_ — begetting of offspring, so Kṛṣṇa is the attraction that leads to new life `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### The thunderbolt [12:00]

- Indra's thunderbolt could reduce a whole mountain to powder with one blow — what nuclear weapon can do that?
- What terrified Indra in fighting Hiraṇyakaśipu was that this thunderbolt could not even scratch Hiraṇyakaśipu's body, such was the blessing he had obtained from Brahmā by austerities.

#### Desire-fulfilling cows and the joy of service [12:00 – 14:00]

- Vṛndāvana also has desire-fulfilling trees, yet the residents hardly use them. When Rādhārāṇī comes to cook for Kṛṣṇa, she does not ask a desire tree for a sweet to offer Him — the joy of love is in service, in striving to cook the best food for her beloved.
- In Vṛndāvana the cows are not just giving milk; they are offering their love to Kṛṣṇa. In the spiritual world the essential point is the reciprocation of love.
- Prabhupāda would ask whether human beings can make a machine that takes grass in one end and gives milk out the other — the cow does exactly that, yet she is no machine but a sensitive, caring, warm living being.

#### Kandarpa and the sanctity of new life [14:00 – 18:00]

- Kandarpa's flower arrows look like flowers from the front but, once inside the heart, burn and wound like arrows.
- _Kāma_ generally induces lust and we must be careful, but not all of it is bad: when attraction within a proper _dhārmika_ marriage leads to the begetting of children, that is the act by which God sends new life into the world.
- Children are to be seen as gifts of God. A _jñānī_ perspective that dissects everything for detachment may help renunciation in some cases, but otherwise can make us hard-hearted: if parents see children merely as products of lust, how can they give the love and care children need? That perspective is also incomplete, because it misses Kṛṣṇa's hand `16:00`.
- When a devotee told Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu that his wife was expecting, Mahāprabhu was very happy, blessed the child to be a great Vaiṣṇava and gave him a name `18:00`.

#### Vāsuki [18:00]

- Vāsuki, a very powerful snake, served as the rope when the milk ocean was churned. An ordinary rope would break with everyone pulling, but he did not — in fact, under the mode of ignorance he went peacefully to sleep while everyone pulled on his body.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even attraction between man and woman, when it leads to new life within dharma, is Kṛṣṇa's opulence; children are His gifts, and in His abode even abundance exists for the joy of loving service. [12:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.29 [18:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.29 -->

**Context.** Among the Nāgas Kṛṣṇa is Ananta, among aquatics Varuṇa, among the ancestors Aryamā, and among dispensers of law Yama `18:00 – 22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nāga_ — a multi-headed serpent, distinct from ordinary single-headed snakes; Kāliya had many heads, and Kṛṣṇa danced on them `18:00 – 20:00`.
- _Pitṛloka_ — the abode where ancestors who lived lives of average piety are expected to go `20:00 – 22:00`.

#### Ananta and Varuṇa [20:00]

- Among the multi-headed serpents the most celebrated is Ananta; seeing his power, we can remember Kṛṣṇa.
- Varuṇa controls the waters, with his abode deep inside the water. In the Tenth Canto (Chapter 28), a Mahārāja who went into the water was arrested by Varuṇa's servants and taken to Varuṇa's abode deep inside the water.

#### Aryamā and the progressive Vedic idea of afterlife [20:00 – 22:00]

- Those who worship the forefathers go to the forefathers (9.25).
- Not everyone's ancestors go to Pitṛloka regardless of karma. In Vedic culture: average piety leads to Pitṛloka, above-average piety to Svargaloka, and sin can lead to Naraka. Vedic culture offered a gradual, progressive way of living that led to some elevation.
- The predominating deity in charge of Pitṛloka is Aryamā, and his power represents Kṛṣṇa's power.

#### Yamarāja [22:00]

- People want to break free of rules; if powerful, they may even break them and get away with it in this world.
- But they cannot escape Yamarāja, the cosmic system of justice: those who refuse to be regulated by God's rules or the state's are ultimately regulated by his, and endure the results of their karma.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Whoever escapes the rules of God and state in this world is still regulated by Yamarāja's cosmic justice; the controllers of waters, ancestors and law all display Kṛṣṇa's power to govern. [20:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.30 [22:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.30 -->

**Context.** Among the Daityas Kṛṣṇa is Prahlāda, among subduers time, among beasts the lion, and among birds Garuḍa `22:00 – 30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Daitya_ — sons of Diti; the sons of Aditi are the Ādityas (the gods). Kaśyapa had two major wives, Aditi and Diti; their sons are like step-brothers with opposite natures, and hence in conflict `22:00 – 24:00`.
- _Mṛgendra_ — the lion, king of the animals `26:00`.
- _Vainateya_ — Garuḍa, son of Vinatā `26:00`.

#### Prahlāda [24:00]

- Daityas are normally inimical to Viṣṇu, but Prahlāda is very special. He may not have seemed physically powerful, but his spiritual power was so great that Hiraṇyakaśipu — who had the greatest physical power in the universe and terrorised even the gods — could do nothing against him.

#### Time subdues everyone [24:00 – 26:00]

- People may refuse to be subdued by anyone, but time subdues all: it makes shiny faces shrivelled, the strong weak, the healthy sickly, the active passive, and the living dead.
- Time will conquer even the greatest conqueror.

#### The lion [26:00]

- The lion has extraordinary majesty and ferocity.
- At a zoo, when people were poking sticks at a caged lion, Prabhupāda told devotees not to do that, recalling this verse: the lion is meant to be king and deserves respect; caged in an unnatural, miserable situation, it should not be insulted further.

#### Garuḍa wins the nectar [26:00 – 30:00]

- The _Mahābhārata_ tells how the mother of the snakes cheated Vinatā in a bet, so that Vinatā and her son Garuḍa became slaves of the snakes.
- Asked what they wanted to free them, the snakes demanded the nectar of the gods. Garuḍa flew to where it was stored; when the guards tried to stop him, the wind of his wings blew them away.
- Indra struck with his thunderbolt. Garuḍa said it could do nothing to him, but out of respect for the king of the gods he let it cut part of one feather. Shocked, Indra diplomatically offered friendship — if you cannot defeat someone, befriend him.
- Indra learned that Garuḍa only had to deliver the nectar, not let the snakes drink it. Garuḍa left it for them; as they came to take it, he beat his wings and the wind temporarily blinded them, and Indra took the nectar back.
- So Garuḍa freed his mother from slavery; there are also many stories of him competently serving Lord Viṣṇu in His pastimes `30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Spiritual power like Prahlāda's outlasts the greatest physical might, and time subdues every conqueror; even a caged lion deserves respect because its majesty is Kṛṣṇa's. [24:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pavana_ — the wind, a purifier `30:00`.
- Among those who carry weapons, Rāma here can refer to Paraśurāma or to Lord Rāmacandra `30:00`.

#### Shark and Gaṅgā [30:00 – 32:00]

When Bali Mahārāja's three steps were offered, Vāmana's third step expanded to the top of the universe, broke the universal covering, and the water that came out became the Gaṅgā `32:00`.

A devotee uses sights like the Gaṅgā as stimuli for devotional remembrance — the river that flowed from Vāmana's foot at once recalls Kṛṣṇa's opulence. [30:00 – 32:00]

### Bhagavad-gita 10.32 [32:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.32 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa is the beginning, middle and end of all creations; among sciences He is spiritual knowledge; and among logicians He is the conclusive truth `32:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sarga_ — creations; here non-living, insentient things, whereas in 10.20 _bhūta_ referred to living beings `32:00 – 34:00`.
- _Adhyātma-vidyā_ — spiritual knowledge `34:00`.
- _Jalpa_ — attacking the person (ad hominem) instead of the argument; considered malicious `34:00`.
- _Vitaṇḍā_ — destructive argument: "I don't care whether I am right; I want to prove you wrong" `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Vāda_ — the conclusive argument for which there is no comeback `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Spiritual knowledge [34:00]

- Material knowledge helps us in the material world to some extent but cannot get us out of it; spiritual knowledge can.
- Not only that — spiritual knowledge can grant the best possible fulfilment even within the material world.

#### Kinds of argument [34:00 – 38:00]

- Proving oneself right through mutual discussion can be a process of discovery; _vitaṇḍā_ only aims to put the other down, especially where there is animosity.
- Normally there is argument, counter-argument, counter-counter-argument, and so on — the counting can stop but the arguments never will. At the logical level there is no end.
- Logic is a weapon, and some are more skilled with it: an atheist skilled in logic may defeat a theist who is not — not because theism is intellectually weaker, but because the representatives are unequally matched.
- The point here is not the strengths of schools of thought but kinds of argument: the decisive, knockout argument with no comeback represents Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Endless counter-argument never ends at the logical level; the conclusive truth that settles a question is Kṛṣṇa's opulence, and spiritual knowledge is the one knowledge that can take us beyond the material world. [34:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.33 [38:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.33 -->

**Context.** Among letters Kṛṣṇa is the letter A, among compounds the dual compound, He is inexhaustible time, and among creators Brahmā `38:00 – 42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Akṣara_ — letters; the alphabet begins with A, and according to some analyses the A sound is present in all Sanskrit syllables `38:00`.
- _Samāsa_ — compound words, e.g. "devotee children" or "devotee scientist" `38:00 – 40:00`.
- _Dvandva_ — a compound in which both parts have equal weight, as in "Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa" (Prabhupāda's example) `40:00`.

#### A devotional vision of language [38:00]

- We usually think of language as technical, not about Kṛṣṇa. Jīva Gosvāmī rewrote a whole grammar using the names of Kṛṣṇa and Viṣṇu, to help us see the spiritual even in the grammatical. The Gītā tells us the same in principle.

#### The dual compound and eloquence [40:00]

- In some compounds the first word is more important, in others the second; the most special are those where both are equally important.
- In a sentence there should not be one word more than necessary, in a paragraph not one sentence more, in an essay not one paragraph more — eloquence is truth spoken concisely. Using words so that each has potency is a sign of expertise in language.

#### Inexhaustible time and Brahmā [42:00]

- Time exhausts everyone — the young become old, the energetic become energyless — but time itself is never exhausted; it keeps moving forward inexorably.
- A builder may boast of 500 or 1000 buildings; a king who built many temples and palaces may go down in history. But only Brahmā can say he built the whole universe, and he gets that power from Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa can be seen even in language — in the first letter and in compounds where both words carry equal weight — and in time that exhausts all yet is never exhausted. [38:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.34 [42:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.34 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa is all-devouring death and the generating principle of all that is yet to be; and among women He names a list of qualities `42:00 – 46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nārī_ — feminine; can refer to women, or to feminine nouns in Sanskrit, among which these qualities are special `46:00`.
- _Vāk_ — fine speech; also often used to refer to the Sanskrit language, said to be capable of expressing every thought the human mind can come up with `46:00`.

#### Death, the thorough thief [42:00 – 44:00]

- Some thieves take money, some take the credit card and bank balance too, some even take the clothes.
- Death takes not only our possessions and our ID card but our identification itself — "I am an engineer, a doctor" — leaving only the conceptions, desires and karma we have developed. Death does the most thorough job, and that all-devouring capacity represents Kṛṣṇa.

#### The generating principle [44:00 – 46:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is not only the destroyer but also the creator: the arrival of new life in any species is extraordinary and ultimately God's hand.
- In the Sixth Canto, the Citraketu pastime explains that sometimes the husband is potent and the wife fertile, yet no children are born; doctors may say everything is biologically fine. We are not the ultimate doers — life needs God's sanction.

#### Feminine qualities [46:00]

- The list: fame, fortune (beauty), fine speech, memory, intelligence, firmness and patience.
- Different ācāryas give different explanations: at one level, when women have these qualities that is a manifestation of Kṛṣṇa; another explanation is that these are special feminine nouns.
- Without going into technicalities, the point is to see whatever is an opulence as Kṛṣṇa's and so be attracted to Him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is both the death that strips away everything but our desires and karma and the hidden sanction behind every birth — we are not the ultimate doers in either. [42:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

<!-- vedabase:10.35 -->

**Context.** Among hymns Kṛṣṇa is the Bṛhat-sāma, among meters Gāyatrī, among months Mārgaśīrṣa, and among seasons flower-bearing spring `48:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bṛhat-sāma_ — a special, very melodious song in the Sāma Veda used for praising and glorifying `48:00`.
- _Chandas_ — Vedic meters; Gāyatrī is one special meter. The word is sometimes said to refer to the Gāyatrī mantra chanted by brāhmaṇa-initiated devotees — the word can have many meanings `48:00`.
- _Kusumākara_ — that which brings forth flowers: spring, as the ocean is called the maker of pearls `50:00`.

#### Nature's bounty and beauty [48:00 – 50:00]

- Different crops grow in different seasons; from one agricultural understanding, Mārgaśīrṣa (roughly November–December) is when crops grow bountifully in a proper climate. The month when nature gives her bounty lavishly is Kṛṣṇa's manifestation.
- In spring nature becomes beautiful with varieties of flowers, as if a painter had decorated all of nature with a magnificent hand; that luxuriant beauty is Kṛṣṇa's opulence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Melodious hymns, the sacred meter, the month of abundant harvest and the beauty of spring are all ways nature and scripture display Kṛṣṇa's opulence. [48:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.36 [50:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.36 -->

**Context.** Of cheats Kṛṣṇa is gambling, of the splendid the splendour; He is victory, adventure and the strength of the strong `50:00 – 58:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Chala_ — cheating `50:00`.
- _Vyavasāya_ — translated by Prabhupāda as enterprise or adventure `54:00`.

#### Why gambling? [50:00 – 54:00]

- Normally no one wants to be cheated and resists it. Gambling is extraordinary cunning: a person is so deluded that he voluntarily participates in being fleeced.
- Everyone knows one is more likely to lose than gain; if gamblers were meant to gain, gambling houses would go bankrupt. They run expenses, employees and profits because the system is designed so that gamblers lose more than they win.
- The point is not that cheating or gambling is good. When people ask, "Yudhiṣṭhira gambled, so why can't we?", the lesson of the _Mahābhārata_ is the exact opposite: if even a king as virtuous as Yudhiṣṭhira gets into trouble by gambling, we will get into far greater trouble. The _Mahābhārata_ sometimes uses exalted characters to teach extreme lessons unforgettably.
- Cheating is accepted as a fact of nature; within it, some forms are extraordinary.

#### Splendour and victory [54:00]

- Sociologists and historians find charisma very difficult to deconstruct; others wonder why disciples are so attracted to a spiritual master. Material and spiritual charisma differ, but the splendour of the splendid is a manifestation of Kṛṣṇa.
- Many players live for the exhilarating thrill of victory. At the material level it is fleeting. A devotee sees it as an experience of Kṛṣṇa that does not, by itself, take one close to Him — so a devotee does not strive just for material success but serves Kṛṣṇa wholeheartedly, and then such feelings become constant and very sweet.

#### Adventure [54:00 – 58:00]

- People practise "living on the edge" sports — skydivers who delay opening the parachute until the last moment — for the kick it gives them.
- One man used to run along railway tracks toward an oncoming train for the thrill of the driver's face, jumping off at the last moment. Once he came too close, jumped and fell — onto a copy of _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_, read it and became a devotee.
- Kṛṣṇa can make even our adventures spiritual: sharing Kṛṣṇa consciousness with others brings extraordinary adventures that are supremely meaningful, because they help others come close to Kṛṣṇa and give them the highest happiness.
- People love adventure novels because that experience is special — an experience of Kṛṣṇa's manifestation.
- Strength too is an opulence that comes from Kṛṣṇa `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even gambling's cunning and the fleeting thrills of victory and adventure reflect Kṛṣṇa's splendour without leading to Him; the devotee finds lasting sweetness and supremely meaningful adventure in serving Kṛṣṇa and sharing Kṛṣṇa consciousness. [50:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 10.37 [58:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:10.37 -->

**Context.** Among His own family line Kṛṣṇa names Vāsudeva, among the Pāṇḍavas Arjuna, among sages Vyāsa, and among thinkers Uśanā (Śukrācārya) `58:00 – 60:00`.

#### Vāsudeva and Arjuna [58:00]

- The teacher explains that Vāsudeva here does not refer to Kṛṣṇa Himself but to Balarāma: both are sons of Vasudeva, through Devakī and Rohiṇī. Since Kṛṣṇa is speaking about opulences, not about Himself, He naturally refers to His brother.
- Among the Pāṇḍavas one might expect Yudhiṣṭhira, the king of dharma; but Kṛṣṇa says Arjuna, because Arjuna had not only extraordinary archery but special, intense devotion for Kṛṣṇa.
- Arjuna, on hearing this, does not say "Kṛṣṇa, I did not know we two are one — I am also God." He understands the principle: Kṛṣṇa is talking about opulences.

#### Vyāsa and Śukrācārya [60:00]

- Among the sages Vyāsa is the greatest: he compiled all the Vedic literature — who else can do that?
- Uśanā is Śukrācārya, priest of the demons. That may seem a less-than-complimentary position, but even the demons have some faith in Vedic rituals and want a powerful priest for their sacrifices; Śukrācārya is that.
- Śukrācārya also knows about Viṣṇu, though his attachment sometimes covers his position. At the end of Bali Mahārāja's pastime, Lord Vāmana does not chastise him but treats him as a respectable brāhmaṇa and asks what fault in Bali's sacrifice led to his defeat. Śukrācārya reveals profound knowledge: whatever faults there may be in a sacrifice, chanting the holy names counteracts them all.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna is named not for archery alone but for his intense devotion — and he hears it without claiming oneness with Kṛṣṇa; even Śukrācārya, priest of the demons, ultimately testifies that chanting the holy names corrects every fault in sacrifice. [58:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
