## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 7.13 [00:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.13 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains that the whole world, bewildered by the three modes, does not know Him, who is above the modes and imperishable `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mohitam_ — bewildered; everything and everyone in this world is bewildered by the three modes `00:00`.
- _Param_ — transcendental; correlated with the limits of space `02:00`.
- _Avyayam_ — imperishable; correlated with the limits of time `02:00`.

#### Tinted glasses [02:00]

- The modes colour our perception of everything, like tinted glasses: with red-tinted glasses, everything looks red.
- Goodness, passion and ignorance are each a different tint; all three are material, so together they form a material covering — a distortion of our perception.
- Because of this, we cannot understand Kṛṣṇa's transcendental position: everything in this world is limited by time and space, but Kṛṣṇa is beyond both.

#### Even those in goodness are bewildered [02:00 – 04:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport notes that even those in goodness are bewildered about Kṛṣṇa.
- The teacher points to the Tenth Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_, where practically everyone — demigods such as Indra, even Brahmā — is bewildered; Śiva too, later, offers prayers after his side is defeated in a fight with Kṛṣṇa's forces `04:00`.

#### Illusions within the world versus illusion about Kṛṣṇa [04:00 – 06:00]

- There are relative illusions within the world: a drunkard thinks alcohol is a source of happiness, though it brings bondage and misery `04:00`.
- A person may move between such illusions — for example, giving up an addiction (ignorance) through willpower or therapy, only to think "now I can work hard, have a big house and a family, and be happy" (passion) `04:00 – 06:00`.
- One may move from ignorance to passion, even to goodness, but overall one stays in illusion as long as one does not understand Kṛṣṇa properly `06:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa focuses on this deeper illusion about Him, because that is what keeps us in material bondage `06:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The modes tint our vision like coloured glasses; we may shift from one material illusion to another, but the illusion that really binds us is not knowing Kṛṣṇa, who is beyond time, space and the modes. [02:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.14 [06:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.14 -->

**Context.** Answering the natural question of how one escapes the modes, Kṛṣṇa says this divine energy of His is very difficult to overcome, but those who surrender to Him cross beyond it `06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Daivī_ — divine; Māyā is called divine because her purpose is ultimately good and she is connected with Kṛṣṇa `10:00`, `12:00`.
- _Mama māyā_ — "My energy": the covering is not independent of Kṛṣṇa `10:00`, `14:00`.
- _Duratyayā_ — formidable, insurmountable, very difficult to overcome `20:00`.
- _Ānukūlya_ and _prātikūlya_ — accepting what is favourable and rejecting what is unfavourable to devotion, from the six elements of surrender `20:00`, `24:00`.

#### The sun and the clouds [08:00 – 10:00]

- Picture three levels: the sun, the clouds, and observers on the earth.
- When clouds are present, it may appear that the sun is covered; but the sun is far greater than the clouds and cannot be covered — what is covered is the observer's vision.
- Likewise, Kṛṣṇa is not covered; we are.
- BG 7.13 described the covering of the modes; BG 7.14 adds that the covering itself is under Kṛṣṇa's control — it is His energy.

#### Why Māyā is called divine [10:00 – 14:00]

- Objection: Māyā makes people lusty, greedy and angry and drives them away from Kṛṣṇa; how can she be divine? `10:00`
- Answer: the absurdities and perversities in people's actions are not caused by Māyā; they are facilitated by her and caused by people's own free will `10:00`.
- **Multiple-choice paper:** the teacher provides four options, three wrong, and also provides the education to choose the right one. If a student chooses wrongly, the student cannot blame the teacher for including wrong options `10:00 – 12:00`.
- Māyā provides the options and the temptations; we make the choices, and each of us has the power to stay out of Māyā. With guidance we will choose rightly `12:00`.
- The purpose of the exam is not to fail students but to promote them; even if some fail, the exam's purpose is good. So Māyā is divine: her purpose is ultimately positive, and she is connected with Kṛṣṇa `12:00`.

#### Why Māyā is so hard to overcome [14:00 – 16:00]

- Prabhupāda's example: a loving parent engages a strict tutor to discipline the child so that the child is properly educated; the parent is like Kṛṣṇa, the strict tutor like Māyā `14:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa ensures that only those who use their free will properly get through, and Māyā Devī implements this `14:00`.
- When a devotee once asked why Māyā is so strong, Prabhupāda immediately replied: because your purpose is not strong `14:00`.
- Temptations trouble us as long as we are not determined to serve Kṛṣṇa; once we are, temptations may come, but we can put them aside `14:00`.
- A student serious about cracking an exam is not distracted by a favourite sports match, a popular TV show or a hit movie; distractions distract only those whose purpose is not strong `16:00`.
- Making our purpose strong means taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa — wholehearted surrender `16:00`.

#### The guard dog [16:00 – 20:00]

- A visitor arrives at a house guarded by a ferocious, trained dog that charges at him; fighting it is impossible, and his telling it to stop will not work `16:00 – 18:00`.
- If the visitor calls the owner, and the owner says "Stop, he is my friend," the same dog that was about to pounce becomes calm with one word `18:00`.
- Likewise, when we surrender to Kṛṣṇa, we become free from Māyā, because surrender is the choice Māyā herself ultimately wants us to make `18:00`.
- Māyā's temptations (promises of pleasure) and tribulations (threats of pain) are withdrawn for such a person: Kṛṣṇa tells the material energy there is no more need to test this devotee `18:00`.
- The first half of the verse shows Māyā's power; the second half introduces a greater power — Kṛṣṇa's — accessed by surrender `20:00`.

#### Surrender is active, not passive [20:00 – 26:00]

- Surrender will be discussed more in the 18th chapter; here it suffices to say that surrender is active `20:00`.
- Accepting the favourable and rejecting the unfavourable involves constant, conscientious choice `20:00`.
- Two pictures of surrender `20:00 – 22:00`:
  - **Draupadī** — when she was being dishonoured, she tried her best to defend herself; when that failed, she raised her hands and surrendered to Kṛṣṇa.
  - **Arjuna** — at the start of the Gītā he folds his hands and asks Kṛṣṇa what he should do; later, fighting intelligently and fiercely on Kṛṣṇa's instruction is also surrender.
- The basic principle of spiritual life is harmonising our will with Kṛṣṇa's will — sometimes through activity, sometimes through outer inactivity with inner deference `22:00`.
- How Kṛṣṇa's will is to be accepted varies with time, place and circumstance, so we cannot know it in advance; we must keep asking what is favourable and what is unfavourable `24:00`.
- Forms surrender can take `24:00`:
  - doing our _sādhana_ diligently;
  - moving away from unfavourable situations;
  - rejecting agitation of the senses with determination.
- Much of surrender happens within the mind: entertaining thoughts and fuelling desires that take us away from Kṛṣṇa is, in effect, rebelling against Him, even without intending to; choosing thoughts and desires that bring us closer is surrender `24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Māyā is divine because she only offers the options, and her purpose — like an exam's — is to promote us; her grip is released, like a guard dog called off by its master, when we actively choose Kṛṣṇa through a strong purpose and constant favourable choices. [08:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.15 [26:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.15 -->

**Context.** If surrender frees one from illusion, why doesn't everyone surrender? Kṛṣṇa names four kinds of people who do not surrender to Him `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Duṣkṛtina_ — not meritorious but "notorious" activity: activity involving some talent and intelligence, but in the wrong direction `26:00`.
- _Mūḍha_ — foolish, like a donkey; the opposite of _buddha_, a wise person (related to _buddhi_) `32:00`.
- _Narādhama_ — the lowest among human beings (_nara_ = human being) `38:00`.
- _Māyayāpahṛta-jñāna_ — those whose knowledge is stolen by illusion `46:00`.
- _Āsura_ — outright demonic people, envious of God `26:00`, `48:00`.

#### Text and context: why ācāryas explain differently [28:00 – 32:00]

- Different commentators describe these four categories as slightly different kinds of people `28:00`.
- Scriptural knowledge is not only in the text but also in its context: it is meant to be relevant `28:00`.
- Preserving purity — no speculation, no deviation — is essential, but relevance is also part of the tradition; each _ācārya_ explains the scripture in a way relevant to his time, so a presentation that is not relevant is not fully faithful to the tradition `28:00`.
- Tradition is not immobile and static; it is living `28:00`.
- _Avidyā_ keeps coming in "newer and newer brands" — ultimately the same old excuse for forgetting Kṛṣṇa — and the _ācāryas_ keep updating their refutations, drawing on time-independent principles while addressing the issues of their age `30:00 – 32:00`.
- Examples: a commentator on the _Vedānta-sūtra_ refutes the other philosophical systems as they stood in his time; in Prabhupāda's time there was the conflict between communism and capitalism and the fear of a third world war, which is less directly relevant now `32:00`.

#### The mūḍhas — hogs and donkeys [32:00 – 38:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport: hogs who eat night soil do not care for sweets made of sugar and ghee; similarly the foolish worker keeps hearing about the flickering mundane world but has little time to hear about the eternal living force that moves it `34:00`.
- The teacher clarifies the example: the point is not that the hog has its own natural diet — animals act according to their nature — but the lesson of lacking discrimination; a human being who chooses a foul-smelling substance over a delicious one lacks intelligence `34:00 – 36:00`.
- The donkey carries huge bundles of clothes for a little grass that it could get anywhere `36:00 – 38:00`.
- Eating, sleeping, mating and defending are available to all living beings; humans who work terribly hard only for these deprive themselves of the higher taste available through devotional connection with Kṛṣṇa `38:00`.
- Much of today's stress from overwork comes from self-created duties — setting one's own goals and getting into trouble pursuing them `38:00`.

#### The narādhamas — lowest among humans [38:00 – 44:00]

- What makes human beings human is their spiritual potential: the human form means a level of consciousness that includes the capacity for spiritual realisation `38:00 – 40:00`.
- On the continuum of consciousness, those with love for Kṛṣṇa are highest; at the lowest end are those with no interest in spiritual life at all, who have sabotaged their own spiritual prospects `40:00`.
- Prabhupāda: those who are socially and politically developed but have no religious principles must be considered _narādhamas_ `40:00`.
- Some systems today called religions do not acknowledge God; but the essence of _dharma_ is to follow God's will and instructions `40:00 – 42:00`.
- The tragedy: in the womb, when the pain becomes excruciating, we call out to Kṛṣṇa and beg for His mercy; but that surrender is forced, not mature — mature surrender means we have the option not to surrender and still surrender `42:00`.
- After birth, the pleasures of this world tempt us again, and we put aside the spiritual journey we began in the womb `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Such people are so busy in sensual pleasure that even if they have some spiritual inclination, it is pushed into a corner; by Kṛṣṇa's mercy they can be elevated `44:00`.

#### Knowledge stolen by illusion [44:00 – 48:00]

- In the Īśopaniṣad Mantra 10 discussion of so-called knowledge, the class saw BG 2.42 – 43: those who follow the Vedas but think their goal is sense gratification, and those who do not follow the Vedas but use intelligence only for material improvement `44:00 – 46:00`.
- These people have knowledge, but its purpose has been stolen by Māyā — scientists, philosophers, authors, speakers `46:00`.
- **PhD on the jail:** a prisoner may do a PhD on the walls and ceiling of the jail; that knowledge may have some use, but the most important knowledge is how to get out — perhaps by reforming oneself and being released `46:00`.
- In material existence, the most important knowledge is what frees us from misery — the knowledge Kṛṣṇa gives in the Gītā `46:00`.
- This category also includes those who study the Gītā and write commentaries but reject its purpose, surrender to Kṛṣṇa, and give a concocted purpose instead `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### The demonic [48:00]

- Unlike the previous groups, who end up against God by their interpretations without a clear agenda, these people have a deep, demoniac envy of God.
- They make it their mission to defy, deride and demolish other people's faith in God — fanatical atheists whose whole purpose is to defy Kṛṣṇa, so they cannot surrender.

#### Not just "other people" [48:00 – 50:00]

- We should not think these are only four categories of other people; we may have these tendencies in small ways ourselves, and they check and choke the force of our surrender `48:00 – 50:00`.
- Prabhupāda quotes a prayer in the purport: the Lord remains unknowable to those with atheistic principles, despite His uncommon qualities and activities, despite being confirmed by scriptures in goodness and acknowledged by great authorities in transcendental science `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
People fail to surrender not for lack of talent but because it is misdirected — into animal pleasures, worldly development, knowledge robbed of its purpose, or envy of God; we must watch for traces of these tendencies in ourselves, since they choke our own surrender. [26:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.16 [50:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.16 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now names four kinds of pious people who begin to render devotional service to Him; He does not yet use the word "surrender" — they begin with worship and will eventually come to surrender `50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sukṛti_ — pious credit, because of which their talent is piously directed `52:00`.
- The four: the distressed, the seeker of wealth, the inquisitive, and the seeker of knowledge (_jñānī_) `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### Examples of the four [50:00 – 52:00]

- The scriptures give examples for each; not all of them were already enlightened sages — some were inquisitive and wanted to know `50:00`.
- One wanted a kingdom and approached the Lord for it `52:00`.
- The _jñānī_ can refer especially to the Kumāras, who had knowledge of the impersonal aspect but moved forward and came closer to Kṛṣṇa `52:00`.

#### Where people take shelter in trouble [52:00]

- A person in ignorance takes shelter of drinking, smoking or violence.
- A person in passion takes shelter of money and sex — "throw money at it and the problem will be solved."
- A person in goodness takes shelter of knowledge, trying to reason out how to deal with the problem.
- A transcendentalist takes shelter of Kṛṣṇa: "Kṛṣṇa, please relieve my misery."
- These four pious people are in goodness: they do not yet surrender, but they begin worshipping Kṛṣṇa — "God is supreme; maybe He will resolve my misery."

#### From worship to pure devotion [52:00 – 54:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that they are not pure devotees, citing the definition of pure devotion `52:00`.
- They may begin worship out of culture, curiosity or other factors; what raises them to pure devotion is association with pure devotees `52:00 – 54:00`.
- Then the Lord becomes not just a supplier of their desires but the object of their love `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even mixed motives — distress, wealth, curiosity or knowledge — are a pious start when they bring a person to Kṛṣṇa rather than elsewhere; association with pure devotees then turns the Lord from a supplier of desires into the object of love. [50:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.17 [54:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.17 -->

**Context.** Among these four, Kṛṣṇa says the _jñānī_ who is always engaged in one-pointed devotion is the best and very dear to Him `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Eka-bhakti_ — one-pointed devotion `54:00`.
- _Jñānī_ — here, not an impersonalist, but one who has transcended material temptations and tribulations and aspires for pure devotion `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Why the jñānī is the best [54:00 – 56:00]

- The other three come for the fulfilment of material desires, so their devotion is not considered very elevated `54:00`.
- The _jñānī_ thinks: there is some highest truth beyond this material world; I want to know it and ultimately connect with it `54:00`.
- Material desires are what make our intelligence wander in different directions; the _jñānī_ has understood that this world has little to offer, so his devotion is not occasional but perennial `56:00`.
- In contrast, one who approaches the Lord for material reasons may say, "God, help me," and when the trouble is over, "thank you, bye-bye" — perhaps not literally, but by giving up serious devotional service `56:00`.
- Such a person sees nothing in this world so dreadful that he must be agitated about it, and nothing so wonderful that he must be agitated about it `56:00`.

#### Dry wood catches fire [56:00 – 58:00]

- When dry wood is touched by fire, it immediately blazes; wet wood does not `56:00`.
- Material desires are like the water that makes the wood wet; the _jñānī_'s heart has no such distracting factor, so once attracted to Kṛṣṇa, that attraction blazes forward `58:00`.
- This happened with the Kumāras: the fragrance of the _tulasī_ leaves from the Lord's lotus feet attracted them, and they moved forward and surrendered `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The jñānī free from material desires is like dry wood: once touched by attraction to Kṛṣṇa, his devotion blazes up and stays constant, which is why Kṛṣṇa calls him the best and very dear. [54:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.18 [58:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.18 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says all these devotees are magnanimous, but the one in knowledge is situated in Him, as His very self `58:00 – 60:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Udārāḥ_ — magnanimous, "charitable": the word Kṛṣṇa uses for all four kinds of devotees `58:00`.

#### The Lord's large-heartedness [58:00]

- The commentary explains that the Lord is so hungry for devotion that, like someone desperate for something who exclaims "you are so kind, so charitable" when it is given, He calls even those with mixed or motivated devotion magnanimous.
- He calls all four magnanimous because, instead of going elsewhere when in trouble, they come to Kṛṣṇa.

#### Dearer than His own self [60:00]

- The _jñānī_ is as dear to Kṛṣṇa as Himself; but the pure devotee, Kṛṣṇa says, is even dearer to Him than Himself.
- Here Kṛṣṇa is slowly but surely revealing His heart — how He loves those who choose to love Him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is so eager for devotion that He calls even motivated worshippers magnanimous for coming to Him — and the pure devotee, who chooses to love Him, He holds dearer than Himself. [58:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
