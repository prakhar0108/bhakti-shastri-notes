## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 7.8 [00:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.8 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa begins His list of examples — among them the taste of water, the syllable _oṁ_ at the beginning of Vedic mantras, sound in ether and ability in human beings `00:00`, `24:00 – 30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pantheism_ — the philosophy that "everything is God"; the teacher distinguishes Kṛṣṇa's teaching from it `00:00 – 02:00`.
- _Oṁkāra_ — the syllable _oṁ_, the beginning sound of the Vedic mantras `00:00`, `24:00`.
- _Element_ — in our scriptural study, a fundamental characteristic of matter; in modern science, one particular type of existing substance `08:00`.
- _Etymology_ — the study of word roots `22:00`.
- _Jurisdiction_ — the area over which an authority applies `34:00`.

#### Essence, not reduction [00:00 – 04:00]

- When Kṛṣṇa says He is the taste of water, it does not mean Kṛṣṇa is reduced only to that; He is that, and much more `02:00`.
- He is speaking of how He is the essence of everything.
- The taste of water seems to differ from place to place — in India, Australia, America or Africa — because different things are added to it; what Kṛṣṇa refers to is the essential characteristic of the element itself `02:00 – 04:00`.

#### Elements in the scriptures and in science [06:00 – 10:00]

- Each element is associated with a sense object; for example, ether is what enables sound to be transmitted `06:00`.
- The teacher notes that science once spoke of an ether that allowed transmission, but that idea was later rejected `06:00`.
- The scriptural idea is an _element theory_ of matter: matter is made of certain characteristic elements, each with its own characteristics `08:00`.
- In modern science, "element" means one type of substance; elements bond to form compounds `08:00`.
- Today's scientific view is primarily an _atomic theory_ of matter: elements can be broken down into fundamental particles `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### The Taj Mahal problem [10:00 – 12:00]

- The subatomic world of particles is almost incomprehensible, and most of these particles have no sense qualities — no colour, no fragrance, no taste `10:00`.
- Yet the world we experience has variety and individuality.
- It is as if the Taj Mahal suddenly emerged out of building blocks that have none of its artistry: how does that happen? `10:00 – 12:00`
- The connection between the scientific worldview and the world of daily experience is not clear `12:00`.

#### Science as a map [12:00 – 14:00]

- Science is like a map: a map shows certain features of the territory.
- A political map of India shows political divisions; other features are not on it `12:00`.
- A map is a useful tool for navigation, but the map is not the territory `14:00`.
- Science is remarkably useful, but what it describes is one representation of reality, not ultimate reality `14:00`.
- That science cannot explain the link between subatomic particles and the world we experience simply shows the map is incomplete `14:00`.
- Incomplete is not incorrect — unless the incomplete is thought to be complete; then it becomes incorrect. So we accept science without making that mistake `14:00`.

#### Water and taste [16:00 – 20:00]

- Taste becomes accessible to us through water: if the salivary glands did not secrete, we could not taste `16:00`.
- A chapati need not be liquid like soup, but some liquid must be present for it to be tasted `16:00`.
- So the essence of water is its taste; water is the medium by which we experience taste `16:00`, `20:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa is the underlying unifying essence of everything, and seeing Him this way helps us remember Him — the main aim of this whole section `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### Mechanism and the one behind it [20:00 – 22:00]

- Light from the sun and moon comes through some mechanism, but mechanisms are not a complete explanation of Kṛṣṇa's actions `20:00`.
- In a game with balls on a table, one explanation is the force and angle of the shot; another is the player who chose them — the two explanations are complementary `22:00`.
- There may be a physical mechanism, but the overseer of the mechanism is Kṛṣṇa `22:00`.

#### The sound _oṁ_ [22:00 – 28:00]

- The teacher notes, from etymology, that the word "amen" in the Abrahamic traditions and the "omni-" words used for God's attributes (omnipresent, omnipotent) share a root with _oṁ_ `22:00 – 24:00`.
- _Oṁ_ is considered holy because it is connected with the Absolute; important Vedic mantras generally begin with it, for example _oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya_ `24:00`.
- The Vedas contain many instructions in the _karma-kāṇḍa_ for gratification within _dharma_, _artha_ and _kāma_, but their transcendental aspect is also present in the references to Viṣṇu `24:00`.
- _Oṁ_ is given in the second (_brāhmaṇa_) initiation with the _Gāyatrī_ `26:00`.
- Devotees generally prefer the Hare Kṛṣṇa _mahā-mantra_ `26:00`:
  - it is the _yuga-dharma_ for this age;
  - it invokes the Absolute Truth in a personal way, whereas _oṁkāra_ refers to the Absolute Truth in a non-personal way.
- The same person can be addressed with different degrees of intimacy; devotees prefer to address the Lord more distinctly and personally `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Ability in human beings [28:00 – 30:00]

- Ether is the means by which sound is propagated `28:00`.
- The distinctive abilities of human beings come from Kṛṣṇa: the ability of an excellent speaker to speak, of an excellent corporate leader to lead — whatever abilities a person has come from Him `28:00`.
- Human beings also have the ability to improve themselves, an inner capacity that can catapult them on the spiritual path `30:00`.

#### The personal aspect is not a "convenient fiction" [30:00 – 34:00]

- The Māyāvādīs consider the personal aspect lower and the impersonal higher `32:00`.
- For them, the Lord's particular form, features and pastimes are just a "convenient fiction" used to attain the ultimate impersonal realisation `32:00`.
- Against this, Prabhupāda states that the jurisdiction of Kṛṣṇa consciousness extends everywhere; knowledge of Kṛṣṇa is liberation, and ignorance of Him is bondage `32:00 – 34:00`.

#### The jurisdiction of Kṛṣṇa consciousness [34:00]

- "Jurisdiction" is normally used when a crime happens in an area: if someone is robbed, the question is which area's authority will take up the complaint.
- The jurisdiction of Kṛṣṇa consciousness extends everywhere: we can be Kṛṣṇa conscious anywhere and remember Kṛṣṇa anywhere.
- The more we know about Kṛṣṇa, the more advanced we are; without knowing Him we stay in illusion.
- Seeing the connection of everything with Kṛṣṇa becomes the cause of liberation.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa is the essence that makes each thing what it is — the taste of water, the sound in ether, ability in human beings — yet He is never reduced to it; because His jurisdiction extends everywhere, every experience can become a way to remember Him. [00:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.9 [36:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.9 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa continues: He is the original fragrance of the earth, the heat in fire, the life of all that lives and the austerity of ascetics `36:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Fragrance_ — the sense object that, in the Sāṅkhya system of evolution, comes from earth `36:00`.
- _Tapasvī_ — an ascetic; one who performs austerities `40:00`.

#### Experiential and conceptual understanding [36:00 – 38:00]

- We know experientially how sweet a fragrance from the earth can be; this verse adds a conceptual understanding — fragrance is the essence of earth `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### The heat of fire [38:00]

- Sun and moon are usually spoken of in terms of light (moonlight being cooling), but of fire Kṛṣṇa says, "I am the heat."
- Things in this world have essential characteristics that matter to us: when it is cold, people light a fire or use a heating system because it gives out heat.
- We are attracted to fire because of its heat — and Kṛṣṇa says, "I am that."

#### How spirit affects matter [40:00]

- How the soul affects matter is a problem that has perplexed Western thought, and because of it the very concept of the soul has largely been set aside.
- The Vedic conception: the soul and matter interact through the Supersoul, who controls both spirit and matter.

#### The austerity of ascetics [40:00 – 42:00]

- Some people can live a day without water on _nirjalā ekādaśī_; this seems difficult, yet some fast on water alone for three, five or fifteen days `40:00`.
- That capacity ultimately comes from Kṛṣṇa; He gives different capacities to different people `42:00`.
- So when we see someone's austerities, we can respect them instead of competing, recognising that their capacity comes from Kṛṣṇa, and make our own advancement by connecting everything to Him `42:00`.

#### Life and death [42:00 – 44:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is the life in all beings; life and death are not ultimately in the hands of doctors `42:00`.
- A doctor can offer medicine and whatever else is required, but medicine is not an exact science that can say "take this chemical and you will get this result" `42:00`.
- Some people with the best medicines and the best doctors still pass away, while others with terrible diseases survive `44:00`.
- Ultimately, life and death rest with Kṛṣṇa `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Fragrance, heat, austerity and life itself are Kṛṣṇa's presence in the world; recognising Him in another person's strength lets us respect rather than compete, and connecting everything to Him is how we advance. [36:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.10 [46:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.10 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says He is the original, eternal seed of all living beings, and the intelligence of the intelligent `46:00 – 48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sanātana_ — eternal; Kṛṣṇa is the eternal seed `46:00`.

#### The eternal seed [46:00 – 48:00]

- When an embryo forms from an egg and grows into a chick, the egg no longer exists `46:00`.
- Normally a seed produces only one kind of tree; Kṛṣṇa is the seed of all kinds — whatever exists comes from Him `46:00`.
- All the variety of living beings in the universe has come from Kṛṣṇa, yet Kṛṣṇa remains the original, unchanged truth `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Abilities that impress [48:00]

- Some people have such bodily power that they can knock someone else out, and people are attracted to them.
- We can recognise that such abilities exist, but the power ultimately comes from Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Unlike an ordinary seed that is used up, Kṛṣṇa is the eternal seed of all kinds of beings — everything comes from Him, yet He remains unchanged, and every impressive ability we see is ultimately His. [46:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.11 [48:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.11 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says He is strength free from desire and attachment, and also desire that is not contrary to _dharma_ `48:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kāma_ — desire in general; also one of the _puruṣārthas_ (goals of human life) `50:00`.
- _Rāga_ — attachment, which one should give up while using one's strength `50:00`.
- _Dharma_, _artha_, _kāma_ — religion, prosperity and pleasure `52:00`.

#### Desire as a goal of life [50:00 – 52:00]

- Kṛṣṇa first speaks of strength free from _kāma_ and _rāga_, and then of _kāma_ not contrary to _dharma_ — here _kāma_ as one of the _puruṣārthas_ `50:00`.
- The goals of human life lead finally out of the cycle of birth and death and back to the spiritual world `52:00`.

#### Balance among dharma, artha and kāma [52:00 – 54:00]

- The teacher cites the Mahābhārata on balance: pursuing only religion and giving up profit and pleasure, only profit, or only pleasure, is not recommended; one should live in balance `52:00`.
- _Artha_ is holistic prosperity — having the necessities of life and natural comforts; Vṛndāvana is a place where people worked hard yet lived comfortably `52:00 – 54:00`.
- _Kāma_ refers not just to sensual gratification but to the overall satisfaction of family life `54:00`.

#### Sex life within dharma [54:00 – 58:00]

- Sex life that is not contrary to religious principles — that is, within the precincts of _dharma_ — is not mundane; Kṛṣṇa calls it Himself, so _kāma_ can actually be divine `54:00`.
- Some say sex is a taboo in Vedic culture, something bad that should not be talked about; rather, it is considered a manifestation of the divine, which therefore must be understood properly `54:00`.
- Glamorised depictions focus on looks, physical contact and pleasure — pleasure that is basically the secretion and reception of chemicals `56:00`.
- The more people glamorise sex in imagination, the more it is reduced in real life to something merely physical, a tool for release — a trivialisation of what could be an expression of God when done properly with _dharma_ `56:00`.
- Sex is a part of life, but it has to be made a proper part — not the whole or central purpose of life, not the constant object of thought, and not an activity merely for release; in its proper place it becomes an experience of Kṛṣṇa `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Strength and desire become Kṛṣṇa's manifestation when they stay within dharma — desire balanced with religion and prosperity, and sex life kept as a proper part of life rather than glamorised into its central purpose. [48:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.12 [58:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.12 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa states that all states of being in the three modes come from Him, but He is not in them `58:00`.

#### All modes from Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa beyond the modes [58:00]

- Whatever exists in the material world exists in some way in the three modes, or in some combination of them, and all of it comes from Kṛṣṇa.
- Having given so many examples of how He is the essence of things, Kṛṣṇa clarifies: do not think He is reduced only to this.
- Kṛṣṇa is never contained within material nature; He transcends it.
- Even after saying He is the unifying thread underlying everything, Kṛṣṇa makes clear that this does not mean He is exhausted by it.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Everything in the three modes comes from Kṛṣṇa, yet He is never contained by them — He pervades nature as its essence while always remaining transcendental to it. [58:00]
</Callout>
