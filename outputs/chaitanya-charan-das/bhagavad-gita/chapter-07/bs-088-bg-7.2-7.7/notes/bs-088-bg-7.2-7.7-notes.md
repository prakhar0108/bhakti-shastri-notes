## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 7.2 [00:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.2 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa promises to explain knowledge of Himself together with its realised dimension; the teacher first explains why "knowledge" here means going beyond how things merely appear `02:00 – 06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jñāna_ and _vijñāna_ — the two aspects of the knowledge Kṛṣṇa promises; lust destroys both (BG 3.41), and Kṛṣṇa now offers both together `02:00`, `06:00`, `14:00`.
- _Phenomena_ — things as they appear to us `04:00`.
- _Noumena_ — things as they actually are, independent of our sense perception `04:00`.
- _Transcendental knowledge_ — knowledge that sees the world in its connection with the Absolute and so directs us towards transcendence `16:00`.

#### Reality is not as it appears [04:00 – 06:00]

- A fundamental insight in both Western intellectual history and the Vedic tradition: the way things appear and the way they actually are differ.
- The question that follows: we access the world only through the senses, so how can we know things as they actually are?
- In Western philosophy this became a debate between:
  - _Empiricism_ — knowledge through sense perception;
  - _Rationalism_ — a logical framework of concepts that helps us make sense of the world around us `06:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's point is that there is a framework we need in order to understand reality, and He will give it in two aspects `06:00`.

#### Knowing everything "in principle" [08:00 – 12:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's claim that nothing further remains to be known rests on the understanding that everything ultimately comes from Him; when one knows the essential principles, one knows everything in the sense of principle `08:00`.
- This is not a claim to complete detail. In Chapter 10, while describing His _vibhūtis_, Kṛṣṇa will say He can give only samples of His glorious manifestations, and it will be said that even the _devas_ cannot fully know Him `08:00`.
- So Kṛṣṇa can be known in terms of principles; in terms of detail, His glories are never exhausted `10:00`.

#### The Gītā's place among the scriptures [10:00 – 16:00]

- The teacher describes a progression `10:00 – 12:00`:
  - The Gītā helps us understand who God is — that the Absolute Truth is a person, that He can be attained through _bhakti_, that He is _bhakta-vatsala_ (affectionate to His devotees).
  - The _Bhāgavatam_ helps us taste the love of His devotees for Him.
  - The _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ gives a secret insight into the heart of the Lord.
- The Gītā outlines fundamental principles; the details given elsewhere also illuminate Kṛṣṇa's glory and help us remember Him `12:00 – 14:00`.
- _Mādhurya_ knowledge — of Kṛṣṇa's sweetness, such as His conjugal pastimes, or His giving up His greatness so that He fights with the cowherd boys and is defeated by them — is not discussed directly in this chapter, which focuses on _aiśvarya_ (majesty) `14:00 – 16:00`.
- Complete details can never be given about any subject, what to speak of Kṛṣṇa; this chapter gives the basic outline of what His energies are and how they interact `16:00`.

#### What makes knowledge transcendental [16:00 – 18:00]

- Any knowledge that:
  - helps us remember Kṛṣṇa,
  - directs our consciousness towards Him,
  - reveals the connection of everything with Him,
- is transcendental knowledge, because it directs us towards transcendence `16:00 – 18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa gives knowledge of Himself in principle — complete enough that, by seeing everything in connection with Him, our consciousness is directed to transcendence, even though His glories can never be exhausted in detail. [08:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.3 [20:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa highlights how rare real knowledge of Him is — even among those who strive for and attain perfection `20:00`, `24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Perfection_ — as most people understand it, liberation; even among those who attain it, hardly anyone knows Kṛṣṇa in truth `24:00`.
- _Revelation_ — the means by which real knowledge of Kṛṣṇa comes, rather than our own thinking `28:00 – 30:00`.

#### Most people stay on the animal level [20:00 – 22:00]

- Most people remain caught in the animal propensities of eating and sleeping; even religion is often used only to pursue these `20:00`.
- The teacher shares a light example of a person approaching a lifelong celibate (_bāla-brahmacārī_) and asking him for the blessing of a wife `20:00`.
- Choosing a compatible spouse is important for a good life, but most people get stuck at that level `22:00`.

#### The real soulmate [22:00 – 24:00]

- Movies and novels romanticise reincarnation: a couple who cannot unite in one life reincarnate and unite in the next; people speak of finding their "soulmate" `22:00`.
- The teacher redirects the focus: it should not be on the "mate" part but on the "soul" part — not "who is my mate?" but "what is my soul?" `24:00`.
- The ultimate soulmate of the soul is Kṛṣṇa; we have an eternal relationship with Him whether we are in a male or female body `24:00`.

#### Why Kṛṣṇa stresses the rarity [26:00]

- By underscoring how rare this knowledge is, Kṛṣṇa draws Arjuna's attention — just as people are attracted to "breaking news," wanting to know something before anyone else, or something very few people know.

#### Knowledge comes by revelation [28:00 – 30:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport stresses that real knowledge of Kṛṣṇa comes by revelation, not merely by our own reflection — "I just think and think and think, and I understand."
- Kṛṣṇa reveals Himself; that is how such knowledge is received.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowing Kṛṣṇa in truth is rarer even than attaining perfection, because it comes by His revelation, not by our own thinking — and it redirects our search from a "mate" for the body to Kṛṣṇa, the eternal soulmate of the soul. [20:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.4 – 7.5 [30:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.4 -->

<!-- vedabase:7.5 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa begins explaining how He is the source of both the material and the spiritual: matter is His separated, inferior energy, and the living beings are His superior energy `30:00 – 42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Separated energy_ — an energy that comes from its source but is not present with it in the same way, and can have a different nature `32:00`.
- _Twenty-four elements_ — the material categories within which material analysis stays `38:00`.
- _Gross and subtle creation_ — the gross physical elements and the subtle elements such as mind and intelligence `36:00`.

#### The recording example [32:00]

- Milk comes from the cow, yet it is separated from the cow.
- When the teacher speaks, his speech is recorded and the recording is made available for people to hear. The recording is not different from the speaker — it is the speaker's energy — yet the speaker is not present in it.
- In this sense, a separated energy comes from its source but can have a different nature.

#### Why the world is imperfect if it comes from God [32:00]

- A common perplexity in Vedānta and in religious thought generally: if God is good, why is the world filled with evil?
- The idea of separated energy answers this: the world comes from God, but as His separated energy it does not have His qualities in the same way.

#### The limits of material science [34:00 – 38:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that the material world is a temporary manifestation of one of the Lord's many energies `34:00 – 36:00`.
- The field of material science is limited to material categories: the five sense objects (sound, touch, form, taste, smell), the subtle creation and the gross creation `36:00`.
- Modern science says little about mind, intelligence or the soul; it is not equipped to conceive of them, so its scope focuses on gross matter and its picture of reality is incomplete `36:00`.
- Complete understanding comes only when gross matter, subtle matter and the soul are all understood; all such analysis remains within the external energy of Lord Viṣṇu `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### "Maintaining" the world [38:00 – 42:00]

- The teacher notes a slightly sarcastic sense in Kṛṣṇa's words that the living beings "maintain" this world `38:00`.
- Obviously we are not the real maintainers of the world `38:00`.
- Realising this frees us: we do not have to be trapped in the conception that we are the maintainers; the Supreme is `40:00`.
- A verse quoted in the purport is used to show that the living beings are limited, distinct from the unlimited Supreme `40:00 – 42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Matter is Kṛṣṇa's separated energy — from Him, yet without His qualities — and we living beings are His superior energy, not the real maintainers of the world; recognising this frees us from a burden that was never ours. [32:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.6 – 7.7 [42:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.6 -->

<!-- vedabase:7.7 -->

**Context.** Having described the two energies, Kṛṣṇa concludes that everything comes from Him, and that nothing is higher than Him `42:00 – 58:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prabhava_ and _pralaya_ — Kṛṣṇa is the beginning and the dissolution of everything `42:00`.
- _Transcendent_ — God beyond matter `48:00`.
- _Immanent_ — God within matter `48:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa's reasoning [42:00]

- The teacher sets out Kṛṣṇa's argument as a chain:
  1. These two energies come from Kṛṣṇa.
  2. Everything we see is a combination of these two energies — matter and spirit — in various ways.
  3. Therefore everything comes from Kṛṣṇa; He is the beginning of everything, and everything ends in Him.
- Example: a computer screen is made of matter, but matter that has been acted upon by a soul `42:00`.

#### Spirit is the active principle [44:00 – 46:00]

- Spirit is the basic field of creation: it activates creation, and matter is shaped by spirit `44:00`.
- A small child makes a toy; a grown person builds a skyscraper — in both, spirit acts on matter `44:00`.
- Our body is present and active because it is activated by the soul `44:00`.
- We exist as Kṛṣṇa's energy: we come from Him and are meant for His service `44:00`.

#### The Supersoul as maintainer [46:00]

- The Lord activates, maintains and is the final destination of the whole universe.
- The teacher describes three Viṣṇu forms as the Supersoul — of the individual body, of the universe, and of the total material creation.
- The living being is also conscious, but distinct from the Lord; the Lord's speciality is that He is the maintainer of everyone.

#### Transcendent and immanent [48:00 – 50:00]

- God is both:
  - _Transcendent_ — beyond matter;
  - _Immanent_ — within matter.
- Though He is the highest, He is also accessible to us within creation; how He pervades the world will be described in the next set of verses, 7.8 – 7.11 `50:00`.
- Underlying the many things we see in this world is a unifying reality — Kṛṣṇa; the teacher comments that the impersonal conception is not the highest `50:00`.

#### Nothing higher than Kṛṣṇa [52:00 – 58:00]

- The point of BG 7.7 is that there is no truth higher than Kṛṣṇa `52:00`.
- Prabhupāda quotes a Vedic verse in the purport: the Supreme Person is beyond darkness, and only by knowing Him can one attain liberation and get off the cycle of birth and death — there is no other path `54:00 – 56:00`.
- Prabhupāda thereby confirms that the Absolute Truth is a person, not impersonal; that person is the source of all energies, material and spiritual `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Everything is a combination of Kṛṣṇa's two energies, so everything comes from Him; He is both beyond matter and within it, and there is no truth higher than Him — the personal Absolute whom one must know to be liberated. [42:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
