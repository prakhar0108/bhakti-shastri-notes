## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 7.25 [02:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.25 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says He is not manifest to everyone; covered by _yoga-māyā_, the foolish do not know that He is unborn and inexhaustible `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Aham_ — "I," used twice: Kṛṣṇa speaks of Himself as the person before Arjuna, not of some reality higher than Him `04:00`.
- _Mūḍha_ — the foolish, who do not know His unborn, imperishable nature `14:00`, `20:00`.
- _Yoga-māyā_ vs. _mahā-māyā_ `14:00 – 16:00`:
  - _mahā-māyā_ lets souls enjoy separately from God through false conceptions — "I am the body, male or female, independent of God";
  - _yoga-māyā_ is an "oxymoron": _yoga_ (connection) and _māyā_ (disconnection) — disconnection from the idea that Kṛṣṇa is God, reconnection through intimacy ("this God is a small boy," "this God is my friend");
  - _yoga-māyā_ is illusion that increases devotion; _mahā-māyā_ is illusion that depletes it.

#### Kṛṣṇa's right to reveal or conceal [04:00 – 08:00]

- Society talks about everyone's rights — of women, of lower castes, of African Americans. What about the rights of the Supreme Lord?
- His right is to reveal or conceal Himself by His own sweet will.
- Even ordinary powerful people have such rights:
  - we cannot demand that the Prime Minister come before us; even if someone meets him, he chooses whether to meet;
  - a child born in a forest tribe who has never heard of the Prime Minister needs to understand the Prime Minister's position — the Prime Minister does not need to fit within the child's understanding.
- We are not the order-givers to God; we are the order-carriers of God `04:00`.

#### Defiance vs. dependence [06:00 – 12:00]

- Kṛṣṇa reciprocates not only with our spoken desires but with our overall mentality; demanding "let God appear before me" in a spirit of defiance does not give access to Him.
- We are tiny (_aṇu_) and Kṛṣṇa is mighty; we can access Him only by turning to Him in devotion `06:00`.
- **The sun** `08:00`, `10:00`: no one on earth can demand sunrise; the sun rises of its own accord, and then we are blessed by its warmth and light. Our attitude towards it must be dependence, not defiance.
- When we want to play God, Kṛṣṇa lets us — and reveals His omnipotence finally only as death `08:00`:
  - this is not cruelty but an arrangement by which we can continue our game if we wish, or realign with reality;
  - death is a reminder and an educator.
- We show Kṛṣṇa we want to see Him by dependence and compliance — recognising our subordinate position and serving Him `10:00`.
- Often, "wanting to know God" is really imposing one's opinion on God — playing God in the name of seeking Him `12:00`.
- What we need is "not an attitude of defiance but a habit of compliance"; as we follow instructions, Kṛṣṇa progressively reveals Himself `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Unborn and inexhaustible [12:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is beyond time; His existence has no beginning and therefore no end.
- This continues 7.24: those who think His form had a particular beginning and ending are mistaken — He manifests that form at all times.

#### Why "yoga-māyā" here? [16:00 – 18:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is describing Himself: "for them I am covered by My internal potency" — but only for them.
- **Sun and cloud**: for people on the ground the sun is covered by the cloud, yet the sun is far greater and cannot really be covered.
- **The heater/cooler**: electric energy can heat or cool — one energy, different functions. From Kṛṣṇa's point of view, _yoga-māyā_ and _mahā-māyā_ are ultimately the same energy.
- So the word is not used here in its strict technical sense; it simply means a covering that keeps these people — especially the demigod worshippers and impersonalists — from seeing His personal form `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### Seen with the heart, not just the eyes [18:00 – 22:00]

- Even when Kṛṣṇa was on earth, He was visible but not truly seen: He is seen with the heart, and devotion is the real vision.
- Many warriors at the time of the Mahābhārata war thought of Him as just another powerful warrior.
- A demoniac king even claimed to be Kṛṣṇa and sent a message demanding that the real Kṛṣṇa return "his" paraphernalia; Kṛṣṇa killed him — not out of hatred, but to save him from the offence of pretending to occupy His position `18:00 – 20:00`.
- The teacher links this to the earlier-discussed prayer (mantra 15) asking the Lord to remove His covering so His true nature can be seen `20:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa has the right to reveal or conceal Himself; He stays covered to the defiant but progressively reveals Himself to those who approach with dependence and a habit of compliance, because He is seen with the heart, not merely the eyes. [04:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.26 [22:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.26 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa knows everything in the past, present and future and all living beings — but no one knows Him `22:00`.

#### Knowing about Kṛṣṇa vs. knowing Kṛṣṇa [22:00]

- People may know Kṛṣṇa as a historical person, or as someone others consider the Supreme God, without understanding who He actually is.
- That can be understood only through devotion — a point the 18th chapter and many other places discuss.
- The teacher also speaks of Kṛṣṇa's reassurance to Arjuna: do not worry about relatives who must be killed — Kṛṣṇa will see that they are taken care of; and if even partial devotional service gives such a result, how much greater is the result of wholehearted devotional service `22:00`.

#### How can Kṛṣṇa know the future? [24:00 – 26:00]

- Futurologists and astrologers try to predict the future so that people can prepare; ultimately no one can protect themselves by such knowledge.
- Does Kṛṣṇa's knowing the future mean our future is programmed? The teacher's answer: Kṛṣṇa's knowledge of the future is like our knowledge of the past — knowledge without control.
- **Oversleeping**: if I meant to sleep seven hours but slept fifteen, I know it, but I can't change it.
- The major difference: Kṛṣṇa's position is unlike ours — even time, which destroys all beings, is His servant `26:00`.

#### Kṛṣṇa hides His omniscience for rasa [26:00]

- In His pastimes Kṛṣṇa knows everything that will happen, but does not act that way, because His goal is not to parade His divinity but to reciprocate His devotees' love.
- Example: when Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma come home hungry, they are told to wash and bathe quickly, and are served a big feast.
- He creates a sense of unpredictability that is resolved by His intervention alone — this enhances the _rasa_ of His _līlā_.

#### Free will and trajectories [26:00 – 30:00]

- **Teacher and student** (a rough example): a teacher can see the trajectories — the student who studies will learn the culture of hard work and succeed; the one who doesn't will fail. Pathways exist, and each choice has an inevitable result.
- Kṛṣṇa could make us act like robots, but He gives us freedom — so that we are tested, and so that we become purified by seeing how trivial worldly concerns are.
- Kṛṣṇa knew what would happen in the Kurukṣetra war, yet let the fight go on, because His goal was to perform a _līlā_.
- Devotees need not worry about "what will happen to me or to that person"; keep doing devotional service steadily, with patience and perseverance, and Kṛṣṇa's inner revelation will come `30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa knows the future as we know the past — without overriding our free will; rather than worrying about outcomes, a devotee steadily serves and patiently awaits Kṛṣṇa's inner revelation. [24:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.27 [30:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.27 -->

**Context.** All living beings are born into delusion, overcome by the dualities that arise from desire and hate `30:00 – 32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Icchā_ — here, the desire to enjoy separately from Kṛṣṇa `32:00`.
- _Dveṣa_ — resentment of Kṛṣṇa's position as the enjoyer, and of those who serve Him `32:00 – 34:00`.
- _Dvandva_ — the dualities (good/bad, happiness/misery) that come from labelling things by likes and dislikes `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Sammoha_ — the complete, foundational illusion underlying the dualities `36:00`.

#### Born in delusion [32:00]

- We say "happy birthday," but birth itself is not joyful — the baby cries and the mother screams in pain. The new life is a joy, and if the parents practise devotional service with the child, that life can go successfully out of this world.
- Since all beings are born in delusion, we must become liberated from it by steady "baby steps" in spiritual life.

#### Icchā and dveṣa in this context [32:00 – 36:00]

- The words are used many times, but in the seventh chapter — where Kṛṣṇa gives knowledge of Himself — they refer specifically to the desire to enjoy apart from Kṛṣṇa and the dislike of His position as the enjoyer.
- From this we suffer in two ways:
  - when we are separated from what we like (no sweet when I want one);
  - when we meet what we don't like.
- So we label things good and bad according to our own likes and dislikes.

#### The movie-theatre illustration [36:00 – 38:00]

- A viewer forgets he is a spectator and becomes a vicarious participant, identifying with a character.
- Within the movie he likes some things (the hero winning) and dislikes others (the hero being beaten) — the dualities.
- But the foundational illusion is wanting to watch the movie at all — that is _sammoha_.

#### Sun, clouds and the observer [38:00]

- 7.25 – 7.27 together distinguish Kṛṣṇa's position from ours: not visible to all; knowing everything; while all beings are deluded.
- Kṛṣṇa is like the sun, the illusory potency like the clouds, the living entity like a person on the earth.
- The clouds are vast; they cannot be brushed away like cobwebs with a stick — we must depend on divine grace.

#### The question of origins [38:00 – 44:00]

- How we fell from the spiritual world is a "vexed question": what is beyond time and space is very hard to understand within time and space.
- **Drowning in the ocean**: one should think about getting out rather than how one fell in.
- **Adam and Eve**: the teacher retells the biblical story — the serpent tempts Eve, she and Adam eat the forbidden fruit, become conscious of being unclothed, and are expelled from Eden for their defiance. The story is symbolic; the palindrome "Madam, I'm Adam" — each of us is Adam, each has defied the Lord `40:00 – 42:00`.
- One explanation the teacher gives: the soul, always happy with Kṛṣṇa, has not explored life apart from Him; or it desires to serve in a way not possible there, such as creation, becomes Brahmā, and then gradually takes on lower desires `42:00`.
- Free will needs an arena: the spiritual world for choosing to serve Kṛṣṇa, the material world for choosing otherwise `42:00`.
- **Falling in and out of love**: relationships and emotions are not an exact science; logic does not fully explain why people fall in love or fall out. Likewise the soul's choice to turn from Kṛṣṇa is not a matter of precise cause and effect `44:00`.

#### Why the liberated do not fall again [44:00 – 48:00]

- The teacher cites an analysis in the commentary on the last aphorism of the _Vedānta-sūtra_, which declares that the liberated soul does not return to material existence. Reasons given `46:00 – 48:00`:
  1. It is a scriptural promise, and scripture is the word of God.
  2. The Lord loves His devotees who rejected this world for Him; He will hold them in a tight embrace and never let them fall.
  3. The devotees love the Lord, have sacrificed everything for Him, and find Him completely fulfilling — why would they ever want to leave?
  4. Having experienced how horrible life apart from Kṛṣṇa is, if such a desire ever arose, Kṛṣṇa would remind them of the consequences.
- So all of us are deluded, but all of us can be liberated — and once liberated, we will not return `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every soul is born into the delusion of wanting to enjoy apart from Kṛṣṇa and resenting His position; rather than dwelling on how we fell, we should seek His grace to get out — and once back with Him, love on both sides ensures we never fall again. [32:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.28 [48:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.28 -->

**Context.** Those whose sinful actions have ended through pious activities become free from the delusion of dualities and worship Kṛṣṇa with determination `48:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pāpa_ / _puṇya_ — sinful / pious activity `50:00`.
- _Dṛḍha-vrata_ — determined, steady worship `50:00`.

#### The flow of the argument [50:00]

- 7.14: knowing Kṛṣṇa brings liberation; 7.25 – 7.27: not everyone knows Him, and He is not under illusion while living entities are.
- 7.28 answers how they come out: give up _pāpa_, do _puṇya_, and come to the position where they can practise _bhakti_ steadily.

#### Bhakti can start anywhere, but steadiness needs purity [50:00 – 52:00]

- A drunk person dancing and singing at a _kīrtana_ is also performing some devotional activity — but cannot wake up every day for chanting and _maṅgala-ārati_.
- In 7.16 four kinds of people come to Kṛṣṇa, but not all are steady; 7.17 says the _jñānī_ is best because his practice is not materially motivated.
- For devotees, breaking the regulative principles is sinful activity whose consequences must be avoided.
- Duality exists in all three modes but is far more crippling in ignorance and passion; by doing piety and avoiding sin, the lower modes' influence decreases, goodness increases, and from goodness one can come to transcendence `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### Temptation, fall, lamentation [54:00]

- Sin is a break in _bhakti_, but it is surrounded by agitation: temptation before ("should I? I won't...") and lamentation after ("why did I do it?").
- The cycle keeps consciousness from staying steady or rising; determinedly avoiding sin lets the process of _bhakti_ work.

#### The wet wood [54:00 – 56:00]

- The heart is like wood; the flame of _bhakti_ comes from association with serious devotees.
- If the wood is wet (the heart in ignorance), contact does not make it catch fire — at best a small dry part ignites.
- The wood must be dried, and further water kept off it: giving up sin and doing piety bring the heart to goodness so _bhakti_ can progress steadily.
- Prabhupāda's purport stresses giving up the association of materialistic non-devotees and associating with great devotees `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti can begin anywhere, but to become steady and determined the heart must be "dried" by giving up sin and doing piety, and kept dry by good association. [50:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 7.29 – 7.30 [56:00 – 62:00]

<!-- vedabase:7.29 -->

<!-- vedabase:7.30 -->

**Context.** Those who seek liberation from old age and death take shelter of Kṛṣṇa and endeavour; they know Brahman and everything about transcendental activities; and those who know Him as the governing principle of the material manifestation, the demigods and sacrifice are well situated even at the time of death `56:00 – 60:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Brahman_, _adhyātma_, _karma_ (7.29); _adhibhūta_, _adhidaiva_, _adhiyajña_ (7.30) — new terms Kṛṣṇa introduces; Arjuna will ask their meaning at the start of the next chapter, and Kṛṣṇa will answer briefly `56:00 – 60:00`.
- _Prayāṇa-kāla_ — the time of death `60:00`.

#### Another category of devotee [58:00 – 60:00]

- 7.29 introduces another category among those who come to Kṛṣṇa: those aspiring for liberation (_mokṣa-kāmī_); the teacher says this will be discussed at 8.12.
- The teacher counts five categories of devotees across the chapter — the four of 7.16 plus this one — noting that the liberation-seeker may come from an intellectual (_jñāna_) background or a yogic background.
- All of them come to Kṛṣṇa ("_mām_") and by that make spiritual advancement.

#### Well situated at death [60:00 – 62:00]

- Those who know Kṛṣṇa as the ultimate truth will be well situated even at the time of death, and the implication is that they attain Him.
- Summary of 7.25 – 7.30: Kṛṣṇa's position is supreme; He is not revealed to everyone; to know Him one must practise steady devotional service — giving up sin, doing piety, becoming determined. Then one understands Brahman and these terms, and most importantly understands Kṛṣṇa as the ultimate reality, is well situated at death, and attains liberation `60:00 – 62:00`.

#### Overview of the seventh chapter [62:00]

- Section 1 — the Absolute pervades and underlies everything.
- Section 2 — whatever people do is in some relation with the Absolute: defying, obeying, or serving something connected with it.
- Section 3 — when we surrender to the Absolute and understand Kṛṣṇa as the Absolute Truth, we attain supreme auspiciousness.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Steady devotional service brings real understanding of all the philosophical categories — and above all of Kṛṣṇa as the ultimate reality — so that one stays fixed on Him even at death. [58:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
