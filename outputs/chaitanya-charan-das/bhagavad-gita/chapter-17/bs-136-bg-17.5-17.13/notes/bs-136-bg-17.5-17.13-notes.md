## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 17.5 – 17.6 [00:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.5 -->

<!-- vedabase:17.6 -->

**Context.** Before describing the three modes, Kṛṣṇa goes to the bottom of the frame of reference: people who perform severe austerities not recommended in scripture, out of pride and ego, tormenting themselves and Kṛṣṇa within them. Kṛṣṇa declares that they are definitely _asuras_ `00:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Not in scripture_ — austerity that is not talked about in the scriptures `00:00`.
- _Ghora_ — dreadful; harmful to others `00:00`.
- Arrogance and pride; motivated by desire and attachment `02:00`.
- The body made of material elements; consciousness misdirected, not turned toward spiritual reality `02:00`.
- _Asura_ — demoniac; Kṛṣṇa says such people are "definitely" _asuras_ `02:00`.

#### Why demons appear in this chapter [02:00 – 04:00]

- Chapter 17 is not about the demoniac nature — that was Chapter 16 — so Kṛṣṇa mentions them only to give context.
- These people are completely misdirected in thought, lifestyle and even their conception of religion: instead of religion as an approach to God, they treat it as an approach to their own ego.
- Everyone in this world has an ego and most do not try to purify it, but these people are so extreme in their obsession with ego that they will torture themselves for its sake.

#### Self-torture versus self-transcendence [04:00 – 08:00]

- Not every form of self-control is self-torture. Fasting may look like self-torture to some, but if it has a positive, spiritual purpose it is the way to self-transcendence.
- **Every choice is a sacrifice:** at a fork in the road, choosing one road means giving up the other.
- **Indecision is also a decision**, and rarely the best one: someone undecided about spiritual life who therefore does not practise is sacrificing the opportunity to practise.
- A sacrifice chosen on scriptural understanding for a higher purpose is auspicious; a sacrifice that ignores _śāstra_ and harms others backfires, entangling the person further in bodily consciousness and self-centredness, which is self-defeating and self-destructive.
- Examples of extreme austerity: self-flagellation (beating oneself with whips) and plucking out body hair instead of shaving. Even when done for a spiritual purpose these reflect a misconception; when done without spiritual purpose they can be demoniac.

#### Austerity is not automatically glorious [08:00]

- We usually think of austerity as positive and glorious because it requires self-control and self-discipline.
- Kṛṣṇa says those who torture themselves, do nothing positive for their spiritual advancement, are driven only by pride and ego, and care nothing for _śāstra_ are demoniac — the mentality of Chapter 16.

#### The Śambuka episode from the Rāmāyaṇa [08:00 – 14:00]

- After Lord Rāma returned to Ayodhyā and was ruling, a _brāhmaṇa_ came outside the palace crying for help, holding the body of his dead son.
- He said he had done his brahmanical duties properly, so the only reason his son could have died — reversing the natural order, in which the father dies before the son — was something inauspicious happening in the kingdom.
- Lord Rāma asked Nārada Muni and the sages present; Nārada meditated and said serious _adharmic_ activity was happening somewhere in the kingdom.
- Lord Rāma had the boy's body preserved in oil, searched in all directions, and finally found, on a riverbank, an effulgent, dark-complexioned person hanging upside down from a tree by his legs.
- He said he was a _śūdra_ named Śambuka doing austerity to attain the heavenly planets. Lord Rāma took out His sword and cut off his head; at that moment the _brāhmaṇa_'s son came back to life and order was restored `10:00 – 12:00`.

#### Reading the episode in full context [12:00 – 18:00]

- The episode is often framed as caste discrimination, but the teacher says the essential message is different `08:00`.
- **Accept the whole story or none of it:** if we apply today's moral standards, we must also take its full context. Today a scientist whose son died would never blame the Prime Minister, and no one would believe a dead child could revive because someone else was killed. That the Rāmāyaṇa links a death with misrule, and Śambuka's death with the boy's revival, shows forces at work — laws of _dharma_ and _karma_ — that are unfamiliar, even incomprehensible, to us `12:00 – 14:00`.
- **The hospital analogy:** the world is like a hospital; different people have different diseases and need different treatments. The duties of the different _varṇas_ are spiritually a treatment that gradually elevates everyone, and materially a system of engagement according to one's propensities. A patient who barges into another ward and takes its medicines or disrupts its high-security functions commits a culpable offence `14:00 – 16:00`.
- **Lord Rāma is not casteist** — other pastimes refute that notion `16:00`:
  - He embraced Guha, a fishing tribal considered an outcast, stayed at his place and reciprocated lovingly.
  - He accepted Śabarī's food, even food she had half-eaten.
  - He befriended Vibhīṣaṇa, born in a _rākṣasa_ family.
- Lord Rāma accepts anyone with a devotional disposition and punishes those with a power-hungry disposition. The _ācāryas_ describe that Śambuka wanted to displace Indra and enjoy Indra's wife — a demoniac mentality that was severely disruptive of the Vedic order `16:00 – 18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Austerity cannot be judged by its externals: when it ignores scripture and is driven by pride, ego and the desire to dominate, even severe self-discipline is demoniac and marks the very bottom of the scale of faith. [08:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.7 [18:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.7 -->

**Context.** Having set the bottom reference, Kṛṣṇa says that even the food people find dear falls into three categories, as do sacrifice, austerity and charity, and asks Arjuna to hear about them `18:00 – 22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Āhāra_ — food `22:00`.
- _Yajña_ — sacrifice; _tapas_ — austerity; _dāna_ — charity `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### Why these four [22:00 – 24:00]

- _Yajña_, _dāna_ and _tapas_ are central activities in Vedic culture; in Chapter 18 Kṛṣṇa quotes thinkers who say they purify even the great souls.
- They express the faith within the heart: when giving charity or doing austerity we gain nothing tangible or immediate — we give up something we could enjoy — so these acts require a certain kind of faith.

#### Three circles of obligation [24:00 – 26:00]

- The soul is caught in three concentric, expanding circles — psychophysical (body–mind), social and cosmic — and whoever gives us something places us under obligation to return something.
- **Body–mind circle → _tapas_:** we use the body for _dharma_, so we do something for it through austerity.
- **Social circle → _dāna_:** we take so much from different people, so we return through charity.
- **Cosmic circle → _yajña_:** we may doubt the gods even exist, but they give us a great deal through universal utilities — we cannot provide our own air, water, heat or light — and we return this through sacrifice.
- Depending on their situation in the modes, people perform these activities differently; even our food reveals our faith `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Food, sacrifice, austerity and charity are windows into faith: through austerity, charity and sacrifice we repay what we take from our body–mind, from society and from the cosmos, and how we do so reveals our mode. [22:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.8 [26:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.8 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa begins with food in the mode of goodness. Śrīla Prabhupāda gives a common purport for 17.8–17.10, so the teacher discusses the verses one by one and then the purport `22:00`, `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Āyu_ — duration of life `26:00`.
- _Sattva_ — existence; Prabhupāda translates "purify one's existence" `30:00`.
- _Bala_ — strength; food gives energy `26:00`, `34:00`.
- _Ārogya_ — health `34:00`.
- _Sukha_ and _prīti_ — happiness and satisfaction `34:00`.
- _Snigdha_ — fatty; also juicy, enduring, and pleasing to the heart `34:00`.

#### Can lifespan be increased? [26:00 – 30:00]

- Some say our lifespan is fixed — the number of breaths is fixed by past _karma_. Then how can food increase it?
- If the attitude were fatalistic, the whole Vedic science of health would be pointless; its principle is to prolong and maintain life with quality and quantity.
- How this interacts with _karma_ is complex, but the basic principle is that the body is the tool for _dharma_, so maintaining it is also a duty.
- One way to understand it: if the breaths are fixed, living in passion makes us breathe faster and so use up our life faster; that is why yogis regulate their breath and breathe slowly to live longer.
- We can observe that people with healthy dietary habits live healthier, and improper food has consequences.
- An old Egyptian saying: one quarter of what you eat maintains you; the other three quarters maintain your doctor — people tend to overeat.
- Prabhupāda would often quote a saying: fill half the stomach with food, a quarter with water and a quarter with air, so that air and water help digestion.

#### Food shapes consciousness [30:00 – 34:00]

- Whatever we take in affects us inside — physically, emotionally and spiritually. Every interaction with matter shapes our perception; looking at an obscene picture, for instance, creates a certain mentality.
- With food we usually notice only attraction and revulsion (_rāga_ and _dveṣa_), but food also has a subtle component that shapes consciousness — we become what we eat.
- Food cooked with love for Kṛṣṇa and served with a devotional disposition increases our attraction to Kṛṣṇa; Caitanya Mahāprabhu says that eating food cooked by the wrong kind of people makes the mind wicked — absorbed excessively or exclusively in self-centred desires — and such a mind cannot think of Kṛṣṇa.
- Goodness itself is purifying (Chapter 14), so food in goodness stops further contamination and situates us in goodness. Higher still, _prasāda_ offered to Kṛṣṇa is transcendental: it helps us transcend the modes, become free from _karma_ and become attracted to Kṛṣṇa `32:00`.

#### Emotional effects of goodness versus passion [34:00]

- Food in goodness leaves one happy and satisfied — "this was good, I like it, thank you."
- Overeating leaves us so loaded we can do nothing.
- Food in passion leaves us agitated, craving "more and more," never satisfied.

#### Prabhupāda's purport: fat, milk and slaughter [34:00 – 38:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that the purpose of food is to increase the duration of life, purify the mind and aid bodily strength.
- "Fatty" does not mean meat: animal fat is available as milk, "the most wonderful of all foods." Animal fat from slaughter is _tāmasic_; Prabhupāda calls slaughter the way of subhumans.
- Slaughter happened in the past too, but it was not the norm and animals were not brutalised lifelong. Today's slaughterhouses are an industry of monstrous insensitivity: hens crowded so tightly that they peck each other are de-beaked while alive and conscious, without painkillers — one of hundreds of such practices.
- Food produced with such brutality is definitely not in goodness.

#### Is milk a product of violence? [38:00 – 44:00]

- Vegans avoid milk too, saying it is a product of violence. In today's industrial dairies, the teacher says, it often is: machines squeeze milk until cows cry and bleed, and cows are kept sick and congested.
- In Vedic culture cows were treated like mothers — cared for, valued and served — and gave milk in abundance as an expression of affection.
- In Chapter 18 Kṛṣṇa names _go-rakṣya_ as a duty of the _vaiśyas_. Some editors wanted to change Prabhupāda's "cow protection" to "cattle rearing," but cattle rearing generally means rearing cattle for slaughter; that is not the Vedic understanding `40:00`.
- A cared-for cow gives more milk than her calf needs; the calf is given some and gradually weaned, and the surplus can be used by humans `40:00 – 42:00`.
- **Practical stance:**
  - Avoid industrialised milk as far as possible; take milk produced traditionally, with love and care.
  - Where that is not possible, it is up to the individual; we do not take the extreme position that all milk is bad, which the _ācāryas_ do not support — Prabhupāda called milk a miracle food.
  - Excess of anything becomes passionate: when someone said milk increases brain substance, so more milk means more brain, Prabhupāda replied that one needs basic brain substance to know how much milk to take.
  - Discrimination according to time, place and circumstance is required of each devotee `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Food in goodness nourishes life, purity, strength, health and contentment, and shapes consciousness subtly; but how food is produced matters too — milk from cared-for cows is a miracle food, while food born of brutality is not in goodness. [26:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.9 [42:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.9 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes food dear to those in the mode of passion. The teacher points out that the words themselves sound sharp and jarring, matching their meanings `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kaṭu_ — bitter; _amla_ — sour; _lavaṇa_ — salty `44:00`.
- Extremely hot, pungent, too dry and burning `44:00`.

#### Perverse stimulation [44:00]

- Some people eat extremely hot, spicy food until tears come from their eyes and call it wonderful; the body is reacting instinctively, but they find it stimulating.
- It is like watching horror movies to get stimulated in a perverse way — giving negative sensations to the body.

#### Industrialised food [44:00 – 48:00]

- Salt, sugar and spice are all important, but in excess they harm people a lot.
- In the past food was never an industry; Prabhupāda would say food was always given in charity.
- Industrialisation dehumanises: to sell more and earn more, too much salt or sugar is added, with little thought for its effects. Much fast food tastes good but is not nutritious or healthy in the long run.
- Obesity comes not only from eating too much but from eating the wrong food — excessively fatty food causes far more fat than an excess of other food. It is becoming one of the world's leading causes of avoidable death.
- The tragedy of passion: millions starve while millions die from eating too much.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Food in passion stimulates through excess — too hot, too salty, too sweet — and leaves the eater agitated and unhealthy; the modern food industry amplifies exactly this mode. [44:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.10 [48:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.10 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes food dear to those in ignorance, and the teacher concludes the food section with how devotees should think about _prasāda_ `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Food cooked and stored for a long time, whose juice has gone `48:00`.
- Foul-smelling and decomposed food `48:00`.
- Remnants eaten by others — the remnants of the spiritual master are considered auspicious, but others' remnants are _tāmasic_ and untouchable `48:00`.

#### Prasāda first, modes second [48:00 – 52:00]

- Specific correlations of foods to modes vary with time, place and circumstance; some say long-preserved baked food is in ignorance.
- For devotees the primary consideration is whether food is properly cooked and offered to Kṛṣṇa; _prasāda_ is transcendental, so we do not over-analyse whether it is in passion or goodness.
- Yet material things have material effects: a diabetic told not to take sugar cannot eat many sweets calling them _prasāda_. Kṛṣṇa's mercy comes also as the doctor's advice to regulate eating, so we use intelligence and common sense `50:00`.
- **Travelling devotees:** in unavoidable situations they may take vegetarian food from outside and offer it; others prefer to carry food. Food cooked hours earlier, or made to keep for a long time, should not be branded _tāmasic_ in an extreme way — offered food that keeps well is preferable to outside food. The spiritual principle (food offered to Kṛṣṇa) gets priority over the material principle `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### Antiseptic, eatable, palatable [52:00]

- Prabhupāda concludes that to make food antiseptic, eatable and palatable for all, one should offer it to the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
  - _Antiseptic_ — karmic contamination is no longer there.
  - _Eatable_ — truly worthy of human beings to eat.
  - _Palatable_ — Caitanya Mahāprabhu would say Kṛṣṇa's saliva is on the _prasāda_, which is why it tastes special — a mystical explanation for why _prasāda_ tastes so good.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Stale, decomposed and impure food is in ignorance, but the devotee's first question is whether food is offered to Kṛṣṇa — prasāda is transcendental — while still using common sense about food's material effects. [48:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.11 [52:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.11 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now describes sacrifice in the mode of goodness: done without constant concern for the result, because it is enjoined by scripture `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Without desiring the result `52:00`.
- _Vidhi_ — scripture; "this is what the scripture has told me to do" `52:00`.
- One who fixes the mind in this way — "this is what is expected of me" `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### The modes work internally [54:00]

- The modes affect us much more internally than externally. We usually think of alcohol as ignorance and promiscuity as passion — true — but Arjuna's question was about religious people whose faith is non-scriptural.
- So Kṛṣṇa takes things deeper: even _yajña_, which we would assume is good, must be examined by the mode in which it is done.
- Sacrifice in goodness is done selflessly, with the understanding that scripture enjoins it and it will purify and elevate, with no motive of immediate material gain.

#### Beyond worship for benefit [54:00 – 56:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that going to the temple just to worship God for material benefit is not recommended in the scriptural injunctions.
- _Dharma_ is not only for _artha_; it leads to _artha_ and _mokṣa_, and _mokṣa_ is the most important — yet even _mokṣa_ is not the selfish goal, because we want to go beyond _mokṣa_ to pure love.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even sacrifice is judged by its mode: done as a scriptural duty, for purification and without desire for material gain, it is in goodness. [52:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.12 [56:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.12 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes sacrifice performed for results and out of pride as being in the mode of passion `56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dambha_ — pride; showing off one's piety `56:00`.
- Attachment to the result `56:00`.

#### Two attachments [56:00 – 58:00]

- **Attachment to the result** — "I should get the result."
- **Attachment to doership** — "see how much I am doing, how pious I am; everyone should know."
- This links to Chapter 16, verse 15, where those who give charity thinking "I am so great" are called deluded by ignorance.
- If I think I am the doer and the enjoyer of the result, I am simply in material consciousness — pursuing eating, sleeping, mating and defending in a better way through religion, which is nothing glorious.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sacrifice becomes passionate when it is done for the result and to display one's piety; then religion is merely a refined way of pursuing material enjoyment. [56:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.13 [58:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.13 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes sacrifice in the mode of darkness — without regard for scripture, without distribution of food, without mantras, without gifts to the priests and without faith `58:00 – 60:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Without distribution of food `58:00`.
- Without mantras chanted `58:00`.
- Without _dakṣiṇā_ to the priests performing the sacrifice `58:00`.
- Without _śraddhā_ — no faith in what one is doing `60:00`.

#### Food distribution is integral [58:00]

- Whenever sacrifice is performed, food should be distributed. In the Govardhana pastime, food was offered to Govardhana, accepted and returned, and then distributed to everyone — not just to the _brāhmaṇas_ or the sages.
- In his early days in America, Prabhupāda was in dire financial straits, without guarantee of his own food or stay; yet when people came to his programme he would slice an apple into small pieces so that each person got at least one slice.

#### A sacrifice in name only [60:00]

- Such sacrifice is demoniac, leads to inauspiciousness for everyone and ultimately harm; no real sacrifice is involved.
- Next class: speech in the mode of goodness, as part of the further discussion of the three modes.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A sacrifice without scripture, food distribution, mantras, gifts to the priests or faith is a sacrifice only in name; even Prabhupāda in poverty shared what little he had, showing that giving is integral to sacrifice. [58:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>

### Core Analogies & Metaphors

- **Fork in the road** — choosing one road sacrifices the other `04:00`.
- **Hospital wards** — different _varṇa_ duties as different treatments; barging into another ward is an offence `14:00 – 16:00`.
- **Egyptian saying** — one quarter of what we eat maintains us, three quarters maintain the doctor `28:00`.
- **Obscene picture** — every interaction with matter shapes consciousness `30:00`.
- **Tears from spicy food / horror movies** — perverse stimulation through negative sensations `44:00`.
- **Diabetic and sweets** — even _prasāda_ requires common sense `50:00`.
- **Prabhupāda's apple slices** — food distribution even in poverty `58:00`.
