## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 17.23 [00:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.23 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now gives the solution: how one whose faith is not scriptural or not transcendental can elevate it — by reciting _oṁ tat sat_. These three words are references to the Absolute Truth `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Oṁ tat sat_ — three references to, or indications of, the Absolute Truth `02:00`.
- _Brahman_ — can mean a person who is a _brāhmaṇa_ or the Absolute Truth; in this verse both senses appear `02:00`.
- _Purā_ — in the past; this is how _oṁ tat sat_ has been used by the _brāhmaṇas_, the Vedas and sacrifices `04:00`.
- _Praṇava_ — _oṁ_; Kṛṣṇa says "I am _praṇava_" `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### Four levels of faith [00:00 – 04:00]

1. **Faithless** — simply materialistic, no faith in anything higher.
2. **Non-scriptural faith** — faith coming from one's own tradition or culture.
3. **Scriptural faith** — e.g. the worship of various beings in the three modes described in 17.4; scriptural, but within the modes. Not everything taught in scripture is transcendental.
4. **Transcendental faith** — the summit of scriptural faith.

- Chanting _oṁ tat sat_ elevates one from non-scriptural to scriptural faith, and from scriptural to transcendental faith `02:00`.

#### Oṁ: non-personal, not impersonal [04:00 – 08:00]

- _Oṁ tat sat_ is a familiar Vedic invocation, still used in Hindu society; Prabhupāda concludes many of his introductions with it, such as the introduction to the _Bhāgavatam_.
- One can refer to the Absolute Truth personally — as Kṛṣṇa, Viṣṇu, Rāma. Those who do not want to, or do not know the personal aspect, may say _oṁ tat sat_.
- _Oṁ_ is non-personal — no direct reference to personality — but not necessarily impersonal.
- The word is cognate with words for God elsewhere: English _omni_ (omnipotent, omniscient — all-pervading, all-inclusive) and _amen_ in the Abrahamic religions.

#### Primary and secondary names [06:00 – 10:00]

- Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura speaks of two kinds of names of the Lord:
  - **Primary names** — such as Kṛṣṇa and Rādhikā-vallabha — refer to His personal life and pastimes with His devotees in the spiritual world.
  - **Secondary names** — such as Paramātmā, Parama-puruṣa and names meaning the universal person or the all-pervading — refer to His administration of this world. In the spiritual world no Paramātmā is needed to escort living beings, and no supervisor is needed, because everyone serves Kṛṣṇa lovingly.
- _Oṁ tat sat_ are secondary names `08:00`.
- _Oṁ_ is often combined with other words in mantras. In 7.8 Kṛṣṇa, describing Himself as the essence of everything, says that among the Vedas He is _praṇava_, and a similar reference comes in Chapter 10, making clear what _oṁ_ refers to `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### Tat and sat [10:00]

- _Tat_ means "that," as in _tat tvam asi_, "you are that." The _māyāvādīs_ read it as "you are He"; the Vaiṣṇava understanding is "you are His" — of the same quality as the Absolute Truth, not the same in quantity.
- _Sat_ refers to the Absolute Truth as the eternal underlying cause and source of everything.
- In one sense Kṛṣṇa is speaking here about chanting the holy names and its power to elevate the faithless to faith, and the non-scripturally faithful to scriptural faith — a very inclusive understanding of _bhakti_.

#### Prabhupāda's non-sectarian spirit [10:00 – 16:00]

- When people of other faiths asked whether they had to chant Kṛṣṇa's name, Prabhupāda said they could chant the names of the Lord according to their own tradition.
- In Iran, when the call to prayer began, Prabhupāda closed his eyes, folded his hands and listened absorbed; afterwards, with tears in his eyes, he said, "Wasn't that beautiful?" When a devotee asked whether it would have been better if they had chanted Hare Kṛṣṇa, Prabhupāda looked hurt: "Why are you making me sectarian? They are glorifying God in their way and we are glorifying God in our way."
- The name Kṛṣṇa has a specialty — it refers to the Lord in the highest abode — but from a universalist perspective we must know what to present when. Our goal is not to spread one organisation but to raise global consciousness; that is why Prabhupāda named it the International Society for Krishna _Consciousness_ `12:00 – 14:00`.
- **According to commitment** `14:00`:
  - To a nominal Christian, Prabhupāda would recommend practising Kṛṣṇa consciousness: love for God can be developed either way, but Caitanya Mahāprabhu's mercy and the holy name give access to Goloka Vṛndāvana.
  - To a committed pastor, monk or teacher, he would not try to convert, but say: follow the Bible more faithfully; studying the Gītā, the science of God, will make you a better Christian.
- If people can take the "elevator" directly to Kṛṣṇa, wonderful; if not, they can rise from wherever they are `14:00`.

#### Exclusivist statements in context [16:00 – 20:00]

- Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism and Hinduism have borne the brunt of fanatical vandalism; many temples were desecrated and many deities moved — many Vṛndāvana deities are now in Jaipur. Prabhupāda acknowledges this in the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ but does not harp on it.
- Every religion has sectarian, exclusivist elements ("my way is the only way"). Statements like Kṛṣṇa's "surrender only to Me" or the Bible's "none shall come to the Father except through me" can be read fanatically, but they mean that we must follow the word of God — and God's representatives come in different ways at different times.
- **The doctor's focus:** a patient who keeps quoting other doctors is told, "Forget everything else; just follow what I'm telling you." This does not mean all other doctors are false; it creates focus.
- When a hippie in New York told Prabhupāda his philosophy sounded like the Buddha's, Prabhupāda asked whether he followed the Buddha, and said: follow Jesus, follow Kṛṣṇa, follow someone — don't just talk. Talking with a doctor does not cure; only by practice can we discover whether a teaching is genuine `18:00`.

#### Candour about other scriptures [20:00 – 24:00]

- In his purport to Caitanya Mahāprabhu's discussion with the Chand Kazi, Prabhupāda says the Old Testament, New Testament and Quran are recently composed, not eternal and not necessarily perfect, and do not contain much advanced knowledge of God.
- Prabhupāda is candid — but he says this in the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_, not in his Gītā or public classes. A faithless person is helped one step up at a time; someone reading the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ is already serious about Kṛṣṇa consciousness and need not be sent elsewhere.
- Elevation ultimately depends on devotion to God, whatever the tradition. Often religion becomes a tool for material enjoyment — the _dharma–artha–kāma_ level — with paradise as the goal. The teacher contrasts such hopes of heavenly pleasure with love of God, noting that the Quran too ultimately teaches pure surrender to God `20:00 – 22:00`.
- Pointing out these limitations is an objective fact, not a sectarian imposition: anyone can compare the depth and detail of knowledge of God in the Vedic and non-Vedic scriptures `22:00`.
- To a Christian who says Jesus never called Kṛṣṇa the Father: Jesus said, "I have much to say, but you have not the ears to hear"; he preached for practically five years and was killed by those he taught "thou shalt not kill" `22:00`.

#### College versus knowledge [22:00 – 26:00]

- A computer engineering student at IIT who learns that MIT has more advanced knowledge does not say, "I will study only at IIT." His interest is knowledge, not the college.
- Religions are like colleges for knowing and loving God. If the Vedic tradition objectively offers more knowledge of God, why not take it? One who refuses because "it is not in my tradition" is the one being sectarian.
- **The description and the person:** Prabhupāda said Kṛṣṇa is an objective conception of God. All religions agree God is all-attractive — with six opulences such as being all-beautiful, all-powerful, all-wealthy, all-knowing and all-renounced. Just as anyone claiming to be the Prime Minister of India must show the powers that post entails — commanding the armed forces, declaring an emergency — Kṛṣṇa exhibits the six opulences that fit the description of God `24:00 – 26:00`.
- Prabhupāda would say: if you don't accept Kṛṣṇa as God, give us a better, sweeter conception of God, and we will accept it `26:00`.
- _Oṁ tat sat_ is a universal way of addressing God: even one who does not know God is a person named Kṛṣṇa can be elevated by the generic names `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Oṁ tat sat are secondary, non-personal names of the Absolute Truth; through them — and through any authentic name of God — people can rise from faithlessness to scriptural and finally transcendental faith, which is why Prabhupāda's outreach was focused yet never sectarian. [00:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.24 [28:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.24 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says that the _brahmavādīs_ begin their sacrifices, charity and austerities, performed according to scriptural direction, by uttering _oṁ_ `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kriyās_ — actions in the Vedic tradition considered pious `28:00`.
- _Vidhāna_ — scriptural direction `28:00`.
- _Brahmavādīs_ — those seeking the Brahman, the Absolute Truth, whether by the rapid, direct transcendental path or gradually through _dharma_, _artha_ and _kāma_ `28:00`.

#### Setting a precedent [28:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is setting precedent: this is how _oṁ_ has been chanted before, and how it can be chanted by those who want to elevate their faith.

#### Two senses of brahmavādī [28:00 – 30:00]

- In common usage, _māyāvādīs_ consider everything except Brahman to be illusion, while _brahmavādīs_ may know of the personal aspect and not offend it, but are personally attracted to the impersonal aspect.
- Here the word is broader: those who adhere to scripture or aim to practise spiritual life, so that ultimately they may become transcendental.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Those seeking the Absolute begin their scriptural acts with oṁ, connecting even gradual, regulated religious activity with the Absolute Truth. [28:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.25 [30:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.25 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says those who seek liberation perform sacrifice, austerity and charity with the word _tat_, without desiring fruitive results `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Without desiring the fruits; without attachment `30:00`.
- _Mokṣa_ — liberation, sought by those mentioned here `30:00`.
- _Tat_ — a name of the Absolute Truth `30:00 – 32:00`.

#### From results to liberation [30:00 – 32:00]

- One may chant _oṁ_ even for material results and get them; but chanting _tat_, the name of the Absolute, without attachment to fruits, elevates one towards liberation.
- Kṛṣṇa is again distinguishing levels: religious faith (some kind of faith, not necessarily scriptural), scriptural faith and transcendental faith `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Chanting tat while giving up desire for fruits turns religious acts towards liberation — a step up from using sacred sound for material ends. [30:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.26 – 17.27 [32:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.26 -->

<!-- vedabase:17.27 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains the word _sat_, which comes poetically twice: it names the Absolute Truth, the devotee, and any prescribed work, sacrifice, austerity or charity done for the Supreme `32:00 – 34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sat_ — the Absolute; _sādhu_ — the devotee `32:00`.
- _Praśaste karmaṇi_ — prescribed duties `34:00`.
- Prabhupāda's translation: the Absolute Truth is the objective of devotional sacrifice and is indicated by _sat_; the performer and all works done to please the Supreme are also called _sat_ `32:00 – 34:00`.

#### Material and spiritual sva-dharma [34:00]

- Prescribed duties are of two kinds:
  - **Material _sva-dharma_** — duties according to one's _varṇa_ and _āśrama_.
  - **Transcendental _sva-dharma_** — what we do to practise _bhakti_ and gain liberation from material existence.
- Spiritual _sva-dharma_ is directly connected with the Absolute Truth; material _dharma_ can also be connected with it.
- This answers the chapter's starting question: one with non-scriptural faith must ultimately come to scriptural faith, and chanting _oṁ tat sat_ helps that move.

#### Any authentic name of God [34:00 – 36:00]

- Within the Vedic context _oṁ_, _tat_ and _sat_ have specific references, and they can also be used generically as secondary names of God.
- Prabhupāda would say chanting any authentic name of God elevates us. The basis of authenticity is whether the name refers to God's attributes — a person or truth with those attributes. Names like Allah and Jehovah refer to God as all-merciful and all-powerful, in harmony with scripture.
- The Aztecs or Native Americans may have had other names for God; all such names can be accepted if they refer to God's attributes as scripture describes.
- **The sun:** the sun is called Sūrya, Ravi, Bhāskara and by other names; similarly the Absolute Truth has many names `36:00`.

#### Work spiritualised [36:00]

- As in 4.24, when work is done in spiritual consciousness, all of it becomes spiritualised. Whatever we do for the Absolute Truth elevates and, over time, liberates us.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Sat names the Absolute, the devotee and every duty done for the Supreme; when material or spiritual duties are offered to the Absolute — under any authentic name of God — they become spiritual and elevating. [32:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.28 [36:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.28 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes: sacrifice, charity or austerity performed without faith is _asat_ — impermanent or false — and useless in this world and the next. Faith is absolutely essential: best is transcendental faith, then scriptural faith, but at least some faith is required `36:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Without _śraddhā_ — faithless `38:00`.
- _Asat_ — temporary, false, of no use `38:00`.
- Neither in this world nor the next `38:00`.

#### Echo of 4.40 [38:00]

- Kṛṣṇa said in 4.40 that one filled with doubts cannot gain happiness in this life or the next; here He reiterates the same point.

#### Atheism is faith in faithlessness [38:00 – 42:00]

- In principle faithlessness is impossible: even atheism has faith — faith that there is no God. No experiment can prove God's non-existence.
- **Suffering argument:** "There is so much suffering, so there is no God" is flawed logic. A city may have a doctor and still have sickness, because patients do not go to the doctor; the presence of evil does not disprove God.
- Self-described "non-believers" have belief in disbelief.
- No one lives without faith: if we believed only what we see, nobody would board a plane, since gravity and most scientific concepts are invisible `40:00`.

#### Three senses of faithlessness [40:00 – 44:00]

1. **Broad sense** — having no faith in anything; impossible.
2. **Intermediate sense** — no faith in God or spirituality, the sense of "non-believer." This is how Kṛṣṇa uses it here.
3. **Narrow sense** — lacking faith specifically in Kṛṣṇa.

- The teacher cites a definition of faith as unshakable confidence that by serving Kṛṣṇa all other work is done `42:00`.
- The _gopīs_ of Vṛndāvana showed this faith: leaving their duties as wives, mothers and community members, they went to Kṛṣṇa, confident that serving Him does everything — just as watering the root nourishes the whole tree.
- Kṛṣṇa calls for this level of faith at the end of the Gītā: give up all varieties of religion and surrender to Me; even if you fear reactions for neglected duties, I will take care of them `44:00`.

#### Faith is polyvalent [44:00 – 48:00]

- Faith is not monovalent (one meaning) but polyvalent (many meanings).
- In the nine stages of _bhakti_ described in _Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu_:
  - **_Śraddhā_** — the first stage; Prabhupāda translates it as favourable curiosity: "there may be a higher reality; I want to know." This faith is like a banana tree — shakable `44:00 – 46:00`.
  - **_Niṣṭhā_** — strong, unshakable faith, assimilated, internalised and realised; like a banyan tree `46:00`.
  - **_Prema_** — the ultimate stage, where faith matures into love; in love one is ready to give up everything, so faith is implied `46:00`.
- **Love and faith are not identical:**
  - _Love without faith_ — a mother loves her child but does not trust it near fire or a knife.
  - _Faith without love_ — I trust my doctor and am grateful, but I don't love him.
  - At the highest level, love implies faith.

#### Levels of surrender [48:00]

- Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura prays: "You may destroy me or protect me, as You desire" — implicit faith that whatever Kṛṣṇa does is for one's good, accepted gracefully and gratefully.
- The night before his crucifixion, Jesus prayed, "Let Thy will be done," considered an exalted expression of surrender.
- At the Gītā's conclusion Arjuna goes further: "I will do Your will" — not only letting God's will happen, but making it happen.

#### How faith grows [48:00 – 50:00]

- **Like a muscle:** the more we exercise faith, the stronger it becomes — through associating with devotees, studying scripture, and learning from those with knowledge, faith and conviction.
- **By divine grace:** transformational experiences — a pilgrimage, a special _darśana_ — can boost faith in rapid steps.
- Faith is both the result of human choice and of divine grace.
- The five potent forms of devotional service from _Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu_ — chanting the holy names, studying scripture, associating with devotees, visiting holy places and worshipping the deity — increase faith tremendously until it matures into _niṣṭhā_ and finally love, life's supreme perfection `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Acts without faith are useless here and hereafter; since no one truly lives without faith, the real question is its object and depth — faith must grow, by practice and grace, from curiosity to unshakable conviction to love. [36:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>
