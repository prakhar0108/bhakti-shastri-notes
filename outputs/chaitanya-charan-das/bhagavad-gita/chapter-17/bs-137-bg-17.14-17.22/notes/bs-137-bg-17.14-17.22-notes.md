## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 17.14 [00:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.14 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa lists the austerities of the body. All our activities can be classified as bodily, verbal and mental; the _sannyāsīs_' staff with three parts indicates that they will serve the Lord with body, mind and speech `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pūjā_ — basically respecting; doing worship is one way of expressing respect `00:00`.
- _Deva_ — can refer to the demigods, but Prabhupāda explains it refers to the Supreme Lord, the greatest of all _devas_ `00:00`.
- _Dvija_ — the _brāhmaṇas_; _guru_ — the specific spiritual master; _prājña_ — anyone advanced in knowledge `00:00 – 02:00`.
- _Śauca_ — cleanliness, internal and external `02:00`.
- _Ārjava_ — simplicity or straightforwardness; related to the name Arjuna `02:00`, `08:00`.
- _Brahmacarya_ — living on the spiritual platform; _ahiṁsā_ — non-violence `02:00`, `10:00`.

#### Whom to respect [00:00 – 02:00]

- The Supreme Lord, the _brāhmaṇas_ as spiritual teachers in general, one's own spiritual master, and anyone wise.
- Cāṇakya Paṇḍita says a powerful person may be respected in his own area, but a knowledgeable person earns respect everywhere because of his wisdom.

#### Brahmacarya and ahiṁsā paired [02:00]

- Just as Kṛṣṇa repeatedly couples _kāma_ and _krodha_, here He couples _brahmacarya_ and _ahiṁsā_: the way to control lust is _brahmacarya_, and the way to control anger is _ahiṁsā_.

#### Offer, and the feeling will come [04:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that one should offer, or learn to offer, respect. Austerity is something we may not feel like doing, but we do it.
- When a devotee told Prabhupāda he did not feel like offering obeisances, Prabhupāda said: offer them, and gradually the feeling will come.
- Advanced devotees sometimes faint in ecstasy at the sight of the deities, as Mahāprabhu did; if we cannot faint, at least we can fall down — offer obeisances. Doing it when we do not feel like it creates proper cultural impressions in the mind.

#### Ahiṁsā and the pause button [04:00 – 08:00]

- When someone provokes us, it takes willpower and discipline not to lash out — that is austerity.
- Worldly advice is to count to ten; a devotee may chant the _mahā-mantra_ ten times or remember verses on the danger of anger.
- Every devotee must develop their own **pause button**: not becoming motionless, but stopping the mind from following its default trajectory towards temptation and a fall.
- The pause button may be chanting, hearing _kīrtana_, or reading inspirational passages collected from scripture and classes.
- This is the individual aspect of Kṛṣṇa consciousness: the principles are universal, but the way each person fights their mind differs.

#### Simple living, high thinking [08:00 – 12:00]

- The simpler we live, the more time we have for higher thought; the more complex our living, the more our thoughts get caught up in living itself.
- A devotee may use technology, but media and cultural pressure push people to acquire gadgets and trinkets for glamour and prestige. Each devotee must decide what engagement with material things is necessary for their service and social position, and what is sucking them in.
- Many people end up with "simply living and hardly thinking." Everyone thinks — even a dog attracted to another dog is thinking — but thinking at the bodily, animal level is not real thinking; real thinking is at the spiritual level `10:00`.
- The more wealth and status we gain, the more we must struggle to maintain it, bringing entanglements, irritations and agitations; so a devotee lives as simply as time, place and circumstance allow.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bodily austerity is respect for the worthy, cleanliness, simplicity, celibacy and non-violence — practised even when we do not feel like it, so that the right feelings and impressions gradually develop. [00:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.15 [12:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.15 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes austerity of speech. He is speaking here about austerity in general, not specifically _bhakti_: even from a material point of view one should discipline one's speech `12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Anudvega-kara_ — words that do not agitate others `12:00`.
- _Satya_ — truthful; _priya_ — pleasing; _hita_ — beneficial `12:00`.
- _Svādhyāya_ — study; _abhyāsa_ — practice or repetition; Prabhupāda translates the pair as regularly reciting scripture `12:00`, `22:00`.

#### Sarcasm is a misuse of cleverness [12:00 – 14:00]

- Sarcasm is often considered intelligent — the more one hurts others, the cleverer one feels.
- The desirable use of cleverness is to bring others closer to us, and through us closer to Kṛṣṇa, or at least to the right track. Agitating speech usually alienates rather than educates.

#### The sādhu cuts — but what does he cut? [14:00 – 16:00]

- The _sādhu_ cuts the material attachments of conditioned souls. In the First Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_, Vidura speaks strongly to free a relative from his attachments.
- But Vidura had a long, deep and intimate relationship with him beforehand; that is why his words penetrated.
- Reckless chastisement may cut not the _anarthas_ but the seed of _bhakti_, which is meant to be nourished; people may then get lost.

#### Good intentions are not good enough [16:00 – 18:00]

- People must feel trust in us and see us as well-wishers; otherwise they view us through default filters of suspicion and scepticism and close down.
- Most relationship conflicts in families and social circles come from careless, insensitive speech.
- Kṛṣṇa puts non-agitation first, then truth: not "speak falsehood because it is palatable," but speak palatable truth. If some truth is unpalatable, better not to speak it — but never speak falsehood.
- The teacher cites a saying that the most important power for one who wants to rule the world is proper speech: by it one strikes alliances, makes friends, forges treaties, builds bridges and mobilises people and resources `18:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's compassion [18:00 – 20:00]

- Prabhupāda said devotees should call a spade a spade, but he distinguished philosophical differences from cultural etiquette.
- When he first came to New York he stayed with Dr. Mishra, a _māyāvādī_; despite strong philosophical differences, Prabhupāda cooked for and served him, and Dr. Mishra later said Prabhupāda's regulated habits kept him from falling sick.
- People could feel Prabhupāda's compassion because of his purity and potency. Preaching is not a licence to speak harshly; it means speaking so that people come closer to Kṛṣṇa.

#### Feedback with care [20:00 – 22:00]

- As an editor for _Back to Godhead_, the teacher follows a "plus–minus–plus" formula when giving feedback on articles: some encouraging positives, some room for improvement, and end with a positive.
- Non-agitating speech helps people calm down and open their minds and hearts.
- A doctor giving a cancer diagnosis should neither panic nor be stone-hearted, but maintain objectivity and deliver bad news in a non-agitating way.

#### Prabhupāda's recitation of verses [22:00 – 24:00]

- Prabhupāda would initially just quote verses in his classes, but when he started _Bhāgavatam_ classes he took a dramatic step: verses were written out, he recited them, and he had all devotees recite after him, correcting their pronunciation.
- The devotees there were much wilder than in New York's Lower East Side; people who had gone high on drugs were treated like "LSD saints" whose words were wisdom. Recitation forced everyone to become serious.
- Hearing is passive; when we must speak after hearing, we take things more attentively and gravely.

#### Memorisation unburdens the heart [24:00 – 28:00]

- Memorising verses may seem a strain, but nothing worthwhile is easy. Memorisation will not burden the head; it will unburden the heart.
- In times of stress and distress, reciting an appropriate verse — such as one about not being agitated by misery and pleasure — can have a tremendously calming effect. Meditating on translations also helps, and the original Sanskrit has its own potency.
- **Bank account and pocket:** knowledge in books is like money in the bank; knowledge in memory is like money in the pocket — readily accessible.
- **Soldier without a weapon:** a soldier who says "wait, let me go get my weapon" will be shot; memorised verses are ready weapons for taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa.
- In spiritual circles, one should quote scripture to back up what one says `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Austere speech is truthful, pleasing, beneficial and non-agitating — palatable truth spoken by a trusted well-wisher — and is anchored in memorised scripture, which becomes ready shelter in distress. [12:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.16 [28:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.16 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes austerities of the mind; the teacher takes them one by one `28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Manaḥ-prasāda_ — satisfaction of the mind `28:00`.
- _Saumyatva_ — gentleness `34:00`.
- Simplicity — here a simplicity of mind, without duplicity `28:00`, `34:00`.
- _Mauna_ — literally silence; Prabhupāda translates it here as gravity `34:00`.
- _Ātma-vinigraha_ — self-control `38:00`.
- _Bhāva-saṁśuddhi_ — purification of one's existence `28:00`, `40:00`.

#### Satisfaction is a decision [28:00 – 32:00]

- Satisfaction is not just an emotion that comes on its own; it is also a choice.
- If a relative has a bigger house and I keep thinking about it, I cannot be satisfied; but if I decide that the size of my house is not my goal, I can be. We are determined not by our possessions but by our decisions, values and commitments.
- Prabhupāda writes that the more we think of sense enjoyment, the more dissatisfied the mind becomes; satisfaction comes only by taking the mind away from such thoughts.
- The mind defaults to seeing what we do not have; satisfaction comes from thinking of what we do have.
- The advertising industry is a dissatisfaction-producing industry; customer dissatisfaction fuels the consumer economy. The essential economy is the necessities economy — food, clothing, shelter. One needs a cell phone, but not the latest model every time a feature is added `30:00 – 32:00`.
- We cannot just "not think" of things; we need something to think of. Prabhupāda says: think of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes. With an all-attractive alternative object of thought, the battle with dissatisfaction becomes much easier to win `32:00`.

#### Gentleness and simplicity of mind [34:00]

- After seeing the frightening universal form in Chapter 11, Arjuna asked Kṛṣṇa to show His gentle form; gentleness of mind is valued similarly.
- Harsh thoughts about others make us complex. We are not God and cannot understand everything; we should not get lost in philosophical intricacies but focus on what is most important for our spiritual life.

#### Mauna as gravity [34:00 – 38:00]

- _Mauna_ would normally be an austerity of speech, but Kṛṣṇa puts it under the mind, and Prabhupāda aptly translates it as gravity.
- Someone on a vow of silence who writes endlessly on a slate ends up communicating more, with more trouble for everyone — is that serving a purpose?
- People speak because they find sound more tolerable than silence: their minds are agitated, screaming and screeching, so they talk, or turn on the television, to get away from the mind. Real _mauna_ is a peaceful mind.
- The opposite of gravity is frivolity or levity — like telling an unrelated joke at a funeral.
- We are all on the verge of death; understanding the impermanence and fragility of material existence makes us grave.
- Prabhupāda writes that another way to become grave is to think of the welfare of others. Thinking about our problems without a plan only makes them bigger; meditating on Kṛṣṇa for long may be hard without love, but we can think — as a doctor thinks about curing patients — how to connect others with Kṛṣṇa `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Self-control and purity begin in the mind [38:00 – 40:00]

- All falls begin with the mind, with contemplation on sense objects — whether the trigger is something seen outside or a flashback from memory. Unchecked contemplation leads to degradation.
- Self-control means not frivolously thinking about sense objects, but focusing on Kṛṣṇa and on connecting others with Him.
- Pure and impure actions begin with pure and impure thoughts. It may not be easy, but as soon as we notice the mind has gone off track, we bring it back.
- For a devotee, _mauna_ is not just silence but gravity, which comes from meditating on, thinking about and even talking about Kṛṣṇa `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Mental austerity is choosing satisfaction, gentleness, gravity, self-control and purity — achieved not by blanking the mind but by giving it a better object: Kṛṣṇa's pastimes and the welfare of others. [28:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.17 [40:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.17 -->

**Context.** Having described austerities of body, speech and mind, Kṛṣṇa explains that these can be performed in the three modes, beginning with goodness `40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- With great, strong faith — Prabhupāda translates it as transcendental faith `40:00`.
- Without desire for the results `42:00`.

#### Non-attachment to results [42:00]

- Again and again Kṛṣṇa makes non-attachment to results the characteristic of goodness; the attachment is primarily to Kṛṣṇa, not to the results.

#### Transcendental faith in goodness? [42:00]

- If the faith is transcendental, why is the austerity said to be in goodness? Gradually it will become transcendental as the person reaches transcendental consciousness.
- By the association of devotees we may have faith in transcendence even before we are at that level, and so practise the principles of pure devotional service, inspired by a pure devotee, though not yet pure ourselves.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Austerity in goodness is done with strong faith and without attachment to results; inspired by pure devotees, such faith can carry us towards transcendence even before we are pure. [40:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.18 [42:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.18 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes austerity in passion — performed for respect, honour and worship, out of pride — and calls it flickering and impermanent `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Satkāra_ — respect, especially being felicitated in a public forum `42:00`.
- Honour and worship — performed to obtain them `42:00`.
- _Dambha_ — the desire to show off with pride `42:00`.
- Flickering, not lasting long `42:00`.

#### Why passionate austerity does not last [42:00 – 44:00]

- Someone vows never to eat sweets again, partly to show others how austere they are; it lasts a day, a week, a month — then in private they compensate with extra instalments.
- Avoiding sweets for a valid reason is fine; the point is about austerity done for honour.

#### Body fasting, ego feasting [44:00]

- If I fast on Ekādaśī while watching who is eating how much and judging their lack of sense control, my body may be fasting but my ego is feasting. Such fasting boosts the ego and becomes a hurdle to advancement.
- Fasting is generally good, but austerity done with passionate intentions increases passion and becomes counterproductive.
- What we do is important, but why we do it is even more important.
- This is for introspection, not for judging others: someone else's fasting may come from serious commitment `44:00`.

#### Arranging to be worshipped [46:00]

- Some arrange to be worshipped by subordinates, having their feet washed and offered articles of worship.
- Some _sādhus_ pose for pictures raising their feet for people to take the dust; this is not a saintly attitude. Devotees photographing the spiritual master's lotus feet is okay, but posing like that can come from pride.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Austerity done for honour is unstable and inflates the ego — the body fasts while the ego feasts; the motive behind austerity matters more than the act. [42:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.19 [46:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes austerity in ignorance: performed out of foolishness, with self-torture, or to destroy others `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mūḍha_ — foolishness `46:00`.
- Tormenting oneself `46:00`.
- Austerity aimed at destroying others `46:00`.

#### The demon who pleased Lord Śiva [46:00 – 48:00]

- Prabhupāda gives the example of a demon who performed austerity to please Lord Śiva by cutting off his own flesh and offering it into the fire.
- When he was about to cut off his own head, Lord Śiva appeared — not because he was pleased, but out of pity.
- Asked for a benediction, the demon asked that whomever he touched would die — austerity for causing distress and devastation to others.

#### The neighbour gets double [48:00]

- An anecdotal story that illustrates the principle: God appeared to a man after his austerity and offered anything he wanted, on condition that his neighbour would get double, to cultivate a bond between them.
- The man asked God to make him blind in one eye, so that his neighbour would lose both.
- In ignorance, others' misery is one's own pleasure.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Austerity in ignorance is foolish self-torment or effort aimed at harming others; envy can make a person accept loss for himself just to see others suffer more. [46:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.20 [50:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.20 -->

**Context.** After _yajña_ and _tapas_, Kṛṣṇa now describes _dāna_, beginning with charity in goodness `48:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Given because it is worth giving and appropriate to give `50:00`.
- Without expecting anything back `50:00`.
- At the right place, time and to the right person `50:00`.

#### Right person, right time [50:00]

- The common thread of goodness: no attachment, no expectation of a material result.
- Sacrifice and charity should be given to the right person at the right time; for example, charity to _brāhmaṇas_ on Ekādaśī. Certain times, places and circumstances are more auspicious.

#### No indiscriminate charity [50:00 – 52:00]

- Prabhupāda says there is no recommendation to give charity indiscriminately.
- This does not mean the starving should not be helped; it means help should actually help. Traditionally the poor were given food.
- Money given to someone with a _śūdra_ mentality may be misused — as in families where an alcoholic husband spends money on alcohol and abuses his wife and children. Better to give resources that cannot be misused, like food.
- The point is not to be hard-hearted, but intelligent, so that our help really helps.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Charity in goodness is given as duty, expecting nothing back, at the right place and time to a fit recipient — compassionate yet intelligent, so that the gift truly helps. [50:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.21 [52:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.21 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes charity in passion: given expecting a return, or given grudgingly `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pratyupakāra_ — expecting something in return; the opposite of the earlier "not expecting a return" `52:00`.
- _Parikliṣṭa_ — grudgingly, resentfully `52:00`.

#### Charity as an investment [52:00 – 54:00]

- In passion the focus is "what will I get in return?" — like a savings account, or hoping God will multiply the gift tenfold and return it later. That is not a noble motive.
- Charity given only because of social pressure, while internally resenting it, is grudging.
- That grudging attitude creates impressions that keep us from rising above passion.
- Our actions are important, but our motivations are even more important `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Charity becomes passionate when it is an investment expecting returns or a grudging response to pressure; the motive shapes the impression it leaves on the heart. [52:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.22 [54:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.22 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes charity in ignorance: given without considering place, time or person, and without respect or attention `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Wrong place, wrong time, unworthy person `54:00`.
- Without respect and without proper attention `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Harmful and humiliating gifts [56:00]

- **Harmful:** a drunkard coming out of a bar with bottles hands a half-open bottle to a beggar — "take this, drink and enjoy." The gift harms the recipient.
- **Humiliating:** recipients made to grovel, fall at the feet and come back ten, twenty, a hundred times before getting anything.
- Charity means offering back to Kṛṣṇa what we have received from Him. Respecting others as human beings and as parts of Kṛṣṇa means that even a wealthy benefactor does not assert authority over the recipient.
- In charity in ignorance there is no consideration at all of place, time or person, which makes it very unfortunate.

#### Next class [56:00 – 58:00]

- Kṛṣṇa will next explain how all activities can be made transcendental by reciting _oṁ tat sat_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Charity in ignorance is careless — harmful gifts, the wrong recipient, or giving that humiliates; real charity respects the recipient as a part of Kṛṣṇa and returns to Kṛṣṇa what is His. [54:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
