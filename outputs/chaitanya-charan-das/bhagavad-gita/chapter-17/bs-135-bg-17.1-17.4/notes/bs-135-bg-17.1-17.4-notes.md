### Bhagavad-gita 17.2 [04:00 – 08:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.2 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says faith is of three kinds — goodness, passion and ignorance — according to the nature the embodied soul has acquired `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Nature (_svabhāva_) — the way we think, act, perceive and respond; born of past _karma_, as discussed in an earlier class `04:00`.
- _Śraddhā_ — faith; our nature determines our faith `04:00`.

#### No one is faithless [04:00 – 08:00]

- "Faithless" usually describes an atheist, but atheists too have faith — in the non-existence of God.
- Non-existence of God cannot be proved: invisibility is no proof of non-existence `06:00`.
- From the standpoint of empirical analysis, theism and atheism are equally matters of faith; the difference:
  - atheism offers no process for experiential verification;
  - Vedic theism offers yoga, including _bhakti-yoga_, through which what scripture teaches can be experientially verified `06:00`.
- Both must ascribe inconceivability to something — how the universe and its majestic variety came about, how matter could give rise to consciousness, organising capability and complex design. Atheists ascribe inconceivable characteristics to matter, yet have no process to realise it `06:00`.
- So atheism is "faith in faithlessness" — itself a kind of religion `06:00 – 08:00`.

#### Faith can change [08:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that the nature we have acquired can be changed by associating with a bona fide spiritual master and abiding by his rules and the scriptures — considering things carefully with intelligence.
- We are born with a nature that shapes our faith, but the whole Gītā is spoken to elevate our faith.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Everyone lives by faith — even atheists, who put faith in God's non-existence; faith follows our acquired nature, but under a bona fide spiritual master and scripture that nature, and therefore our faith, can be elevated. [04:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.3 [08:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says one's faith accords with one's existence, and that a person is made of faith: as is one's faith, so is the person `08:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sattva_ (here) — one's existence; faith is shaped according to one's existence `08:00`, `12:00`.
- _Śraddhā-maya_ — made of faith, just as _ānanda-maya_ means made of bliss `08:00`.
- _Puruṣa_ — the person `08:00`.

#### Faith in everyday life [10:00 – 12:00]

- **Paycheque** — after a month's work we accept a piece of paper instead of grains or cattle; we may call someone who doubts its value a fool, but we are putting faith in the monetary system.
- **Currency collapse** — in the 1929 American depression and in Zimbabwe currencies were terribly devalued; people once took a bucket of notes to buy grain for one meal, and the shopkeeper threw away the notes and kept the bucket, which was worth more. The teacher clarifies these are extreme cases, cited only to show faith's role everywhere `10:00`.
- **Flying** — we put faith in the airline and the pilot. It may be reasonable faith, but the point is that faith is present and essential `12:00`.
- How much faith shapes us we may not know until we seriously introspect `12:00`.

#### Faith as our essence [12:00]

- In 17.2 faith was a product of nature; here Kṛṣṇa says faith is, in one sense, our essence — intrinsic to our make-up, not just externally expressed.
- Prabhupāda explains that faith originally comes out of the mode of goodness; faith in a demigod, a created god or a mental concoction, if strong, can produce works in goodness `12:00`.

#### Where faith is situated [14:00 – 16:00]

- The pure soul has nothing to do with gross or subtle matter; its original, transcendental faith is in Kṛṣṇa — loving and serving Him makes it happy.
- In the material world even goodness is not pure; it is contaminated by passion and ignorance.
- The soul is covered by a subtle body that shapes its perceptions and actions; our current faith is situated in the subtle body and is in goodness, passion or ignorance accordingly.
- This faith gradually evolves — becoming stronger and purer as we are purified.
- By regular hearing, faith arises as the sun rises in the morning: always there, just not realised `16:00`.
- **Filtered light** — a lamp that gives soothing white light emits jarring red light if covered by a red filter; the soul's natural faith in Kṛṣṇa, covered by the modes, is directed elsewhere `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
"As is the faith, so is the person": faith pervades every part of life; the soul's original faith is in Kṛṣṇa, now filtered through the subtle body's modes, and it is gradually purified by regular hearing. [08:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 17.4 [16:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:17.4 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes how worship differs in the three modes; the teacher focuses on worship of the _devatās_ in goodness and of ghosts and spirits in ignorance. He uses the verse to discuss levels within the modes, ghosts, and the gradual evolution of worship `16:00 – 18:00`, `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Devatās_ — demigods; their worship is generally in goodness `18:00`, `30:00`.
- _Bhūta_ — a soul with only a subtle body, without a gross body: a ghost `30:00 – 34:00`.
- _Preta_ — a soul in a transition body after death; becomes a _pitṛ_ (ancestor) when the next body is prepared `44:00`.
- _Pañcopāsanā_ — worship of five chosen deities `46:00 – 50:00`.

#### Sāttvika and tāmasika Purāṇas [16:00 – 18:00]

- The teacher calls a common categorisation of Purāṇas by the deity worshipped "a simplistic understanding."
- All Purāṇas describe the same universe and mention Viṣṇu, Brahmā and Śiva; what changes is the aspirations and conceptions of the audience they address.
- Tāmasika Purāṇas focus on achievements and ambitions in ignorance; they are not wrong, but cater to a lower level, and are accepted to the extent they agree with the higher reality `18:00`.

#### Levels within the modes [18:00 – 28:00]

- Apparent contradiction: worship of _devatās_ is called goodness, yet worship of Śiva or Kālī is said to be in ignorance.
- Resolution: faith in scripture itself already requires a basic level of goodness; within that spectrum there are gradations `18:00 – 20:00`:
  - Viṣṇu worship — goodness-goodness;
  - Brahmā worship — goodness-passion;
  - Śiva worship — goodness-ignorance.
- **Animals** — all animal bodies are generally in ignorance (unable to perceive spiritual reality), yet within that `20:00`, `24:00`:
  - ignorance-goodness — cows;
  - ignorance-passion — tigers and lions;
  - ignorance-ignorance — vultures, monkeys, jackals.
- Exceptions like Gajendra: spiritual life cannot be _started_ in an animal body, but can be _continued_ there if begun in a previous human birth `20:00`.
- **Devotee animals** — a book by one of Prabhupāda's _sannyāsī_ disciples describes temple experiences: a dog that came every evening at _ārati_ time and sat peacefully, not retaliating when chased away; a cow that came by itself at _ārati_ time `20:00 – 22:00`.
- **Two principles** — real life is complex and no principle applies in isolation `22:00 – 24:00`:
  - Spiritual principle: attraction to Kṛṣṇa, once developed, may be covered but is never lost.
  - Material principle: what one is attached to at death, one attains.
  - Bharata Mahārāja thought of a deer at death and got a deer's body, but not deer consciousness: he ate whatever grass was near the sages' hermitages and sought their association to hear about spirituality.
- A writer the teacher cites observes that a pet and an over-attached owner may exchange positions in the next life; even a devotee too attached to a pet may get a dog's body yet keep advancing spiritually — hence a "devotee dog" `24:00`.
- **Why a fool is likened to a cow** — the _Bhāgavatam_ compares to a cow (or donkey) one who thinks he is the body, takes bodily relations as his own and his country as worshipable, and goes to holy places only to bathe, not to associate with the learned. Though sacred, the cow is in an animal body with ignorance — e.g., it can be made to give milk by a look-alike calf `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### Scripture reciprocates with our preconceptions [28:00 – 30:00]

- The teacher cites a Purāṇic statement that any scripture about Lord Śiva is to be accepted only when it agrees with the Vaiṣṇava scriptures: knowing that Viṣṇu is supreme is the means of liberation, and anything else is for illusion.
- Why would scripture contain illusion? Scripture does not _delude_ us; rather, the truth _eludes_ us — "elusion," not "delusion."
- Scriptures reciprocate as Kṛṣṇa does: if we are attached to ignorance or passion, they reveal corresponding realities `30:00`.
- To rise beyond material existence, we must rise beyond our preconceptions — approaching scripture submissively and open-mindedly and learning from those learned in its conclusions `30:00`.
- The teacher skips a full discussion of demigod worship, having covered it in the Īśopaniṣad classes on _sambhūti_ and _asambhūti_ `30:00`.

#### What ghosts are [30:00 – 36:00]

- The teacher draws on an article he wrote (also published in _Back to Godhead_) giving the Vedic explanation of ghosts `30:00 – 32:00`.
- Three levels of being: soul, subtle body, gross body.
- Normally at death the soul leaves with the subtle body and enters another body — transmigration `32:00`.
- After suicide or extremely sinful acts, a new body may not come easily: one destined to live 60 years who commits suicide at 25 may remain disembodied for the remaining 35 years — a soul with only a subtle body, a ghost `32:00`.
- Not every eerie event is a ghost; there can be hoaxes, mistakes or illusion `34:00`.
- **Possession** — the ghost (soul plus subtle body) enters someone's gross body; the original person becomes dormant and the ghost takes over, sometimes speaking in a different voice with different behaviour. It happens especially to those living in ignorance — intoxication and the like `34:00`, `36:00 – 38:00`.
- In the Fifth Canto's hierarchy of beings, ghosts are placed above humans — not more evolved in consciousness, but more powerful because they exist on the subtle level `34:00`.
- **Why they possess** — ghosts are miserable: they have a mind and knowledge-acquiring senses full of desires, but no working senses to enjoy `36:00`.
- **Sick at a feast** — like being restricted to soup while everyone else enjoys delicacies at a feast; for us the sickness passes, but for ghosts it lasts as long as their ghostly existence `36:00`.

#### Evidence and openness [38:00 – 42:00]

- Alfred Wallace, co-proposer of the theory of evolution with Darwin, studied spiritism extensively and said he did not expect others to believe him, only to doubt the infallibility of their own preconceptions `38:00`.
- The book _Human Devolution_ presents evidence for a subtle realm: for consciousness (past-life memories, near-death experiences) and for mind (concentration altering random-number generators) `38:00`.
- Three levels of ghostly intervention `38:00 – 40:00`:
  1. **Communication** — through mediums, e.g. a hand moving to write messages;
  2. **Apparition** — seeing a deceased relative's outline, sometimes reported by multiple people;
  3. **Possession** — the strongest, where the being enters the person.
- **Kīrtana at a haunted estate** — when devotees lived on John Lennon's estate, an area reputed to be haunted was feared even by the gardeners; during _kīrtana_ there they felt pressure increase, as if the ghosts resisted, then a release — and no disturbance thereafter `40:00`.
- Those who live in goodness and practise devotion need not be afraid or paranoid; even a possessed person who chants regularly will be freed `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### Worship of ghosts in ignorance [42:00]

- **Extra-sensory deception** — beside extra-sensory perception (e.g. clairvoyance, reading a sealed envelope), there is "extra-sensory deception": knowledge seeming to come from a higher being actually comes from a lower one, as with authors who claim a spirit guides their writing.
- Ghosts are lower in spiritual receptivity but more powerful than humans; if appeased, they give power — but also take control of the worshipper. This is worship in ignorance.

#### Bhūta and preta [44:00 – 46:00]

- _Bhūta_: a soul with a subtle body only.
- _Preta_: according to the _Garuḍa Purāṇa_, everyone at death receives a transition body — grosser than subtle, subtler than gross — making them a _preta_.
- In the _piṇḍa-dāna_ ritual, by a subtle transformation the food offered goes toward making the person's next body; once complete, the _preta_ becomes a _pitṛ_ (ancestor). A grossly impious person goes to _naraka_ `44:00`.
- This is not for devotees to perform, as Kṛṣṇa takes care of their arrangement `44:00`.
- Prabhupāda's purport mentions people who worship anyone and everyone — e.g. those who made money in the black market during the war — as non-scriptural worship in ignorance `46:00`.

#### Pañcopāsanā: the historical view [46:00 – 50:00]

- Before Śaṅkarācārya, Buddhism and Jainism (both arising around the 5th century BC) had spread across the land; after Aśoka adopted Buddhism, state resources were used to spread it and many kings followed `46:00`.
- The teacher notes a strong caution to kings against Buddhism's influence: it focuses on negating the world, while kings must be responsible and competent in administration `46:00 – 48:00`.
- Śaṅkarācārya re-established Vedic culture; seeing many varieties of worship, he said one may worship five primary deities `48:00`:
  - Viṣṇu, Śiva, the Goddess, Sūrya and Gaṇeśa.
- Śaṅkarācārya was a covered personalist — songs like _Bhaja Govindam_ speak of devotion — but taught _advaita-vedānta_: beyond the forms of gods is the impersonal Brahman. So the idea was to choose one's deity and, by worshipping, go beyond form to the formless `48:00`.
- _Pañcopāsanā_ differs from _pāñcarātrika-vidhi_, which concerns how the Lord manifest as the Deity is to be worshipped `48:00 – 50:00`.

#### Pañcopāsanā as an evolution of consciousness [50:00 – 56:00]

- Prabhupāda gives an "intriguing explanation": five kinds of worshippers forming a gradual progression `50:00`:
  1. **Śāktas** — nature worshippers, seeing only matter and energy; in a broad sense scientists are _śāktas_. A person sees one lightning bolt destroy a house built with great labour and bows to nature's power `50:00`.
  2. **Sūrya worshippers** — asking where nature's power comes from, one sees the sun as its source: from material phenomena to the energy behind them `52:00`.
  3. **Gāṇapatyas** — seeing majestic energetic beings (lions, elephants, whales), one conceives the supervising reality in animal-like form — _zoomorphism_; Gaṇeśa's elephant-like head corresponds to this level `52:00 – 54:00`.
  4. **Śaivas** — beyond temporary matter is eternal spirit, but, as matter has form, spirit is thought formless: impersonalism `54:00`.
  5. **Vaiṣṇavas** — a human-like being controls all existence, as a government that seems impersonal to the ignorant has a person in charge `54:00`.
- The teacher clarifies that this is not about worshippers of Gaṇeśa alone: all systems of worship in the world can be classed into these five levels `58:00`.

#### Material versus transcendental Vaiṣṇavism [54:00 – 58:00]

- If consciousness is still materialistic, even Vaiṣṇavism is affected by the modes.
- In the Third Canto, Kapiladeva tells Devahūti of _bhakti_ in the modes (roughly, as the teacher notes) `56:00`:
  - devotees quarrelling, or seeking name, fame, power and prestige — lower modes;
  - _bhakti_ for liberation rather than Kṛṣṇa's pleasure — goodness.
- _Bhakti_ itself is transcendental, but our practice of it can be within the modes, and such Vaiṣṇavism can be restrictive and divisive `56:00`.
- Example: we say the movement is for the whole world, yet in practice we may insist a person take initiation from "my guru," and feel betrayed if they choose another. We never owned that person; "we can't legislate inspiration" `56:00`.
- Transcendental Vaiṣṇavism — the pure soul serving the supreme spiritual personal Absolute with pure spiritual love — is the highest worship; situated there, one sees the true unity of all living beings and works for everyone's welfare on Kṛṣṇa's behalf `58:00`.
- All systems of worship are like a pyramid, meant gradually to lead to its pinnacle: pure devotional love and the reality where it is eternally relished `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Worship reveals the mode of one's faith — from ghosts, through demigods, up through the stages of _pañcopāsanā_ — and every level is meant, like a pyramid, to rise to transcendental devotion to Kṛṣṇa, which alone truly unites all beings. [16:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
