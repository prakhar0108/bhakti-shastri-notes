## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 16.7 [00:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.7 -->

**Context.** The demoniac do not know what is to be done and what is not to be done; neither cleanliness, proper behaviour nor truth is found in them `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Acting properly and acting improperly_ — the scriptural do's and don'ts, the prescriptions and prohibitions the demoniac disregard `00:00 – 02:00`.
- _Cleanliness_ — Prabhupāda usually means internal cleanliness, achieved by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa `08:00`.
- _Demoniac_ — a mentality, not a physical form `04:00`.

#### Scripture as the doctor's guideline [00:00 – 02:00]

- Our behaviour should be guided by scripture; only then can we act properly.
- **Sick patient:** as a sick person must follow the doctor's guidelines, we are all sick in material existence, and scriptures guide us to recover spiritually and regain our eternal happiness and loving relationship with Kṛṣṇa.
- Scripture says that devotees of Lord Viṣṇu are divine and those opposed to Him are demons; opposing Viṣṇu includes opposing not only His incarnations but also His words `02:00`.

#### Who is a demon? [02:00 – 06:00]

- When Prabhupāda was asked whether everyone not divine is demoniac, he was clear about the point; the teacher stresses care in using the word `02:00 – 04:00`.
- A demon need not have horns, fangs and a hideous face — that is a caricature.
- The teacher recalls an article in _Back to Godhead_ arguing that your local politician, postman, grocer, neighbour — even you — might be a demon: it is a mentality `04:00`.
- **Mahābhārata example:** the _Rāmāyaṇa_ war was between humans and _rākṣasas_, but the _Mahābhārata_ war was among humans, with _rākṣasas_ on both sides:
  - Bhīma's son, born of a _rākṣasī_, fought for the Pāṇḍavas.
  - On the other side was the _rākṣasa_ Alambuṣa, a relative of the Bakāsura killed by Bhīma, seeking revenge.
  - Duryodhana did not have a _rākṣasa_'s body, yet had a demoniac mentality.
- Some are overtly demoniac; many are only under demoniac influence — Godless not by deliberate choice but because their society never gave them scriptural knowledge. This can be corrected, which is the theme of the chapter's end: act according to _śāstra_ `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### Scripture as a manual for living [06:00 – 08:00]

- Today many see scripture as outdated, pre-scientific mythology or mere metaphor for morality.
- Scriptures are a manual for living; without the manual we at best underuse a gadget, at worst misuse it and destroy it — or ourselves — like an electronic device that catches fire when used wrongly.

#### Manu-saṁhitā: principle, not literal replication [08:00 – 10:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes the _Manu-saṁhitā_, the law of the human race, and then speaks about women.
- Many of its instructions Prabhupāda did not try to implement today, since the cultural situation is very different; out of context some statements may seem casteist or sexist.
- The teacher cites Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura as saying the present _Manu-saṁhitā_ has been interpolated by _brāhmaṇas_ wanting to promote their own position.
- Prabhupāda was expert at presenting according to time, place and circumstance; what he upheld is the principle — to live in harmony with God's guidelines for spiritual advancement.

#### The position of women: maternal vs sexual identity [10:00 – 18:00]

- Statements about women needing protection may seem a throwback, but the point is that everyone should be situated where they can best advance spiritually, contributing according to their psychophysical nature `10:00`.
- No one can take a mother's place, physically or emotionally; today motherhood has been trivialised and sexuality glamorised.
- A woman can take up a **sexual identity** or a **maternal identity** — both body-based; at the level of the soul she is a part and devotee of Kṛṣṇa. Vedic culture addressed women as "mother" and emphasised the maternal identity `10:00 – 12:00`.
- Why the maternal role is more conducive, according to the teacher `12:00 – 16:00`:
  - Identity built on sex appeal creates insecurity — it fades with age, leaving loneliness, and even while it lasts women feel used for their bodies.
  - Sexualisation (reducing a person to a sexual creature) harms women through insecurity, men through agitation, and children, since motherhood becomes unattractive — sociologists have noted that children deprived of a mother's love often grow up disturbed and disturb society.
  - Motherhood is a unique God-given privilege; the idea of independence often reduces to women becoming "copycats of men." The question is not whether women can succeed in any field, but what life's purpose is and how one's role is best played.
  - Maternal care can be offered throughout life, whereas sex appeal cannot; a more maternal dress, conduct and lifestyle brings peace to women and men alike.
- **This world is a station, not a destination** — life should be lived for maximum spiritual advancement `14:00`.
- When crimes happen against women, the perpetrators are to be blamed, not the women; the point is only that women need not self-identify as sexual creatures. Protection by father, husband or son is meant to let women contribute according to their nature and find fulfilment `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The first mark of the demoniac is conduct unguided by scripture; "demoniac" names a mentality anyone may fall under, and the remedy is to treat scripture as the manual for living — applying its principles, as Prabhupāda did, according to time, place and circumstance. [00:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.8 [18:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.8 -->

**Context.** The demoniac say the world is unreal, without foundation, with no God in control, produced merely of mutual combination and caused by lust alone `16:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Unreal_ — the world is false; the teacher says this generally refers to _Māyāvāda_ `18:00`.
- _Without foundation_ — the world may be real but has nothing beyond it `18:00`.
- _No God in control_ — the third Godless approach `18:00`.
- _Caused by lust_ — the world arose from lust and is meant only for fulfilling desires `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### From activity to mentality [18:00]

- The previous verse described activities; this one describes mentality.
- Activity, quality and mentality are interrelated; the Gītā does not separate them in watertight compartments.

#### Atheism throughout history [20:00 – 22:00]

- Atheism is not new: there have always been souls who rebel against God, and this world lets them either reform or continue rebelling.
- We should not whitewash Indian culture: foreign invaders did a lot of damage, but earlier ages were not "rosy-cosy" either.
- According to the teacher, among the classical languages Sanskrit produced the world's largest store of atheistic literature, through Buddhism and Jainism — "rebel religions" against Vedic authority.
- Interestingly, they were atheistic without being grossly materialistic: they had renunciates, regulated material engagement and held that there is no absolute controller; their enlightened beings are not the Absolute Truth, which they see as a kind of passive negation `22:00`.
- Prabhupāda writes that Buddhism, impersonalism and Vaiṣṇavism share the understanding that this world is a place of misery and that trying to enjoy it increases misery; they differ on what lies beyond `22:00`.

#### Atheism's stolen scientific glamour [22:00 – 28:00]

- In modern times atheism has become intellectually glamorous — a glamour it has stolen from science, masquerading in scientific garb.
- Science is a tool — like a map, mirror or microscope, an intellectual tool for studying nature — usable to infer the Transcendent controller or to deny Him.
- Theories that claim to explain creation without God are portrayed as scientific but, the teacher says, are pseudo-scientific; many of the world's greatest scientists have inferred God from their studies `24:00`, `26:00`.
- The teacher's historical sketch `24:00 – 28:00`:
  1. **Before Newton** — theism: God is both Creator and controller.
  2. **After Newton's laws of motion** — phenomena explained by laws; God may be Creator but not controller (deism).
  3. **After Darwin** — the theory of evolution, whose evidence the teacher calls flimsy ("unfit to survive" by its own standard), became popular as a bulwark for atheism and a tool to revile God as old-fashioned and unscientific.

#### No God, no morality [28:00 – 30:00]

- With no next life, no higher reality and no controller, there is no basis for right and wrong: "if I am clever or powerful enough to get away with it, who will stop me?" — the floodgates of immorality open.
- Within 50 to 100 years of _The Origin of Species_ (1859), Freud's theories reduced everything to sexuality and made religion seem a neurosis; the teacher calls them speculation taught as science.
- Sex is a predominant force in material life, but reducing everything to it perverts reality; "sexual freedom" and "sexual expression" are attractive-sounding words for immorality, with devastating results — skyrocketing divorce, children born outside marriage and a troubled society.

#### Is the world a dream? [30:00 – 34:00]

- Prabhupāda: though the demons say life is a dream, they are very expert in enjoying this dream.
- Those who fancy impersonalism call the world false, yet want to enjoy it without regulation — a double standard: if the world is unreal, why entangle oneself further in it?
- The world is a dream only in the sense that it is **temporary**; while it lasts it is real for us, since matter is Kṛṣṇa's energy.
- A dream conveys temporality, not necessarily unreality; the waking world and our bodily condition are also temporary and should not be taken too seriously.
- We cannot wish away the world or our entanglement; we must go beyond it through a process that involves regulating our conduct — which the demons refuse.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The demoniac worldview — no foundation, no God in control, nothing but lust — removes any basis for right and wrong; the world is a "dream" only in being temporary, so the response is not to deny or exploit it but to transcend it through regulated spiritual practice. [18:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.9 [34:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.9 -->

**Context.** Following such conclusions, the demoniac, lost to themselves and without intelligence, engage in unbeneficial, horrible works meant to destroy the world `34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vision / understanding_ — the conclusion that there is no God and no purpose but lust `34:00`.
- _Ugra-karma_ — terrible, painful activities, painful for themselves and for others `34:00`.
- _Unbeneficial_ — at best unbeneficial, at worst devastatingly destructive `34:00`.
- _Lost souls_ — metaphorical: the soul cannot be destroyed, but its spiritual awareness and inclinations are killed `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Alpa-buddhi_ — very little intelligence; intelligence restricted to the realm of matter `36:00`.

#### The earthbound are dearth-bound [36:00 – 38:00]

- Those whose vision is limited to "earth" (matter) feel perpetual dearth — shortage.
- The fundamental problem of economics: limited resources and unlimited desires; hence haves and have-nots.
- Even the haves are dearth-bound: **"greed makes the wealthy needy"** — they think they need more and cannot live without it.
- With a materialistic vision, one works in a frenzy for more and is never satisfied, because desires are complex, entangling and insatiable.

#### WWS — "worldwide stupidity" [38:00 – 40:00]

- **Work** — 8, 10, 12, 14 hours a day.
- **Watch** — TV filled with wealthy and sexually attractive people, which fills viewers with lust and greed; advertisements indirectly say "your hairstyle, shoes, dress, watch, phone, house are all wrong — get a new one."
- **Shop** — even to the point of "shopaholism," spending with credit cards and loans.
- Spending forces more work, which makes us tired, so we watch more TV, then shop more — an endless entanglement.

#### Weapons of mass destruction [40:00 – 46:00]

- Human intelligence should benefit other living beings; using it to build weapons that could wipe out the Earth's population is a great tragedy `40:00`.
- Ancient weapons like the _brahmāstra_ were powerful but precise — able, the teacher says, to kill an embryo in the womb without killing the mother; danger arose only when two met improperly, which is why Arjuna was anxious when his opponent was about to deploy one and both had to be expertly withdrawn `40:00 – 42:00`.
- Today's weapons are weapons of indiscriminate destruction.
- **Terrorism is the opposite of the kṣatriya mentality:** a _kṣatriya_ fights opponents who are equal, equipped and engaged (alert); terrorists attack civilians who are none of these — in malls, theatres and offices `42:00`.
- In Kali-yuga, people with destructive mindsets can access weapons that let one perverse person kill thousands at the click of a button `42:00`.
- Such weapons are hard even to destroy: buried they poison the soil, burnt the atmosphere, dumped in the ocean the water and marine life; proposals to rocket them into outer space would simply pollute space too `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Science cannot be blamed entirely: in the Second World War technology came into big play (aircraft turned trenches into graves); some scientists resigned rather than build weapons, but others took up the work, since for most people money rules `44:00`.
- Einstein bitterly regretted his letter urging America to develop nuclear weapons; the teacher finds it sadly symbolic that the best insight of the 20th century's best scientific brain — the interchangeability of matter and energy — became the basis for the atom bomb and one of the worst man-made destructions `44:00 – 46:00`.
- Prabhupāda also mentions animal killing: materialistic inventions are hailed as advancement of civilisation, but people grow more violent and cruel to animals and humans `46:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A vision limited to matter kills the soul's spiritual awareness and turns even great intelligence toward insatiable consumption and indiscriminate destruction — the earthbound remain dearth-bound however much they have. [34:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.10 [46:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.10 -->

**Context.** Taking shelter of insatiable lust, absorbed in pride and false prestige, and illusioned, the demoniac are attracted by the impermanent and sworn to unclean work `46:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kāma_ — lust, which everyone has; here it becomes the whole and soul of life `46:00`.
- _Āśraya_ — shelter: taking shelter of _kāma_ instead of Kṛṣṇa `46:00`.
- _Insatiable_ — lust can never be satisfied; the threshold for pleasure keeps rising `48:00`.
- _Asat_ — the temporary, which the illusioned accept as real, almost eternal `50:00`.
- _Unclean vows_ — determined commitment to inauspicious activities `50:00`.

#### Shelter of lust vs shelter of Kṛṣṇa [46:00 – 48:00]

- The demoniac think there is no problem so big that sex cannot solve it; in fact problems are only postponed, and often aggravated.
- To keep getting pleasure one must keep raising the threshold — more kinds of sex, more partners — because lust is insatiable.
- Everyone has lust, and there is a civilised way to engage and purify it: marriage, as in the Vedic progression and in other cultures too.
- Devotees understand there is no problem so big that Kṛṣṇa cannot solve it.
- Uncontrolled lust brings disease, divorce and sexual crime `48:00`.

#### Gross and subtle sex [48:00]

- Pride and prestige accompany lust; Prabhupāda speaks of gross sex and subtle sex — the subtle form being the desire for honour.

#### "Happily ever after" — accepting the temporary as real [50:00]

- Films end with "they lived happily ever after" — a white lie, the exact opposite of the reality (unhappy, and not forever); yet people want to believe it.

#### Unclean vows [50:00 – 54:00]

- Prabhupāda explains this can refer to left-handed _tantra_, where indiscriminate sex is considered a means to spiritual advancement, along with black magic.
- Examples of perversity done with determination, from a lack of any reference point for life:
  - A world record for a couple kissing continuously for 47 hours, splashed across newspapers `50:00 – 52:00`.
  - A model's headline-making promise to strip if India won the cricket World Cup final `52:00`.
  - An animal-rights organisation that does good work against cruelty but goes overboard, using women's exposed bodies for publicity — "not selfless but brainless"; some of its members told the model to strip for the animals instead `52:00 – 54:00`.
- When people lose scripture as a balanced moral reference, they become imbalanced.
- Next class: sense gratification and wealth as the primary infatuations of the demoniac `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Taking shelter of lust instead of Kṛṣṇa leads to insatiable craving, belief in temporary "happily ever after" fantasies and determined pursuit of the perverse; without scripture as a moral reference point, people lose all balance. [46:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
