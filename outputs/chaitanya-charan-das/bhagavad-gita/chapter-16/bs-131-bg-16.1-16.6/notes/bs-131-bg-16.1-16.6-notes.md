**Context.** Kṛṣṇa lists the transcendental qualities of those born of the divine nature; Prabhupāda groups the three verses because they describe the divine qualities as a group and a closing line at the end of 16.3 summarises all three `06:00`.

#### What we treasure becomes our nature [00:00]

- The demoniac consider arrogance and harshness their strengths — their treasure, something to have more of so they can enjoy.
- The divine consider other qualities their wealth.
- What we consider our wealth we hold close; eventually it becomes a part of us — our nature.

#### Structure of chapter 16 [04:00 – 06:00]

1. Verses 1–3 — divine qualities (described in only three verses; the rest of the chapter describes the demoniac).
2. Verses 1–9 — divine and demoniac qualities.
3. Verses 10–18 — activities of the demoniac; qualities show up in activities.
4. Verses 19–22 — consequences and causes of demoniac activities.
5. The conclusion — the choice to act in the divine or demoniac way, and how to act in the divine way.

#### Nature, nurture, culture and free will [06:00 – 10:00]

- **Nature** — tendencies, psychophysical characteristics, likes and dislikes we are born with; innate at the level of body and mind (not the soul), based on previous _karma_.
- **Nurture** — how we are cared for: parents' values and the environment we grow up in, and today also television.
- **Culture** — the larger society and our association.
- These shape our behaviour but do not determine it; the system is not fatalistic or completely deterministic.
- **Identical twins:** monozygotic twins share identical genetics and similar parenting, yet grow up with different moods and dispositions — because beyond nature, nurture and culture we are individual souls with free will, which is covered but never lost.
- Kṛṣṇa describes the two natures so we can choose: the divine nature is the way to liberation, the demoniac to bondage `10:00`.

#### Protecting the child: garbhādhāna-saṁskāra [10:00 – 12:00]

- The godly environment for a child begins with the _garbhādhāna-saṁskāra_: by purifying their consciousness, parents can attract a pure-hearted soul into the womb.
- Every soul has free will, but parents can do their best to protect the child — physically, emotionally (the child should feel cared for and loved) and spiritually (facilitated to make choices that bring it closer to Kṛṣṇa).

#### Qualities for sannyāsīs: fearlessness, purification, knowledge [12:00 – 22:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that all godly people need these qualities, but certain ones are especially important for certain _varṇas_ and _āśramas_ `14:00`.
- **Fearlessness** `12:00 – 14:00`:
  - Fearlessness comes not by the absence of fear but by the presence of a cause that dwarfs fear.
  - A patriot may overcome fear of injury for the nation's sake, but the highest cause is pure devotion to Kṛṣṇa, which alone brings eternal benefit to ourselves and others.
  - A devotee's fearlessness comes from awareness of Kṛṣṇa's presence in the heart: "Don't tell Kṛṣṇa how big your problems are; tell your problems how big Kṛṣṇa is."
  - Especially for _sannyāsīs_, who traditionally travelled alone through the jungles without others to protect them.
- **Purification of existence** `14:00 – 20:00`:
  - The focus is on the impurity of lust, avoided by shunning external triggers (close association with the opposite sex) and internal triggers (recollections of past sensual pleasure).
  - Prabhupāda gives an example of a teacher so strict that his female disciples offered obeisances from a distance.
  - _Brahmacārīs_ are traditionally told to avoid eye contact with women. This can seem offensive or dehumanising, but its purpose is to spiritualise, not to disrespect: it is an admission of humility and weakness, and the needed service (such as answering questions) is still done.
  - Today some renunciates make eye contact as a courtesy to avoid offending; either way, the focus on purification must not be misread as antipathy toward any gender.
- **Cultivation of knowledge** `20:00 – 22:00`:
  - _Sannyāsa_ life is for distributing knowledge to householders, whose material life is endlessly entangling — "loose ends never end": fix one thing and ten others come undone.
  - Regular contact with renunciates reminds householders not to neglect spiritual life.
  - _Sannyāsīs_ traditionally beg — not because they are beggars, but to express humility and to get an opportunity to share transcendental knowledge with householders.
  - A renunciate lacking knowledge should cultivate it and then share it.

#### Qualities for householders: charity, self-control, sacrifice [22:00 – 26:00]

- **Charity** `22:00 – 24:00`:
  - Charity should be not just an activity but a mentality, developed by habit.
  - What matters is regularity, not quantity; those who postpone giving until they can give a lump sum often never give, because the habit of holding on to money has been developed instead.
  - Kṛṣṇa calls charity itself part of the divine nature — giving as a quality, not just an act.
- **Self-control** `24:00 – 26:00`:
  - Required of everyone, including householders; marriage is not an arrangement for sense gratification but for both partners to move toward Kṛṣṇa.
  - Sexuality is part of embodied human life, but **sexualisation** reduces everything to sexual terms.
  - With serious practice, husband and wife come to see each other as fellow souls and partners in a spiritual journey.
- **Sacrifice** `26:00`:
  - Householders traditionally performed various Vedic sacrifices; today the primary sacrifice, meant for everyone, is chanting the Holy Name.

#### Qualities for brahmacārīs and vānaprasthas: study and austerity [26:00 – 32:00]

- **Study** `26:00 – 28:00`:
  - Especially for _brahmacārīs_ — "student" is linked to "study."
  - Deep understanding of scripture keeps a former _brahmacārī_ from getting entangled even if he becomes a householder; household life too becomes a path to spiritual fulfilment.
- **Austerity** `28:00 – 32:00`:
  - Required of everyone, but Prabhupāda explains it especially for the retired _vānaprastha_, who leaves household comforts to focus on spiritual life.
  - The traditional system cannot be replicated today: _brahmacārīs_ once began at five and continued to twenty-five, whereas today many become _brahmacārīs_ at twenty-five after their education.
  - _Vānaprastha_ is "almost an extinct species"; Prabhupāda did not recommend going to the jungle in Kali-yuga — the point of going was to meet saintly people, and today few jungles or saints remain there; saints now live in temples.
  - One can still minimise worldly engagements and increase spiritual ones according to time, place and circumstance, preparing for the "final exam" of death.
  - Prabhupāda: the entire _varṇāśrama_ system is meant for austerity; without it no one can attain liberation — like a sick person who must follow a treatment that is not always likeable `32:00`.

#### Qualities for brāhmaṇas [32:00 – 42:00]

- **Truthfulness / straightforwardness** — everyone should be truthful, but the _brāhmaṇa_ especially speaks the Absolute Truth; one should not distort the truth for personal interest, and Prabhupāda speaks against misinterpreting scripture `32:00`, `34:00`.
- **Non-violence** `32:00 – 34:00`:
  - Primarily a _brāhmaṇa_'s quality rather than a _kṣatriya_'s; a _brāhmaṇa_ depends on _kṣatriyas_ for protection.
  - From the _Rāmāyaṇa_: when Viśvāmitra, earlier King Kauśika, tried to take away a sage's possession, the sage did not protest.
  - In extreme cases, not for self-defence but for general welfare, _brāhmaṇas_ may act strongly — as when a king committed atrocities, disrupted social and spiritual order and prevented people from practising spiritual life; when he defied their instruction and condemned them, the _brāhmaṇas_ reduced him to ashes simply by uttering sound.
  - Prabhupāda's broader definition: non-violence means not checking anyone's progressive life; even animals are progressing in their own way and should not be killed.
- **Freedom from anger** `36:00`:
  - Prabhupāda says that when one becomes angry the whole body becomes polluted — anger shows physically as fast breathing, sweating, clenched fists and grinding teeth.
  - One tolerates provocation by remembering life's higher purpose; one's greatness is measured by one's ability to tolerate provoking situations.
- **Aversion to fault-finding** `36:00 – 42:00`:
  - Gossip delights in others' faults; devotees delight in the glories of Kṛṣṇa and His devotees.
  - The only time to speak of others' faults is when we get no joy in doing so — as a necessity, to benefit or protect others.
  - "Laugh with others, not at others" — not by descending to their level but by elevating them.
  - Even when exposing the fallacies of materialism, we should feel compassion, not derision, toward materialistic people; Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura lists criticism of ordinary living entities among offences.
  - An advanced devotee has no inclination to criticise because he sees everyone's spiritual potential.
  - **Rāmānujācārya and the infatuated young man:** while others mocked a man captivated by a woman's eyes during the Lord's procession, Rāmānujācārya asked what attracted him, then showed him the Lord's eyes in the temple; captivated, the man became a great devotee `40:00`.
  - Prabhupāda called some people "fools and rascals" to educate about misconceptions, but he was not a fault-finder: he lived among the hippies, fed them, washed their plates and served them, seeing and fanning their spiritual potential `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### Qualities for kṣatriyas and others [42:00 – 49:00]

- **Modesty** — not acting in an immodest, inappropriate way `42:00`.
- **Determination** — staying focused, not restless `42:00`.
- **Vigour** — _kṣatriyas_ should be assertive in whatever the Lord's service requires `42:00`.
- **Cleanliness** `42:00`.
- **Freedom from envy** `42:00 – 44:00`:
  - If others have more wealth, power or prestige within _varṇāśrama_, one should not envy them.
  - **The hospital:** this world is a hospital; a patient should not envy another's "sweet medicine" — the point is not sweetness but appropriateness. By doing the duties suited to one's psychophysical nature one is purified and finally "discharged" — liberated.
- **Not demanding respect** `44:00 – 48:00`:
  - Accept respect, but do not expect it; respect is part of Vaiṣṇava etiquette and a means of reciprocating love, and it is ultimately offered to Kṛṣṇa, whom we represent — so we offer respect back to Kṛṣṇa in the other's heart.
  - **Praise should deflate the mind, not inflate the ego:** after giving a good class, a devotee can think, "I know my impurities, yet Kṛṣṇa is using even an unworthy instrument like me — let me not obstruct His mercy flowing through me."
  - Praise should also check the mind's **frivolousness** — wasting time in gossip and chit-chat — by reminding us of the gravity of our mission.
  - **The flirting doctor:** we are assistant doctors to Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Doctor; a doctor flirting with a nurse while patients cry in pain is terrible — so is frivolousness in one entrusted with a serious mission `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The divine qualities are meant for everyone, though each āśrama and varṇa emphasises some; they are cultivated as habits and mentalities — fearlessness from awareness of Kṛṣṇa, charity given regularly, praise used to deflate the mind, and others' faults overlooked to see their spiritual potential. [06:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.4 [49:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.4 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa lists the qualities of the demoniac nature `48:00 – 50:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dambha_ — wanting to parade good qualities one does not have `50:00`.
- _Darpa_ — having good qualities but wanting to parade them `50:00`.
- _Abhimāna_ — hunger for honour; living only for it `50:00`.
- _Krodha_ — anger `52:00`.
- _Pāruṣya_ — harshness: anger expressed through insensitive, hurtful, sarcastic speech `52:00`.
- _Ajñāna_ — ignorance: at most material knowledge, with spiritual life completely neglected `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### Pride, arrogance and conceit [50:00 – 52:00]

- These seem similar but differ by degree.
- **Egoist vs egotist:** the egoist thinks he is great; the egotist talks about himself constantly — an "autobiographical hearer" for whom everything heard triggers his own story.
- The teacher distinguishes praise from others from the feeling of honour; _abhimāna_ is hunger for honour — as kings once kept bards whose only job was to glorify them (different if the king is a devotee, whose glory is connected with Kṛṣṇa's).

#### Harsh speech [52:00]

- Gentle, sensitive speech characterises a godly person.
- Materialistic people often taunt and use foul words and think it "cool" — "that is not cool, that is the sign of a fool."
- The way we speak is the way we think, and it shapes our whole culture.

#### The royal road to hell [52:00 – 54:00]

- Unlike the godly, who have or cultivate knowledge, the demoniac stay in darkness about spiritual life.
- Prabhupāda's phrase: this verse describes "the royal road to hell" — royal because the demoniac walk it proudly, proud of their harshness and arrogance.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The demoniac qualities — pride, arrogance, conceit, anger, harshness and ignorance — form a "royal road to hell" precisely because those on it are proud of them; our speech in particular reveals and shapes which road we are on. [50:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.5 [54:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.5 -->

**Context.** The divine qualities lead to liberation and the demoniac to bondage; Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna not to worry, for he is born with divine qualities `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Arjuna hears like a patient [54:00]

- Like a patient listening to a doctor describe symptoms, Arjuna wonders whether this applies to him.
- As a _kṣatriya_ he is about to do violence; before battle _kṣatriyas_ goad each other with harsh words to bring out the best fighting skill; and he is seeking a kingdom — "am I demoniac?"

#### Righteous fighting is divine [56:00]

- For a _kṣatriya_, fighting in a righteous war is not demoniac but divine and essential; otherwise how would order be maintained?
- Kṛṣṇa Himself comes to re-establish _dharma_ and must use violence; violence is bad when unwarranted, not when _dharma_ requires it.
- "Do not fear, do not lament" — a phrase that recurs in the eighteenth chapter.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Outward harshness or violence does not by itself make one demoniac: Arjuna's fighting in a righteous war is divine, because it serves dharma — so he need not fear or lament. [54:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.6 [56:00 – 59:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.6 -->

**Context.** There are two kinds of created beings in this world, the divine and the demoniac; Kṛṣṇa says He has explained the divine at length and will now describe the demoniac `56:00`.

#### "I have described in detail" [56:00 – 58:00]

- In chapter 16 the divine is described in only three verses, while the demoniac nature runs from verse 7 practically to verse 20 — so why "in detail"?
- Because Kṛṣṇa has described godly qualities throughout the Gītā:
  - Chapter 2 — the _sthita-prajña_, 2.54–2.72.
  - Chapter 3 — kings like Janaka who live in detachment.
  - Chapter 4 — the _niṣkāma karma-yogī_, 4.19–4.23.
  - Chapters 5 and 6 — a few verses.
  - Chapter 10 — qualities that come from Kṛṣṇa.
  - Chapter 12 — qualities of devotees, 12.13–12.20.
  - Chapter 13 — characteristics of those in knowledge, 13.8–13.12.
  - Chapter 14 — those in goodness and those who transcend the modes.
- The description of the demoniac nature is taken up in the next class `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The divine nature has been described all through the Gītā — in the sthita-prajña, the karma-yogī, the devotee and the person of knowledge — so chapter 16 can now focus on the demoniac nature as a warning. [56:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
