## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 16.11 – 16.12 [00:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.11 -->

<!-- vedabase:16.12 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the inner condition of the demoniac: they are filled with immeasurable anxiety until death, take sense gratification as the supreme goal, are bound by hundreds of desires, give themselves to lust and anger, and collect wealth by unjust means `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pralaya_ — can refer to cosmic destruction or to the destruction of a person at death; here the anxieties last until the moment of death `02:00`.
- _Paramā_ — supreme: sense gratification is taken as the prime necessity and highest goal of life `02:00`.
- _Cintana_ and _cintā_ — contemplation (especially on Kṛṣṇa, the highest truth) versus anxiety `04:00`.
- _Kāma_, _krodha_, _lobha_ — lust, anger and greed, which trap the soul whether desire is frustrated or fulfilled `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### Contemplation or anxiety [04:00 – 06:00]

- In life we either do _cintana_ or we do _cintā_.
- If the mind has no steady, satisfying, stabilising object of thought, it naturally thinks of worldly things.
- No worldly thing is everlasting, so none can give lasting shelter; thinking of sense objects produces anxiety:
  - Will I get it or not?
  - If I get it, will it stay with me?
  - How long will it stay?
- Anxieties are immeasurable because there are so many desires, and each desire produces its own anxieties.
- Without a stabilising shelter, people are left at the mercy of the mind's vagaries; because the mind is restless, they remain restless.
- The teacher calls this a principle that cuts across religious boundaries, quoting St. Augustine: the Lord has made us for Himself, and without Him we never find rest `06:00`.

#### The modern anxiety epidemic [06:00 – 10:00]

- The material world always has anxiety, but modern civilisation has multiplied it many levels — wherever it has gone, stress has come.
- The teacher cites the World Health Organization view that stress and mental-health problems will be among the biggest health challenges of the current century, contributing to many disorders.
- Some health researchers speak of an "anxiety epidemic" in many Western societies, like an epidemic of AIDS or TB `08:00`.
- Saying "don't worry" is easy, but giving up anxiety is not — especially when people do not understand its cause: anxiety comes from desires and attachments, so the more desires, the more anxiety `08:00`.
- The teacher refers to a renunciate brāhmaṇa in the _Bhāgavatam_ who says a person works hard, craves and slaves for wealth, but ultimately gets anxiety `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### "Cheers" and tears [10:00]

- The teacher quotes a poem he once wrote: at parties people say "cheers," but their hearts are filled with fears and their minds pierced by desires, so they are on the verge of tears.
- Every desire is like a sharp stab — "I want this, I want this."
- It is a case of smiling faces and crying hearts: a façade of happiness over real unhappiness.
- Because the objects people chase are not in their control and hundreds of others want them too, competition for sense gratification becomes savage `10:00 – 12:00`.

#### Desires as masters and ropes [12:00]

- When sense gratification becomes the goal, desires become one's masters — invisible ropes pulling a person in hundreds of directions.
- Instead of being devoted to Lord Nārāyaṇa, such people become devoted to lust and anger.

#### How desire traps both ways [12:00 – 16:00]

- **Desire unfulfilled → anger:** "I want to be the enjoyer; if I cannot enjoy, nobody should." The teacher gives the example of a jilted partner in a love affair who wants to attack, deface or destroy the other.
- **Desire fulfilled → greed:** one wants more and more; a man trying to please a woman must keep bringing gifts as demands grow.
- **Ajāmila** — a cultured brāhmaṇa for whom even speaking a lie would be a great wrong; after falling for a prostitute he became a common dacoit, looting and wounding people `14:00`.
- The teacher contrasts charity (others give voluntarily, as to a brāhmaṇa) with dacoity (taking forcibly, even under threat of killing) — showing how far one can fall for sense gratification `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### Protecting life versus prolonging it [16:00 – 20:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda writes that such foolish people do not know that no physician can prolong life even for a moment beyond what is allotted.
- Protecting the body is not wrong: Vasudeva protects Devakī (and himself) from Kaṁsa; Śukrācārya tells Bali Mahārāja that a tree can give fruit only while alive — similarly the human body gives the fruit of _dharma_ only while alive `16:00 – 18:00`.
- So one must protect one's life and livelihood, but letting the material monopolise all one's time and endeavour is disastrously wrong, because death is certain at the material level `18:00`.
- The teacher's wordplay: rather than trying to _prolong_ life, we should _long_ for eternal life — attainable at the spiritual level by purification and elevation of consciousness. The demoniac do not believe in that level, so they strain at the material level to fulfil desires they never can `18:00`.

#### Advertising and the tightening rope [20:00 – 22:00]

- Every advertisement is like a "desire-producing industry": its evil genius is to make people believe they cannot live without things they do not need at all.
- Example: an advertisement of an attractive icon with blonde hair makes dark-haired people feel they must have blonde hair.
- The more desires, the stronger the pull and the bondage. Each indulgence makes the rope:
  - **thicker** — every enjoyment adds a strand, as the impression (_saṁskāra_) goes deeper;
  - **tighter** — one has less and less freedom.
- Example: alcohol addiction progressing from once a week to every hour — indulgence increases the torture and the bondage `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
When the mind has no stable spiritual object of contemplation, it fixes on sense objects and anxiety becomes endless; every desire then acts as a rope that grows thicker and tighter with indulgence, driving people to anger, greed and even violence. [04:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.13 – 16.15 [22:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.13 -->

<!-- vedabase:16.14 -->

<!-- vedabase:16.15 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa voices the demoniac person's inner monologue: so much wealth today and more tomorrow; this enemy killed and others to be killed; I am the controller and enjoyer, perfect, powerful and happy; I am wealthy and surrounded by aristocratic relatives; who is equal to me; I will perform sacrifices and give charity and rejoice — all of it bewildered by ignorance `22:00 – 40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Manoratha_ — the "chariot of the mind": sitting in the mind and day-dreaming about what one will gain `22:00 – 24:00`.
- _Īśvara_ — controller; the demoniac want to be controller and enjoyer in God's place `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Ajñāna_ — ignorance; why all this is illusion: none of it will last `40:00`.

#### The mind as a chariot [22:00 – 24:00]

- In Vedic times a horse was fast but uncomfortable; a chariot was reasonably fast and comfortable, driven by someone else while one simply sat behind.
- Similarly we sit in the chariot of the mind and dream: "I'll do this, I'll gain that."
- The teacher distinguishes this from the well-known chariot analogy of the body (where the mind is the reins and the intelligence is the driver): here the mind itself is the chariot and we are the passenger.

#### The milk-pot day-dream [24:00 – 26:00]

- A poor man with a little milk imagines selling it, buying more, getting a cow, many cows, a nice house, a wife who must obey him — and if she does not, he will hit her.
- As he swings his hand in imagination, the real milk pot topples over.
- The point: the mind imagines so much in the future, while what is actually in hand is lost.

#### Wealth, enemies and the "God project" [26:00 – 28:00]

- Wealth is limited and desires are unlimited, so people compete — wars are fought and people beg, borrow, steal, kill and even commit massacres for money.
- The description sounds like underworld dons who hire killers: "this enemy I have killed, that one I will kill."
- The teacher calls this the "God project" of the demoniac: they want to be number one — the controller and the enjoyer.
  - First one appropriates property ("these things are mine"),
  - then controls it,
  - then enjoys it.
- Kṛṣṇa has said earlier in the Gītā that He is the enjoyer of all sacrifices; the demoniac try to displace Him `28:00`.
- Some say it explicitly — the teacher cites King Vena in the _Bhāgavatam_'s Fourth Canto, who told the brāhmaṇas to worship him instead of the Lord — while others only harbour the subconscious desire to be perfect, strong and happy `28:00`.

#### Excelling for Kṛṣṇa, not for the ego [28:00 – 32:00]

- The fascination with champions — being number one in hockey, cricket, tennis or chess — is not bad in itself: Kṛṣṇa is the best, and as His parts we also want to do our best.
- **A devotee** does his best not for worldly recognition but to give Kṛṣṇa recognition — using God-given talents in His service, gratefully acknowledging that the abilities come from Him.
- **Hanumān** — reminded of his forgotten abilities by Jāmbavān, he did stupendous feats, single-handedly devastating one of the universe's most formidable armies and burning much of Laṅkā, yet he never thought "I did this" `30:00 – 32:00`.
- **The demoniac** excel to glorify their own ego: "I am wealthy, surrounded by aristocratic relatives," moving in glamorous circles and enjoying proximity with VIPs `32:00`.

#### "Who is equal to me?" [32:00 – 38:00]

- One of Kṛṣṇa's characteristics is that no one is equal to Him; the demoniac think no one is as good as themselves `32:00`.
- **Boxing champion** — in old age, a shadow of his former self, he was easily beaten by a challenger he had once defeated; the challenger said he held back so as not to hurt him seriously `34:00`.
- Sports stars cling to the top, but the body takes it away; it is helplessly frustrating.
- **Cricketer** — when his run record was broken, he said he felt like a bird whose wings had been clipped, plummeting helplessly; retired, he could never regain it `36:00`.
- Thinking "no one is greater than me" is the illusion of the godless; this becomes egomania — an irrational obsession with one's own greatness, even megalomania `36:00`.

#### Religion as a social cosmetic [38:00 – 42:00]

- The demoniac want religious fame too: "I will perform sacrifices, give charity, and be known as a religious person."
- Religion is meant to be a heart-transformer, but they use it as a "face-transformer" — a social cosmetic to look good without surrendering to God.
- People may want their names prominently displayed where they donate.
- The teacher's principle of donation: we are meant to give our heart to Kṛṣṇa; if we cannot, we should give what is _in_ our heart — for example money. That gives Kṛṣṇa a place in the heart, and He, being very intelligent, expands that place by revealing His sweetness until He attracts the whole heart. So any donation to Kṛṣṇa is good, whatever the motive `40:00`.
- Krishna's description here, however, is of those with no intention to surrender, for whom religion is just another feather in the cap or medal on the chest `40:00`.
- **Temple competition** — rivals building ever taller deities ("10 feet," "20 feet," "the world's highest") risk taking _darśana_ of their own ego rather than of the Lord. Religion should be a sanctuary for the soul, not a gymnasium for the ego `42:00`.
- The "twist in the tale": all this is bewilderment by ignorance, because none of it lasts — we cannot carry even a penny into the next life `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The demoniac mind rides a chariot of fantasies — wealth, eliminated rivals, supremacy, even religious fame — trying to become controller and enjoyer in God's place; a devotee may excel too, but to give Kṛṣṇa recognition, not to feed the ego. [22:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.16 [42:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.16 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes what happens to such people: perplexed by many anxieties, covered by a network of illusion and strongly attached to sense enjoyment, they fall into an unclean hell `42:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Aneka_ — many (_eka_ is one) `42:00`.
- _Citta_ — consciousness; _vibhrānta_ — completely perplexed, pulled in many directions `42:00`.
- _Moha-jāla_ — the network of illusion; _jāla_ is a trap or net `44:00`.
- _Aśuci_ — unclean, the opposite of the cleanliness Kṛṣṇa has praised several times in the Gītā `44:00`.

#### From noose to network [42:00 – 44:00]

- Earlier Kṛṣṇa used the language of traps — desire (_āśā_) as nooses.
- Now illusion is a net or network: not a single strand, but a mesh that completely traps a person.
- Covered over, people cannot perceive spiritual reality or think beyond their own material desires and hopes.

#### Distractions at every stage of life [46:00 – 48:00]

- The teacher refers to verses by Śaṅkarācārya exposing the emptiness of material life through its endless distractions:
  - **Childhood** — absorbed in play.
  - **Youth** — hormones activate and lust plays one "like a fiddle"; the mind searches for a partner and, wherever thoughts go, they return to sense gratification.
  - **Old age** — _cintā_, anxiety: one has no capacity, cannot control others or one's own body, is often neglected, and is lost in worry `48:00`.
- So at no stage does material life give time for spiritual life; we must forcefully put distractions aside. "Later" will never come — it must be done now `48:00`.

#### The modern intensification [48:00 – 56:00]

- **Childhood anxiety** — schools hold stress-management seminars for children in the fourth to sixth standards because of workload and expectations `48:00 – 50:00`.
- **Sexualisation of childhood** — apart from the horror of child abuse, commercial media present sexualised models to "tweens" (between childhood and the teens) who are neither emotionally nor physically ready `50:00`.
- **Youth** — young people lie in bed shooting images on digital screens, imagining themselves heroes; people pour energy, money and emotion into watching sports to forget the boredom and emptiness of life `50:00 – 52:00`.
- **Old age** — people try surgeries and medicines to prolong sexual potency:
  - Prabhupāda's remark on monkey glands transplanted into humans: one monkey loses his enjoyment so another monkey can enjoy `52:00`.
  - A "get young" drug proved dangerous for heart patients and was restricted to prescriptions; some bribed other physicians to obtain it, suffered heart attacks in the middle of enjoyment and died `52:00 – 54:00`.
  - Media now target the elderly with gadgets and products promising enjoyment even now `54:00`.
- There is no end to the distractions that keep people bound in material existence `54:00 – 56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Illusion is not a single noose but a whole network: at every stage of life — and far more today — some distraction pulls consciousness in many directions, so spiritual life must be taken up now, not postponed. [42:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.17 [56:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.17 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes this description: self-complacent and impudent, deluded by wealth and false prestige, the demoniac sometimes perform sacrifices in name only, proudly and without following scriptural rules `56:00 – 58:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Self-complacent — caring for no authority: "I am my own authority" `56:00`.
- _Dhana_ and _māna_ — wealth and prestige; they are caught in the external with no inner substance `56:00`.
- _Dambha_ — wanting respect through outer appearance without inner substance `58:00`.
- _Nāma-yajña_ — scripture recommends the chanting of the holy name, but they perform sacrifice for their own _name_ `58:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's purport [56:00]

- Relating also to the preceding verses on earning money, Prabhupāda writes that the demoniac think whatever they have was gained by their own ability, and that each can live at the sacrifice of all others — others matter only as factors in one's own enjoyment.
- He gives the example of Rāvaṇa.

#### "All bluff, no stuff" [56:00 – 58:00]

- The demoniac show "I have this, I have that," but inwardly have no understanding of life's reality and no intention to harmonise with it.
- They may worship, but for name and fame; not believing in scripture, they disregard its rules and perform rituals only to be respected in society.

#### Face-lift, not faith-lift [58:00 – 60:00]

- For them religion becomes "a face-lift, not a faith-lift": it lifts neither faith nor heart, and is a monstrous under-utilisation of spiritual life.
- Prabhupāda also mentions those who take the dress of the renounced order for prestige without following its rules; they ruin themselves, ruin the reputation of the renounced order and of religion, and live hypocritically `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Without inner substance, even religion becomes a show — sacrifice for one's own name, renunciation for prestige — whereas real spiritual life is meant to lift the heart and faith, not just the face. [56:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
