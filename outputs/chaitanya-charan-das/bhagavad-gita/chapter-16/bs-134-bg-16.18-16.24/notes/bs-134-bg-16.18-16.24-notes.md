## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 16.18 [00:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.18 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa lists qualities the demoniac take shelter of — false ego, strength, pride, lust and anger — and then, for the first time in the chapter, speaks of their relationship with Him: they are envious of Him, present in their own and others' bodies, and blaspheme Him `00:00 – 06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Ahaṅkāra_ — false ego or arrogance `00:00`.
- _Balam_ — strength; the demoniac brandish and brag of physical or political power as if it were the ultimate power, not knowing there is a higher spiritual, divine power `00:00`.
- _Kāma_ and _krodha_ — lust is the desire to enjoy; anger is the frustration and rage when that attempt is frustrated `02:00`.
- _Saṁśrita_ — taking shelter; instead of Kṛṣṇa, they take shelter of these negative material emotions `02:00`.
- Envy and blasphemy — envy may stay inside; blasphemy is when it comes out as unreasonable, harsh verbal criticism `06:00 – 08:00`.

#### Misdirected shelter [02:00 – 04:00]

- When one makes lust into a god, lust makes one into a devil — it makes one do devilish things.
- "Making lust a god" means taking shelter of it: thinking it will be the source of pleasure and relief from problems — which it is not.
- Others take shelter of power, bragging or arrogance, or of brutal anger, thinking fists will solve all problems.
- Shelter can be understood physically first: in heavy rain a tree or shack gives protection and some comfort. The demoniac seek such protection from the wrong things — their shelter is completely misdirected `04:00`.

#### Why Kṛṣṇa now speaks of Himself [04:00 – 08:00]

- Until this verse the chapter does not use "I" or "Me"; from 16.18 it appears prominently (also in 16.19 and 16.23) `04:00`.
- The divine qualities listed earlier did not include _bhakti_: they are features of our material nature (Prabhupāda calls them transcendental qualities in the sense that they help one move toward transcendence). At the level of the soul, one is always devoted to Kṛṣṇa — the soul itself cannot be demoniac `04:00 – 06:00`.
- The divine qualities were described in terms of _varṇa_ and _āśrama_, and the demoniac ones in terms of obsession with lust, greed and power — neither in terms of relationship with Kṛṣṇa, until now `06:00`.

#### Envious of the well-wisher [08:00 – 10:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is present as the Supersoul in everyone's heart (15.15) and is the well-wisher of all living beings (5.29) — including the demoniac.
- Being hostile to an enemy may seem normal; being hostile to one's well-wisher is unfortunate.
- Kṛṣṇa is not only a well-wisher but also a benefactor who tries to do good, and knows what is good, since He knows everything (7.25 – 7.26).
- **Hospital analogy** — if this world is a hospital, the least a patient can do is be hospitable to the doctor; being hostile ruins the chances of cure `10:00`.
- Prabhupāda's purport: such a person puts forward false arguments against God's existence, denies scriptural authority, and plans to cut down anyone who checks his sensual advancement `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The demoniac take shelter of ego, power, lust and anger instead of Kṛṣṇa — and end up envious of the very Lord who sits in their heart as their well-wisher, like a patient who is hostile to his own doctor. [02:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.19 [10:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says that those who are envious and mischievous, the lowest among men, He casts again and again into demoniac species `10:00 – 12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Narādhama_ — the lowest among human beings (contrasted with "best among persons") `10:00`.
- "Mischievous" — Prabhupāda's translation of a word that can often mean "cruel" `14:00`.
- Demoniac species — not only a mentality (Duryodhana had a human body but a demoniac mentality) but also species such as _asuras_ and _yakṣas_, in which a demoniac mentality is normal `12:00`.

#### Exceptions [12:00]

- The body does not entirely determine mentality; Prahlāda is an exception, whom Kṛṣṇa names in the tenth chapter as His representative among the demons.

#### Vendetta or eternal damnation? [12:00 – 14:00]

- The teacher raises two serious questions:
  - Does Kṛṣṇa hold a personal vendetta — "you envy Me, so I will throw you into hell"?
  - Does "again and again" support the idea of eternal damnation found in some traditions?
- Principle: a statement divorced from its context can be very misleading `14:00`.

#### The father's warning [14:00]

- To the father, even terrible deeds of small children are "mischief."
- A parent may say, "If you do this, you will never be allowed into my house again" — the point is not exile but "don't do it."
- Similarly, Kṛṣṇa's emphasis is not that He will throw them into hell, but that one should not act this way.

#### Words as containers and carriers of meaning [14:00 – 20:00]

- Prabhupāda's translation is a _bhāvānuvāda_, conveying emotion and intention, whereas a literal translation preserves semantic accuracy; proper understanding needs both semantics and aesthetics `14:00 – 16:00`.
- Two theories of meaning the teacher contrasts:
  - **Carriers** — words are like pipelines into which we put meaning.
  - **Containers** — words hold fixed meanings, as recorded in dictionaries.
- The reality is a combination `16:00 – 18:00`:
  - A sour-faced "thank you" expresses annoyance, not thanks.
  - An angry child's "I love you" to the mother expresses anger more than love.
  - Yet one cannot say "I hate you" and mean "I love you."
- **Machine translation** — the teacher tells a story (which he notes may be apocryphal) of "the spirit is willing but the flesh is weak" coming out as "the vodka is strong but the meat is rotten": a statement about human nature became one about food on the table `18:00 – 20:00`.
- So translation needs experts in both content and context; English speaks of _denotation_ (what a word means) and _connotation_ (what it implies) `20:00`.

#### Reading the Gītā by the Gītā [20:00 – 24:00]

- Elsewhere Kṛṣṇa says even the lowest can be elevated:
  - even the most sinful can be elevated (9.30 – 9.32, 4.36);
  - whatever one's situation, one who takes shelter of Him attains perfection (18.57).
- So this verse expresses Kṛṣṇa's displeasure — His "loving anger," a tough love that parents sometimes show so children learn the intended lesson `22:00`.
- "Mischievous" because, from Kṛṣṇa's view, they are like children who should grow up and act maturely, or they ruin their lives `22:00`.
- Prabhupāda's purport: demoniac species are always lustful, violent and unclean; hunters in the jungle are counted among them. He uses "species" in terms of consciousness, not only biology — even in human form, living insensitively and hurting others is demoniac `22:00 – 24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Read in context and alongside the rest of the Gītā, Kṛṣṇa's words about casting the envious into demoniac species are a father's loving warning against "mischief," not a personal vendetta. [12:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.20 [24:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.20 -->

**Context.** Continuing the thought, Kṛṣṇa says such people, taking birth again and again among the demoniac, never attain Him and gradually sink to the most abominable destination `24:00`.

#### Misused free will [24:00]

- They go further and further from Kṛṣṇa if they keep misusing their free will; they need not misuse it, but if they do, they must suffer.

#### Hell as a classroom [24:00 – 28:00]

- In the Abrahamic religions hell is eternal torment; the Vedic understanding is never that.
- The universe is like a university with many classrooms; hell is one — difficult, but not compulsory.
- The Gītā reveals a concerned, compassionate, committed God who stays in our heart and works tirelessly for our elevation and liberation, like a teacher who wants students promoted.
- **Hell is meant to save us from suffering** `26:00`:
  - Disconnection from Kṛṣṇa is the basic denominator of suffering in this world.
  - Kṛṣṇa's education through _guru_, _sādhu_ and _śāstra_ helps souls come back; if they defy Him, they suffer for many lifetimes.
  - Like an unruly student sent to a stricter class so that he returns disciplined and gets promoted.
  - If actions had no consequences, we would suffer more, endlessly cycling in birth and death; consequences remind us, so the soul becomes sober and returns to service `28:00`.
- Life is a "school of hard knocks"; hell is a "school of super hard knocks" — but all are meant to learn `28:00`.

#### No one is hopeless [28:00 – 30:00]

- Even in demoniac species, the Supersoul does not leave the heart and works to redeem them.
- Prabhupāda, based on a previous _ācārya_'s commentary, explains that demons killed by the Lord when He descends also get elevated `28:00`.
- Devotees' mission is to make the unfortunate fortunate; the seed of _bhakti_ comes by the mercy of guru and Kṛṣṇa `28:00 – 30:00`.
- Asked whether hell is eternal (reported in _The Hare Kṛṣṇa Explosion_), Prabhupāda smiled and said nothing except ecstatic devotional service to Kṛṣṇa is eternal; everything else is temporary `30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Hell in the Vedic view is a tough classroom, not eternal damnation — its consequences exist to sober the soul and send it back to Kṛṣṇa; only devotional service is eternal. [24:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.21 [30:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.21 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa shows it is not personal vendetta that takes people down: there are three gates leading to hell and the destruction of the self — lust, anger and greed — and therefore one should give them up `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dvāra_ — door or gate; a metaphor for mentality `32:00`, `34:00`.
- _Nāśana_ — destruction; a metaphor for destruction of the soul's spiritual inclination `36:00`.

#### First metaphor: the door [32:00 – 34:00]

- A door is the means by which we go somewhere; lust, anger and greed are not physical doors.
- Being consumed by these emotions becomes the pathway to hell, because they make us act in ways whose consequences take us there.
- Advertisements with explicit imagery inject lust, anger and greed — each is like a gate to hell. Today there are so many gates that instead of people going to hell, hell comes to the people: madly lusty or greedy people kill, torture, exploit and dehumanise others `32:00`.
- Major crimes come from these three `34:00`:
  - sexual abuse — from lust, or from anger seeking power over others;
  - brutal violence — from anger;
  - corruption and nepotism — from greed.
- Countering them needs something substantial — a higher way to live and experience happiness, which scripture gives (discussed shortly); here Kṛṣṇa says "beware" `34:00`.
- Acronym: lust, anger, greed = **LAG** — they make the soul lag behind on the journey to Kṛṣṇa `34:00`.

#### Second metaphor: destruction [34:00 – 36:00]

- In 2.17 Kṛṣṇa called the soul indestructible; so "destruction" here is metaphorical.
- It means the destruction of the soul's spiritual inclination — its capacity to live spiritually and perceive spiritual reality — when overwhelmed by these _anarthas_.
- The teacher correlates this with the "killer of the soul" mentioned in the Īśopaniṣad and elsewhere `36:00`.
- Prabhupāda: one should be sane; succumbing to these makes one insane, killing the self so that liberation is impossible — impossible not permanently, but as long as one keeps succumbing `36:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Lust, anger and greed are the three gates to hell — mentalities that make the soul "lag" and destroy its spiritual inclination; Kṛṣṇa's instruction is simply to give them up. [30:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.22 [36:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.22 -->

**Context.** One who has escaped these three gates of darkness acts for the well-being of the soul and thus gradually attains the supreme destination `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### We are often our own greatest enemies [38:00 – 40:00]

- Self-destruction at different levels:
  - grossest — suicide;
  - "slow suicide" — uncontrolled smoking, drinking or drugs, without intending to die;
  - self-defeating acts driven by lust, anger and greed, harmful materially as well as spiritually.
- Examples: promiscuity leading to fatal or crippling disease; greed leading to fraud and jail `38:00 – 40:00`.
- Those who are corrupt and not caught are only enjoying past good _karma_; consequences will catch up sooner or later `40:00`.

#### Freedom is possible [40:00 – 42:00]

- Kṛṣṇa first says "give them up" and then speaks of "becoming free" — indicating it is possible to become free.
- Like recovering from a fever or a ghost's possession: once possession goes, one can act calmly and clearly.
- Then one acts for long-term well-being — enlightened self-interest — both perceiving and pursuing what is truly good, and gradually reaches the destination `40:00 – 42:00`.
- Prabhupāda's purport: the _varṇāśrama_ system is meant to elevate a person; following its rules raises one automatically to the spiritual platform and liberation `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Lust, anger and greed possess us like a fever or a ghost; once free, we can think clearly, perceive and pursue our true long-term good, and progress steadily to the supreme destination. [38:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.23 [42:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.23 -->

**Context.** One who discards scriptural injunctions and acts according to whim attains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the supreme destination `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śāstra-vidhi_ — the injunction of scripture; giving it up means rejecting or neglecting it `42:00`, `44:00`.
- _Kāma-kāra_ — acting by selfish desire, whimsically `42:00`, `46:00`.
- _Siddhi_ — perfection: material, yogic (also material, though subtler) and spiritual `44:00`.

#### Why lust blocks all three [42:00 – 44:00]

- Lust cannot help us think about long-term perfection; it covers our spiritual nature, so there is no question of the supreme goal.
- Lust keeps us perpetually dissatisfied — "more, more"; it is like a fire, and indulgence is fuel that makes it blaze more — so there is no happiness.
- It also blocks _siddhi_: success in any field, even sports, requires regulated eating and acting and control of short-sighted desires; unregulated desire lets one do nothing properly `44:00`.
- Example: traditional education (in Vedic and other cultures) was generally not co-educational, because after puberty mutual attraction can make it hard to focus on study `44:00`.

#### Feelings guided by scripture [44:00 – 48:00]

- Prabhupāda says one in the human form is expected to be sane — not driven by short-term impulses.
- Acting whimsically: knowing something is forbidden and doing it anyway because "I feel like it" `46:00`.
- "Feelings can sometimes kill faster than bullets" — they can make us act impulsively and destructively `46:00`.
- _Bhakti_ is about feelings, but pure, sublime spiritual feelings; our material feelings can weaken, wound or destroy us `46:00`.
- So we neither deny nor reject feelings; we regulate and direct them by intelligence based on scripture — Kṛṣṇa's intelligence.
- Example: attraction between a man and a woman within the sacred bond of marriage can help them live harmoniously and advance spiritually; outside scripture, the feeling has to be rejected `46:00 – 48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Acting on whim — doing what is known to be forbidden because "I feel like it" — forfeits perfection, happiness and the supreme goal; feelings must be regulated and directed by scripture, not denied. [42:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 16.24 [48:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:16.24 -->

**Context.** Therefore one should understand by scripture what is to be done and what is not to be done, and act accordingly `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kārya_ and _akārya_ — what is to be done and what is forbidden, as decided by scripture `48:00`.
- _Śāstra-pramāṇa_ — scripture as the authority `60:00`.

#### Four common guides — and why they fail [48:00 – 56:00]

- **Fashion** — "everyone is doing it" (blonde hair, high-heeled sandals, a particular jacket). Fashions have no basis in truth; we become leaves blown by the wind. A devotee need not be defiantly unfashionable, but fashion cannot be the parameter for life `48:00 – 50:00`.
- **Tradition** — "we have always done it." Traditions can be wrong. Example: meat-eating in much of the Western world `50:00 – 54:00`:
  - Animals have consciousness and emotions like ours; a mother animal's affection resembles human maternal love, and they suffer terribly when killed.
  - To the objection that plants also suffer: yes, which is why being vegetarian is not enough — one should take _prasāda_; but pain differs with the development of the nervous system, and a sensitive person minimises it.
  - Intuition and behaviour show the difference: a slaughterhouse is ghastly, while a harvest is a celebration; parents would allow a school trip to a harvest but not to a slaughterhouse.
  - The point is not vegetarianism as such, but that tradition alone does not make something right `54:00`.
- **Mood** — "I feel like it." Watching a movie the night before an exam and then failing it `54:00`.
- **Logic** — "it makes sense." The most sensible of the four, yet it can mislead through incomplete understanding: a surgeon chooses surgery for faster healing of a fracture, not knowing the patient's condition makes anaesthesia potentially fatal `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Scripture as a fixed lighthouse [56:00]

- Scripture is the first and final authority for action.
- Other guides are like moving lighthouses: a lighthouse must stay fixed to mark what is safe; if it moves, the boat hits the rocks.
- Logic and other factors may contribute as subordinate considerations, but the decision rests on scripture.

#### Prabhupāda's purport [58:00 – 60:00]

- He speaks of the basic process of devotional service — chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa — and of the _ācāryas_ being free from the four defects `58:00`.
- Personalists and impersonalists differ in their conclusions about the Absolute Truth, but both follow Vedic culture — protecting cows, respecting the Vedas, the _varṇāśrama_ system — and so harmonise with following scripture `58:00`.
- Aversion to understanding the Supreme Personality of Godhead is the root cause of all falldowns, and therefore the greatest offence of human life `58:00`.
- **Billion dollars and the dustbin** — throwing away a billion dollars to search garbage for food is not just tragic but an offence; sense gratification is available in all species, while the human form offers eternal life and happiness `58:00 – 60:00`.
- Guided by the spiritual master, one can attain the supreme destination; we study the Gītā to understand life's principles and mould our lives by them `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Fashion, tradition, mood and even logic are moving lighthouses; scripture is the fixed one, so it must be the first and final authority in deciding what to do and what not to do. [48:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
