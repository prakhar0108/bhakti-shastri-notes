### Bhagavad-gita 4.24 [00:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.24 -->

**Context.** The word _brahma_ comes six times in this verse, which can cause some confusion; the teacher explains each occurrence as a component of a sacrifice `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Arpaṇam_ — that which is offered; the contribution `02:00`.
- _Haviḥ_ — clarified butter, the main fuel added to the fire, along with grains and other items `02:00`.
- _Agni_ — the fire `02:00`.
- _Hutam_ — the offering, and the one who offers `02:00`.
- _Gantavyam_ — the destination `02:00`.
- _Brahma_ — here not the _brahmajyoti_ in particular; a generic word for the spiritual `02:00`.
- _Brahma-karma-samādhi_ — one doing spiritual work, absorbed in _samādhi_ `02:00`.

#### Everything in the sacrifice is spirit [02:00 – 04:00]

- The offering is spirit, the butter is spirit, the fire is spirit, the person offering is spirit, and the destination attained is also spirit; the person is doing spiritual work, absorbed in _samādhi_ `02:00`.
- When one acts in the spirit of _yajña_, all of existence becomes like a sacrifice `02:00`.
- Ultimately everything is Kṛṣṇa's energy, present everywhere and all-encompassing; everyone may use it for whatever purpose they want — for Kṛṣṇa's service or for sense gratification — but it remains Kṛṣṇa's energy `04:00`.
- When we understand this and decide to use it for Kṛṣṇa's service, everything can be understood spiritually `04:00`.

#### Two senses of "spiritual" [04:00 – 06:00]

- **Compositional sense** — what something is made of: if made of _sac-cid-ānanda_ it is spiritual; if made of inert elements such as earth, water, fire, air and ether it is material `04:00`.
- **Application sense** — what something is used for: _prasāda_ may be made of material elements, but because it is used in Kṛṣṇa's service it is spiritual `04:00`.
- So when we say the spiritual master's body is spiritual, it means it is used for spiritual purposes; it may grow old and get diseases, but that does not make it material in the way ordinary people's bodies are, because it is used for Kṛṣṇa's service `04:00 – 06:00`.
- **Gold box and gold-plated box** — what is _sac-cid-ānanda_ is like a solid gold box; what is spiritualised is like a gold-plated box. Both are actually gold `06:00`.
- Things can be spiritualised by the practice of Kṛṣṇa consciousness — by engagement in devotional service `06:00`.

#### The battlefield as a yajña [06:00 – 08:00]

- The teacher cites the _ācārya_ Cakravartīpāda's explanation of the battlefield as a _yajña_ `06:00`.
- Arjuna's question: "If You want me to perform _yajña_, let me go to the forest" `06:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's answer: the battlefield itself is the place of sacrifice. A sacrifice needs a ladle to make the offerings, and the offerings themselves; here the offerings are the Kauravas `06:00`.
- The sacrificial fire that accepts the offerings is the universal form, as will be discussed in the eleventh chapter: the warriors — Dhṛtarāṣṭra's sons — are seen entering its fearful mouths with terrible teeth, showing how the offering is being accepted `06:00 – 08:00`.
- So _yajña_ is not one specific religious activity; it is a principle. If you act in the principle of sacrifice, for the pleasure of the Absolute Truth, even your battlefield can be a _yajña_ `08:00`.

#### Hearing Kṛṣṇa-kathā as a sacrifice [08:00 – 10:00]

- Right now, discussing _Kṛṣṇa-kathā_ is a sacrifice: Kṛṣṇa is manifesting as the sound, and by speaking and hearing we offer our consciousness to Him `08:00`.
- So everything becomes spiritualised, and by regularly hearing the sound vibration we ultimately attain the spiritual platform `08:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa is telling us to fix our mind on the spiritual element underlying everything. This is not sentimental fantasy or intellectual conception; it is insight into the true nature of things `08:00 – 10:00`.
- Everything is situated in the spiritual, but it is covered by illusion; covered by illusion, we think things are meant not for Kṛṣṇa's service but for sense gratification, and then we become entangled. To avoid that entanglement, we must develop the right vision `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Because everything is Kṛṣṇa's energy, anything used for His service is spiritualised — like a gold-plated box that is still gold — and so any activity, even fighting on a battlefield or hearing Kṛṣṇa-kathā, can become a yajña. [02:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.25 [10:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.25 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now begins listing different kinds of _yajña_. So far: the literal sacrifice of BG 4.24, which is _niṣkāma karma-yoga_; worship of the _devatās_, which is _sakāma karma-yoga_ (first half of BG 4.25); and offering the self into Brahman, which is _jñāna-yoga_ (second half of BG 4.25) `10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Daiva-yajña_ — sacrifice to the demigods; such people connect with a reality higher than themselves, though not the highest `12:00`.
- _Brahmāgni_ — the fire of Brahman, into which some offer the self, conceiving of Brahman as the Absolute Truth into which they must merge `12:00`.
- _Bahv-īśvara-vādī_ — Śrīla Prabhupāda's term for those interested in material benefits who worship the demigods according to Vedic rituals: believers in many gods `12:00`.

#### Different kinds of sacrifice [12:00]

- Those who offer themselves into Brahman also perform a kind of sacrifice: they want to offer themselves `12:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa is giving a vision of how the various religious activities people perform within the broad Vedic culture are all forms of _yajña_ `12:00`.
- After describing them, He will describe their purpose and then recommend to Arjuna what he should do `12:00`.

#### Polytheism and multi-level monotheism [12:00 – 16:00]

- Several "-isms" are distinguished `14:00`:
  - **Polytheism** — belief in many gods.
  - **Monotheism** — belief in one God.
  - **Pantheism** — the belief that everything is God.
  - A further view — that the one Absolute Truth appears in many forms.
- Polytheism faces an unsolvable logical problem: the word "God" generally connotes the Supreme, so how can there be many supremes? `14:00`
- The correct understanding: there are many gods, but they are all assistants of the one supreme God — like department heads and cabinet ministers in a universal government, above whom is the Supreme `14:00`.
- So Vedic philosophy is actually monotheistic — a multi-level monotheism with many gods under the one Supreme `14:00`.
- In the Abrahamic traditions the general idea is of a true God and false gods — that the other gods do not exist `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Demigod worship and merging into Brahman are both forms of sacrifice within the broad Vedic culture; the many gods are assistants of the one Supreme, making the Vedic vision a multi-level monotheism. [10:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.26 [16:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.26 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes two more kinds of sacrifice, involving the senses `16:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Saṁyamāgni_ — the fire of mental control or self-restraint, into which the hearing process is offered `16:00`.
- _Śabda_ — sound vibration, an example of the sense objects `18:00`.

#### The essential principle of sacrifice [16:00]

- What could be used for our own pleasure, we use as an offering to Kṛṣṇa for His pleasure; whatever remains from that, we use for our own pleasure — and we get a higher pleasure, because higher pleasure comes by connecting with Kṛṣṇa `16:00`.

#### Offering the hearing into the fire of restraint [16:00 – 20:00]

- Some people turn their ears inward and offer the hearing process into the fire of mental control `16:00`.
- With the ears we can hear many things — movies, music, many kinds of sound — which may give some pleasure; instead, the mind is used to control what we hear: we do not expose ourselves to material sound vibrations, and we focus on hearing spiritual sound vibrations `16:00`.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda explains this as the practice of _brahmacārīs_, who do not look for sensual pleasure and use their senses only for spiritual purposes; the _brahmacārī_ hears only words concerning Kṛṣṇa consciousness — the holy name and the Bhāgavatam `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### Offering the sense objects into the senses [18:00]

- Others offer the sense objects, such as sound, into the fire of the senses: this refers to the regulated householder `18:00`.
- When sense contact takes place within the principles of _dharma_, it is not unrestricted or irreligious; it is also based on the principle of sacrifice `18:00`.
- The teacher notes that Kṛṣṇa says elsewhere in the Gītā that desire not contrary to _dharma_ is His own manifestation `18:00`.
- Using the senses in a regulated way is itself a sacrifice; by such regulation people become purified `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The brahmacārī sacrifices by restraining the senses, and the regulated householder by enjoying them within dharma; in both cases what could be used for our own pleasure is offered first, and regulation itself becomes the sacrifice. [16:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.27 [20:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.27 -->

**Context.** So far we have seen demigod worship (_sakāma_ karma-kāṇḍa), _karma-yoga_, _jñāna-yoga_, and the regulated _brahmacārī_ and householder. Now Kṛṣṇa describes another kind of _jñāna-yoga_ `20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sarvāṇīndriya-karmāṇi_ — the activities of all the senses, which are offered `20:00`.
- _Prāṇa-karmāṇi_ — the activities of the life airs, referring to _prāṇāyāma_, in which breathing is regulated and disciplined to turn consciousness inward `20:00`.
- _Pratyāhāra_ — a stage in _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ where one cuts off all the senses `20:00 – 22:00`.
- _Ātma-saṁyama-yoga_ — self-control, control of the mind, trying to turn inward `22:00`.
- _Jñāna-dīpita_ — Prabhupāda translates it as "because of the urge for self-realisation"; _dīpa_ is light and _jñāna_ is knowledge — they are illumined by knowledge `22:00`.
- _Parāg-ātmā_ and _pratyag-ātmā_ — the soul directed outward through attachment, and the soul turned inward; becoming _pratyag-ātmā_ is the ultimate goal here `22:00`.

#### Different sādhanas of the jñāna-yogī [22:00]

- A _jñāna-yogī_ may use the _sādhana_ of _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_, because the primary focus is on turning consciousness inward `22:00`.
- Some _jñānīs_ use the _sādhana_ of bhakti-yoga, thinking the personal form is a temporary manifestation of the Absolute Truth, and on that basis try to turn inward `22:00`.
- Others do it by speculation — "not this, not this" `22:00`.
- Whichever way they do it, they turn away from the outer world toward the inner `22:00`.

#### The five vāyus [22:00 – 26:00]

- Prabhupāda briefly mentions five _vāyus_ to explain _prāṇa-karmāṇi_ `22:00 – 24:00`:
  - **_Prāṇa_** — basically respiration.
  - **_Apāna_** — the air that goes downward (as stated at `34:00`).
  - **_Vyāna_** — contracts and expands.
  - **_Samāna_** — adjusts the equilibrium.
  - **_Udāna_** — goes upward.
- _Prāṇa_ is both one of the five and a generic word for all of them — just as "mind" is one of the subtle elements but is also used generically for the whole subtle body `24:00`.
- **Vertigo** — when a person spins round and round and feels vertigo, it is because the _vāyu_ is getting disoriented `24:00`.
- There are different ways of looking at the body: modern medicine offers one; the Vedic culture offers another, in terms of _prāṇa_ and _śakti_; Āyurveda offers a related but slightly different way. This will be discussed more in the eighth chapter `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### Turning inward is the real goal [26:00]

- Nowadays _prāṇāyāma_ is practised for health — "do _anuloma-viloma_ and your respiratory disease will go away" `26:00`.
- That may happen and may be beneficial, but it is not the primary goal; all these regulative activities are ultimately meant to turn our consciousness inward `26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The jñāna-yogī sacrifices the activities of the senses and the life airs into the fire of self-control, illumined by knowledge; breath control may bring health, but its real purpose is to turn consciousness inward. [20:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.28 [26:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.28 -->

**Context.** In one verse Kṛṣṇa describes several more kinds of _yajña_: some sacrifice wealth, some practise austerity, some practise _yoga_, and some study scripture `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dravya-yajña_ — sacrifice of wealth or possessions `26:00`.
- _Tapo-yajña_ — sacrifice through austerity `26:00`.
- _Yoga-yajña_ — sacrifice through the practice of _yoga_ `26:00`.
- _Svādhyāya-jñāna-yajña_ — sacrifice through serious study of scripture `26:00`.
- _Saṁśita-vratāḥ_ — those who take strict vows and act accordingly `26:00`.

#### Charity as dravya-yajña [28:00]

- Prabhupāda lists charitable activities called _dravya-yajña_: _dharmaśālās_, _anna-kṣetras_, _atithi-śālās_, orphanages and _vidyā-pīṭhas_ `28:00`.
- People doing charitable activities are to be commended for the principle of sacrifice, even if they do it for name and fame `28:00`.
- At least they seek fame in a way that benefits others — not by partying or by competing over "how many bottles of liquor you drank; I'll drink more." Seeking fame while thinking of others' benefit indicates consciousness situated in a higher mode `28:00`.

#### Cāturmāsya and the ācāryas' beards [28:00 – 32:00]

- Prabhupāda speaks about _cāturmāsya_, which can become quite an intricate ritual; he does not go into the technicalities but gives general principles — avoid this in this month, this in that month `28:00`.
- Some people take just one morsel of food per day during _cāturmāsya_; there is also the _cāndrāyaṇa_ austerity `28:00 – 30:00`.
- During _cāturmāsya_ the candidate may not eat certain foods, may not eat twice a day, may not leave home, and may not shave for four months `30:00`.
- People sometimes ask why ācāryas such as Gaura Kiśora dāsa Bābājī are pictured with beards — as if a beard were something fanciful to show off. Hairstyle is considered a sign of beauty and a defining characteristic of a person `30:00`.
- The paintings are made according to how the ācāryas were remembered by their followers; when performing austerities like _cāturmāsya_, one puts aside the body, minimises its needs and focuses more and more on the Absolute Truth `30:00`.
- There are two ways not to care for the hair: neglect it completely and let it grow however it grows, or shave it off. Vaiṣṇavas normally shave, but some let it grow without bothering about it; both are austerity, because one is not trying to make oneself look attractive `30:00 – 32:00`.
- All these _yogīs_ are engaged in different types of sacrifice, seeking a higher status of life; all sacrifices are ultimately meant for elevation `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Charity, austerity, yoga and scriptural study are all sacrifices that seek a higher status of life; even charity done for fame is commendable as sacrifice, and the austerities of the ācāryas reflect absorption in the Absolute Truth, not concern for appearance. [26:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.29 [32:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.29 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the next kind of _yajña_: controlling the breath, related to the process of _aṣṭāṅga-yoga_ `32:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Apāna_ — the air that goes downward; _prāṇa_ — the air that goes outward `32:00`.
- _Prāṇāpāna-gatī ruddhvā_ — checking the movement of the airs and bringing them to a steady state of neutrality `34:00`.
- _Pūraka_, _recaka_, _kumbhaka_ — technical stages of _prāṇāyāma_: breathing in, breathing out, and the complete stopping of both currents, called _kumbhaka-yoga_ `34:00`.
- _Niyatāhārāḥ_ — those who regulate their eating `34:00`.

#### How Prabhupāda divides the verses [32:00]

- Based on the continuity of thought, Prabhupāda divides the verses so that a part of the following verse is brought up into BG 4.29; this verse therefore has six lines (a to f), and a later verse has only two `32:00`.

#### The technique of breath control [32:00 – 34:00]

- One offers the outgoing air into the incoming, and the incoming into the outgoing; in this way one checks the movement of the airs and brings them to a steady state of neutrality `32:00 – 34:00`.
- Prabhupāda explains that the _prāṇāyāma-yogī_ practises breathing the opposite way until the currents are neutralised in _pūraka_, equilibrium; this increases will power and regulates oneself `34:00`.
- These techniques are not part of the _yuga-dharma_ for Kali-yuga, so the teacher does not go into them `34:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's consistent theme [34:00 – 36:00]

- This was one of the spiritual processes used for gradual spiritual advancement, and Kṛṣṇa mentions it; but it is ultimately meant for attaining Brahman `34:00`.
- In all these purports Prabhupāda maintains the consistent theme that we should practise Kṛṣṇa consciousness: these are all valid rituals and processes, part of the broad Vedic culture, but in Kali-yuga we practise Kṛṣṇa consciousness `34:00 – 36:00`.
- Seeing all these different processes helps us understand one part of the rationale of Kṛṣṇa's final instruction in BG 18.66 — give up all varieties of religion present in the Vedic culture and surrender to Him `36:00`.
- Prabhupāda is not cancelling what Kṛṣṇa is giving; he is giving the conclusion throughout, so that we do not misunderstand or lose it `36:00`.

#### Regulated eating [36:00 – 38:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that reducing eating is very helpful for sense control, and without sense control there is no possibility of getting out of material entanglement `36:00`.
- The idea is not to eat so little that we have no energy for service and our responsibilities `36:00`.
- The important point is not to make eating a primary activity or a primary fantasy — always thinking "what is the next _prasāda_, the next food coming" — and not to take whatever item in whatever quantity `36:00`.
- The sixth chapter speaks of regulation in eating and recreation; if we keep both the quantity and the quality of _prasādam_ regulated, it goes a long way in keeping our senses controlled, and sense control is the foundation for liberation `36:00 – 38:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Breath control and regulated eating are valid Vedic sacrifices aimed at sense control and Brahman, but in Kali-yuga Prabhupāda keeps Kṛṣṇa consciousness in view throughout — regulating even prasādam so that eating never becomes life's central fantasy. [32:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.30 – 4.31 [38:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.30 -->

<!-- vedabase:4.31 -->

**Context.** Having given different kinds of _yajña_, Kṛṣṇa now tells the principle underlying them — their purpose and essence — in the next set of verses, through BG 4.33 `38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yajña-vidaḥ_ — knowers of _yajña_ `38:00`.
- _Yajña-kṣapita-kalmaṣāḥ_ — _kalmaṣa_ is impurity; they become purified of all impurity by _yajña_ `38:00`.
- _Yajña-śiṣṭa_ — the remnants of _yajña_; part of the famous BG 3.13 also `38:00`.
- _Amṛta-bhujaḥ_ — those who taste nectar; the remnants of _yajña_ are nectar `38:00`.
- _Brahma sanātanam_ — the eternal spiritual platform `40:00`.
- _Kuru-sattama_ — best of the Kuru dynasty: Kṛṣṇa indicates that Arjuna is not a person who neglects sacrifice `42:00`.

#### Two results: purification and liberation [38:00 – 40:00]

- The first result of _yajña_ is purification; then, enjoying the fruit, one attains liberation — the eternal spiritual platform `40:00`.
- Not all _yajñas_ lead to purification at the same speed, naturally, because there are different levels of commitment and different kinds of sacrifice `40:00`.
- But Kṛṣṇa gives a unified vision: a diversity of forms — householders, _yogīs_ regulating the breath, others absorbed in study — with a unified principle and purpose: purification from impurity, and attaining the eternal Brahman `40:00`.

#### No happiness without sacrifice [40:00 – 44:00]

- A very short but potent statement: without the performance of _yajña_, one cannot be happy even in this world — what to speak of the next `40:00 – 42:00`.
- People who go out just for enjoyment — party, movie, beach, picnic — are pleasure-driven and pleasure-centred, not thinking of others: "me first and me only" `42:00`.
- Living like this, they get some titillation but no deep satisfaction; when we sacrifice for something higher, even in this world we get deep fulfilment `42:00`.
- **Sacrifice for the nation** — at one level it is illusion, since the person thinks "I am the body, my body is connected with my country"; but within material reality, sacrificing for one's country is nobler than just living to seek one's own pleasure `42:00`.
- Living by this principle purifies one, at least in giving up gross sensory desires; living for a cause higher than oneself brings fulfilment, while living only for one's own pleasure makes satisfaction dry up `44:00`.

#### Smiling faces, crying hearts [44:00 – 46:00]

- The teacher recites a short poem: hearts filled with fears, minds pierced by desires, eyes on the verge of tears — and they say "cheers" `44:00`.
- **Fears** — "What are people thinking about me? Do they think I am attractive?" `44:00`
- **Desires** — "That person has a better dress, a better car, a better-looking or wealthier spouse than mine — I want that" `44:00`.
- So it is a show, not happiness: people put on a big smile because they want to show the world they are successful; nobody wants to be thought a failure `44:00 – 46:00`.
- Their hearts are crying — out of loneliness, purposelessness and, ultimately, lovelessness; people talk about love, but love has no meaning without sacrifice, and the highest level of sacrifice is love for Kṛṣṇa `46:00`.
- This principle applies at the material, religious and spiritual levels: the higher the cause for which one sacrifices, the greater the happiness and fulfilment `46:00`.
- Sacrifice means giving up something little for something great; the great is the eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa and its unending, increasing fulfilment, for which we give up the flickering pleasures of this world `46:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
All forms of sacrifice share one purpose — purification and liberation — and without sacrifice there is no real happiness even here: self-centred pleasure leaves smiling faces with crying hearts, while giving up something little for something great, ultimately for Kṛṣṇa, brings growing fulfilment. [38:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.33 [48:00 – 56:01]

<!-- vedabase:4.33 -->

**Context.** Among the different kinds of _yajña_, Kṛṣṇa now says which is better `48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śreyān_ — better `50:00`.
- _Dravya-maya-yajña_ — the sacrifice of one's wealth or possessions `50:00`.
- _Jñāna-yajña_ — the sacrifice of knowledge `50:00`.
- _Sarvaṁ karmākhilam_ — "all" and "entire": all activities `50:00`.
- _Parisamāpyate_ — culminates, attains its highest perfection `50:00`.
- _Jñāna_ — here the knowledge of our true identity and our eternal activity; the Gītā uses the word generically for spiritual knowledge, not just impersonal knowledge `50:00`.

#### Why the sacrifice of knowledge is better [50:00]

- Some people sacrifice their possessions; others engage their intellectual faculty. Those who engage the intellect are better `50:00`.
- It is the intellectual faculty that is to be educated, and it is the intellect that will redirect our heart from matter toward Kṛṣṇa — from the world to the reality beyond it `50:00`.
- Spiritual knowledge ultimately culminates in knowledge of Kṛṣṇa, as later chapters show: in the fifteenth chapter Kṛṣṇa says He is the one to be known through all the Vedas, and that one who knows Him as the Supreme knows everything `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### Renouncing in knowledge [52:00]

- If someone gives up a kingdom and goes to the forest, that is one way; but it is better to understand and act in knowledge `52:00`.
- The sacrifice of knowledge is better because it leads to the development of the intellect, and ultimately one attains proper spiritual knowledge `52:00`.
- Many people give charity, and that is good; but if they are not advancing in spiritual knowledge, their charity will at best be in _sattva-guṇa_ and keep them bound `52:00`.
- When they understand "I am not the body, I am a soul with an eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa," their charity becomes part of the process that leads to purification and, ultimately, liberation `52:00`.
- The glory of knowledge, and how to cultivate it, Kṛṣṇa will speak about in the next verse `52:00`.

#### Recap of the whole section [54:00]

- **BG 4.24** — the general principle: everything is latently spiritual, being the Lord's energy, so everything can be spiritualised; the principle of _yajña_ is to spiritualise, as Kṛṣṇa recommended in BG 4.23 `54:00`.
- **BG 4.25–4.29** — various forms of _yajña_ performed by different people, all striving for purification `54:00`.
- **BG 4.30–4.33** — the principles and purpose of _yajña_: purification and liberation; without _yajña_ one cannot be happy; all forms come from the Vedas and are rooted in work; and _yajña_ done in knowledge is better than _yajña_ done in ignorance, because acting in knowledge deepens that knowledge until it becomes internalised and realised `54:00`.
- Ultimately all _yajña_ should lead to knowledge; how Arjuna should acquire that knowledge will be discussed in the next verse `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The sacrifice of knowledge excels the sacrifice of possessions because it educates the intellect that turns the heart from matter to Kṛṣṇa; all work culminates in knowing Him, and even charity liberates only when joined with that knowledge. [50:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
