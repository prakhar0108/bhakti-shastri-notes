## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 4.14 [00:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.14 -->

**Context.** When we act, two things happen: we act to get some result, and while acting we get emotionally involved — excited, annoyed, delighted. Something outside affects us inside. Kṛṣṇa says neither happens to Him `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma-phala_ — the fruit of action; Kṛṣṇa does not seek the external result of work `02:00`.
- _Theoretical versus transformational knowledge_ — knowing about Kṛṣṇa versus knowing Kṛṣṇa `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### Complete, and not affected by work [02:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is complete; He is not affected internally by the work He does, and does not seek its external result `02:00`.
- He appears in the material world like the living beings, but He is not in the material realm; His motivation is transcendental, so He is not bound by _karma_ `02:00`.
- If He is not materially motivated, He acts out of love — and love is not a material _karma-phala_ `02:00`.

#### Knowledge that transforms [02:00 – 08:00]

- One who understands Kṛṣṇa in this way is also not bound; such understanding is not theoretical but transformational `02:00`.
- **The Battle of Waterloo** — as devotees we are not much interested in Waterloo, where Napoleon was defeated, or in battles from Indian history such as Plassey, but we are interested in the Battle of Kurukṣetra, because it is connected with Kṛṣṇa `04:00`.
- **Doctor and mother** — a doctor examining a child checks heartbeat, pulse rate and other parameters, and knows about the child; the mother may not know the blood counts, platelet count or urine composition, but she knows the child `04:00`.
- Similarly, a clinical approach can give much knowledge about God — "God did this, God is like that" — but that is theoretical knowledge `06:00`.
- Transformational knowledge changes our direction: when we come to know Kṛṣṇa, we become purified, redirect our love toward Him and return back to Him `06:00`.

#### Is the Lord responsible for what happens? [08:00 – 10:00]

- He is the ultimate cause, but what happens to people is the result of their _karma_; He is the overseer of the whole system, not the specific cause of one person's punishment or another's compensation `08:00`.
- **Two fields** — two places get the same proper rain, yet crops grow well in one and not the other, because different seeds were sown and different cultivation was done `08:00`.
- **Twins** — two children born in the same family, given the same love and care by the parents, grow up very differently, because they carry different inclinations from their previous lives `08:00 – 10:00`.
- So the Lord is not responsible, and not partial to anyone; we ourselves are responsible for what we do `10:00`.

#### Reciprocity is not partiality [12:00 – 14:00]

- Imagine meeting someone with a stone-like face who reacts the same no matter what happens — like a statue. At one level one may appreciate how strict the person is, but how could one develop a relationship with such a person? `12:00`
- What makes a person lovable is reciprocity; someone who does not reciprocate does not have much meaning as a personality `12:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's partiality will be discussed in the ninth chapter; the point here is that reciprocity is not partiality `12:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa is not personally involved in what happens in this world and has no favourites; He does not normally interfere with the laws of _karma_, but above the laws of _karma_ He functions by the laws of love `12:00 – 14:00`.
- One who understands this is liberated `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa acts out of love, untouched by work and its fruits; knowing Him — not just knowing about Him — frees us, and it also shows that He is not responsible for or partial in our karma, but simply reciprocates. [02:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.15 [14:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.15 -->

**Context.** Many people in ancient times became liberated by understanding Kṛṣṇa in this way and acting accordingly `14:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Time-tested process_ — the path is not untried: like an experienced doctor, it has a long record of results `14:00`.

#### Neither modern nor ancient, but eternal [14:00 – 16:00]

- One of the wonderful things about Kṛṣṇa consciousness is that it is neither modern nor ancient; it is eternal `16:00`.
- It has been going on for a long time, and it purified and liberated many people in the past `16:00`.

#### How to act without bondage [16:00 – 18:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is speaking about _karma-yoga_. If a person wants to retire from activities without knowledge, Kṛṣṇa says one needs to know how to act `16:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa's message to Arjuna: "You are concerned about karmic bondage — I agree, it is an important concern. But do not assume that by renouncing action you will break free and by doing action you will get bound." There is a way to act by which one stays free from bondage `16:00`.
- It is not _karma_ that binds; it is the motivation that makes _karma_ binding `16:00`.
- **Milk and curd** — if a person takes too much milk he gets a disorder, but the same milk transformed into curd helps cure that disorder. Similarly, _karma_ itself causes bondage, but _karma_ appropriately transformed cures it `16:00`.
- _Karma_ done with fruitive intentions brings bondage; _karma_ done with devotional intention brings liberation `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Liberation through acting in knowledge is an eternal, time-tested path; it is not action but motivation that binds, and work transformed by devotional intention cures the very bondage that selfish work creates. [14:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.16 – 4.17 [18:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.16 -->

<!-- vedabase:4.17 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now explains how _karma-yoga_ frees one: even the wise do not know what is action and what is inaction, and He will explain it `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma_ — action that produces reaction `26:00`.
- _Vikarma_ — action that produces bad reactions `26:00`.
- _Akarma_ — action that produces no material reaction; this is what Kṛṣṇa will recommend `26:00`.

#### Even the wise are confused: an example from the Mahābhārata [20:00 – 24:00]

- After the Mahābhārata war — at the end of which many soldiers were killed in a cowardly night attack — a lengthy discussion took place `20:00`.
- The king wanted to renounce: "the best thing I should do is to go to the forest; Bhīma, you can become king" `20:00`.
- The others replied: "When we were waiting in the forest, you were also waiting for the kingdom, and you consented to the war"; and, "if your mind is uncontrolled, it will go to the sense objects even in the forest" `20:00 – 22:00`.
- The discussion went on and on, and finally came to the solution: the right course of action is doing the will of the Lord. It was the Lord's will that the battle take place and that you had a part in it; now it is His will that you become king `22:00`.
- Essentially: be a part in the Lord's plan. Nobody can fully understand the Lord's plan, but if we act as He wants, the Lord ultimately takes care of everything `22:00`.

#### Three kinds of action [26:00]

- The whole system of _karma_ — which action brings what reaction — is very intricate and difficult to understand `26:00`.
- Essentially understand three things `26:00`:
  - certain actions produce reactions;
  - certain actions bring bad reactions;
  - certain actions bring no material reactions — and these Kṛṣṇa will recommend.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Even the wise are confused about whether to act or renounce; the resolution is to do the Lord's will as a part in His plan, which is the action that brings no binding reaction. [18:00 – 26:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.18 [26:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.18 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa's style here is to put things in a precise way that titillates the minds of intellectuals; the teacher calls the translation both a tongue twister and a brain twister `26:00 – 28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Buddhimān_ — intelligent; one who sees action in this way is truly intelligent `28:00`.
- _Yukta_ — well situated, well connected, even while performing all kinds of activities, such as fighting a war `28:00`.
- _Karma_ — four general meanings `30:00`:
  1. **Action** — "we are doing _karma_."
  2. **Reaction** — "I am suffering because of my _karma_."
  3. **The system of action and reaction** — the law of _karma_, which nobody can escape.
  4. **One category within the three kinds of action** — as in the previous verse.
- _Akarma_ — two meanings `30:00`:
  1. **Inaction.**
  2. **Action that produces no reaction** — action done on behalf of Kṛṣṇa.

#### Seeing inaction in action [30:00 – 36:00]

- If we act on behalf of a higher authority, we do not become bound by those actions; if we act for our own sake, we do get bound `32:00`.
- So action that produces no reaction means action done on behalf of Kṛṣṇa `32:00`.
- In the first half of the verse, _karma_ refers to action in general (the first meaning), and _akarma_ refers not to inaction but to action without reaction: one sees in external activity no karmic reaction `32:00 – 34:00`.
- **The sannyāsī and the ocean** — the traditional standard is that a _sannyāsī_, having broken free from worldly bonds, should not cross the ocean to other lands lest he become contaminated. That is true at one level — one must be careful about bad association — but Śrīla Prabhupāda went to America to share Kṛṣṇa's love; far from being contaminated, he went to the most contaminated places and transformed them `34:00`.

#### Seeing action in inaction [36:00 – 40:00]

- In the second half, _akarma_ means not doing any external activity, and _karma_ means reaction: one sees karmic reaction even in external inactivity `36:00`.
- A person may be externally renounced but internally dwelling on the sense objects; though doing no external activity, that person still gets bound `38:00`.
- If Arjuna renounced the world and went to the forest, internally he would still be a _kṣatriya_; he would be desiring things and become bound by that `38:00`.
- Inactivity can even be criminal: if someone has a particular duty and does nothing, that too is an activity, with consequences `38:00 – 40:00`.
- When the inner contemplation and motivation are not pure, that fruit eventually manifests at the physical level also; so see seriously what is actually happening and act on that vision `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Work done on Kṛṣṇa's behalf brings no reaction, while outward renunciation with inner desire still binds; the intelligent person judges action not by its outer form but by the motivation behind it. [30:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.19 [42:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.19 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the person whose action does not bind, and how the wise see him `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jñānāgni_ — the fire of knowledge, which burns the seeds of _karma_ `42:00`.
- _Paṇḍita_ — learned; the person who acts without selfish motivation is called a _paṇḍita_ by the _budhāḥ_ `42:00`.
- _Budha_ — the enlightened `42:00`.

#### Burnt seeds [42:00]

- _Karma_ is like a seed: every action is going to produce some fruit afterwards; but if the seed is burnt, it will not sprout `42:00`.
- By the fire of knowledge the seeds of _karma_ are burnt, and such a person is not bound `42:00`.

#### Superficial vision versus deep vision [42:00]

- Superficially, people think: "this person is acting, so he will get bound and is ignorant; that person is inactive, so he is renounced" `42:00`.
- Those with deep knowledge see that a person who acts but is not selfishly motivated is the _paṇḍita_ `42:00`.
- This is a call to sacrifice selfish motivation and to have a higher motivation in life `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge burns the seeds of karma so they cannot sprout; the enlightened recognise as truly learned not the one who stops acting, but the one who acts without selfish motivation. [42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.20 [44:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.20 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa repeats the point: the criterion is not giving up work but giving up attachment to its fruits `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma-phalāsaṅga_ — attachment to the fruit of work; this is what must be given up `44:00`.
- _Nirāśraya_ — here, not dependent on material supports; it does not mean "not taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa" `46:00`.

#### Renounce selfishness, not action [44:00]

- Ultimately the call is to divine selflessness — acting not "for the good of God" (we cannot do good to God) but for the pleasure of God and the service of God `44:00`.
- The one considered a _paṇḍita_ by the enlightened is not the one who renounces action, but the one who renounces selfishness `44:00`.
- "Be a meticulous renouncer — but renounce selfishness, not action. Let no action be motivated by selfishness" `44:00`.

#### Why nirāśraya does not mean rejecting Kṛṣṇa's shelter [46:00 – 48:00]

- Normally we say we have to take shelter of Kṛṣṇa, so why does Kṛṣṇa praise one who is without shelter? `46:00`
- Here Kṛṣṇa is not speaking directly about higher spiritual truths or devotional concepts `46:00`.
- He gave knowledge about Himself briefly in the earlier part of the chapter — His supreme position, how knowing Him frees one, and that He is the goal of all paths — but the focus of the rest of the chapter is not on Himself `46:00`.
- From BG 4.16 onward He is basically speaking about _karma-yoga_, returning to where He left off in BG 3.35; Arjuna's question in BG 3.36 and his question in BG 4.4 about the sun-god led to the intervening topics `46:00`.
- So right now He is not introducing Himself as the ultimate shelter; the focus is first on detachment from things, and attachment to Kṛṣṇa will come in later chapters `46:00 – 48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The renunciation Kṛṣṇa asks for is of selfishness, not of action; "without shelter" here means not depending on material supports, because at this stage of the Gītā the focus is detachment, with attachment to Kṛṣṇa unfolding later. [44:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.21 – 4.22 [48:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.21 -->

<!-- vedabase:4.22 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa continues describing the transcendentalist through verse 23: one with no possessive mentality, satisfied with what comes of its own accord, and steady in success and failure `48:00 – 56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _No possessive mentality_ — the person does not act out of a sense of ownership over the results `50:00`.
- _Satisfied with what comes of its own accord_ — by the arrangement of nature, whatever comes is accepted with satisfaction `54:00`.

#### What motivates action? [50:00]

- Generally we act out of some desire: "I study because I want to come first in class and get good marks; I want a job, so I need a degree; I work because I want a salary; I have hobbies because I enjoy them" `50:00`.
- So what is the motive of a transcendentalist? If someone wins a war and becomes king, he will be living in a palace and eating delightful feasts — how is that not selfish? `50:00`
- Yes, we have to act for the maintenance of the body; but how we act depends on our nature and duty, and a _kṣatriya_ who acts in this spirit will not be bound `50:00`.

#### Like a part of a machine [50:00 – 52:00]

- A machine part requires maintenance, cleaning and lubrication to stay fit for action; similarly the body is maintained to stay fit for action in the Lord's loving service `50:00`.
- We are not machines; the point of the example is coordination and cooperation `52:00`.

#### The faithful animal [52:00 – 54:00]

- The example of an animal can seem disturbing: an animal has no proprietorship even over its own body, and does not protest whatever happens `52:00`.
- The point is not that Kṛṣṇa is a cruel proprietor. In Western civilisation dogs are kept in houses, and a dog is faithful to its master till its last breath — under the master's leadership, always with the master, even saving the master `52:00`.
- This is not a glorification of whatever the master does; it is simply an acknowledgement of a virtue present in a faithful animal `54:00`.

#### Steady in success and failure [54:00 – 56:00]

- Because the person is not possessive about the results of activity, he does not get too excited or depressed `56:00`.
- His mood: "I will go on doing my duty, because I understand that results come not just by my efforts but ultimately by past karmic reactions also. Sometimes results come, sometimes they don't, but I will stay steady" `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The transcendentalist maintains the body only to stay fit for service, cooperates like a faithful part of the Lord's arrangement, and, not possessive of results, stays steady whether success comes or not. [48:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.23 [56:00 – 59:03]

<!-- vedabase:4.23 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes this description: one who works in the spirit of _yajña_ finds that his _karma_ dissolves entirely `56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yajña_ — essentially sacrifice; the person acts in the mood of _yajña_ `58:00`.

#### Work for the purpose of yajña [56:00 – 58:00]

- When one's intention in work, in totality, is _yajña_, that _karma_ dissolves; work done for the purpose of _yajña_ does not keep one bound `56:00 – 58:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa earlier described the system of _yajña_ in the third chapter, from BG 3.9 onward; now He brings back that point: _yajña_ is not just fire — it essentially means sacrifice `58:00`.
- This will be expanded in the next section, BG 4.24–4.33 `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Work done in the spirit of sacrifice — not merely fire ritual, but offering one's activity to the Lord — dissolves completely and leaves no binding trace. [56:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
