## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 4.34 [02:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.34 -->

**Context.** A well-known, frequently quoted verse: the knowledge spoken of in 4.33 is acquired by going to a spiritual master who has seen the truth, through three things `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Jñāna_ — knowledge; Kṛṣṇa speaks of _jñāna_, not _bhakti_, here because He has not yet introduced _bhakti_ directly — He will explain later (7.19) how all _jñāna_ culminates in _bhakti_ `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Jñānī_ — one who has knowledge; in 3.39 Kṛṣṇa described all living entities as _jñānīs_ whose knowledge is covered by lust. The spiritual master is one who has removed that covering and seen the truth, so he can explain it to us `04:00`.
- Humbleness — admitting "I don't know the way"; it includes offering obeisances, falling down before the spiritual master `06:00`.
- Inquisitiveness — vital inquiry, which develops our own capacity for spiritual perception `06:00 – 08:00`.
- Service-mindedness — applying what is learned; the "taking of the medicine" `10:00`.
- _Paramparā_ — disciplic succession; a link not just of heads but of hearts `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### Tap His grace by humbleness, inquisitiveness and service-mindedness [04:00 – 12:00]

- The teacher draws on one of his daily Gītā articles on this verse; "His grace" is Kṛṣṇa's grace `04:00 – 06:00`.
- **Humbleness** — the spiritual journey is an inner journey: we are the soul, eternally connected with Kṛṣṇa who is also within, but we cannot discern the internal landscape, so we need a guide `06:00`.
  - Like a patient before a doctor: if the patient imagines he knows better than the doctor, he cannot be treated.
- **Inquisitiveness** — the injunction calls not for blind faith in the name of humbleness, but for developing our own spiritual perception `08:00`.
  - Deep thinking leads to deeper observation: advertisements, movies and novels show smiling, laughing people and happy endings, but look down at the road and people are sulking, frowning and scowling; few, if any, are happy, and even then only for a short time.
  - So we start seeing for ourselves what the _guru_ is saying. Humbleness is the launching pad for learning, not the whole of it.
- **Service-mindedness** — knowing the diagnosis in exhaustive detail does not cure; the prescription must be taken `10:00`.
  - The most transformational realisation is that Kṛṣṇa alone is the source of the best enjoyment; once convinced of this, the world's temptations no longer allure us.
  - We get this conviction by living according to it — serving Kṛṣṇa with as affectionate an attitude as we can, even if we do not yet feel much satisfaction.
- The three work as progressive steps `10:00 – 12:00`:
  1. Humbleness enables the _guru_ to show us the way.
  2. Inquisitiveness enables us to start seeing the way ourselves — to internalise it.
  3. Service-mindedness enables us to move along the way, toward being reinstated in our eternal spiritual position.

#### Why a guru is needed [12:00 – 14:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport begins: the path of spiritual realisation is undoubtedly difficult `12:00`.
- _Bhakti_ is the easiest among spiritual paths, but spiritual paths in general are difficult: we must fight inner demons — lust, anger, greed, pride and illusion — formidable forces that want to keep us enslaved life after life.
- Mental speculation, dry arguments and independent study of books can give some preliminary knowledge and slight movement on the right path; serious progress needs a spiritual master.

#### Mutual testing [14:00]

- Prabhupāda makes the point that the spiritual master tests the disciple to see that he is sincere and serious.
- In Kṛṣṇa consciousness this is standardised — e.g. chanting sixteen rounds and following the four regulative principles, generally for a minimum of about one year, depending on the spiritual master.
- The disciple also has to test the spiritual master, since many people today claim to be spiritual masters.

#### The paramparā: a link of hearts [14:00 – 18:00]

- The spiritual master turns us towards Kṛṣṇa through his own example and his connection with Kṛṣṇa; he must come in _paramparā_ `14:00`.
- A link of hearts, not just heads: Prabhupāda's books differ greatly from those of his predecessors — not in content, but in presentation, style and approach. A disciple is not a robot or recorder repeating verbatim, though that may be required in some situations `14:00 – 16:00`.
- The spiritual master lives the scriptural principles of selfless devotion; seeing this, the disciple is inspired; the principle in the master's heart is transferred to the disciple's, and their hearts are linked. That disciple later inspires others in turn — selfless devotion is what links the succession `16:00`.
- Piety transforms into spirituality when there is a connection to the _guru_ and a commitment to the process `16:00 – 18:00`:
  - Piety — belief in being good, avoiding sin, immorality and illegal activity — is good, but it is in goodness.
  - Impiety is in the lower modes; spirituality is transcendental.

#### The guru removes the wall [18:00 – 20:00]

- Some say, "I want a direct relationship with God; why should a _guru_ come in between?" The teacher answers: the _guru_ does not come between us and God; he removes what is between us and God `18:00`.
- The soul and the Supersoul are neighbours, like the two birds in the tree, but there is a wall between them:
  - **Misconceptions** — "I am independent, I am the body, I can enjoy at the bodily level."
  - **Misconceived, misdirected desires** — which take the soul's consciousness away from Kṛṣṇa.
- The spiritual master gives knowledge that shatters the misconceptions, and the chanting of the holy names and remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, which redirect our desires back to Kṛṣṇa `18:00 – 20:00`.
- "He who is his own _guru_ has a fool as a disciple" — we cannot cure ourselves by our own efforts `20:00`.

#### Why doesn't Kṛṣṇa reveal Himself directly? [20:00 – 24:00]

- The question is not what Kṛṣṇa can do, but what He wants us to do — and here He categorically says to approach a spiritual master `20:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa loves all, but He is reciprocal: He especially loves, and wants to glorify, devotees who have sacrificed everything in this world for His sake `20:00 – 22:00`.
- Their ultimate reward is inner (a flood of love) and spiritual (return to the spiritual world). But how can He reward them in this world, when they want nothing of it? `22:00`
- The prison analogy `22:00 – 24:00`:
  - The material world is a prison, and Durgā Devī its presiding deity; with her three prongs she inflicts the threefold miseries, whose message is "go back to Kṛṣṇa."
  - The purpose of a reformatory is reform, not suffering.
  - In a jail, the most precious thing is not buried treasure but the key to the door. Kṛṣṇa gives His devotees authority over that key — they become the distributors of the knowledge of how to get out.
- Without becoming submissive to devotees, this knowledge may be understood theoretically, but it will not transform us or redirect our heart's love to Kṛṣṇa `24:00`.

#### Everyone follows someone [24:00 – 26:00]

- Someone who says "don't go to any _guru_" is effectively saying "I am a _guru_ telling you not to go to any _guru_"; following them is accepting them as _guru_.
- Rather than resenting the principle of following, redirect it by knowledge: choose the right authority, and by that become purified, enlightened and liberated.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge is received, not manufactured: humbleness lets the guru show the way, inquisitiveness lets us see it ourselves, and service lets us walk it — and the guru does not stand between us and Kṛṣṇa but removes the wall of misconceptions and misdirected desires. [04:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.35 [26:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.35 -->

**Context.** Having said where knowledge comes from, Kṛṣṇa describes its results: one who has it will not fall into illusion again, and will see all living beings as spiritual and as belonging to Kṛṣṇa `26:00 – 28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Illusion — the present state; by this knowledge one will not fall into it again `26:00`.
- "All living beings without exception" — the knower sees them as spiritual, and as "Mine," i.e. Kṛṣṇa's `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Māyā_ — Prabhupāda explains that the sense of an existence separate from Kṛṣṇa is called _māyā_ `30:00`.

#### The first explicit statement of our relationship with Kṛṣṇa [28:00]

- This is the first verse in the Gītā where Kṛṣṇa speaks of His relationship with the living entities.
- In the second chapter He spoke of the soul but not explicitly of its relationship with the Supreme Soul; He did say to fix the mind on Him (2.61) and to have faith in Him (3.30), but the eternal relationship was not stated.
- Applied to the battlefield: "All the warriors assembled here are My parts and parcels, My children; I am concerned for them, and what I am telling you is ultimately for their best. Don't see them only as grandfather, uncle, cousin, brother — those are temporary relationships."

#### Two results of jñāna [28:00 – 30:00]

1. Freedom from illusion.
2. Acquisition of spiritual vision — seeing all beings in relation to Kṛṣṇa.

- A practical test: if those who claim to have a _guru_ are still caught in worldly attachments and "I and mine," they have not internalised the knowledge `30:00`.
- Most disciples are still seekers, but some change should be visible; a seriously practising initiated devotee's life, centred on Kṛṣṇa, will differ significantly even from that of ordinary pious people `30:00`.

#### Unified vision [30:00 – 32:00]

- Not only the things of this world, but even the millions of incarnations and expansions should not be seen as separate from Kṛṣṇa — this unified vision is the result of spiritual knowledge.
- Bodily distinctions ("I am a man, a woman, this or that") arise from forgetfulness of our service to the Lord `32:00`.
- Liberation, in the well-known Bhāgavatam verse 2.10.6 the teacher cites, is giving up all other forms — male, female, Indian, American, human, demigod, non-human — and being situated in one's eternal, original form `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Real knowledge shows itself in vision: freedom from "I and mine," and seeing every being — and every incarnation — as connected with Kṛṣṇa; this is the Gītā's first explicit statement that all souls are His. [26:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.36 [32:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.36 -->

**Context.** The process is so exalted that anybody can be purified and liberated, no matter how sinful — even the most sinful of all, by boarding the boat of transcendental knowledge, crosses the ocean of miseries `32:00 – 34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Superlative degree — the verse refers to the most sinful of all sinful people `34:00`.
- Boat — transcendental knowledge; the ocean — material existence, the ocean of miseries `34:00`.

#### Where we are vs. where we are looking [34:00 – 36:00]

- What matters is not where we are among the modes (ignorance, passion, goodness, transcendence), but where we are looking.
- Many Indians of that time still lived traditional pious lives in goodness — relatively close to Kṛṣṇa — but were looking toward the West, largely in passion and ignorance, toward the hope and hype of material enjoyment `34:00`.
- Many of Prabhupāda's early disciples were hippies — in ignorance, breaking all regulative principles, misled into drugs — yet fed up with material enjoyment and looking upward for something higher; intrigued by Indian spirituality, they took up Kṛṣṇa consciousness and swiftly became exalted devotees `36:00`.

#### The logical paradox [36:00 – 38:00]

- If someone is sinful, how can he be knowledgeable — how can the most sinful board the boat of knowledge?
- At one level the verse is a rhetorical glorification: knowledge is so powerful that even a sinful person who chooses to live by it is elevated (the teacher attributes this explanation to an _ācārya_).
- Practically, that rhetoric becomes real through the intervening mercy of the spiritual master: if sinful souls somehow become attracted to Kṛṣṇa consciousness, they can be elevated.
- From the standpoint of _karma-yoga_ and _jñāna_ the verse is rhetorical; from the standpoint of _bhakti-yoga_, where the Gītā is ultimately leading, any person can be elevated `38:00`.

#### Different boats [38:00 – 40:00]

- "Boat" is used for different things — Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, the human body, and here transcendental knowledge.
- The human body alone is not enough; the boat must be directed in the right direction, and that direction comes from transcendental knowledge.
- Prabhupāda's purport: one dropped in the ocean can swim only so long; if someone places him in a boat, he crosses. The teacher applies this: ISKCON is the boat Śrīla Prabhupāda has brought into our lives — our task is not to jump out, even amid difficulties, conflicts or disasters; being in the boat is always safer than being in the ocean `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
No past disqualifies anyone: what counts is where we are looking, and the guru's mercy makes the "rhetorical" promise real — so having boarded the boat, our only job is to stay in it. [34:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.37 [40:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.37 -->

**Context.** A second example: as fire burns firewood to ashes, the fire of knowledge burns all karmic reactions `40:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Fire of knowledge — the same point as 4.19 (one whose _karma_ is burnt by the fire of knowledge); knowledge received from the spiritual master becomes an inner fire `42:00`.
- Seed — all _karma_ is like a seed; a burnt seed gives no result `42:00`.
- _Bīja_ — reactions stored in seed and other forms; these too are burnt `42:00`.

#### Two examples, two progressive points [42:00]

- **Boat** — the material world is an ocean of misery and knowledge takes us out of it; but the water remains in the ocean.
- **Fire** — what is inside us (our _anarthas_, our bad _karma_ and its stored reactions) is itself completely burnt, as firewood is consumed. The destruction of _karma_ is supremely auspicious.
- Prabhupāda's discussion of the stages of karmic reactions will be taken up in the ninth chapter `42:00`.

#### Beyond good and bad karma [44:00]

- As Prabhupāda quotes, one overcomes both good and bad reactions — both auspicious and inauspicious activities.
- There is then no good karmic bondage either: one need not come back even in goodness; spiritual knowledge takes one beyond, to transcendence.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge does more than lift us out of the ocean — like fire, it consumes the seeds of karma within, both sinful and pious, so that nothing remains to bind us. [42:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.38 [44:00 – 55:56]

<!-- vedabase:4.38 -->

**Context.** After the specific examples of the boat and fire, Kṛṣṇa concludes the glorification of knowledge with a general principle: nothing is as pure as transcendental knowledge, and one mature in _yoga_ in due course of time delights within oneself `44:00 – 46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- Purity — nothing in this world is as pure as transcendental knowledge `46:00`.
- Mature / perfected in _yoga_ — when one is accomplished, one delights in oneself `44:00`.
- "In due course of time" — things happen according to their own course and cannot be artificially accelerated `48:00 – 50:00`.

#### What this knowledge is [46:00]

- Knowledge of the _ātmā_ and, ultimately, of the _ātmā_'s connection with the _Paramātmā_.
- Kṛṣṇa has not stated it explicitly yet; He has described it by its results — freedom from illusion and seeing all beings connected with Him.
- He will say more in the seventh chapter (knowledge of the Absolute) and the ninth (the most confidential knowledge, the king of all knowledge).

#### Delighting within: the pond analogy [46:00 – 48:00]

- The soul is by nature joyful; as we keep practising devotional service we become aware of our spiritual identity and of our connection with Kṛṣṇa, and naturally feel joy.
- Prabhupāda: one situated in transcendental knowledge need not search for peace elsewhere; he enjoys peace within himself `48:00`.
- Normally, when we look within, the mind is an agitated mass of "I want this, I want that."
- The pond analogy `48:00`:
  - The person outside the pond — gross bodily consciousness.
  - The water — the subtle, mental level.
  - The bed of the pond — the spiritual level.
  - When the water is agitated we cannot see the bottom; by the practice of _yoga_ the mind becomes calm, like still water, and we begin to see within and relish happiness.

#### No instant spirituality [50:00]

- A bud becomes a fully blossomed flower in its own course; extra water does not make it faster.
- "I'll chant 64 rounds and expect to be devoted by the time I finish" — it does not work like that.
- We live in an age of instant tea, instant coffee, instant food — and want instant spirituality, as if pressing a button like an air-conditioner. Consciousness requires purification, which takes time, but Kṛṣṇa assures us it will definitely happen.
- Every single round of chanting and every service is purifying us.

#### When unwanted desires remain [50:00 – 54:00]

- If desires involve breaking the regulative principles, be very rigid in giving them up; if they do not grossly contravene them, do not become too "problem-conscious" — we will gradually be purified `50:00 – 52:00`.
- As we advance, we naturally find satisfaction in simple things and do not need a big show `52:00`.
- Stability grows: the teacher contrasts how he might once have reacted to someone's harsh words with how his connection with Kṛṣṇa now gives inner strength `52:00`.
- Be aware of shortcomings so as not to provoke situations where they knock us down — but focus on Kṛṣṇa, not on the shortcomings `52:00`.
- One cannot gate-crash into the kingdom of God — like a truck smashing open a closed door; it cannot be forced `52:00 – 54:00`.
- This is not licence for complacency ("I'll sleep through chanting, skip fasting, skip the temple until Kṛṣṇa makes me feel like it"). Diligent practice is definitely required; the point concerns inner feelings that fluctuate despite practice `54:00`.
- With steady practice the higher taste increases, and other things become progressively irrelevant, insignificant and non-existent `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Transcendental knowledge is the purest thing, and its joy is found within — but only as the mind stills through steady practice; we cannot gate-crash or rush purification, yet Kṛṣṇa assures that in due course it will surely come. [46:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
