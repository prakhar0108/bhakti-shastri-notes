## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 4.10 [00:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.10 -->

**Context.** Having spoken of His transcendental appearance and activities, Kṛṣṇa explains what prevents people from taking shelter of Him, and what happens to those who become free of it `00:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Rāga_ — attachment; here, attachment to matter and to enjoyment of matter `04:00`.
- _Bhaya_ — fear; here, the fear of remaining a person after liberation `06:00`.
- _Krodha_ — not just ordinary anger, but something deeper: the anger that comes from frustration at not being able to understand the ultimate truth `08:00`.
- _Jñāna-tapasā_ — an interesting compound word the teacher highlights: purification through knowledge `14:00 – 16:00`.
- _Mad-bhāvam_ — attaining Kṛṣṇa's _bhāva_: experiencing remembrance of Kṛṣṇa along with feeling and affection, which finally culminates in spontaneous _prema_ `16:00 – 20:00`.

#### Why the Supreme as a person is hard to accept [04:00]

- For materialistic people it is almost impossible to understand how the Supreme can be a person `04:00`.
- When we are too materially conscious, we think of everything in terms of matter `04:00`.

#### Rāga: using God as a tool [04:00]

- When attached to matter, the main thought is "I am enjoying"; the only reason such a person cares for God is the hope that God can help him enjoy matter `04:00`.
- That is not really caring for God — it is caring for the things of the world and using God as a tool for getting them `04:00`.
- So _rāga_ prevents one from becoming absorbed in Kṛṣṇa: we may pray, but even while praying we think of the worldly things we want God to give `04:00`.
- This is a level of religion that is practised but has not passed through the heart; real shelter of Kṛṣṇa is not possible at that level `04:00`.

#### Bhaya: the fear of being a person again [04:00 – 08:00]

- People in general have the bodily idea, so for them "form" means material form; they assume form and personality belong only to matter `04:00 – 06:00`.
- Many turn toward God out of frustration: they tried the forms and relationships of this world and found them disappointing, so they look for something beyond `06:00`.
- Because they are so averse to material things, the idea of retaining personality after liberation frightens them; told that spiritual life is also individual and personal, they become afraid of "becoming persons again" and naturally prefer merging into an impersonal absolute `06:00`.
- The recoil works on two levels `06:00 – 08:00`:
  - **Emotionally** — "whenever I had form and relationships I was hurt," so they recoil from a personal conception.
  - **Intellectually** — in their conception, form and matter are rooted together; they cannot think how there can be form without matter.
- Their idea of spirit is primarily negative ("not matter"), so they cannot think of spirit in its own glory; since matter has form, they conclude spirit must be formless `08:00`.

#### Krodha: frustration with the search for truth [08:00 – 12:00]

- When one cannot understand the ultimate truth, frustration arises; people say "God is like this only," or decide not to get into the matter at all `08:00`.
- The teacher's response: make a commitment to find out what the truth is `10:00`.
- Truth is one, and there may be many paths toward it, but that does not mean everything is right; progressively we get a clearer understanding as we move forward `10:00`.
- We understand which path is right not only by analysing with intelligence, but by investing our consciousness in it — just as, by taking a medicine, we gradually understand that it works `10:00 – 12:00`.
- If we experience benefit from spiritual practice, we move forward in life by practising it `12:00`.
- He contrasts this with the claim that drug-induced experiences are a spiritual vision: that is not sensible `12:00`.

#### Purified by knowledge, attaining love [14:00 – 20:00]

- Detachment from the material concept changes when there is attachment to one's personal spiritual identity `14:00`.
- To illustrate trusting the one who knows, the teacher gives a surgery example: before surgery one asks the specialist about his experience — how many patients he has operated on — because it is our life at stake `14:00`.
- Understanding Kṛṣṇa's glory purifies us; hearing about Kṛṣṇa's glories purifies us, and when we are purified our original, natural attraction to Kṛṣṇa is revived `16:00`.
- The conclusion is _mad-bhāvam_: we start remembering Kṛṣṇa with positive feeling and affection, and finally comes _prema_, which is spontaneous `16:00 – 18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Attachment makes God a tool for enjoying matter, fear makes the impersonal seem safer, and frustration makes people give up on truth; those who are purified by knowledge of Kṛṣṇa's glories revive their natural attraction to Him and come to bhāva and finally spontaneous prema. [04:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.11 [20:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.11 -->

**Context.** A new section begins here: Kṛṣṇa is the goal of all paths. Those who take shelter of Kṛṣṇa through bhakti come back to Him, but not everyone wants to; so what happens to those who practise other processes? `20:00`

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vartma_ — path; the phrase about everyone following Kṛṣṇa's path is often rendered "all paths lead to Me" `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### Sections are a framework for understanding [20:00]

- The _ācāryas_ (the captions name Cakravartī and Vidyābhūṣaṇa) have given outlines dividing the Gītā into sections, and different devotees classify the sections in different ways `20:00`.
- Such divisions are not part of the text itself; they are a framework we create for our own understanding, and that is perfectly fine as long as it gives continuity and clarity `20:00`.
- Connection with the previous section: Kṛṣṇa explained _dharma_ as the practice of religion that purifies; for that purification multiple processes are recommended, and this section discusses what happens to those who follow different processes `20:00`.

#### Mistaking the Lord's activities for material [22:00 – 24:00]

- We are to consider the Lord's birth and activities as transcendental `22:00 – 24:00`.
- People mix up His activities with material ones because they are too focused on the material conception: they think all forms are material and cannot imagine anything free from matter `24:00`.

#### "As they surrender, I reciprocate" [24:00]

- Kṛṣṇa says that as people surrender to Him, He rewards them accordingly; different people are reciprocated with differently `24:00`.
- This will be expanded later in the Gītā `24:00`.

#### Not "all paths lead to Me" [24:00 – 28:00]

- The translation "all paths lead to Me" is not the proper grammatical sense `26:00`.
- In BG 9.25 Kṛṣṇa says very categorically that worshippers of the demigods go to the demigods, worshippers of the ancestors go to the ancestors, worshippers of ghosts go to the ghosts, and His worshippers come to Him — four different objects and four different destinations `26:00`.
- If whatever anybody does leads to Kṛṣṇa, why would Arjuna need to fight? So the verse must mean something else `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Everyone is attracted to a spark of Kṛṣṇa [28:00 – 32:00]

- Whatever a person is attracted to is actually a manifestation of Kṛṣṇa `28:00`.
- Different people are attracted to different things — the opposite sex, learning, alcohol and intoxication `28:00`.
- Everything beautiful has the capacity to attract because it is a spark of Kṛṣṇa's splendour; the beauty of the opposite sex is also Kṛṣṇa's beauty `28:00`.
- So what is wrong with being attracted? Ultimately the attraction is a spark that comes from the fire — but is that attraction taking us toward the fire `28:00 – 30:00`?
- Even a person drinking alcohol is experiencing something of the spiritual, but in an impure, degraded way `30:00`.
- So "My path" means: people are attracted to different manifestations of Kṛṣṇa, but the real question is whether the attraction is taking them toward Him or away from Him `30:00`.
- Some people are attracted to separated sparks of Kṛṣṇa; the devotee is attracted to the whole `30:00`.

#### Whatever we want, we can get from the Supreme [32:00]

- Whatever we are attracted to, we will get; and if we turn toward Kṛṣṇa, we get Him `32:00`.
- The teacher cites a Bhāgavatam verse: whether one has no desire, all desires, or desire for liberation, one should worship the Supreme with intense bhakti — ultimately, whatever we want, we can get from the Supreme `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The verse does not mean every path ends in Kṛṣṇa: everyone is attracted to some spark of His splendour, and He reciprocates with each accordingly, but only attraction to the whole — Kṛṣṇa Himself — takes one toward Him. [24:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.12 [34:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.12 -->

**Context.** If Kṛṣṇa is the ultimate rewarder, why don't people worship Him directly? This verse answers that question `34:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma_ — here, material activities of this world and their results `34:00`.
- _Devatā_ — the demigods; the teacher notes the word's semantic scope can stretch to anyone people approach for favours — a "god-man" or a politician `38:00`.

#### Demigod worship as a contract [36:00]

- Demigods give results because demigod worship is like a contract: if the prescribed ritual is performed, they are obliged to give the result `36:00`.
- **The child and the sweets** — a child wants sweets; the mother, knowing the child's teeth are likely to get spoiled, says "not now — first take your treatment properly, then I will give you special sweets" `36:00`.
- Similarly, when people worship the demigods they generally do it in the mode of business — for the sake of getting something, not primarily for spiritual advancement, and what they get is limited `36:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa will also fulfil material desires, but as the mother does not want the child spoiled, He may hold back things that would hurt our spiritual progress `36:00`.

#### Quick results and "god-men" [38:00 – 40:00]

- People want quick results — and quick results come from demigod worship `38:00`.
- Like flattering a politician to get something done, people go to whoever seems to give results `38:00`.
- Much of the worship of "god-men" happens because people get something; followers publish a hundred and one stories of apparently miraculous fulfilment of prayers `38:00`.
- But there may be ten thousand stories where it did not happen; choosing an object of worship this way is not sound from the logical point of view, and definitely not from the scriptural point of view `40:00`.

#### Quick results go away quickly [40:00]

- If we choose what to worship based on whether we get quick results, those results also go away quickly `40:00`.
- What we carry with us is our _karma_, not the results; people think short-term — "in this lifetime I want this, I want that" — and so cannot practise spiritual life seriously `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
People turn to demigods because their worship works like a contract that quickly delivers limited results; Kṛṣṇa, like a caring mother, gives what helps our spiritual progress — and quick results, chosen for their speed, leave just as quickly. [36:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.13 [42:00 – 56:36]

<!-- vedabase:4.13 -->

**Context.** So that people can be elevated step by step, Kṛṣṇa has created a social system — the four _varṇas_. The teacher says every line of this verse is significant `40:00 – 42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Cātur-varṇyam_ — the system of four _varṇas_ `42:00`.
- _Mayā sṛṣṭam_ — "created by Me": it means the system is natural and inherent, not man-made `42:00`.
- _Guṇa_ — character; _karma_ — competence `50:00`.
- _Akartāram avyayam_ — Kṛṣṇa is the non-doer and unchangeable: He does not get directly involved and does not take sides `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Natural, not the modern caste system [42:00 – 44:00]

- America and Europe do not have the caste system as it is used nowadays, yet the same four types of humans are found everywhere — the teacher names, for example, the intellectuals, the administrators and the mercantile class `42:00`.
- **The school example** — even in a classroom, one child organises, one loves learning, and one, when a new hi-tech pencil box comes out, buys it at thirty rupees from the shop and sells it at fifty in class: the only one thinking of profit `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Every enterprise and every company has these types `44:00`.

#### Varṇa and āśrama: structure and progression [46:00]

- _Varṇa_ provides a social structure for spiritual advancement: with structure, the struggle becomes less and the fruit comes more easily `46:00`.
- Without a social structure of support, education and training — just reading one book after another to work out what to do — spiritual advancement becomes very difficult `46:00`.
- _Āśrama_ provides a time-wise progression in spiritual commitment: one practises while in the world and the family, and then increasingly commits and focuses more on spiritual life `46:00`.

#### Least complicated, most harmonious [48:00 – 50:00]

- Today a student faces a vast spectrum of careers; that is called freedom, but the freedom brings complications `48:00`.
- In _varṇa_, one born in a _brāhmaṇa_ family has a head start, having practised brahminical culture from childhood `48:00`.
- Under peer pressure, many people choose careers that do not suit their nature — everyone wants medicine or engineering though by nature someone may be a painter or a photographer; then they cannot do it well and it is not fitting for them `48:00 – 50:00`.

#### Guṇa and karma: character and competence [50:00 – 52:00]

- A surgeon needs competence — the skill to open the body, remove the damaging part and heal it — but also character; skill without good character is dangerous `50:00`.
- Administrators likewise need both: competence for administration, and the character that comes from training and an overall understanding of the philosophical purpose of life — so that, as _kṣatriyas_, they can guide their subjects back to God and care for them responsibly `52:00`.

#### Creator, yet not the doer [52:00 – 56:36]

- Kṛṣṇa made these divisions, but that does not mean He personally put people into various places `52:00`.
- People are placed according to how they acted in the past, as a reaction to their _karma_; Kṛṣṇa is not discriminating — He simply rewards according to how people act. It is not "I like this person, make him a _brāhmaṇa_" `54:00`.
- **School divisions** — in some schools the 10th standard is divided A, B, C, D, with the top students and the best teacher in A; the principal is responsible for the distribution, yet is not partial `54:00`.
- **The judge** — a judge may give an award to some and punishment to others without being partial; he responds to what each has done `54:00`.
- _Akartāram avyayam_: "I am unchangeable" means "I do not get directly involved in this world; I do not take sides" `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The four varṇas are Kṛṣṇa's natural social structure for spiritual advancement, matching character and competence to each person's nature; He creates the system yet remains the non-doer, placing each according to their own past actions without partiality. [42:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
