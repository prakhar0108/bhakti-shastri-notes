## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 4.7 [00:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.7 -->

**Context.** Whenever and wherever there is a decline in religious practice and a predominant rise of irreligion, the Lord manifests Himself `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yadā yadā_ — whenever and wherever: at different times and different places `00:00`, `14:00`.
- _Glāni_ — decline; Prabhupāda translates it as "discrepancy" `00:00`.
- _Abhyutthānam_ — increase, prominence (of _adharma_) `00:00`.
- _Sṛjāmi_ — here not "create" but "manifest": the Lord manifests Himself as He is `06:00 – 10:00`.
- **Multivalent** — having multiple meanings (as opposed to univalent); the word _dharma_ is multivalent `04:00`.

#### The inner and outer tension of dharma and adharma [02:00]

- There is a perennial tension between _dharma_ and _adharma_ in the world — and first within our hearts: a good side and a bad side, a virtuous and an inauspicious side.
- As Native Americans put it, there is a good dog and a bad dog within us, both barking and prompting us to act; scholars call them the higher self and the lower self.
- The same conflict is reflected externally: people whose good side is dominated by the bad become adharmic; those whose bad side is dominated by the good are dharmic. What is actually good or bad we learn from scripture.

#### Three levels of dharma [04:00 – 06:00]

- Prabhupāda usually says _dharma_ means our essential nature: the _dharma_ of water is liquidity, of fire heat and light, of sugar sweetness.
- But _dharma_ has multiple meanings — e.g. when Kṛṣṇa says to give up all _dharmas_, He cannot mean giving up our essential nature.
- Essentially it refers to three things:
  1. **Ultimate/spiritual** — our essential nature: to love Kṛṣṇa.
  2. **Material** — what is given in _varṇāśrama_; it keeps us in harmony with the world around us and fulfils our necessities without disrupting our spiritual prospects; Prabhupāda calls it conditional _dharma_, _sva-dharma_ (e.g. some people are _brāhmaṇas_).
  3. **Transitional** — _sādhana-bhakti_: also a _dharma_, a duty (chant Hare Kṛṣṇa even if we don't feel like it), which elevates us from the material to the spiritual level of consciousness. _Bhakti_ is eternal, but the struggle of _sādhana_ lasts only until we are purified `04:00 – 06:00`.
- The spiritual-level _dharma_ — "I am a soul, a servant of Kṛṣṇa; I will love and serve Him" — is inviolable and cannot be disrupted; Kṛṣṇa's "decline of _dharma_" does not refer to that level `06:00`.
- Prabhupāda explains the decline as the prominence of irreligion and the subjugation of religion `06:00`.

#### Sṛjāmi and the word kāma for the gopīs [06:00 – 10:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport focuses on _sṛjāmi_: it cannot mean creation, because the previous verse established that the Lord's body is not created; He manifests as He is.
- Comparison: in Kṛṣṇa's relationship with the _gopīs_ the word _kāma_ is sometimes used, yet it is not material lust — not the sensual, selfish, physical craving of male–female attraction in this world, but the supreme, spiritual, selfless attraction between the _gopīs_ and Kṛṣṇa, so intense that the word is used to give a feeling of it `08:00`.
- In the last verse of the five chapters on the _rāsa_ dance, hearing these pastimes removes the heart disease of lust — something lust-pastimes would only increase `08:00`.
- So words may be used, but we must not carry our own baggage into them: the word _kāma_ there does not mean lust as we know it, and _sṛjāmi_ does not mean creation `10:00`.

#### Dharma is given by God [10:00]

- _Dharma_ is that which is given by the Lord — the definition from the sixth canto of the _Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam_, where Yamarāja instructs his servants after the discussion between the Yamadūtas and Viṣṇudūtas.
- The specifics vary, but the essential principle is the same: the same Lord wants souls — His children who have gone away from Him — to return. For that purpose He gives _dharma_, scripture, saints, holy places, the holy name and holy days.

#### Different forms of the same mission [10:00 – 12:00]

- Prabhupāda: sometimes the Lord descends personally, sometimes He is represented as His son (referring to Jesus) or servant, and sometimes He comes in a disguised form — referring to Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu, the hidden (_channa_) incarnation.
- The mission is the same though it externally appears different. Example — Buddha: because of the mentality and culture of his times, he superficially rejected the Vedas, but he made people follow principles he gave; as he is God, by following him people were elevated at least from the lower modes to goodness `12:00`.
- The _Bhāgavatam_ says the Lord's incarnations are innumerable, like waves in the ocean — because His love for us is unlimited; hence "_yuge yuge_," again and again `12:00`.

#### Against religious exclusivism [12:00 – 14:00]

- This reveals Kṛṣṇa's compassion far more than exclusivist ideas such as "this person is the last messenger of God" or "the only way to God" — ideas that don't do justice to God's glory: if God is great and infinite, why would He make only one way to Him and limit access to certain times and people?
- Certainly there is a way to return to God, and not anyone doing anything goes back. But the love of God is not the monopolised commodity of any particular religion; it is available through various manifestations according to time, place and circumstance `14:00`.
- _Yadā yadā_ — whenever and wherever — makes the point universal. Kṛṣṇa does not say, "I am the only incarnation" `14:00`.

#### Exoteric and esoteric [14:00 – 16:00]

- How to understand differences between religions: **exoteric** (external) forms differ; **esoteric** (internal) principles are the same.
- A Hindu folds hands, a Muslim offers _namāz_, a Christian attends mass — the forms differ, but the principle of offering respect and devotion to God is the same.
- If we focus only on the external, we conclude "their God is different, our God is different." External differences are real and provide a culture, but the internal principle is shared.

#### Arousing Kṛṣṇa consciousness [16:00]

- Prabhupāda takes the discussion deeper: the whole mission of the incarnations is to arouse Kṛṣṇa consciousness everywhere; it is manifested or non-manifested according to circumstances.
- Kṛṣṇa consciousness does not have to be created — it is the soul's nature, and everyone has some level of God consciousness; it just has to be aroused.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Whenever and wherever dharma declines, the Lord manifests Himself — not created but appearing as He is — and because His love is unlimited, His incarnations and representatives are innumerable and not the monopoly of any one religion; their common mission is to arouse the Kṛṣṇa consciousness already in every soul. [02:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.8 [16:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.8 -->

**Context.** To deliver the pious, annihilate the miscreants and re-establish _dharma_, the Lord appears millennium after millennium `16:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paritrāṇāya_ — for the deliverance (of the _sādhus_) `18:00`.
- _Vināśāya_ — for the annihilation (of the miscreants) `18:00`.
- _Dharma-saṁsthāpana_ — re-establishing _dharma_; the Lord's goal `18:00`, `22:00`.
- _Avatāra_ — that which descends: the Lord, eternally in the spiritual world, chooses at a particular time to descend to the material realm `26:00`.

#### Adharma is spread by people [18:00]

- _Adharma_ is not like a cloud that comes automatically into the sky; adharmic people propagate _adharma_, and dharmic people practise and propagate _dharma_.
- When adharmic people grab power, they persecute the dharmic, who become tormented; Kṛṣṇa comes to protect them.

#### Lord Rāma and Rāvaṇa [18:00 – 22:00]

- Rāvaṇa tormented the _devatās_; with his son Indrajit he defeated Indra; he even fought Yamarāja, who withdrew — not defeated, but because Brahmā asked him to, to honour a promise Brahmā had given Rāvaṇa `18:00 – 20:00`.
- With practically no challenger left, Rāvaṇa thought himself the unchecked ruler and terrorised the spiritually inclined — the _devatās_ above and the _ṛṣis_ on earth.
- When Lord Rāma was in the forest, He came to a place (Rāmṭek) where there was a pile of human bones of those eaten by the cannibal _rākṣasas_, and He resolved to rid the earth of them `20:00`.
- Rāma destroyed Rāvaṇa and his hordes but did not destroy Laṅkā entirely: He re-established _dharma_ by installing the dharmic Vibhīṣaṇa `22:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa likewise, after the war, established Yudhiṣṭhira as king — _dharma-saṁsthāpana_ `22:00`.

#### Primary and secondary purposes [22:00 – 24:00]

- Prabhupāda makes an interesting point: the primary purpose of the Lord's descent is to satisfy His devotees, who long to see Him — and especially when troubles come, their desperation for His shelter and _darśana_ increases.
- Killing the demons is an incidental by-product. The Lord need not come for that: while Rāvaṇa was thumping his chest boasting, the Lord in his heart could simply have given him a heart attack.
- Devotees present at the time get His _darśana_ and association; devotees at other times get His association by hearing His pastimes — which the next verse brings out `24:00`.

#### The science of avatāras [24:00 – 32:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport discusses _avatāras_; this science is presented in the _Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta_, a book written by Rūpa Gosvāmī based on Caitanya Mahāprabhu's teachings, and in the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_ (_Madhya-līlā_), where Mahāprabhu instructs Sanātana Gosvāmī, largely about _avatāras_ `24:00`.
- Prabhupāda quotes a verse from that section: the forms of the Lord that descend, for the sake of creation, from the spiritual sky beyond _māyā_ into this world are called _avatāras_. _Māyātīta_ means beyond _māyā_ (_atīta_ = beyond, as opposed to under) `24:00 – 26:00`.
- Categories of _avatāras_ described `26:00 – 32:00`:
  - **_Puruṣa-avatāras_** — Mahā-Viṣṇu, Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu and Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu; they interact with _prakṛti_ in the process of creation and are among those directly connected with the material world.
  - **_Guṇa-avatāras_** — those who supervise the modes: the _trimūrti_ (sometimes called the trinity). Lord Śiva is in charge of the mode of ignorance — though not himself in ignorance; he is an extremely powerful _devatā_ — Brahmā of passion, and Lord Viṣṇu of goodness `28:00`.
  - **_Līlā-avatāras_** — who come to perform particular pastimes; Kṛṣṇa and Rāma are _līlā-avatāras_. As part of His _līlā_, Kṛṣṇa may also teach — as He teaches the Gītā here `28:00 – 30:00`.
  - **_Yuga-avatāras_** — whose primary purpose is to teach the _yuga-dharma_. The Kali-yuga _dharma_ is _harināma-saṅkīrtana_, taught by Caitanya Mahāprabhu; the Dvāpara-yuga _dharma_ is Deity worship, taught by the Lord at the start of the _yuga_. Kṛṣṇa comes at the end of the _yuga_, so He is a _līlā-avatāra_, not the _yuga-avatāra_ `30:00`.
  - **_Śaktyāveśa-avatāras_** — living entities empowered by the Supreme `30:00`.
  - **_Manvantara-avatāras_** — who appear in each _manvantara_ `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Lord protects the devotees, removes the demoniac and re-establishes dharma — as Rāma installed Vibhīṣaṇa and Kṛṣṇa installed Yudhiṣṭhira — but His primary purpose is to satisfy His devotees' longing for Him; killing demons is incidental, and His many kinds of avatāras all serve this loving mission. [18:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.9 [32:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.9 -->

**Context.** One who knows the transcendental nature of Kṛṣṇa's appearance and activities does not take birth again after leaving the body, but attains Him `32:00`, `56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Janma_ and _karma_ — the Lord's birth (appearance) and activities `32:00`.
- _Divyam_ — can mean celestial or spiritual; here, spiritual (transcendental) `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Tattva_ — truth; knowing Kṛṣṇa "in truth" `54:00`.
- **Oxymoron** — two contradictory words brought together; _yoga-māyā_ appears to be one `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### The purpose of restoring dharma [34:00]

- Many people have a mistaken conception of religion: God exists to look after us, and this world is a place for us to enjoy under His protection — "it's God's business to provide for our enjoyment."
- Kṛṣṇa does come to restore _dharma_, but not so that we can enjoy here; the purpose is that we can go back to Kṛṣṇa.
- The Lord does provide arrangements by which our life here is taken care of, but the important thing is ultimately returning to Him — and this verse describes how: by understanding His birth and activities as _divya_ `34:00`.

#### Celestial versus spiritual [34:00 – 36:00]

- _Terrestrial_ means earthly; _celestial_ means heavenly — _svarga-loka_, _candra-loka_ and other higher places.
- _Spiritual_ means beyond the three modes, transcendental, not perceivable by the senses right now.
- Kṛṣṇa's activities are neither earthly nor heavenly — they are transcendental.

#### A birth unlike any other [36:00 – 38:00]

- Queen Kuntī prays: how wonderful that the unborn takes birth from the womb of Devakī! Vasudeva and Devakī were in distress and called out, and You appeared through them — possible because You are transcendental.
- What child is born with hair on its head, nicely dressed with clothes and a necklace — a royally dressed child?
- And with four arms — not extra appendages like a sixth finger from genetic misfiring or mutation, but manifestations of transcendental glory: completely beautiful and attractive `38:00`.

#### Not mere entertainment [38:00 – 40:00]

- Without understanding their transcendental nature, people see Kṛṣṇa's activities just as sources of entertainment — like relatives chatting about a child's mischief.
- But here God Himself is concealing His deity so that love can reign supreme; He delights not in being God but in simply loving His devotees and letting them love Him `40:00`.

#### The prime minister's child [40:00]

- In a slum, children are playing; one is clean and well dressed, playing and studying with them. Then we learn he is the prime minister's child, who wants to befriend and help them.
- When we know the position of the person acting, the action acquires far more meaning. Children stealing butter is a nice story; God doing it is completely different.

#### God stealing butter? [40:00 – 44:00]

- When Europeans first ruled India and saw, in north India (toward Mathurā, Vṛndāvana) and Bengal, pictures of Kṛṣṇa stealing butter, it was difficult to digest: their idea of God was a stern person high in the clouds sending thunderbolts and sending sinners to eternal damnation.
- And stealing is immoral — "Thou shalt not steal" — yet here God Himself steals; and if one steals, why something as small as butter? Intellectually, it "short-circuited."
- The teacher's answer:
  - Kṛṣṇa need not steal anything — everything belongs to Him. Taking money from one of my pockets and putting it in another is not stealing.
  - In the universal form, everything is inside Him — the butter pot too; taking it from "outside" and putting it "inside" makes no difference `44:00`.
- For the sake of love He takes the role of a baby, subordinating His position as God to reciprocate love with His devotees and go deeper into the world of love `44:00`.

#### The drama: actor, director, script-writer [44:00 – 54:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's pastimes can be compared to a drama:
  - **Hero/actor** — Kṛṣṇa.
  - **Director** — _Yogamāyā_.
  - **Script-writer** — Kṛṣṇa Himself.
- **Yoga-māyā as an apparent oxymoron** — like "intelligent fool": _māyā_ is illusion, disconnection from God; _yoga_ is connection. We practise yoga to come out of _māyā_. Yet the combination is meaningful `46:00`.
- **Two kinds of māyā** `46:00 – 50:00`:
  - _Mahāmāyā_ — makes us forget Kṛṣṇa, puts us into sense gratification and material consciousness, cutting down and eliminating devotion.
  - _Yogamāyā_ — makes the soul forget Kṛṣṇa's _supreme position_, not Kṛṣṇa Himself, so as to enhance devotion.
- Mother Yaśodā loves Kṛṣṇa intensely and does not think He is God: "God takes care of me — but I take care of Kṛṣṇa." `48:00`
- **Stars in the daytime sky** — the Vrajavāsīs know Kṛṣṇa's supremacy in the background, but it does not come to the foreground, just as stars are present by day but outshone by the sun `48:00`.
- In the _Gopī-gīta_ the _gopīs_ say: You are not merely the son of a _gopī_ — You appeared in response to Brahmā's prayer for the protection of the world. They know His supremacy, yet they still call Him _sakhā_, friend; their awareness is subordinated `48:00 – 50:00`.
- Love slackened by awareness of opulence (_aiśvarya-śithila-prema_) is not pleasing to Kṛṣṇa; love must flow unrestricted, not inhibited by awareness of His supremacy `50:00`.
- **Who is in control?** In a film, the director directs the actor — so is Kṛṣṇa controlled by _Yogamāyā_? No: the director directs according to the script, and the script-writer is Kṛṣṇa Himself `50:00 – 52:00`.
- **Kṛṣṇa really feels it** — chased by Yaśodā for stealing butter, He is not merely acting; by _Yogamāyā_ He truly feels like a small child, wondering "Will she catch me? I must run fast, turn this way!" `52:00`.
- Bound by Yaśodā and unable to break the ropes, He then remembers the trees nearby — devotees He promised to deliver — "I am the Supreme Lord; I must deliver them." By His plan He remembers whenever He wants and forgets whenever He wants `52:00 – 54:00`.
- In the _līlā_ He may not always seem in control, but in _tattva_ everything happens by His will; He creates all this to reciprocate love `54:00`.

#### Tattva makes līlā amazing [54:00 – 56:00]

- Without _kṛṣṇa-tattva_, _kṛṣṇa-līlā_ appears **amusing** — "nice story; I like hearing about small children doing mischief."
- With _kṛṣṇa-tattva_, _kṛṣṇa-līlā_ becomes **amazing** — "God, how can God become so tiny? How can He act like this? How sweet!"
- Understanding Kṛṣṇa in truth attracts us to Him, inspires us to direct our love to Him, and by that we return to Him — giving up the body, never to come back `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Supporting points from the purport [56:00 – 58:00]

- Prabhupāda explains that Kṛṣṇa has many, many forms, and that the Lord, eternally engaged in pastimes, is present in the hearts of all His devotees `56:00`.
- **Tat tvam asi** — a famous Vedic aphorism (_mahā-vākya_). Impersonalists say it means "you are that Absolute Truth," pointing to oneself: "I am God." Prabhupāda applies it to the incarnation: when Kṛṣṇa comes and seems like one of us — with family, children, kingdom — we look at Him and say, "You are that Absolute Truth" `56:00`.
- Prabhupāda also quotes a verse that only by knowing Him does one transcend death; there is no other path `58:00`.
- **Licking the honey jar from outside** — Prabhupāda's famous example: mundane scholars may have technical knowledge about Kṛṣṇa, but it does not purify or liberate them; they keep licking the outside of the jar without tasting the honey. Those who know Kṛṣṇa in truth love Him and attain His mercy `58:00`.
- In 18.55 Kṛṣṇa completes this: we should know Him in truth, and only by _bhakti_ can we know Him `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's birth and activities are neither earthly nor heavenly but transcendental — God subordinating His supremacy, through Yogamāyā, to love and be loved; knowing this tattva turns līlā from amusing to amazing, draws our love to Him, and takes us back to Him — and such knowledge comes only through bhakti. [32:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
