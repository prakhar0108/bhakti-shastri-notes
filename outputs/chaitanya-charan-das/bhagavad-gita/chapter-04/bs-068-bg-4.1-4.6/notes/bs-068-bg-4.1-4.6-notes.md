## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 4.1 [02:00 – 22:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.1 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says He spoke this imperishable science of yoga long ago to the sun-god, who passed it to Manu, who passed it to Ikṣvāku `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yoga_ here — knowledge of one's relationship with the Supreme Lord `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Avyayam_ — imperishable; the knowledge lasts forever `04:00`.
- The verbs of speaking in the verse are synonyms meaning "spoke" `04:00`.

#### Not pulled out of a magic hat [04:00 – 06:00]

- Why does Kṛṣṇa suddenly move from conquering lust to the sun-god, Ikṣvāku and their descendants?
- Like a magician's rabbit that seems to come from nowhere, someone might think Kṛṣṇa concocted this knowledge for Arjuna. He did not: it is eternal, as He already said in 3.31 ("these are eternal words").
- Lust has been present among all living beings from the beginning, so all must fight it — by the power of _jñāna_ as described in this chapter, which fructifies at its highest level in _bhakti_ `06:00`.

#### Kings protect citizens from bondage to lust [06:00 – 08:00]

- Prabhupāda: the kings of all planets are meant to protect the inhabitants — not only from terrorists and thieves but from material bondage to lust; hence the royal order should understand the science of the Gītā.
- Liberation from lust is the citizens' responsibility, but also the kings', who must create a social-cultural environment in which people don't become bound.
- Prabhupāda: executive heads of state are intended to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness so people can utilise the human form of life `08:00`.

#### The brāhmaṇa–kṣatriya union [08:00 – 12:00]

For any system of thought to become socially influential, the intellectual and the administrator must join `08:00 – 10:00`:

- **Buddhism** — a religion of renunciates with little social influence until adopted by King Aśoka, who became practically a missionary, sending his son to Sri Lanka.
- **Christianity** — persecuted by the Romans for three or four centuries, then spread powerfully once the Roman emperor accepted it.
- **Vedic culture** — flourished when _brāhmaṇas_ and _kṣatriyas_ were united.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — captivated King Pratāparudra in Purī, who offered state resources to protect and propagate His mission.
- **Communism** — Marx and Engels wrote as intellectuals, but communism became powerful through Lenin and Stalin after the 1917 revolution, holding Russia for about 70 years.

So Kṛṣṇa gives this knowledge to kings: materialistic kings make society materialistic; spiritually minded kings influence the public spiritually `10:00 – 12:00`.

#### Precision, not discrimination [12:00 – 18:00]

- Spiritual knowledge is available to everyone who seeks it, but when efforts must be concentrated, we focus on the socially influential. Prabhupāda was very pleased when disciples brought leaders of society to meet him `12:00`.
- **Discrimination** rejects people; **precision** means understanding what we represent and how to present it `12:00`, `16:00`.
- Prabhupāda invited everyone — a San Francisco newspaper headlined that the Swami invited the hippies, a radical step for a conservative Swami who did not eat meat or smoke `12:00`.
- Yet he wanted to distance the movement from hippie culture: Allen Ginsberg's foreword appeared in the first (Macmillan) edition, but when the book was reprinted by his own press (later the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust), Prabhupāda dropped it, because Ginsberg — though famous — was controversial and not considered respectable in the mainstream `14:00`.
- In letters he asked devotees to give up any remaining affection for hippie culture: the movement is for everyone including hippies, but if seen as only for hippies, others would stay away `14:00 – 16:00`.
- **Red Cross example** (given by Prabhupāda): with limited staff and many wounded, volunteers often prioritise those likely to survive, who can then help save others `16:00`.
- With few devotees, we focus on those who influence many — knowledge and wealth are two kinds of influence. But projects like village-to-village _padayātrā_ reach rural people too, and there is no guard at any temple door asking for a degree or an income certificate. Prioritisation creates a ripple effect so the maximum number receive Kṛṣṇa consciousness `18:00`.

#### Why the sun-god? [18:00]

- The sun is the eye of the Lord: as the sun gives light to everyone, Kṛṣṇa gives the light of knowledge to the sun-god, who spreads it to everyone.

#### Prabhupāda's calculation of the time [20:00 – 22:00]

- Prabhupāda quotes the _Mahābhārata_ and computes the time technically.
- Of Kali-yuga's 432,000 years, about 5,000 have passed; before it were Dvāpara-yuga (rounded to 800,000 years) and Tretā-yuga (rounded to 1,200,000 years).
- Manu spoke the Gītā to his son and disciple Ikṣvāku, king of the earth, at the start of the Tretā-yuga — about two million years ago `20:00 – 22:00`.
- Prabhupāda thus gives two figures: when it was first spoken (to the sun-god) in this cycle of the universe, and when it came to the earth; then it was repeated 5,000 years ago `22:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Gītā's knowledge is eternal, not concocted for the occasion; Kṛṣṇa first gives it to rulers because those who influence society shape whether people are bound by lust or freed from it — which is why preaching is a matter of precision, not discrimination. [04:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.2 [22:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.2 -->

**Context.** The knowledge came down through disciplic succession and the saintly kings understood it, but in the course of time the succession was broken and the knowledge appears lost `22:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paramparā_ — succession; the standard way in which this knowledge is to be received `22:00 – 24:00`.
- _Rājarṣi_ — _rājā_ (king) + _ṛṣi_ (sage) `24:00`.
- _Kāla_ — time; the generic factor by which the knowledge was lost `28:00`.
- _Naṣṭaḥ_ — lost; the teacher glosses it as "almost not destroyed, but scattered" `22:00`.

#### The rājarṣi: outer and inner ruler [24:00 – 26:00]

- King and sage seem opposite: the king has power, servants and subjects; the sage renounces the world.
- The _rājā_ is the outer ruler (of the outer world); the _ṛṣi_ is the inner ruler, who controls the inner world through sense control and inner growth.
- When both are integrated in one person, he is a _rājarṣi_.
- Some commentators translate it "royal sage"; Prabhupāda translates "saintly kings" — closer to "_ṛṣi-rāja_." Both mean the same, but Prabhupāda's emphasis is that they are not power-hungry kings but rulers who serve others and share life's real purpose `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### Lost even in Kṛṣṇa's time [26:00 – 28:00]

- Reading the _Mahābhārata_, we may wonder why _kṣatriyas_ gambled and so on. Kṛṣṇa's statement shows that the culture of His own time was already deviated — the knowledge was lost.
- Vedic culture was still far more present then than in Kali-yuga today, but not everything was right.
- Kṛṣṇa does not blame Duryodhana, Kaṁsa or Śiśupāla — adharmic as they were — but a more generic factor: time.
- **Milk, curd and butter**: left alone, milk becomes curd and curd spoils — time's transformation is degradation. Making butter and ghee requires conscious effort `28:00`.

#### Misinterpretation today [28:00 – 36:00]

- What happened then happens now: many commentaries on the Gītā by otherwise respectable people are far off track `28:00`.
- Examples the teacher gives:
  - A famous thinker said the Gītā's purpose is to teach nonviolence. Kṛṣṇa speaks of _ahiṁsā_ in only a few verses compared with how often He speaks of _bhakti_, yoga, Himself as Bhagavān, and _yuddha_ (war) `28:00 – 30:00`.
  - Another commentator lamented that had India followed the Gītā, it would have wiped out Pakistan within 24 hours of its creation — not the fighting the Gītā recommends `30:00`.
  - Several significant commentaries were written by leaders of the independence struggle while imprisoned by the British; many state in their introductions that they did not refer to traditional commentaries `30:00`.
  - One commentator wrote that Kṛṣṇa had incarnated as the Indian National Congress to destroy the _adharma_ of British rule `34:00`.
  - A European scholar studied about 100 modern commentaries and concluded the Gītā's conclusion is "inconclusive" — whereas, read fairly, its overall message is very clear despite a few ambiguous spots `34:00`.
- Prabhupāda's point: anyone may write their own book, but if they comment on the Gītā they must not misrepresent it `32:00`.
- **The translator analogy** `32:00 – 34:00`:
  - An English-speaking speaker addresses a Hindi-speaking audience through a translator.
  - The speaker says "Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Personality of Godhead"; the translator says "Brahman is the absolute truth."
  - One bilingual listener objects; others say, "Don't criticise — this is a spiritual gathering, _śānti_."
  - The translator may give his own class under his own name and see who comes — but while sitting as translator, he must translate.
- Prabhupāda: taking the Lord's property and enjoying it for oneself is demoniac — commentators who don't accept Kṛṣṇa yet make good business on His words. Because a proper commentary is needed, he wrote his own `36:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Knowledge descends through paramparā of saintly kings who rule both the outer and inner worlds; time naturally degrades it, as it did even by Kṛṣṇa's time and does today through misinterpretation — so restoring it requires conscious effort and faithful representation, like a translator who translates rather than lectures. [22:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.3 [36:00 – 40:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says He is giving Arjuna that very ancient knowledge because Arjuna is His devotee and friend `36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Rahasyam_ — secret `36:00`.
- _Uttamam_ — transcendental; _tamas_ is darkness, and _uttama_ is that which takes one out of darkness `36:00`.
- **Bhakta** — Arjuna's qualification; without being a devotee one cannot understand the Gītā `36:00`.

#### The ticket collector [36:00 – 38:00]

- A train passenger, asked for his ticket, insists: "Don't you know I have a degree from MIT — the American one — I was university topper, gold medallist, got the best-paying job?" The collector replies: "Very good, sir, but where is your ticket?"
- Every field requires its own qualification.
- Being a Sanskrit _paṇḍita_ does not qualify one to understand Kṛṣṇa — just as a professor of English is not thereby qualified in quantum physics.
- Even austerities like fasting every Ekādaśī are good, but they are not the qualification for understanding the Gītā `38:00`.

#### The finite and the infinite [40:00]

- The Gītā is the word of Kṛṣṇa, and Kṛṣṇa is infinite.
- The finite can never understand the infinite by its own effort; the only way is when the infinite reveals Himself.
- He reveals Himself when the finite living being offers himself to the Lord with _bhakti_.
- Material qualifications — or even austerity and scholarship, which some consider spiritual — do not by themselves qualify one.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The Gītā is received not by scholarship or austerity but by devotion: the infinite cannot be grasped by the finite's effort, only revealed to one who offers himself in bhakti — which is why Kṛṣṇa gives it to Arjuna as His devotee and friend. [36:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.4 [40:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.4 -->

**Context.** Arjuna asks: the sun-god was born long before You and Your birth is recent — how am I to understand that You instructed him in the beginning `40:00 – 42:00`?

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- The words Arjuna uses contrast "junior/lower/recent" (Kṛṣṇa's birth) with "superior/earlier" (the sun-god's) `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### The content of the question [42:00]

- The sun and its predominating deity have existed a very long time; Prabhupāda says Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna were both around 125 years old.
- It is like a child saying, "I taught this to my grandfather" — a valid, logical question, though it can sound like a challenge.

#### A doubt between faith and doubt [42:00 – 44:00]

- The question comes at a significant juncture:
  - In 3.31 – 3.32 Kṛṣṇa said those with faith will be liberated and those without faith will be bound.
  - In 4.39 – 4.40 He will say the faithful attain knowledge and supreme peace, while the doubtful perish and have happiness neither in this life nor the next.
- Right between these strong contrasting statements, Arjuna expresses a doubt — and Kṛṣṇa does not say "I told you to have faith"; He gives a logical answer `44:00`.

#### How to handle doubts [44:00]

- We should not stamp out an inquisitive spirit by saying "don't have doubts."
- If a devotee we ask cannot answer a major doubt, we should find another devotee with enough knowledge who can.
- Meanwhile we can keep the question "in the car park" — pending — and not let it eat us up.
- We don't stop practising Kṛṣṇa consciousness because of doubts; with purification we become qualified for deeper understanding. Nor do we brush doubts aside — we find the appropriate forum.

#### The mood of the question [46:00]

- Arjuna is not saying "You are lying" or "making up stories." His mood is: "I accept what You say is true, but I can't understand it — please help me understand."

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's question shows the right way to doubt: accept the speaker's truthfulness, ask sincerely for understanding, and seek the appropriate authority — without stopping one's practice — while Kṛṣṇa shows that real doubts deserve real answers, not suppression. [42:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.5 [46:00 – 50:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.5 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa replies that both He and Arjuna have passed through many births; He remembers them all, but Arjuna does not `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- The verb in the verse can mean to know or to remember — "you do not remember/know them" `46:00`.

#### Expanding the framework [46:00 – 48:00]

- Answering the "child taught his grandfather" puzzle requires expanding the frame of consideration: Arjuna is thinking within one lifetime.
- Bring in many lifetimes — though for Kṛṣṇa it is not reincarnation as for us, since He does not change His body; He refers to His multiple incarnations.
- Prabhupāda uses this to show that although they sit on the same chariot, there is a world of difference between the _jīva_ and the Supreme Lord: the Lord never forgets anything. Kṛṣṇa will make this clearer in the seventh chapter (the teacher cites verses 25 and 26): "I know everything" `48:00`.

#### Why we forget our past lives [48:00 – 50:00]

- **Memories tied to the body** — memories are stored in the brain and also in the mind, but the reference points for recovering them are bodily. Like a file on a hard disk whose location and name are both forgotten: it is there, but practically irrecoverable. With a new body comes a whole new reference system `48:00`.
- **Trauma** — death and rebirth are traumatic; the mind has a defence mechanism for forgetting painful experiences. Remembering all the miseries of many lifetimes would make it impossible to move forward `50:00`.
- **Volatile, fallible memory** — even in this life we cannot recall what we did at this time last week; memory fades and blurs `50:00`.
- So death and rebirth act as "memory wipers." But the Lord does not change His body or lose His memory, because His body, mind and soul are the same — non-different `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The difference between Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna is not of age but of nature: the soul forgets past lives because memory, body and trauma all wipe it away, while the Lord — whose body and self are non-different — never forgets. [46:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 4.6 [50:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:4.6 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa uses Arjuna's question to explain His own position: though unborn and imperishable, and the Lord of all beings, He appears in every millennium in His original transcendental form `50:00 – 52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Unborn and imperishable** — characteristics shared by the soul; the difference is that the Lord is the supreme controller of all living entities `52:00`.
- _Prakṛti_ — often means material nature, but essentially means energy or nature; "My own _prakṛti_" means it is under His control, not He under its control `52:00`.
- _Sambhavāmi_ — can mean creation, but here means manifestation: "I manifest Myself" `52:00`.
- _Ātma-māyā_ — by His internal potency; Prabhupāda explains _māyā_ here as the Lord's causeless mercy `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### A verse fought over [52:00 – 54:00]

- Vaiṣṇava and Māyāvādī scholars have "practically fought wars" over this verse: does the Lord take a material form, or keep His own self-same form?
- Prabhupāda explains that _prakṛti_ means both nature and _svarūpa_: the Lord appears in His own body and does not change it.
- His translation says "in every millennium" — not in this verse's words but drawn from the next verse, placed here to make the point clear `52:00 – 54:00`.
- He appears by His own arrangement, by His mercy, to benefit all living beings; material nature always remains under His control.

#### The prisoner and the prime minister [54:00]

- A prisoner entering a jail comes under the jail's laws; a prime minister visiting the jail to see the conditions and encourage reform does not.
- The Lord is not sentenced to material existence; He comes by His own will to give souls knowledge and the process for liberation, so He is never bound, and His form remains spiritual.

#### The mystery of incarnation [56:00 – 58:00]

- Normally we cannot see a spiritual form, yet when Kṛṣṇa manifests, people interact with Him as if He were one of them.
- Occasionally He shows extraordinary power — when an attempt was made to arrest Him, He manifested His sky-high universal form and the soldiers were stunned `56:00`.
- Yet most people of His time thought Him just a powerful, heroic person. When Śiśupāla blasphemed Kṛṣṇa at the Rājasūya sacrifice, other kings supported him because they did not understand He was the Lord `56:00`.
- Those whose vision is trapped in matter see the Lord materially; those who become purified understand He is not an ordinary person, devote themselves to Him and attain liberation.
- This verse explains _how_ He comes — by His own nature and free will, remaining unbound; the next verses explain _why_ He comes, discussed in the next class `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's appearance is not a soul's bondage but a visit by His own will and mercy: like a prime minister visiting a jail, He comes in His own spiritual form, always controlling material nature — a mystery visible only to purified, devotional vision. [50:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
