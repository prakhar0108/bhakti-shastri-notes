## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.71 [02:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.71 -->

**Context.** Continuing from 2.70's rivers flowing into the ocean, Kṛṣṇa describes the person who attains peace: one who moves through the world free of material desires and possessiveness `04:00 – 06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Videha-mukti_ — giving up the body and being liberated, going back to the spiritual world; liberation without a body `02:00`.
- _Jīvan-mukti_ — being in the body, yet not affected by the modes while in it `02:00`.
- _Śānti_ — here, peace of the heart, not merely peace of the mind `08:00`.

#### The highest jīvan-mukta [02:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport on 2.70 speaks of liberation from so-called material bondage; for a devotee there is no material bondage, only "so-called" bondage `02:00`.
- One situated in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the highest _jīvan-mukta_ — one who is completely absorbed, with body, mind and words, in the service of the Lord `02:00`.

#### Desires come, but do not disturb [06:00]

- This verse continues the previous one: desires come to the person like rivers into the ocean, but he is not disturbed; he moves about in the world not driven by them, and so is happy `06:00`.
- **Material versus spiritual desire** `06:00`:
  - Material desires take away our peace.
  - The spiritual desire — the desire to serve Kṛṣṇa — does not take away peace; it intensifies our remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, and that is the perfection of life: to become truly happy.

#### Peace of the heart is based on love [08:00]

- Peace of the heart rests on love: we know we are in a loving relationship with Kṛṣṇa, and that relationship is unbreakable `08:00`.
- Whatever we do strengthens and deepens that relationship, and everything that comes up becomes an opportunity to further it — so no situation can break our peace `08:00`.
- Spiritual desires are central to the soul; they bring us closer to Kṛṣṇa and strengthen our relationship with Him `08:00`.
- At present the verses focus on the negative — detachment from material things; later the Gītā speaks of attachment to the positive `08:00`.

#### Serving Kṛṣṇa is our nature [10:00]

- Serving Kṛṣṇa is simply our nature, as being liquid is the nature of water `10:00`.
- There can be many ways in which a devotee aspires to serve, but that aspiration is not a desire foreign to our nature — it is part of our intrinsic fabric `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Peace of the heart does not come from having no desires at all but from having no material desires: the desire to serve Kṛṣṇa is our very nature, and an unbreakable loving relationship with Him turns every situation into an opportunity rather than a threat. [06:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.72 [10:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.72 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes Chapter 2 by naming the state just described — the stage of _śānti_ spoken of in 2.70 and 2.71 — and giving its two results `10:00 – 12:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Eṣā_ — "this": it refers back to what was given in the earlier verses `10:00`.
- _Brāhmī sthiti_ — the spiritual state; grammatically feminine, which the teacher takes as a hint `14:00 – 16:00`.
- _Antakāla_ — the moment of death `12:00`.

#### Two results [10:00 – 12:00]

1. One will not be deluded again.
2. One will become liberated.

#### First reference to the moment of death [12:00]

- This is the first reference in the Gītā to the significance of _antakāla_: we must have a special consciousness at the moment of death and must strive to cultivate it `12:00`.
- It will be discussed emphatically in the eighth chapter; in the Gītā's opening summary the various contents — karma-yoga, bhakti-yoga and so on — are hinted at, and the significance of the moment of death is likewise hinted at here `12:00`.
- At the end of the seventh chapter a question is raised, and immediately in the eighth Kṛṣṇa answers how He is to be known at the moment of death `12:00`.

#### A feminine hint [12:00 – 16:00]

- The word _brahma_ is normally neuter in Sanskrit; here Kṛṣṇa uses _brāhmī_, a feminine form `12:00 – 14:00`.
- Jīva Gosvāmī's _Hari-nāmāmṛta-vyākaraṇa_ — a grammar based on the nectar of the Holy Names — gives spiritual names to the concepts and rules of grammar; it takes the understanding of Brahman as preliminary, not a complete understanding of the Absolute Truth `14:00`.
- The feminine _brāhmī_ can be taken as a hint toward Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī — the ultimate medium by which we attain Kṛṣṇa, the one who gives bhakti to everyone, the feminine aspect of the Absolute `14:00 – 16:00`.
- This is a very exalted topic. Prabhupāda gives some hints of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism in the Gītā, but his primary focus is a systematic understanding of the fundamental concepts, for a book meant for mass distribution `16:00`.

#### Within a second, or not in millions of births [16:00 – 20:00]

- In this very advanced state one cannot fall back, and one will be liberated `16:00`.
- When can it be attained? Prabhupāda's purport: Kṛṣṇa consciousness can be attained within a second — or not even after millions of births; it is simply a matter of understanding and accepting the fact `16:00 – 18:00`.
- Prabhupāda's strategy in the Gītā is deeply thought out: he gives a systematic understanding of concepts, but his focus is ensuring that the conclusion — bhakti — stays consistently at the forefront of our consciousness `18:00`.
- Whatever aspect of scripture he presents, he presents it in connection with the conclusive message, so that what is most important is not lost and we can move forward in spiritual life `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The brāhmī state ends Chapter 2 with two promises — no more delusion, and liberation — and introduces the importance of consciousness at death; Prabhupāda presents even such exalted hints in a way that keeps the fundamental conclusion of bhakti in front. [10:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.1 – 3.2 [20:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:3.1 -->

<!-- vedabase:3.2 -->

**Context.** Chapter 3 opens with Arjuna's confused question: if Kṛṣṇa considers intelligence better than work, why engage him in action? To Arjuna the instructions seem mixed `26:00 – 28:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Karma-yoga_ — the standard name most commentators give Chapter 3 `20:00`.
- _Buddhi_ — intelligence; Arjuna understands it as given an elevated place over activity `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Vyāmiśra_ — mixed; Arjuna feels the instructions are mixed `26:00`.

#### Chapter 3 among the longer chapters [20:00]

- The longest chapter is the eighteenth, with 78 verses; the second longest is Chapter 2, with 72 `20:00`.
- Chapter 3 is among the longer chapters; generally the chapters with more than 40 verses are of this size `20:00`.

#### Overview of Chapter 3 [20:00 – 26:00]

- The opening section: Arjuna's question and Kṛṣṇa's response — karma-yoga is better `20:00`.
- The flow of the chapter, as the teacher outlines it `20:00 – 24:00`:
  1. Karma-yoga is better than jñāna-yoga — be here.
  2. If you are not qualified even for this, then come upward from where you are and practise accordingly.
  3. Even if you are qualified for jñāna-yoga, still practise karma-yoga to set an example for the world.
  4. How to practise karma-yoga so that one actually becomes liberated — first philosophically, then analytically.
  5. The last section: what forces one into sinful action — lust — and how one can be free from it.
- Arjuna's original dilemma behind all this: should he fight or not — renounce the world and go to the forest, or act in the world and practise karma-yoga? `22:00`.
- When Arjuna thinks "I am qualified," Kṛṣṇa's response, as the teacher paraphrases it, is that Arjuna is misunderstanding his own qualification `22:00`.
- The flow is like a spiral, returning to the same point at deeper levels `24:00`.

#### Why Arjuna was confused [30:00 – 34:00]

- After 2.38 Kṛṣṇa said "fight — fight with equality," but from 2.39 to 2.72 there is no direct exhortation to fight `30:00`.
- The earlier instruction is not cancelled, but nothing in those verses reinforces it, and some — such as 2.70 and 2.71 — seem to run contrary to it `30:00`.
- **Two meanings of "peace"** `30:00`:
  - Kṛṣṇa speaks of peace of the heart — the heart in harmony with its spiritual nature, a deep, unflappable peace.
  - Arjuna thinks of peace in terms of what happens outside — and peace is the opposite of fighting.
- Kṛṣṇa also repeatedly used words connected with knowledge — _buddhi_, _prajñā_, _sthita-prajña_, _pratiṣṭhitā prajñā_ — and contrasted them with attachment to material things. So Arjuna reasons: if intelligence is superior, why should I fight? `30:00 – 32:00`.

#### Four options in Arjuna's mind [32:00 – 36:00]

- Arjuna is weighing fighting against not fighting — action against inaction `32:00`.
- The teacher distinguishes four options and asks which one Kṛṣṇa recommends `32:00 – 34:00`.
- Only one of them — inactivity — involves no activity; the other three all involve activity and differ in the consciousness and motivation underlying it `34:00`. Among them:
  - _Karma-kāṇḍa_ activity — fighting to attain heaven.
  - Karma-yoga — acting with the understanding "I am not the body," seeking liberation.
- In 2.31–2.38 Kṛṣṇa spoke at the _karma-kāṇḍa_ level — "if you don't fight you will lose your _dharma_ and incur sin"; from 2.38 to 2.72 He spoke of karma-yoga `34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's confusion is understandable: Chapter 2 spoke of peace and intelligence without repeating "fight," and he heard those words in their external sense; Chapter 3 opens to clear that confusion about action and inaction. [30:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.3 [36:00 – 56:56]

<!-- vedabase:3.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa replies that in this world He has earlier described two kinds of path — knowledge for the _sāṅkhyas_ and work for the _yogīs_ `36:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Loke 'smin_ — "in this world" `36:00`.
- _Niṣṭhā_ — the two kinds of path or faith Kṛṣṇa has already spoken of `38:00`.
- _Jñāna_ and _yoga_ — the path of knowledge of the _sāṅkhyas_ and the path of work of the _yogīs_ `38:00`.

#### An important point about approaching the Gītā [40:00 – 42:00]

- The teacher uses this verse to make an important point about how Prabhupāda approaches the Gītā `40:00`.
- Some Māyāvādī translators say that we surrender not to Kṛṣṇa but to the unborn within Kṛṣṇa — removing Kṛṣṇa from where He is `40:00`.
- To counter the pernicious, misleading effects of that, Prabhupāda puts Kṛṣṇa even where He is not explicitly mentioned `40:00`.

#### Conclusive understanding and contextual requirement [42:00 – 46:00]

- The Gītā is to be understood according to two points `42:00`:
  1. **Its conclusive understanding** — the Gītā's insistent conclusion is "surrender unto Me"; surrender is the essence of bhakti, and what precedes it in that conclusion also clearly points to bhakti. So the conclusion of the Gītā is bhakti.
  2. **The contextual requirement** — the needs of the time in which it is presented.
- Prabhupāda gives the conclusive understanding throughout because of two contextual requirements `44:00`:
  - Māyāvādīs have removed Kṛṣṇa even where Kṛṣṇa is present.
  - People in Kali-yuga do not read much and have little interest or patience; whatever page of the book they open, they should get the essential message.
- Prabhupāda is therefore not contradicting Kṛṣṇa's words; he is fulfilling Kṛṣṇa's mission, which is ultimately to get people to practise bhakti `44:00`.
- When we study the Gītā we see a very systematic flow by which Kṛṣṇa goes through intricate lines of thought; people in Kali-yuga lack the patience to follow them, so Prabhupāda keeps the conclusion clear throughout `44:00`.
- Translating karma-yoga as bhakti-yoga is not a contradiction but a fulfilment of Kṛṣṇa's eventual conclusion `46:00`.
- Some academic scholars say Prabhupāda is a "bhakti fanatic" — bhakti everywhere, "Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa" everywhere `46:00`.

#### Glimpse versus detailed picture [48:00]

- In Chapter 2 itself, at 2.61, Kṛṣṇa gives only a glimpse of devotion; Prabhupāda, quoting the example of Ambarīṣa Mahārāja's engagement in devotional service, gives a much more detailed picture, because he does not want us to miss the conclusion `48:00`.

#### Depth without limit [50:00]

- The Gītā is technical and intricate. Prabhupāda's purports are perfect and complete in taking us to perfection, but in terms of the depth of the Gītā there is no limit — we can always keep going deeper `50:00`.
- Prabhupāda wanted his followers to comment as well. The conclusive understanding comes from Prabhupāda; along with it, we can keep going deeper into devotional understanding `50:00`.
- The chapter overviews the teacher gives focus on the Gītā's inner flow, together with a practical, devotional understanding of how the essential meaning of the verses can be applied in life `50:00`.

#### Hammering the fundamentals [52:00 – 54:00]

- Prabhupāda wanted _Bhagavad-gītā As It Is_ to be a fundamental book for mass distribution. Had he gone into all the technicalities it would have become very big `52:00`.
- So Prabhupāda keeps hammering the fundamentals again and again, rooting us in the basics so that we do not get caught up in technicalities `52:00`.
- The teacher refers to an episode in the Tenth Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_ involving _brāhmaṇas_, in which someone immediately left everything — an example of not getting caught in technicalities but keeping one's vision clearly fixed on the ultimate goal `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### The brāhmaṇa who wept over the Gītā [54:00 – 56:56]

- When Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu was in South India, outside a temple a _brāhmaṇa_ was reading the Gītā apparently improperly, and people were mocking him `54:00`.
- Asked what part of the Gītā gave him so much nectar, he replied that as he reads, he is amazed by the magnitude of the Lord's love, and thinking of that love brings tears to his eyes `54:00 – 56:00`.
- Mahāprabhu said that he had understood the essence: the devotional appreciation of Kṛṣṇa's love `56:00`.
- That devotional attitude is the conclusion of the Gītā, and that is what Śrīla Prabhupāda gives `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa speaks of two paths in this world, but the Gītā's conclusion is bhakti; Prabhupāda keeps that conclusion in view on every page — not contradicting Kṛṣṇa but fulfilling His intent for readers who may never trace the Gītā's intricate flow. [40:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
