## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.22 [00:00 – 20:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.22 -->

**Context.** After establishing that the soul is indestructible, Kṛṣṇa describes the process by which the soul moves from one body to another, using the example of changing garments `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Transmigration_ — the emphasis is on the journey: "trans" means across, and "migrate" means travel, as birds migrate when seasons change; the soul travels from one body to another through 8.4 million species `02:00`.
- _Reincarnation_ — the emphasis is on coming into flesh again: incarnation means to come in flesh, so reincarnation means to come into a body again `02:00`.
- _Metempsychosis_ — a philosophical term that also refers to the journey of the soul `02:00`.
- The essential point behind all these words: life does not begin with birth and does not end with death `02:00`.

#### The body as a dress [04:00]

- A dress is important to us, but we have no permanent relationship with it; when it gets old we change it.
- The essential person is different from the dress, and remains the same while the dress keeps changing; similarly the soul remains the same as bodies change.
- Different dresses serve different purposes, chosen according to what we are going to do and our inclinations:
  - warm woollen clothes for polar regions,
  - particular clothes for work,
  - a tie and coat or other formal attire for a party.
- Likewise, different bodies are like different dresses suited to different purposes.

#### Three stages of reincarnation [04:00 – 08:00]

1. **The soul leaves the body** — giving up the old dress.
2. **The soul travels** — under the guidance of the Supersoul, to where the next body will be; this is arranged by various factors.
3. **The soul acquires a new body** — for a human body, the teacher refers to the Third Canto teachings describing how, under divine supervision, the soul enters the womb by taking shelter of a particle of semen; through the sperm it enters the egg, the zygote and embryo form, and a child is born `06:00`.

#### How the soul leaves the body [06:00 – 08:00]

- The soul can break through anywhere, but generally it leaves through one of the nine holes of the body.
- Leaving through one of the lower holes is considered an inauspicious departure, toward lower species or lower planets; leaving through an upper hole — eyes, ears, nose, mouth — is considered auspicious.
- Realised _yogīs_ in _samādhi_ aim to burst the skull and leave that way, which is understood to bring liberation.
- Most of us have no such awareness or control, so this is not so important for us. As devotees we need not bother which hole the soul leaves from — only whether we are remembering Kṛṣṇa. Kṛṣṇa will discuss this in the eighth chapter, where He ultimately says not to worry about such paths but simply to practise _yoga_, the best of which is devotional _yoga_ `08:00`.

#### Dressed for and dressed by our desires [08:00 – 10:00]

- The next body depends on two factors: desire and karmic destiny.
- When buying a new dress, we look at what we desire and at our budget; similarly we each have a "karmic budget" `10:00`.
- Examples of being dressed _for_ our desires `10:00`:
  - A man who dies thinking of a woman may get a female body; a woman who dies thinking of a man may get a male body.
  - If a master dies thinking of his pet dog and the dog dies thinking of its master, they may exchange positions — the dog becoming the master and the master the dog.
- What we remember at death is itself determined by what we have remembered and desired throughout life `10:00`.

#### The body–soul relationship is peripheral [10:00 – 14:00]

- The relationship between body and soul is temporary, not eternal, and should not be over-emphasised.
- The teacher explains that in traditions without a clear understanding of the soul as a concrete, higher-dimensional entity, soul and body get conflated — for example, the idea of burying the body in a coffin so that the same body will be taken to heaven at judgement.
- This raises serious problems:
  - The body's particles disintegrate and may end up in other bodies.
  - Would someone born crippled, blind or deformed keep that body for all eternity? That would be hell.
  - If instead everyone gets a youthful, healthy body, what body does a child who died young receive?

#### Material conception of life, not afterlife, is the problem [14:00 – 18:00]

- Some atheists argue religion is dangerous because suicide bombers are motivated by promises of sensual enjoyment in heaven.
- The teacher's answer: the problem is not belief in afterlife but the material conception of life. Throughout history many people have believed in reincarnation and future lives without becoming militants.
- A material conception of life makes enjoyment mean sensual enjoyment, so people may do terrible things hoping for sensual pleasure in the future.
- A clear understanding of the difference between body and soul gives a spiritual conception: real enjoyment is loving service centred on God and connected with all living beings `16:00`.
- If entry into the future life depends on a thorough re-education of our head and redirection of our heart — from matter to God — such misunderstandings never happen `16:00`.
- Any belief system can be distorted: the teacher points out that the biggest killers of recent times were atheists `16:00`.
- Properly understood, religion teaches that life is sacred and must be valued; atheism sees life as a chance product, so if enjoyment is the goal, morality becomes an obstruction to be put aside `18:00`.
- The Gītā shatters the material conception of life right at the beginning. In the purport Prabhupāda again cites the verse of the two birds in the tree to show how the soul's journey takes place under the Supersoul's guidance `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The body is a dress chosen for and by our desires; the person inside remains the same. What we remember at death depends on what we remember throughout life — so the devotee's concern is not how the soul leaves the body but whether one is remembering Kṛṣṇa. [04:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.23 [18:00 – 28:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.23 -->

**Context.** Four statements: the soul is not cut by weapons, not burned by fire, not moistened or dissolved by water, and not withered by wind. The emphasis is that whatever harms the body does not harm the soul — Kṛṣṇa is driving home again and again that the soul is indestructible `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Brahmāstra_ and other _astras_ — weapons of the past, which the teacher calls a form of technology as well `22:00`.
- _Kṣatriyas_ — those who protect from harm; they were trained to use weapons responsibly `22:00`.
- _Jīva_ and _prakṛti_ — living entities and material nature; living entities can manipulate material nature for good or bad purposes `24:00`.
- _Devatās_ — controllers of the elements, such as the wind `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### Changing dresses leaves the person unharmed [20:00]

- Just as a person discarding a dress remains unharmed, the soul discarding the body remains unharmed.
- When Arjuna shoots his arrows, the soul will not be destroyed; it will go to the next body.

#### Weapons then and now [20:00 – 22:00]

- Today we have weapons of mass destruction. After Hiroshima and Nagasaki, far more powerful weapons were developed; the teacher describes a test explosion that made an entire island disappear from the world map.
- In the past there were weapons like the _brahmāstra_ — also technology. The major difference: today's technology is often in untrained, irresponsible hands; the more dangerous the weapons terrorists hold, the more terror they cause.
- In Vedic culture, _kṣatriyas_ were trained to act responsibly, so such weapons would not fall into the hands of those who would cause terrible destruction `22:00`.

#### Two ways of controlling nature [22:00 – 28:00]

- Modern weapons tap the energy within the atom and nucleus; this energy can be used for peaceful or destructive purposes `22:00`.
- In the elemental theory (earth, water, fire, air, ether), radiation and electromagnetic waves fall under fire; Prabhupāda says most weapons known today are fire weapons, but there are other kinds `24:00`.
- **Example:** a village can be destroyed by a bomb, or by a tornado. A tornado is not just a chance force of nature — karmic causality is involved, and the movement of wind is controlled by _devatās_, who can give similar power to those who perform the appropriate austerity and worship `24:00`.
- Two approaches to controlling _prakṛti_ `26:00 – 28:00`:
  - **Reductionistic** — reduce the universe to particles and forces operating by laws, and use technology to gain power over nature independently of any higher controller or purpose.
  - **Hierarchical** — among _jīvas_ there is a hierarchy; some have far greater control over nature, and by satisfying them through rituals, austerity and religious practice, they grant us that power.
- So a wind weapon that brings a tornado-like storm is not mythology: a _devatā_ is in charge of wind, and by pleasing that _devatā_ one can receive such a weapon `26:00 – 28:00`.
- Either way, none of these weapons — past or future — can destroy the soul `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Whatever destroys the body — weapons, fire, water, wind, or even the most powerful technology — cannot touch the soul; changing bodies leaves the soul as unharmed as changing clothes leaves the person. [18:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.24 [28:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.24 -->

**Context.** The first half of 2.24 gives four characteristics that explain the four statements of 2.23; the second half adds further descriptions of the soul `28:00 – 30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Sarva-gata_ — not that the soul is all-pervading, but that it is capable of going and living everywhere; Prabhupāda follows the _ācāryas_ here `30:00 – 32:00`.
- _Sthāṇu_ — unchangeable: the soul does not undergo the changes the body undergoes `36:00`.
- _Acala_ — immovable: the soul cannot be moved by anything material, only by its own will `38:00`.
- _Sanātana_ — eternal `40:00`.

#### From assertion to characteristic [28:00 – 30:00]

- If a capsule does not dissolve in water, we explain it by its characteristic — it is insoluble.
- Similarly, 2.23 made the assertions and 2.24 gives the characteristics that explain them: the soul cannot be penetrated because it is impenetrable, cannot be burned because it is not inflammable, cannot be dissolved or dried because of its nature.

#### Why _sarva-gata_ does not mean all-pervading [30:00 – 32:00]

- Kṛṣṇa has just said the soul gives up one body and takes another — implying the soul goes from here to there.
- If the soul were already everywhere, saying it changes bodies would be pointless — like saying a person already wearing all dresses gives up one dress and takes another.

#### Life on other planets [32:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda uses _sarva-gata_ to show there is life on other planets, in bodies suited to their climates and conditions — for example, on the sun one would get a body made primarily of fire.
- All that honest scientists can say is that life _as we know it_ is not possible elsewhere; that assumes life needs oxygen, water and a particular temperature range.
- Some scientists say that even on the moon, nobody has searched beneath the surface `34:00`.
- _Extremophiles_ — "lovers of extreme conditions" — show that life exists where we never imagined `34:00`:
  - organisms in extremely hot environments, for whom temperatures we find intolerably hot are their comfortable range and lower temperatures are too cold;
  - visible organisms deep in the ocean that breathe gases inhospitable to us and draw nourishment from the rocks of the ocean bed.
- Since the soul can acquire a body suited to any environment and draw nourishment from it, _sarva-gata_ is entirely possible `36:00`.
- Prabhupāda's simpler logic: on earth, life crowds every small crack; with so many planets, why would God, an efficient designer, waste such space `36:00`?

#### Why _acala_ does not mean absolutely immovable [36:00 – 40:00]

- The mind is restless, as Arjuna says in the sixth chapter; the soul, by contrast, is not restless.
- If the soul were absolutely immovable, statements such as those in the fourteenth chapter — those in goodness go upward, those in passion stay in the middle, those in ignorance go downward — would be meaningless.
- So _acala_ means the soul cannot be moved by anything material.
- **Yamadūtas:** they are material and cannot catch the soul; they catch the subtle body and drag it away, and because the soul is attached to the subtle body, it goes along `38:00`.
- **Analogy:** a person absorbed in a cricket match on TV — a thief picks up the TV and walks away, and the person keeps watching and goes along with the thief `40:00`.

#### Why Prabhupāda calls the soul "he" [40:00 – 42:00]

- This is conventional, not a statement about the soul's gender; in other places he uses "it".
- For most of history "he" was an inclusive reference for everyone — "man is the maker of his own destiny" includes women. Modern conventions now read "he" as male, so speakers often say "he or she" `40:00`.
- Prabhupāda wrote when the masculine was inclusive. The soul has no material gender; in the spiritual world the soul's _svarūpa_ does have a form that may be of a particular gender — a _gopī_ female, a _gopa_ male — but that is completely spiritual and transcendental `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul's characteristics explain why nothing material can harm it; and Prabhupāda, following the ācāryas, reads sarva-gata and acala so that they agree with the soul's journey: it can go anywhere and cannot be moved by anything material. [28:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.25 [42:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.25 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa gives further descriptions of the soul and concludes: knowing this, do not lament `42:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Avyakta_ — invisible or unmanifest; the soul cannot be perceived with our gross senses `42:00`.
- _Acintya_ — inconceivable; not by our own independent speculation `42:00 – 44:00`.
- _Avikārya_ — unchangeable: the soul does not undergo the six changes of the body `44:00`.

#### Why think about the inconceivable? [42:00 – 44:00]

- One might say: if the soul is inconceivable, why bother — just live happily in the bodily conception.
- But unless we think about the soul, we cannot understand what life is ultimately meant for, because life at the level of the body will soon be terminated.
- _Acintya_ does not mean entirely inconceivable: logical inference gives some understanding, but deeper understanding requires the scriptural tradition.

#### The six changes of the body [44:00]

1. Creation (birth)
2. Growth
3. Reproduction
4. Maintenance
5. Deterioration
6. Destruction

The soul undergoes none of these.

#### "Do not lament" [44:00 – 46:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's instruction not to lament has come before and will come again and again in this section, increasingly emphatically.
- The connection: the soul does not grow old, die or become young — it is always the same.
- Prabhupāda explains that Bhīṣma and Droṇa, for whom Arjuna is concerned, will get new bodies — spiritual bodies if liberated, which is what will happen as they die in Kṛṣṇa's presence, or elevated bodies by their overall _karma_. If old bodies go and new ones come, what cause is there for lamentation `46:00`?

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul is beyond the senses and beyond independent speculation, so we understand it through scripture — and understanding that it never changes removes the cause for lamentation. [42:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.26 – 2.27 [46:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.26 -->

<!-- vedabase:2.27 -->

**Context.** Having explained how the soul gets new bodies, Kṛṣṇa briefly addresses the philosophy that rejects the soul: even if the self is always born and always dies, there is still no reason to lament `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mahā-bāhu_ — "mighty-armed"; Prabhupāda explains Kṛṣṇa uses it to indicate that Arjuna, born in a great culture and a great warrior, will not be overcome by such petty misconceptions `52:00`.
- _Śoka_ — lamentation, which Kṛṣṇa again rejects `46:00`.

#### Reductionist philosophies then and now [46:00 – 48:00]

- Atheistic, reductionistic philosophies existed then and exist now: life begins with a chemical combination at birth and is destroyed at death — just a set of biochemicals that live for a while and die.
- This is promoted in the name of science, yet science has never produced life from biochemicals `48:00`.

#### "Artificial life" and spin doctors [48:00 – 52:00]

- Claimed artificial-life experiments are, on serious examination, manipulations of real life — taking existing DNA, modifying it slightly, and letting it grow.
- Research institutes seeking funding and publicity use spin doctors — media experts who phrase things to catch attention.
- The teacher mentions he has written articles on this on his website for those interested `48:00`, `52:00`.

#### The soundtrack analogy [50:00 – 52:00]

- A soundtrack is connected with the music, and changing it changes how the music plays — but the music did not originate in the soundtrack; someone spoke it and someone hears it.
- Similarly, the body is the medium through which consciousness is expressed; modifying the biochemical structure changes how life is expressed, but consciousness is not produced by the biochemical arrangement.
- The teacher says this example comes from a prominent biologist's book, _The Music of Life_ `50:00`.
- The idea that life comes from chemicals lacks even scientific basis, what to speak of philosophical basis `52:00`.

#### Death is certain either way [52:00 – 54:00]

- Even on the materialist view, it is a universal fact that whoever is born must die.
- By conservation of energy, chemicals are never permanently destroyed; they only change form — from a conscious person to a lump of ashes — so why lament?
- Arjuna does not hold that belief anyway. Either way, his elders will die and take another birth even if he does not fight.
- So rather than giving up duty to avoid the unavoidable, do your duty and attain an auspicious destination `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Whether one accepts the soul or thinks life is only chemicals, death is unavoidable for everyone who is born; trying to avoid it by abandoning duty is futile, so the wise course is to do one's duty. [52:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.28 [54:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.28 -->

**Context.** All things are first unmanifest, then manifest, then unmanifest again — so what cause is there for lamentation `54:00`?

#### Reducing life to chemicals makes everything meaningless [54:00 – 56:00]

- As far as chemicals are concerned, they are initially not alive, become alive temporarily, and then die; chemical transformation is constantly happening.
- If life is reduced to chemicals, all emotions become meaningless: a mother's love for her child becomes just chemicals secreted in her brain.
- But love is something far deeper and richer than chemical secretion `56:00`.
- If everything is just chemicals, everything is temporary and its change unavoidable — so why lament? Kṛṣṇa's essential message to Arjuna: why do you lament? `56:00`
- Kṛṣṇa concludes this section in the next two verses, to be discussed in the next class `56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bodies come out of the unmanifest and return to it; lamenting this unavoidable cycle is pointless — and reducing life to chemicals would make even love meaningless. [54:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
