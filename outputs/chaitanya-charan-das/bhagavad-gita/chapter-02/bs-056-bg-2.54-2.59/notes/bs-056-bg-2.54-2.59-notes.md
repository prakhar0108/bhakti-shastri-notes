### Bhagavad-gita 2.54 [00:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.54 -->

**Context.** Hearing Kṛṣṇa's teaching, Arjuna asks about the person fixed in transcendence — four questions about his characteristics `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bhāṣā_ — description: describing a thing by its characteristics `06:00`.
- _Prabhāṣeta_ — the manner of speech of such a person `06:00`.
- _Āsīta_ — how he sits: how he regulates the senses and keeps them under control — self-mastery `06:00`.
- _Vrajeta_ — how he walks: not a fashionable gait, but how he moves in the world amid temptations `06:00 – 08:00`.

#### Why Arjuna asks this question [02:00 – 06:00]

- Arjuna asks according to his level of thinking. He is a pure devotee, but in this situation, orchestrated by _yoga-māyā_, he has the bodily misconception, so his primary thought is "should I fight or should I not?" `02:00`.
- Listeners generally retain only a fraction of what a speaker says, and pick up what fits their own preoccupation `02:00`.
- In 2.37–2.38 Kṛṣṇa clearly said "fight," but 2.39–2.53 mostly speak about transcendence with no explicit reference to fighting; 2.49 even calls fruitive work abominable — and from Arjuna's point of view, killing one's teacher and grandfather looks abominable `04:00`.
- So Arjuna asks: what kind of person is a _yogī_ — would such a person fight or not? `04:00`.

#### Not a beauty-contest question [04:00 – 06:00]

- From outside, the questions might look like those asked of a "Mr. Universe" contestant — what is his style of walking, his style of speech?
- But this is serious philosophical questioning.

#### The four questions and where they are answered [08:00]

1. What are his defining characteristics? — answered immediately, 2.55.
2. How does he speak? — 2.56–2.57.
3. How does he sit (restrain the senses)? — 2.58–2.63.
4. How does he walk (engage the senses)? — 2.64–2.71.

- Throughout these verses Kṛṣṇa uses various words for the transcendentalist; they all refer to the same person, each word highlighting a different attribute `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna's four questions — characteristics, speech, sitting, walking — are a serious inquiry into how a person fixed in transcendence relates to desire, the senses and the world; Kṛṣṇa answers them one by one through 2.71. [04:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.55 [10:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.55 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa gives the defining characteristic of the person whose intelligence is fixed `10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prajñā_ — intelligence: the faculty for deep thinking `10:00`.
- _Manogatān_ — desires that originate from the mind; these are what is given up `10:00`.
- _Ātmany evātmanā tuṣṭaḥ_ — satisfied in the self, by the purified self `12:00`.

#### Giving up desire without giving up happiness [10:00]

- If someone gives up all desires, does he give up the desire to be happy? Then how can he be happy?
- The key word is _manogatān_: only the mind-born desires are given up, while the soul is self-joyful.

#### Three levels of existence [10:00 – 12:00]

- **Soul** — the spiritual level, the root of consciousness.
- **Mind** — the subtle level, the route of consciousness.
- **Senses** — the gross level.
- Normally consciousness is routed from the soul through the mind and senses out to the external world, generating desires: "I want to do this, I want to do that."
- The mind can look outward or inward; when we direct our thoughts inward, toward Kṛṣṇa, we experience inner happiness, because _ānanda_ is the soul's characteristic `12:00`.
- Defining characteristic: such a person rejects external pleasures and rejoices in inner pleasure. A materialist seeks pleasure externally; a spiritualist seeks it internally `12:00`.

#### Isn't temple worship "external"? [14:00 – 16:00]

- In the temple we take _darśana_ of the deity and hear singing, _kartālas_ and _mṛdaṅga_ — all apparently external.
- But some external things take us inward, while others take us outward and further from the soul.
- The deity, the holy name and the _kīrtana_ instruments are, in different degrees, manifestations of Kṛṣṇa; the pleasure that comes is really the contact of the soul with Kṛṣṇa, who is present both externally and in our heart.
- In the initial stage of spiritual life we need external stimuli for remembering and connecting with Kṛṣṇa; gradually we advance from outer to inner happiness `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### "Look within" — but within what? [16:00 – 18:00]

- People say "look within," but when they look inside they find only agitation.
- Thinking "inside the body is spiritual, outside is material" is a simplistic understanding that still identifies with the body; inside the body is the mind, which is not spiritual.
- The reference point must be the soul, not the body: from the soul's point of view both body and mind are external.
- Desires in the mind are internal to the body but external to the soul; so we must seek happiness inwardly at the spiritual level, not the mental level `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The person fixed in transcendence gives up mind-born desires, not happiness: taking the soul — not the body — as the reference point, he turns from outer stimuli toward Kṛṣṇa and finds the soul's own joy. [10:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.56 [18:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.56 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa begins answering how such a person speaks — speech is determined by our thoughts, so He first describes the inner state `18:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Undisturbed in distress_ — not agitated in miseries `18:00`.
- _Free from craving in happiness_ — happiness usually generates "let me have more" `18:00 – 20:00`.
- _Rāga_ — attachment: "I want this, I want that; only if I have this will I serve" `32:00`.
- _Free from attachment, fear and anger_ — this line (2.56c) will come again in 4.10, where it will be discussed in more detail `22:00`.

#### Happiness breeds craving [20:00]

- If someone praises our speaking — "speak more, speak more" — we feel like speaking more.
- Whatever gives us happiness generates craving: "I want this more." The transcendentalist is not affected by this.

#### Anger and the punching bag [22:00]

- Some people frustrated with their bosses keep punching bags at home with the boss's face on them.
- The transcendentalist's mentality is different: his happiness is not agitated by such things.

#### Speculating about causes [24:00 – 28:00]

- **The bird and the fruit:** a bird came and sat on a tree, and a fruit fell — did the bird cause it to fall?
- **The glass:** "I let go and the glass fell and broke" — but the glass is brittle and the floor is hard; within a particular framework we can link cause and effect.
- In real life there are multiple factors; if we try to determine every cause we can go on _ad infinitum_ in speculation.
- In this world we think "if I do this and get that, I will be happy," but a devotee understands that the relationship with Kṛṣṇa itself is the source of supreme happiness `28:00`.

#### Luck works both ways [28:00 – 30:00]

- When troubles come we can objectively see that many good things also come that we do not deserve.
- Sometimes one studies hard and gets poor marks; sometimes one studies little and gets good marks — luck or destiny works both ways.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport explains the devotee's attitude whenever suffering comes, quoting a verse; its next paragraph explains that the devotee does not become attached even to a comfortable position `30:00`.

#### Śrīla Prabhupāda as the example [30:00 – 36:00]

- He met many problems:
  - he tried a business to support the mission financially, but there was a conspiracy against him;
  - he had to give up the place where he was preaching;
  - his godbrothers did not help him expand;
  - at times he did not get basic facilities; while living in Vṛndāvana and publishing books, people did not take the books seriously;
  - on the way to America he had heart attacks; in America he was at first more an object of curiosity.
- His situation was always daring, yet he was not influenced by attachment and aversion `32:00`.
- **Fearlessness:** he went alone to America and moved about fearlessly, because he understood that Kṛṣṇa is in control `32:00 – 34:00`.
- **Anger:** he sometimes became angry, but the anger never took control of him; he used it for training, and kept it under control `34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The transcendentalist is not jerked by pleasure into craving or by pain into fear and anger; seeing Kṛṣṇa's relationship as the source of happiness, he stays steady — as Śrīla Prabhupāda did amid repeated reverses. [18:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.57 [36:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.57 -->

**Context.** The person fixed in knowledge neither rejoices at good nor hates evil `36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Abhinandati_ — praising, rejoicing at what is good `36:00`.
- _Prajñā pratiṣṭhitā_ — intelligence firmly fixed `42:00`.

#### Not apathy [36:00 – 38:00]

- Some think a transcendentalist is apathetic, feeling nothing good or bad. It is not like that.
- Their focus is on the spiritual; the material is perceived but does not dominate them.
- Śrīla Prabhupāda was welcomed by ten thousand people at public programmes to speak about Kṛṣṇa, and he wanted results and was concerned about how things worked out — yet he was fixed, because he saw it all as service.

#### Good can also be a danger [38:00 – 44:00]

- When we gain success we tend to let our guard down, so one should not be disturbed by the good either.
- The teacher narrates from the Kurukṣetra war: Arjuna, vowing to take revenge, broke tirelessly and fearlessly into the ranks of the Kaurava forces to reach Jayadratha, who was surrounded by six _mahārathas_; arrows came like a cloud, Arjuna barely maintained his position, and the sun was sinking rapidly `38:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa protected Arjuna in that episode `40:00`.
- Later, the teacher continues, warriors on the Pāṇḍava side became distracted by pleasure, thinking they had won the war, and let their guard down — showing that pleasure too can be dangerous if it makes us careless `42:00`.
- The transcendentalist is not affected by good or evil, because he is simply concerned with Kṛṣṇa, who is all-good `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Equanimity is not apathy: the transcendentalist perceives good and bad and still works for results, but neither dominates him — success can lower our guard as dangerously as failure can shake us. [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.58 [42:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.58 -->

**Context.** Answer to the third question — how such a person "sits": he withdraws from the external and rejoices in the internal `42:00 – 44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Drawing in the senses_ — withdrawing the senses from sense objects, as a tortoise draws in its limbs `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### The tortoise and its weak points [44:00 – 46:00]

- A tortoise's shell is almost impenetrable; its limbs are its weak points, vulnerable to attack.
- An enemy always looks for weak points — "hit where it hurts," and the opponent can be broken quickly.
- Our senses are our weak points: through them we perceive the external world, and through them we get caught and knocked down.

#### When to draw in, when to engage [46:00 – 48:00]

- The fully fixed stage of _yoga_ will be discussed later; here the general principle is given: draw in the senses.
- This does not mean around the clock; we have to plan when to engage the senses and when not.
- **Eyes:** there is so much to see, and the whole media industry is trying to catch our eyes; I need not let my eyes wander.

#### A perverted radar [46:00 – 48:00]

- A radar constantly scans the horizon for danger; our eyes also constantly scan, but because our intelligence is somewhat perverted, we are on the lookout for "danger" — and when the enemy comes, instead of running away we welcome and embrace it.
- We do not have to keep looking; that is how we protect ourselves from entanglement with sense objects.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Our senses are our weak points, exploited by a world that is always trying to catch them; like a tortoise, the transcendentalist knows when to draw them in instead of letting them scan for and embrace what entangles him. [44:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.59 [48:00 – 61:56]

<!-- vedabase:2.59 -->

**Context.** Restraint from sense objects is possible, but the taste remains; only a higher experience ends it `48:00`, `56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Nirāhāra_ — not taking in, like fasting: senses are kept from their "food" `54:00`.
- _Paraṁ dṛṣṭvā_ — by seeing something higher; Śrīla Prabhupāda's standard translation is "higher taste" `56:00`.
- _Atīndriya_ — beyond the senses `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Fantasies versus finances [48:00 – 50:00]

- There is always an unscalable gap between what we want to get and what we can get.
- The more we dwell on sense objects, the more this gap increases; the whole advertising industry works to increase our fantasies, and no matter how much we have, there is always more to want.
- Even someone without financial problems faces the body's limitations; no amount of money lets us get everything `50:00`.
- So: minimise exposure. Ask, "Do I need to look at this?" `50:00`.

#### Don't test yourself — the sleeping tiger [50:00 – 52:00]

- Sometimes in service we think, "let me test myself by exposing myself to sense objects and see that nothing happens"; but advancement comes by checking and by dedication to Kṛṣṇa.
- If a tiger is sleeping, let it sleep peacefully; poke it and there can be disaster. Lust sometimes is very agitated and sometimes seems asleep — do not provoke it.
- Haridāsa Ṭhākura was absorbed in the holy name, yet he did not go to a prostitute to show the world that nothing happens `52:00`.
- In our age so much exposure is unavoidable; along with avoiding and minimising exposure, we need positive engagement that keeps us absorbed — discussed in coming verses `52:00`.

#### Subjective versus objective necessity [54:00]

- When fasting, the mind cries "I want food"; likewise eyes are hungry to see, ears hungry to hear.
- Such things are irresistible, yet not essential. For an addict alcohol is irresistible, but nobody needs alcohol to survive.
- Most of us fail in the struggle with sense objects because we mistake the subjective feeling of necessity for an objective fact; an alcoholic who is cured by therapy no longer needs alcohol.
- Sense objects seem irresistible in the mode of passion; as we come to goodness, their pull lessens `54:00`.

#### The higher taste is trans-sensory [56:00 – 58:00]

- Only when we get the higher taste can we overcome the craving. There is even a book named _The Higher Taste_, about _prasādam_.
- But Kṛṣṇa's actual word refers to sight, not taste — _paraṁ dṛṣṭvā_, "having seen the higher" — and the happiness meant is beyond the senses.
- The senses can be a gateway — taking _prasādam_, taking _darśana_ — but they are not the primary source. Experience of Kṛṣṇa begins with chanting the holy name and culminates in the contact of the soul with Kṛṣṇa.
- "Higher taste" is Śrīla Prabhupāda's pointer toward this trans-sensory remembrance of Kṛṣṇa `58:00`.

#### Restraint is the beginning, not the end [58:00 – 61:56]

- Many feel that giving up material pleasure is deprivation; in modern times some consider celibacy unnatural.
- A person deprived of pleasure may get mental problems — but indulgers also get problems; there are sexologists advising on better sex, and many are depressed anyway `60:00`.
- Whether indulging or denying, there are problems at the material level; ultimately we must rise to the spiritual level.
- Without the higher taste, restraint becomes a constant struggle of self-deprivation. We say no to the negative so that we can say yes to the positive; saying only no, without experiencing the positive, is self-torture `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Minimising exposure is necessary, but abstinence alone leaves the craving; it is the higher, trans-sensory experience of Kṛṣṇa that finally ends it — we say no to the negative only to say yes to the positive. [48:00 – 61:56]
</Callout>
