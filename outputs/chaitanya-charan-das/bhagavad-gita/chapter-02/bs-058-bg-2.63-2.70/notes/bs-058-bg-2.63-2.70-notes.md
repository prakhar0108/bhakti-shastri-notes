## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.62 – 2.63 [00:00 – 06:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.62 -->

<!-- vedabase:2.63 -->

**Context.** The teacher completes the purport discussion of the fall described in these verses — contemplation, attachment, anger, delusion, loss of memory and destruction — focusing on how Śrīla Prabhupāda explains who is and who is not subject to it `00:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Higher taste_ — the taste that bhakti-yoga gives, by which one can resist the lower taste `02:00`.
- _Krishna conscious_ vs. _not Krishna conscious_ — Prabhupāda says one who is not Kṛṣṇa conscious is subject to material desires by contemplating sense objects; conversely, one who is Kṛṣṇa conscious will not be subjected to them `00:00`.

#### Lord Śiva and Haridāsa Ṭhākura: a comparison of processes [00:00 – 04:00]

- Prabhupāda gives the example that Lord Śiva was agitated by Pārvatī, whereas Haridāsa Ṭhākura was not `00:00`.
- The obvious question: Lord Śiva is so great — how can he be called agitated? The teacher notes that elsewhere Lord Śiva is described as unagitated even when Pārvatī tried to agitate him, because he was absorbed in meditating on the Supreme Lord `00:00 – 02:00`.
- The resolution: this is not primarily a comparison of _people_ but of _processes_ `02:00`:
  - In this context Prabhupāda is glorifying the Holy Name — one fixed in chanting is fixed in bhakti-yoga, and bhakti-yoga gives a higher taste that resists the lower taste.
  - Lord Śiva is spoken of here in terms of a _yogī_; the _yoga_ process by itself does not necessarily give a higher taste, which is why one may fall away.
- Lord Śiva and Lord Brahmā are exalted, respectable and worshipable; the point is that material position and power alone are not enough against the pull of temptation — one needs the protective grace of the Lord, received by taking shelter of devotional service `02:00`.

#### No higher taste, no lasting control [04:00 – 06:00]

- Prabhupāda concludes: one not in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, however powerful in artificially controlling the senses, is sure ultimately to fail, because there is no higher taste `04:00`.
- The teacher retraces the chain — anger to delusion, delusion to loss of memory, then destruction `04:00`.
- How is the mind fixed on Kṛṣṇa? Often by using the senses themselves in His service. We cannot "close our eyes and walk"; whatever we use, we use in Kṛṣṇa's service `06:00`.
- Example: a devotee can take _prasāda_, whereas a materialist or an impersonalist who tries to renounce without engaging matter spiritually can fall back, because no higher taste is easily available `06:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The contrast between the yogī and the chanter is a contrast of processes, not of persons: position and power cannot hold back temptation, but bhakti gives a higher taste and the Lord's protective grace. [02:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.64 [06:00 – 16:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.64 -->

**Context.** With this the answer to Arjuna's third question (how the transcendentalist "sits" — restrains the senses) is complete; now Kṛṣṇa answers the fourth: _vrajeta_ — how he moves around in the world `06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vrajeta_ / _caran_ — to move around `06:00 – 08:00`.
- _Viṣayān_ — the sense objects, toward which the senses move `08:00`.
- _Vidhi_ — the regulations given in scripture, by which the senses are brought under control `08:00`.
- _Prasāda_ — generically, _mercy_; here the mercy is the higher taste, not only the food honoured with the tongue `16:00`.

#### The answers flow into each other [06:00]

- The answers are not discrete: 2.61 already spoke of being connected (engaged), so the third answer flows into this fourth one `06:00`.

#### Putting aside likes and dislikes [08:00 – 10:00]

- The senses tend toward sense objects; the person fixed in transcendence brings them under control by following the scriptural regulations, keeping attachment and aversion in check `08:00`.
- This is a gradual process of getting the higher taste: some things we like and some we dislike must be put aside so that we can move toward something better `08:00`.
- A child dislikes studies and likes playing. Play is not entirely banned, nor must the child study 24 hours a day — but there has to be balance; the child studies subjects he dislikes and accepts limits on play `08:00`.
- Scripture does not ban everything we like; many of the things it bans are actually harmful to us `10:00`.
- Giving them up stops further impurity, so we stay less impure than we would have been; pure sound vibration and connection with Kṛṣṇa then purify us further `10:00`.

#### Regulation as protection, not deprivation [10:00 – 14:00]

- Regulation is often seen as negative — a deprivation of freedom — but it can just as well be protection `10:00`.
- **A mother's embrace:** a naughty, restless child may resent being held, but the embrace is for protection `10:00`.
- Whether rules feel restrictive depends on what we are looking for `10:00 – 12:00`:
  - If we seek wanton, godless sensual enjoyment, scriptural rules feel like restrictions.
  - If we have redirected our desires and heart toward Kṛṣṇa and seek a richer inner happiness at the level of the soul, the rules act as protection against intruding temptations.
- **The electrified fence:** a building ringed by an electric fence `12:00 – 14:00`:
  - If my treasure and my real business are inside, the fence is protection and going out is only a distraction.
  - If I think everything wonderful is outside, the same fence feels like deprivation.
- So what freedom means depends on our goal. When the goal is love for Kṛṣṇa, the regulative principles keep us in a safe zone when the mind wanders — they free us from distractions that drag us away from Kṛṣṇa `14:00`.
- **The patient and the doctor:** the doctor's restrictions feel like deprivation, but they aim at health, after which the patient has much more freedom; the patient who insists on eating whatever he wants and refuses bitter medicine is never cured. Likewise, once we develop love for Kṛṣṇa we are spiritually healthy and there is great joy in loving, serving and remembering Him `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### Prasāda as mercy [16:00]

- When we follow the rules and regulations, fixing the mind on Kṛṣṇa becomes easier because we have become purer `16:00`.
- The next verse shows clearly that this _prasāda_ is the higher taste, not only sanctified food `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The transcendentalist moves through the world under scriptural regulation; seen from the goal of love for Kṛṣṇa, the rules are a mother's embrace and a protective fence, and their fruit is the mercy of the higher taste. [08:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.65 [16:00 – 24:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.65 -->

**Context.** Once one obtains this mercy — the higher taste, absorption in remembering Kṛṣṇa — all misery goes away and the intelligence becomes rock-solid `16:00 – 18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prasāda_ — the higher taste, absorption in remembrance of Kṛṣṇa `18:00`.
- _Buddhi_ — intelligence; here it becomes fixed and unshakable `16:00`, `20:00`.

#### How can all misery go away? [18:00 – 20:00]

- The threefold miseries continue — if it is hot, we will still feel the heat. What changes is consciousness: the joy of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is so great that the miseries are there but do not matter `18:00 – 20:00`.
- **One dry chapati versus a feast:** if my whole meal is one dry, tasteless chapati, its poor taste makes me miserable; at a feast with many good items, one bad item becomes insignificant `18:00`.
- At the material level we have nothing else to experience, so misery dominates; when we are happy at the spiritual level, the material misery is still there but the spiritual experience is so much richer that it does not matter `18:00 – 20:00`.

#### Why the intelligence becomes fixed only now [20:00 – 22:00]

- From the time we start hearing scripture our intelligence becomes stronger, because what scripture says is logical and sensible `20:00`.
- But when what is logical becomes experiential — when we taste spiritual happiness for ourselves — the fixity of _buddhi_ becomes much greater `20:00`.
- **The doctor's medicine:** I have a little faith when the doctor's explanation makes sense; when I take the medicine and get cured, my conviction becomes firm `20:00 – 22:00`.

#### Scripture is our intelligence [22:00 – 24:00]

- In the initial stages we cannot simply trust our own intelligence; we must expose ourselves to regular scriptural study, because scripture is our intelligence `22:00`.
- To the extent we remember scripture we act intelligently; to the extent we forget it we act unintelligently and put ourselves into suffering. Intelligence here means remembering _guru_, _sādhu_ and _śāstra_ and acting accordingly `22:00`.
- Hearing gives understanding (that is the _buddhi_), and experience reinforces it — so at our stage hearing is extremely important for staying steady `22:00`.
- **Material versus spiritual intelligence** `22:00 – 24:00`:
  - Material intelligence is information-processing capacity; brilliant scientists and scholars write books of literary value or make discoveries that alter technology.
  - Yet many of them lack sense control and fall prey to lust and greed.
  - Spiritual intelligence comes from _guru_, _sādhu_ and _śāstra_, and is internalised when we get the higher taste.
- Scriptures act as our borders — our fences — as we act in the world `24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The higher taste does not remove the world's miseries but makes them insignificant, and it turns intelligence that was merely convinced by logic into intelligence made unshakable by experience. [18:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.66 [24:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.66 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now describes the danger for one who is not connected with Him: no steady intelligence, no steady mind — hence no peace, and without peace, no happiness `24:00 – 26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Buddhi_ — strong intelligence; here its absence is described `24:00`.
- _Śānti_ — peace; primarily internal clarity rather than external quiet `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### The sequence: focus, peace, happiness [26:00]

- Without some basic level of peace there can be no happiness, and peace comes when mind and intelligence are focused `26:00`.
- Without Kṛṣṇa as the ultimate goal, the mind and intelligence go here and there — trying to go everywhere at once, or rushing recklessly into dangerous places that can ruin us `26:00`.

#### Peace is internal clarity [26:00 – 28:00]

- This peace does not mean the world has no anxieties, or that a devotee must live in a peaceful riverside hut where nobody disturbs him `26:00`.
- It means being internally clear: "This is my goal and this is the way I am going; there will be challenges, but I will move toward this goal" `26:00 – 28:00`.
- A devotee does like external order, so that service to Kṛṣṇa goes on effectively and productively — but his essential peace is within, because he is connected with Kṛṣṇa `28:00`.

#### "Disturbance is due to want of an ultimate goal" [28:00 – 30:00]

- The teacher highlights this statement from Prabhupāda's purport, which also refers to the "peace formula" of 5.29, to be discussed later `28:00`.
- The goals we set for ourselves do not by themselves give happiness `28:00`:
  - If I don't achieve the goal, I feel devastated.
  - If I do, there is temporary relief, then disappointment as the pleasure quickly goes, and again "I want something else."
- The ultimate goal is a relationship with Kṛṣṇa. I may want things in the world too, but what I essentially want is to love Kṛṣṇa, and I want the other things so that I can serve Him `28:00 – 30:00`.
- Prabhupāda: Kṛṣṇa consciousness is a self-manifested peaceful condition achievable only in relationship with Kṛṣṇa; without it there is no peace, however much one makes a show of spiritual advancement `30:00`.

#### Appearance is not substance [30:00 – 34:00]

- Some people look very peaceful, dignified, calm and gentle — because of a good disposition, their upbringing, or as a façade `30:00`.
- **Clean countries:** cleanliness and order are good virtues, but people may be externally clean while internally full of contaminated desires `30:00 – 32:00`.
- A person may be peaceful outside and agitated inside, keeping up external calm to influence others or to get them to do what he wants `32:00`.
- To judge whether peaceful behaviour has substance, we must understand a person's values and goals `32:00`.
- A façade can be maintained for a long time, but one cannot avoid cheating oneself; real peace and satisfaction need a deep spiritual connection `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Happiness rests on peace, and peace rests on a mind and intelligence fixed on one ultimate goal — the relationship with Kṛṣṇa; without it, any outward calm is only a façade. [26:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.67 [34:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.67 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes how one can eventually be deluded if not careful: when the mind focuses on even one wandering sense, it sweeps away the intelligence, as wind sweeps away a boat on the water `34:00 – 36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Bhava-sāgara_ — the ocean of material existence in which we are like a boat `36:00`.
- The _winds_ — desires that can sweep us away `36:00`.

#### The boat and the storm [36:00 – 38:00]

- In a storm even big ships can be helpless; the boat is swept away because it is vulnerable `36:00`.
- The way to deal with the winds of desire is first to understand that we are all vulnerable and must protect ourselves `36:00`.
- **Weather forecasts:** when a storm is forecast, a ship's captain takes another route or, if there is none, goes slowly and prepares defences against the wind `36:00`.
- Scripture is our forecast: in the ocean of material existence some areas have far stronger storms of desire, and entering them exposes us to danger unnecessarily `36:00`.
- Each of us has general stormy areas and also specific ones, based on our past conditioning; we know which they are `36:00 – 38:00`.
- Thinking "if I go there, the storm can sweep me away, and I do not want to be swept away" protects us `38:00`.

#### How the sweeping-away happens [38:00]

- The senses keep wandering, searching for pleasurable objects; we cannot close them `38:00`.
- But if the mind focuses on what one sense has caught, it gets involved and then drags away the intelligence — and one is blown away like the boat `38:00`.

#### How 2.67 differs from 2.60 [38:00 – 42:00]

- 2.60 spoke of the senses, so strong and impetuous, carrying away the mind `38:00 – 40:00`.
- 2.67 speaks of the mind, once focused on a sense object, dragging away the intelligence `40:00`.
- Why the difference? Earlier Kṛṣṇa was mainly answering how one regulates the senses — drawing them inward. Now He is speaking of positive engagement in the world `40:00`:
  - We will be among sense objects and exposed to danger.
  - The intelligence has been fortified by following scripture; the task now is not to let the mind fasten on the objects and drag that intelligence away.
- This is a level higher, and how to achieve it comes in the next verse `40:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Living in the world means sailing an ocean of desire; we protect our intelligence by admitting our vulnerability, avoiding our known stormy areas, and not letting the mind fasten on what a wandering sense has caught. [36:00 – 40:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.68 [42:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.68 -->

**Context.** Since even one wandering sense can drag the mind away, none of the senses should be allowed to wander; one whose senses are restrained has steady intelligence `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _All the senses_ — the emphasis is that every sense must be controlled, since even one can cause danger `42:00`.

#### A challenge to Arjuna [42:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's words are a challenge: Arjuna has conquered many great enemies, but now he has something bigger to conquer — his senses `42:00`.
- When contact with sense objects is prolonged, the mind gets involved and delusion begins; controlling the senses strengthens the intelligence `42:00`.

#### Biceps versus willpower [42:00]

- True strength and character are not the size of one's biceps. People with big biceps can have very weak willpower; when temptations come they collapse like sandcastles `42:00`.
- Our strength is our determination — the willpower to discipline the senses, not let them carry us off, and stay on plan `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The real test of strength is not physical might but the willpower to keep every sense under control — the conquest Kṛṣṇa sets before even a victorious warrior like Arjuna. [42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.69 [44:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.69 -->

**Context.** One of the most often-quoted, metaphorical verses: what is night for all beings is the time of awakening for the self-controlled, and what is the time of awakening for all beings is night for the introspective sage `44:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Night_ — not clock time, but a period of inactivity, lack of interest, lack of stimulation `44:00`.
- _Day_ — a period of interest, activity and stimulation `44:00`.

#### Not geographical or chronological [44:00]

- The verse is not about night in America being day in India; it is about where each person's interest lies — their foci are different `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### Two opposite days [46:00 – 48:00]

- **The materialist's day is the spiritualist's night:** sense gratification is the materialist's field of interest; to the spiritualist it is chewing the chewed — something already enjoyed in many lower species. Human life is meant for something higher, so it holds no interest `46:00`.
- **The spiritualist's day is the materialist's night:** self-realisation and finding newer happiness in the relationship with Kṛṣṇa are the spiritualist's field of interest; to the materialist it is incomprehensible — "sit in one place and utter some sound for two hours, and do this for a whole lifetime — what do you get out of it?" `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### We cannot keep the material definition of success [48:00 – 50:00]

- We cannot advance spiritually while holding on to a material definition of success — "I want to make it big in life" `48:00`.
- A devotee may use his talents in Kṛṣṇa's service, and successes from that are wonderful; but his goal is not fame — he does wonderful things as a loving offering `48:00`.
- **"Where is he now?"** When a great-grandson of Henry Ford met Śrīla Prabhupāda, Prabhupāda asked where Henry Ford was now. The point was not paranormal knowledge of his next birth but that the great empire he built is gone, and he is gone; wealth is not eternal. Rather than futilely trying to hold on to it, one uses it in devotional service and gains the one wealth we can always keep — love for Kṛṣṇa `48:00 – 50:00`.

#### Two kinds of intelligence [50:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport speaks of two kinds of intelligent people — materialists and spiritualists. We need not deny that materialists are intelligent: the technology that lets this class be held online required intelligence `50:00`.
- But that intelligence is divorced from ultimate reality — that we are eternal beings who want eternal happiness — and so misses the full potential of life `50:00`.
- When Prabhupāda calls materialists "fools and rascals," that is from the ultimate, spiritual perspective; within the relative perspective we can appreciate what intelligent people achieve while pointing out its ultimate futility if it is not connected to spiritual reality `50:00`.

#### Why this verse comes here [52:00]

- After 2.68's call to control all the senses, the question arises: if all the senses are controlled, where is the enjoyment — what is the point of living? `52:00`.
- The answer: there is a different definition of enjoyment. If we impose the material definition on spiritual life, spiritual life will seem not only unenjoyable but frightening — like being choked at the neck `52:00`.
- The _sādhaka's_ journey is to move from the materialistic definition of enjoyment to the spiritual one `52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The self-controlled and the materialist live by opposite definitions of enjoyment; spiritual life looks like deprivation only when judged by the material definition, and the practitioner's journey is to adopt the spiritual one. [44:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.70 [54:00 – 60:16]

<!-- vedabase:2.70 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa now shows how one moves from a lower to a higher level of happiness: as rivers keep flowing into the ocean yet it never overflows, so one undisturbed by incoming desires attains peace — not one who is a "desirer of desires" `52:00 – 54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Being filled up_ — the ocean keeps receiving water yet stays undisturbed, within its boundary `54:00`.
- _Kāma-kāmī_ — _kāma_ is desire and _kāmī_ the desirer: a "desirer of desires" — one who says yes to incoming desires `54:00`.

#### Desires flowing in, not out [54:00 – 56:00]

- Normally we think of desires going outward — I see an object and desire it. Here Kṛṣṇa compares desires to rivers flowing _in_ `54:00`.
- Alluring sense objects, on contact with the senses, inject desires within us; the question is whether we accept them `54:00`.
- **The salesman at the door:** someone knocks or phones offering a product — a proposition. If not interested, we just say no and close the door. The desire comes, but we do not say yes to it `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### The ocean and the puddle [56:00 – 58:00]

- When an ocean has so much water, more water coming in does not make it overflow; but if a river flows into a small puddle, everything overflows `56:00`.
- The ocean or puddle is our inner happiness `56:00`:
  - If by _sādhana_ we have invoked Kṛṣṇa — an ocean of joy, happiness and love — within the heart, then when a sense perception comes promising enjoyment, we are not carried away.
  - If the heart is like a puddle with no happiness, we get caught by any proposal for happiness, because no other happiness is available.
- So we must make our heart into an ocean: temptations may inevitably flow in, but our attraction does not flow out `58:00`.

#### Peace by transcendence, not indulgence [58:00 – 60:16]

- Peace does not come by indulgence — trying to get rid of agitation by fulfilling each desire does not work. It comes by transcendence: having so much inner happiness that the desire does not attract us `58:00`.
- Prabhupāda: the proof of a Kṛṣṇa conscious person is that he has lost all inclination for material sense gratification, although the desires are present `58:00`.
  - The desires that are "present" are temptations coming in from outside.
  - The "inclination" is the movement from inside going out; for such a person there is no reciprocal attraction `58:00 – 60:00`.
- That is how a person stays protected, becomes purified and attains peace `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Peace comes not by fulfilling desires but by transcending them: when sādhana makes the heart an ocean of Kṛṣṇa-joy, temptations may flow in, but no attraction flows out. [54:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
