## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.47 [00:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.47 -->

**Context.** A _sūtra_-like verse with four parts, each significant; most people quote only the first `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Adhikāra_ — authority, right: we have a right only to do _karma_ `02:00`.
- _Karma_ — here, prescribed duty that purifies us `24:00`.

#### Half-hen logic [02:00 – 04:00]

- A traditional story: a man had a hen that laid eggs; thinking the front part only ate while the back part gave eggs, he cut off the front — the hen died and no eggs came.
- Likewise, if we accept only those sections of the Gītā that fit our worldview and reject the rest, we miss the all-round elevation it offers in understanding ourselves, God and our relationship with Him.
- With this verse people even use "quarter-hen logic": of its four parts, they quote only one `04:00`.

#### The four parts of the verse [02:00 – 24:00]

1. **A right to work** — most people think they have a right to results with the least possible effort (students who want top marks without studying); the Gītā says the opposite `04:00 – 06:00`.
2. **No right to the fruit** — do not demand the fruit or make it your primary motive `06:00 – 16:00`.
3. **Do not think you are the cause of the fruit** `16:00 – 22:00`.
4. **Do not be attached to not working** `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### Why work matters more than its fruit [06:00 – 12:00]

- Work produces not only material results but spiritual results — if it is done according to scripture and with spiritual understanding.
- The soul is _sac-cid-ānanda_; whatever makes it forget its nature is impurity. Gross sensual or immoral pleasures are very anti-spiritual; they choke our spiritual life `06:00`.
- **The student analogy:** the purpose of study is learning — inner growth of the thinking, reasoning and grasping capacity ("mind" in the sense of "Einstein was the greatest mind of the 20th century"); good marks are a by-product `08:00`.
- Likewise, when we act with detachment and spiritual understanding, action transforms us internally; the purpose of action in _yoga_ is the growth of wisdom `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### Not "I don't care about the result" [10:00 – 12:00]

- Every work has an objective. If devotees cooking for a Janmāṣṭamī festival said "I don't care whether the sweets taste good or the _purīs_ are fried properly," that would be a misunderstanding of the Gītā.
- If that were Kṛṣṇa's meaning, Arjuna could have fought half-heartedly — "whoever wins, I don't care."
- For most of us the desire for a result motivates us, and that is not wrong; but we do our duties not _just_ for the result — we do them because through them we get **purification** (impurities subside by contact with Kṛṣṇa) and **illumination** (spiritual understanding increases) `12:00`.

#### Three levels of motivation [12:00 – 16:00]

- **For the result** — the student studies for marks; a devotee distributes books to have his name announced in the temple. If the result does not come, the time feels wasted.
- **For purification** — "this service will purify me." Bhakti Shastri will have exams and marks, which is not bad, but the goal is purification.
- **For Kṛṣṇa's pleasure** — the highest. Kṛṣṇa is pleased not just by results but by our endeavour; if service is wholehearted, He is pleased even when the result does not come — whether 200 books are distributed or books are hard to distribute `14:00 – 16:00`.
- Dependence on results creates insecurity ("will I get it or not?"); offering the endeavour to Kṛṣṇa frees our mental energy and is very liberating `14:00`.

#### Not the only cause of the result [16:00 – 22:00]

- "I hit a six and won the match"; "I prepared and gave the class" — how can Kṛṣṇa say we are not the cause? The teacher explains: do not think you are the _only_ cause.
- Even in sports, where performance matters so much, players acknowledge factors beyond their control through superstitions:
  - a cricketer who kept a red handkerchief exactly one-third out of his pocket;
  - another who made sure all washroom commode lids were closed before he batted;
  - players with amulets, and a tennis player with an earring in one ear.
- They are not foolish — they know performance matters, but also that something beyond it matters. People call it destiny, fate or chance; the point is simply to acknowledge factors beyond our control `20:00`.
- To the extent we think we are the cause, we become bound; thinking "I am the cause" makes us identify with our talents ("I am a preacher") instead of with being Kṛṣṇa's servant `22:00`.

#### Do not be attached to inaction [22:00 – 24:00]

- "Appear for this exam — no marks, no certificate, no credit." "Then why should I do it?" — Kṛṣṇa says do not think like this, because action itself purifies.
- It is not possible artificially to think of Kṛṣṇa constantly; we think of Him generally through rendering service. Inactivity does not purify; engaging body, mind and speech in _bhakti-yoga_ purifies and elevates `24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The four parts of 2.47 work together: do your prescribed duty because it purifies; don't be obsessed with the result; don't see yourself as its sole producer; and don't retreat into inaction — the motive rises from results, to purification, to Kṛṣṇa's pleasure. [02:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.48 [26:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.48 -->

**Context.** In 2.39 Kṛṣṇa promised to describe knowledge in terms of _yoga_ by which one is freed from karmic bondage; here He tells Arjuna to be situated in _yoga_ and do his work without attachment to fruits `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Samatvam_ — equanimity: staying equal in success and failure; Kṛṣṇa's _sūtra_-like definition of _yoga_ (another definition follows in this section) `26:00`.
- _Yoga_ — connection with Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Absolute Truth; _yogāsanas_ are only one limb of one kind of _yoga_, the eight-limbed one `26:00 – 28:00`.

#### Equanimity as the platform for yoga [28:00 – 30:00]

- If _yoga_ means connection with Kṛṣṇa, why call equanimity _yoga_? Because equanimity is the **platform** for practising _yoga_.
- If consciousness is locked onto material things, we cannot think of anything else; when we are equipoised about material things, we can look beyond matter.
- Two kinds of distraction:
  - distraction by pleasure — we get excited;
  - distraction by pain — we get agitated.
- Neither finally matters, because both are temporary: "Be calm, be focused, be clear."

#### The excited driver [30:00]

- A driver who keeps looking this side and that side at everything that excites him cannot drive fast and may have an accident, because he is not looking where he is going.
- Similarly, working calmly lets us stay focused on spiritual reality — the art of _yoga_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Equanimity is called yoga because it is yoga's foundation: only a mind not jerked around by pleasure and pain can stay focused on Kṛṣṇa. [28:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.49 [30:00 – 36:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.49 -->

**Context.** A categorical verse: work for fruits is abominable compared with _buddhi-yoga_ `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Buddhi_ — wisdom; Kṛṣṇa has not yet revealed Himself positively, so instead of "surrender to Me" He says take shelter of _buddhi_ `32:00`.
- _Kṛpaṇa_ — misers: short-sighted, narrow-minded, closed in their conceptions and aspirations; they do not think big enough `32:00 – 34:00`.
- _Karma_ (here) — called an inauspicious, abominable kind of work compared with _yoga_ `30:00`.

#### Two levels of message [32:00]

- In 2.37 Kṛṣṇa said: if you lose you will get heaven; if you win you will enjoy the earth — therefore fight.
- In 2.38 He said: fight with equanimity, without bothering about gain, loss, victory or defeat.
- Now He explains that the earlier calculations ("if I do this, I'll get that") belong to the material level of _karma-kāṇḍa_.

#### Infinite longings or longing for the Infinite [34:00 – 36:00]

- Human life is temporary but can give eternal results; those who seek the temporary suffer by staying in the cycle of birth and death.
- The wisdom to take refuge in: "I am not the body; I am the soul; the soul is eternal and should seek an eternal destination."
- **Rat race:** people "think big," but within the rat race — even if we win, we remain rats. A bigger house, car, job, pay — how much bigger is big enough?
- We can choose either _infinite longings_ that are never fulfilled, or _longing for the Infinite_ — the Supreme Lord. Choosing material fruits condemns us to perpetual dissatisfaction and repeated birth and death.
- This verse leads to a misunderstanding that becomes Arjuna's question in 3.1–3.2, to be discussed there `34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Working only for fruits is a miser's choice — infinite longings that never satisfy; real intelligence redirects desire toward the Infinite, Kṛṣṇa. [34:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.50 [36:00 – 42:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.50 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa gives the second definition of _yoga_; the closing call to engage in _yoga_ echoes 2.38 — similar, not identical `36:00 – 38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yoga_ as a general principle — performed in various ways; for a _kṣatriya_ through fighting, for others in other ways `38:00`.
- _Kauśalam_ — expertise: _yoga_ is the art of all work, expertise in work `38:00`.

#### Is absorption in a skill yoga? [38:00 – 40:00]

- A guitarist says, "I feel spiritual when I play"; a footballer says, "when I hit the ball into the goal, I attain _samādhi_."
- Absorption in an expertise can bring a fulfilment extraordinary compared with our normal bored, distracted state — but absorption alone does not make something spiritual.
- What makes something spiritual is its connection with spiritual reality, centred on Kṛṣṇa.

#### Expertise that makes work Kṛṣṇa-conscious [40:00 – 42:00]

- _Yoga_ in work is the expertise of working so that the work becomes Kṛṣṇa conscious.
- Remembering Kṛṣṇa during day-to-day work is not easy, but by practising devotional service it gradually becomes easier and deeper.
- Kṛṣṇa says one becomes free "in this world, in this life itself" — liberation does not necessarily require many lives if one practises devotional service seriously `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Yoga is expertise in work — not mere absorption in a skill, but the art of working so that the work connects us to Kṛṣṇa; practised seriously, it can free us in this very life. [38:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.51 [42:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.51 -->

**Context.** For the first time in the Gītā, Kṛṣṇa refers to a destination higher than _svarga_ — liberation `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Conquering the mind_ — the mind sees only the superficial; one who has conquered it can see deep truths and is attracted by deeper spiritual realities `44:00`.
- _Anāmaya_ — the place free from all misery `44:00`.

#### A destination stated without naming it [44:00]

- Because Kṛṣṇa has not yet spoken about Himself, He does not say "the spiritual world"; He simply calls it the abode free from all misery.
- By staying detached from fruits and fixing the mind on spiritual reality, one attains that destination.
- This attainment is beyond even the Vedas — Kṛṣṇa's personal abode, richer and deeper than the reality the Vedas describe.

#### The ocean like a calf's hoofprint [44:00 – 46:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda quotes a famous _Bhāgavatam_ verse: for the devotee who takes shelter of the Lord (Murāri), whose glories are all auspicious, the vast ocean of material existence becomes as small as the water in a calf's hoofprint — the impossible becomes easily crossable.
- Such devotees attain the supreme destination (_param padam_) and do not stay in this world, where there is danger at every step.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A conquered mind sees beyond superficial results; detached from fruits and fixed on spiritual reality, the devotee crosses material existence as easily as a calf's hoofprint and reaches the abode free from misery. [44:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.52 [46:00 – 56:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.52 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says that we have to go beyond the Vedas; this verse and the next conclude the section `46:00 – 48:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Forest of delusion_ — the forest in which our intelligence, not just our body, is trapped `48:00`.
- _Indifference_ — becoming indifferent to all one has heard and all one will hear `48:00`.

#### An intellectual trap [48:00]

- We usually speak of a person being lost in a forest; here it is the _intelligence_ that is trapped.
- All of us are in an intellectual trap: we do not know what to do, what the goal is, or what the way to the goal is.

#### False hopes — even in scripture [48:00 – 52:00]

- The misery of false hopes comes not only from the world — "this cell phone, this car, this house will make me happy," as in advertisements — but even from scripture.
- The _karma-kāṇḍa_ sections contain profuse promises of sensual and heavenly pleasures; a Western scholar called the Ṛg Veda the world's most materialistic religious book.
- The Vedas have multiple levels of teaching; when teaching _karma-kāṇḍa_ the focus is on sense gratification, as discussed in 2.42–2.44 `50:00`.
- When one understands there is a higher reality, a richer happiness and a deeper fulfilment, promises of pleasure in this world or other _lokas_ become peripheral `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### Crossing the forest [52:00 – 56:00]

- The intelligence has crossed the forest when it is no longer deluded by "should I do this or that" — it is clear: "I simply have to return to Kṛṣṇa and do whatever is needed for that."
- A prayer the teacher quotes says that even religious rituals done without remembering their purpose — attaining Kṛṣṇa — are useless; even study of scripture then is like a child crying in a forest, where no one hears `52:00`.
- Practising devotional service does not mean we are already out of the forest; we are fully out only when we are no longer attracted by promises of material pleasure in this world or the next (the next world here not meaning the spiritual world) `54:00`.
- In another quoted prayer, a devotee addresses the daily Vedic duties (such as the thrice-daily rituals) with respect — "all glories to you, but I cannot do you, please forgive me" — respecting them from a distance, because remembering Kṛṣṇa, the remover of all sins, is enough `54:00 – 56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Our intelligence is lost in a forest of delusion fed by false hopes — worldly and even scriptural; it comes out when it is clear that only returning to Kṛṣṇa matters, and every other promise of pleasure becomes peripheral. [48:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.53 [56:00 – 59:18]

<!-- vedabase:2.53 -->

**Context.** The last verse of the section repeats and elaborates the previous one `56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śruti_ — scripture that one hears; even if it speaks of fruitive activities, one who has heard eternal spiritual truth is not shaken `56:00`.
- _Samādhi_ — staying fixed, undisturbed `56:00 – 58:00`.

#### Fixed intelligence steadies the mind [58:00]

- Our mind becomes deluded because our intelligence is deluded; when the intelligence is fixed, the mind may shake a little but cannot shake us much.
- **The anchored boat:** waves will shake a boat, but a strongly anchored boat does not shake so much — so too, once we are convinced Kṛṣṇa is the ultimate goal.
- Characteristic of _yoga_: be undisturbed by everything material, and you can stay fixed in the spiritual — and know that you are fixed there.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Once the intelligence is anchored in the conviction that Kṛṣṇa is the goal, neither the mind's waves nor scripture's promises of fruits can shake us — that steadiness is the sign of yoga. [56:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
