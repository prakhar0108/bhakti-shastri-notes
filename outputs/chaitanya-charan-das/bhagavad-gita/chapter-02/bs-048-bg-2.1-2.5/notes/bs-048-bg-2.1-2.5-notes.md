### Overview of Chapter 2 [00:00 – 04:00]

**Context.** Before the verses, the teacher gives the name and structure of the second chapter `00:00`.

#### The chapter's names [00:00]

- Different _ācāryas_ name the chapter differently; one standard name is _Sāṅkhya-yoga_ `00:00`.
- _Sāṅkhya_ means analysis — analysis of the elements of existence: basically matter and spirit, body and soul `00:00`.
- Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura said the second chapter is the whole Gītā in a nutshell, and named it accordingly as the contents of the Gītā summarised `00:00`.

#### The five sections [00:00 – 04:00]

1. **Arjuna's further doubts and surrender** — Arjuna gives one more reason for his indecision `00:00`.
2. **_Jñāna_ (2.11 – 2.30)** — knowledge that the soul is eternal; compassion for the temporary body is ignorant and useless. This counters Arjuna's compassion argument `02:00`.
3. **_Karma-kāṇḍa_ (2.31 – 2.38)** — the first application of _ātma-jñāna_: since you are a soul who will live on after death, doing your prescribed duty according to _dharma_ will elevate you to the heavenly planets; even to enjoy, you must do _dharma_. This counters Arjuna's enjoyment argument `02:00`.
4. **_Niṣkāma karma-yoga_** — the second, higher application of _ātma-jñāna_: the goal is not heaven but to go beyond material existence; fight with detachment, and there will be no sinful reaction. This counters Arjuna's sinful-reaction argument `02:00`.
5. **_Sthita-prajña_** — Arjuna wants to know what kind of person is detached from everything material, and Kṛṣṇa describes such a person `04:00`.

- Arjuna's argument about the destruction of family traditions will be countered in the third chapter `04:00`.

## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.1 [04:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.1 -->

**Context.** Sañjaya describes Arjuna — overwhelmed, eyes brimming with tears, despondent — and Madhusūdana begins to speak `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kṛpayāviṣṭam_ — overcome by compassion, by weakness `04:00`.
- _Aśru-pūrṇākulekṣaṇam_ — _īkṣaṇa_ is the eyes; _aśru-pūrṇa_ is full of tears — not just a few tears, but practically, like a container that overflows `04:00`.
- _Madhusūdana_ — the killer of the demon Madhu `08:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's sūtra-like statement [06:00]

- Chapter 1's purports focused on Arjuna's qualities — thoughtful, saintly, not wanting to hurt others. Now, because this is the _sāṅkhya_ chapter distinguishing body and soul, Prabhupāda focuses on the illusion in bodily emotions `06:00`.
- He begins with a very sūtra-like statement: material compassion, lamentation and tears are all signs of ignorance of the real self; compassion for the eternal soul is self-realisation `06:00`.
- Three things are arising in Arjuna, and all three indicate ignorance of the true self `06:00`:
  - _kṛpā_ — material compassion;
  - lamentation;
  - tears.
- If the true self is known and one wants to be compassionate to it, the way is by cultivating self-realisation `06:00`.

#### Directing soft-heartedness rightly [06:00 – 08:00]

- This does not mean one has to be hard-hearted to be spiritually knowledgeable; we have to direct our soft-heartedness in the right avenue `06:00`.
- Example: when we see someone coughing, we naturally feel bad. Compassion means taking the person to a doctor for proper medicine so the coughing stops — compassion directed in an intelligent, knowledgeable way, not an ignorant way `08:00`.
- Similarly, seeing old age, death or destruction, we naturally feel bad. There may be specific causes of suffering that need to be dealt with; but beyond that, the person is fundamentally suffering because of bodily misidentification, and that ignorance is countered by becoming self-realised `08:00`.
- When we chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, we are being compassionate to ourselves and to others `08:00`.

#### Madhusūdana [08:00]

- Sañjaya's use of this name indicates that the Lord who killed the demon Madhu will now kill the demons of doubt, indecision and illusion that have overcome Arjuna `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Material compassion, lamentation and tears signal ignorance of the real self; real compassion is not hardness but soft-heartedness directed intelligently — toward self-realisation for oneself and others. [06:00 – 08:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.2 [10:00 – 34:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.2 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa's first words in the Gītā: at this critical moment, how has such weakness come upon Arjuna? It does not befit him `10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kaśmalam_ — impurity, weakness `10:00`.
- _Viṣame_ — at a critical moment `10:00`.
- _Anārya-juṣṭam_ — not befitting an _ārya_ `12:00`.
- _Asvargyam_ — will not take you to an auspicious destination `12:00`.
- _Akīrti-karam_ — will bring not fame but infamy, even in this world `12:00`.
- _Denotation_ and _connotation_ — what a word directly means, and what it implies `14:00`.

#### The "choker" [10:00]

- Example: a World Cup final, the last over, and the bowler thinks, "How can I bowl? What will happen?" Under pressure people lose their nerve — they are called "chokers."
- At the crisis moment, the pressure moment, one's real strength should come out; Kṛṣṇa is saying, "Why are you choking?" `10:00`.

#### Unbefitting in three ways [12:00]

1. _Anārya_ — it does not befit who you are.
2. _Asvargyam_ — it does not befit what you will get in the future, in the next life.
3. _Akīrti-karam_ — it does not befit what you will get in this life: not fame but infamy.

#### What "ārya" really means [12:00 – 14:00]

- Today the word has associations with particular groups, and the idea of a historically advanced race `12:00`.
- Prabhupāda explains the essential meaning: _ārya_ refers to those who know the spiritual purpose of life and have spiritual values — not to any race `12:00 – 14:00`.
- In Western intellectual discourse the word has acquired a very negative connotation and become almost untouchable, just as nobody wants to be called a fanatic or an extremist `12:00`.
- Words acquire meanings based on how they are used: the word "gay," fifty years ago, meant cheerful; now it has a completely different meaning `14:00`.
- In Germany the swastika is banned because of its association with a painful memory `12:00 – 14:00`.

#### How the word acquired its connotation [14:00 – 24:00]

- **European expansion** — after Vasco da Gama came to India, Europe was divided between Catholics and Protestants, both wanting to evangelise the world; Britain, a Protestant country, gained power over India `14:00 – 16:00`.
- **Superiority complex** — Europeans thought of others as barbaric; the teacher cites the idea of the "white man's burden" to civilise the rest of the world, as a father must educate a child `16:00`.
- **The shock of Sanskrit** — they were shocked to find that India had magnificent literature and a lofty vision of history spanning far beyond theirs; Europe's history went back to Greece — Socrates, Plato, Aristotle — and then Jesus `16:00`.
- **Indology** — scholars such as Monier-Williams and Max Müller studied India; they found Sanskrit remarkably similar to Latin and Greek, but better: richer vocabulary, and superior in both technique (grammar) and aesthetics (figures of speech, metaphors) `18:00`.
- **The invented common ancestor** — rather than accept that Sanskrit was superior, they proposed that both European and Indian civilisations evolved from another civilisation, which they called the Aryan civilisation, which civilised both `18:00 – 20:00`.
- **Political agenda** — the Muslim rulers (Bābar, Akbar) made India their home; but most British felt a posting to India was a punishment, yet wanted to keep ruling. They could rule only if Indians voluntarily accepted their superiority `20:00`.
- From the Vedic point of view, people who eat meat and drink alcohol are considered low-class; so Indians' respect for the British was grudging, not willing `22:00`.
- So the framework for evaluating cultural superiority had to be changed: Indians' faith in Vedic literature had to be destroyed. Where the Muslims had destroyed temples, the British strategy went deeper — destroy the faith that made people go to the temples, by misinterpreting Vedic literature `22:00`.
- "Long-lost brothers": both peoples came from the ancient Aryans; everything good in Indian culture came from them, not from India, and the original Indians were said to be low-quality people `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### The Aryan theory examined [24:00 – 28:00]

- The teacher notes this is a very complex socio-political issue and keeps the focus on the word's present connotation; a disciple of Prabhupāda, Devāmṛta Swami, has written a book, _Searching for Vedic India_, discussing it extensively, and there is a large field of academic research `24:00`.
- Archaeology found no evidence of such an advanced ancestral civilisation elsewhere in Eurasia `24:00`.
- The correct understanding, the teacher says: the ancient civilisation was in India itself; after the Kurukṣetra war, as the strong rule from Hastināpura weakened, there was emigration and disconnection, and expatriates went from here everywhere else `24:00`.
- Many peoples lived harmoniously in India; the division was artificial `26:00`.
- **The nomad problem** — the Aryans were said to be nomadic tribes who composed the _Ṛg Veda_. But the _Ṛg Veda_ is complex poetry, and understanding Sanskrit grammar takes twelve years. Nomadic peoples, constantly moving, generally do not develop sophisticated literature `26:00`.
- A travelling preacher can read a few books on a train or plane — but not on horseback `26:00 – 28:00`.
- So the Aryan invasion or migration theory is, the teacher says, complete speculation, without evidence `28:00`.

#### Hitler and the word "ārya" [28:00 – 32:00]

- Adolf Hitler claimed the Germans were the original Aryans who had civilised the world and so deserved to rule it; this became a prominent idea of Nazi ideology `28:00`.
- He adopted the swastika as its symbol; the Second World War began from the Nazi conception of superiority, and others were to be either subordinate to the Germans or exterminated `28:00 – 30:00`.
- When he was finally checked, he committed suicide and the war stopped `30:00`.
- So the word became associated with the whole socio-political mess around the Second World War; nobody now wants to call themselves _ārya_. These connotations are completely external to the context of the Gītā `30:00`.

#### The Gauḍīya mission in Germany [30:00 – 32:00]

- Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura sent disciples to preach in Britain and Germany, the powerful countries of Europe at the time `30:00`.
- One disciple spoke in Germany about _ārya_ as a spiritual designation, not a racial one. A Nazi official read this and found it completely antithetical to their understanding; a ban was almost imposed, the disciples had to flee for their lives, and the German mission was scuttled `32:00`.
- So what the word means is not a casual matter; it has serious implications `32:00`.
- Here Kṛṣṇa's _anārya_ means a person who does not act according to spiritual values `32:00`.

#### Infamy [32:00 – 34:00]

- _Akīrti-karam_ will be elaborated by Kṛṣṇa later, in 2.31 – 2.38 `32:00`.
- Arjuna is famous as a heroic warrior; if he leaves the battlefield, people will call him a coward `32:00`.
- Like a famous bowler who refuses to bowl the last over for fear of being hit — people will say, "You're chicken"; it is tough, but you have to do it. One who runs away in a crisis is disgraced `32:00 – 34:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's first words declare Arjuna's weakness unbefitting in three ways — for an ārya, for the next life, and for his fame; and ārya, rightly understood, means one who lives by spiritual values, not a race. [10:00 – 34:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.3 [34:00 – 46:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.3 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa tells Pārtha not to yield to impotence, which does not become him, to give up petty weakness of heart and arise `34:00 – 36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śrī Bhagavān uvāca_ — Prabhupāda's primary focus: the Absolute is understood as Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān, and Kṛṣṇa is the original Bhagavān, as the _Bhāgavatam_ says `34:00`.
- _Klaibyam_ — impotence; a strong verbal slap `34:00 – 36:00`.
- _Kṣatriya_ — the personification of masculinity, meant to be very strong and powerful, with courage and potency `36:00`.

#### A quality-based insult, not a caste insult [36:00]

- _Kṣatriyas_ sometimes had several wives, the teacher explains, because in Vedic culture the male population could be less than the female — men also took _sannyāsa_ — and women had to be married `36:00`.
- For a _kṣatriya_ to be called impotent was a big insult; for a _kṣatriya_ to be called a _śūdra_ was also a big insult `36:00`.
- This is not a caste insult; it is quality-based — like telling a very talented student, a topper, "You're a buffoon, you don't understand anything." The person has the qualities but is acting as if he does not `36:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa is prodding Arjuna into action with a pep talk: "You are not a coward; you are a strong person acting cowardly" `36:00`.

#### The memory the word evokes: Bṛhannalā [38:00 – 46:00]

- The word brings back a memory: during the year of hiding (_ajñāta-vāsa_), Arjuna had become Bṛhannalā, a dance teacher `38:00`.
- Duryodhana's condition was that if the Pāṇḍavas were found, they would go to the forest again for twelve years; he was convinced his spies would find them `38:00`.
- Arjuna was so famous and of such a masculine build that he could not easily hide; and he could not become a female — so the idea was that he would be a eunuch `38:00`.
- **How it became possible** — the story of Arjuna in heaven `38:00 – 42:00`:
  - Arjuna went to heaven, where Indra, his father, welcomed him; Arjuna single-handedly defeated the Nivātakavacas, who had troubled Indra for a long time `38:00`.
  - Indra was so confident in Arjuna that he gave him Mātali, his great charioteer — who had driven Rāma in the final fight with Rāvaṇa `38:00 – 40:00`.
  - In Indra's court the _apsarās_ danced; Indra noticed Arjuna glancing again and again at Urvaśī and sent her to serve him. She came to him at night, dressed to provoke desire `40:00`.
  - Arjuna immediately looked away and explained he had been looking at her because he had heard how his ancestor Purūravā was attracted to her — a story in the _Bhāgavatam_ — and gave up practically everything for her `40:00`.
  - "You have been the wife of my ancestor; therefore you are like my mother." She said the rules of morality do not apply in heaven; he replied, "They don't apply to you in heaven, but I am from earth; I have to follow them" `40:00 – 42:00`.
  - For a woman to offer herself is difficult; to be rejected after offering is extremely mortifying. Furious, she cursed him to become impotent `42:00`.
  - For Arjuna this was doubly painful: he had exhibited self-control with no ordinary woman but an _apsarā_, and instead of being praised he was cursed. Indra told him not to worry — it would be useful in the future `42:00`.
- **The curse becomes a benefit** — during the year of hiding, he used the curse to act as a dance teacher for Uttarā, daughter of King Virāṭa, and so could stay hidden `42:00`.
- **The lesson** — sometimes when we try to stay strong in our principles, we seem to get into more trouble by resisting temptation. But if someone lives for _dharma_, the trouble will eventually work out in a way we cannot conceive at the time `42:00 – 44:00`.
- Before entrusting his daughter to him, King Virāṭa had him examined, and they found he was indeed a eunuch `44:00`.
- **A mortifying period** — in the forest the Pāṇḍavas were at least on their own, discussing Vedic histories and the Lord's pastimes; but now they had to act as servants of a king far lower than themselves: Bhīma a cook, Draupadī a maid-servant for the queen. The most degrading duty of all was Arjuna's — to become a eunuch and dance teacher `44:00`.
- So Kṛṣṇa's word stings: "You acted like a eunuch then — now are you really becoming impotent?" `46:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa tries to goad Arjuna into fighting, but Arjuna's depression is too deep for surface talk to work; Arjuna counters, and Kṛṣṇa then starts giving enlightenment `46:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Kṛṣṇa's "do not yield to impotence" is a quality-based slap and pep talk that recalls Arjuna's humiliation as Bṛhannalā — a humiliation that itself arose from his loyalty to dharma, and which worked out for his good in ways he could not have foreseen. [34:00 – 46:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.4 [46:00 – 48:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.4 -->

**Context.** Arjuna asks how he can counterattack Bhīṣma and Droṇa with arrows in battle, when they are worthy of worship `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Pūjārhau_ — worthy of worship: "I should be worshipping them with flowers, not shooting arrows at them" `46:00`.
- _Madhusūdana_ and _Arisūdana_ — killer of the demon Madhu; killer of enemies `46:00`.

#### The contrast in the names [46:00 – 48:00]

- Bhīṣma is Arjuna's grandfather; Droṇa is his teacher `46:00`.
- Arjuna highlights the contrast: "You are the killer of demons — but would You ever shoot arrows at Mahārāja Ugrasena, Your grandfather, or at Sāndīpani Muni, Your teacher? You killed demons like Madhu; how can You ask me to kill my elders?" `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### A rhetorical question [48:00]

- Arjuna's "how can I fight?" is a rhetorical question: the answer is implied in the question; it is not asked to get an answer but to drive home the point `48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna turns Kṛṣṇa's own names against Him: the killer of demons never shot at His own grandfather or teacher — so how can Arjuna shoot at elders who deserve worship? [46:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.5 [48:00 – 64:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.5 -->

**Context.** Arjuna says it is better to live by begging than to live at the cost of the lives of great souls who are his teachers; even if they desire worldly gain, they are superiors, and any enjoyment would be tainted with blood `48:00`, `62:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mahānubhāvān_ — great souls `48:00`.
- _Niyamāgraha_ — getting caught in the technicalities of rules without understanding their essential principle; discussed in _Upadeśāmṛta_ `58:00`.

#### When a guru may be rejected [48:00 – 50:00]

- Someone might say that a person may have been a guru but has not acted properly, and is therefore no longer the guru `48:00 – 50:00`.
- The _Mahābhārata_ also contains words saying that if a guru acts in an unworthy way, the guru can be rejected; a Gosvāmī discusses this in his writings, and Bali Mahārāja acted against the will of Śukrācārya, who was his priest and also acting as his spiritual master `50:00`.
- Similarly, Droṇa and Bhīṣma stayed silent when Draupadī was insulted; by allowing _adharma_ to happen they practised _adharma_, became contaminated by sin, and were no longer worthy of being gurus `50:00`.
- Arjuna's point is: "No, they are still _mahānubhāvas_ — still great souls" `50:00`.

#### Draupadī's disrobing — the ultimate lesson [50:00 – 52:00]

- The disrobing of Draupadī is a very traumatic pastime; ultimately Kṛṣṇa came and offered unlimited cloth, but it was traumatic for everyone — the Pāṇḍavas lost their kingdom, became slaves, and saw Draupadī insulted; it was traumatic for Bhīṣma, Droṇa and Vidura too `50:00 – 52:00`.
- At the deepest level the pastime demonstrates a truth: no matter how many worthy protectors we have, this world can put us in a situation where none of them can protect us `52:00`.
- Draupadī had five _kṣatriya_ husbands, yet they could not protect her; Bhīṣma was so powerful no one could counter him in war, and Droṇa was the teacher of great _kṣatriyas_ — yet none could help `52:00`.
- The pastime is orchestrated to teach that ultimately we have no protector except Kṛṣṇa `52:00`.

#### Two levels of understanding Bhīṣma [52:00 – 54:00]

- The _Mahābhārata_ can be understood at various levels — the _karma-kāṇḍa_ level and the pure devotional level `52:00`.
- **_Karma-kāṇḍa_ level** — Bhīṣma fought on the Kauravas' side because he was duty-bound: he had taken the Kauravas' salt and had vowed to protect the ruler of Hastināpura `52:00`.
- **Pure devotional level** — he fought on their side because the Lord wanted, through him, to demonstrate that anyone who goes against the will of the Lord will perish `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### The assembly hall [54:00 – 62:00]

- After Draupadī was lost in the gambling, Duryodhana and Karṇa, laughing, said, "Bring her here — she is now our maid-servant" `54:00`.
- Even a maid-servant should be given proper dignity; a _kṣatriya_'s duty is to protect others — all the more so for a woman, and she was not a maid-servant at all `54:00`.
- Prātikāmī, a court attendant, was sent; he knew it was a very _adharmic_ act. Draupadī asked him to put a question to the assembly: whom did Yudhiṣṭhira gamble away first? If he had first lost himself, did he have any right to gamble her? `54:00 – 56:00`.
- Duryodhana called Prātikāmī a coward afraid of Bhīma and sent Duḥśāsana, who went and brutally dragged her. She ran toward Gāndhārī's chambers, hoping at least Gāndhārī would stop this, but he caught her by her hair and dragged her to the assembly, crying bitterly like a helpless person `56:00`.
- Everyone apart from the Kauravas was aghast. Even then, as a cultured lady, Draupadī asked forgiveness for coming in that condition, and asked her question; nobody answered `56:00 – 58:00`.
- **Bhīṣma's answer** — "Your question is very difficult; my mind is bewildered. A person who has gambled himself away has no right to gamble; but scripture also says the wife belongs to the husband. These are contradictory considerations; I don't know the solution" `58:00`.
- From the _karma-kāṇḍa_ point of view, Bhīṣma got caught in _niyamāgraha_. All rules are ultimately there to prevent injustice, and here blatant injustice was happening — a virtuous lady being publicly and flagrantly dishonoured. The technicalities do not matter; the whole thing should have been stopped then and there `58:00`.
- **Vikarṇa** — when nobody answered, Vikarṇa spoke: there are vices — hunting, gambling, drinking and others — under whose influence people lose their intelligence; he felt the whole gambling match should be cancelled and the kingdom restored to its proper owner `58:00 – 60:00`.
- Everyone apart from the Kauravas started cheering, but Karṇa silenced them: of all the improper things seen in the assembly, the most improper was Vikarṇa claiming to protect _dharma_ — nobody forced Yudhiṣṭhira to gamble his kingdom, himself, or his wife `60:00`.
- **The vows** — Bhīma vowed that he would cut off the hand with which Duḥśāsana had dragged Draupadī and drink his blood; when Duryodhana tapped his lap and told Draupadī, "Come and sit on my lap," Bhīma vowed to break his thigh; Arjuna vowed to kill Karṇa `60:00 – 62:00`.
- So the point is that Bhīṣma and Droṇa sat silently in the assembly without protesting; that is why they were no longer worthy to be gurus — yet Arjuna says they are still _mahānubhāvas_ `62:00`.

#### "Better to beg" [62:00 – 64:00]

- Arjuna says: rather than enjoy this world by killing them, it is better for me to become a beggar and live by begging; killing my worshipable superiors for my own desire and enjoyment — what is the use of a kingdom tainted with blood? `62:00`.
- Arjuna is maintaining respect for superiors even though they acted in a grievously wrong way `62:00`.
- For a _kṣatriya_ there is no other way to live than to rule. The Pāṇḍavas lived in the forest only as an exception, by sentence, and then as guests in Virāṭa's kingdom — also temporary. It is _brāhmaṇas_ and renunciants who live by begging `62:00 – 64:00`.
- Yet Arjuna feels it is better to live that way than to eat the spoils of having killed his elders; the next class continues from here `64:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Arjuna still honours Bhīṣma and Droṇa as great souls though they sat silent at Draupadī's humiliation; yet the same episode teaches that no worldly protector can ultimately protect us — only Kṛṣṇa — and that rules exist to prevent injustice, not to excuse it. [48:00 – 64:00]
</Callout>
