## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 2.13 [02:00 – 14:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.13 -->

**Context.** Although the body passes through childhood, youth and old age, the person within remains unchanging; similarly at death the person gets another body, and a sober person is not bewildered `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dehī_ — the owner, the person situated inside the body, who remains unchanging `02:00`, `12:00`.
- _Dhīra_ — a sober person. "Sober" is often used for someone not affected by, or freed from, alcohol; the bodily conception is like an intoxication, and becoming free from it is a key goal of life, promoted by devotional service `02:00 – 04:00`.
- _Incarnation / reincarnation_ — "carnal" relates to flesh; incarnation means to come in flesh, and reincarnation means to come again in flesh, i.e. to get another material body `10:00`.

#### The Gītā builds its message gradually [04:00]

- Devotional service is not mentioned directly yet; the instruction is indirect.
- "Such things do not trouble an intelligent person" implies: if they trouble us, we need to become intelligent.
- Kṛṣṇa teaches by describing how wise people act.

#### "You have grown so big!" [04:00 – 06:00]

- Meeting a child after ten years we say, "You were so small, and now you are so big."
- "You" addresses an unchanging dimension of the person; "were like this" and "are like this" address a changing dimension.
- Even after plastic surgery, bariatric surgery or a nose job, people remain the same person.
- Even someone who says "I have changed completely" still uses the same "I" — something remains the same `06:00`.

#### Biological evidence and the laptop [08:00]

- Science accepts that the cells making up the heart or the arm keep changing even though the organs seem the same.
- If a thing is nothing more than its components, and all components change, is it the same thing?
- **Laptop example:**
  - Replacing the touchpad, then the motherboard, then the hard disk, one at a time — "as good as new," yet something old continues. This is gradual change.
  - Discarding the old laptop for a new one — rapid change.
- Kṛṣṇa is likewise speaking about two kinds of reincarnation.

#### Internal and external reincarnation [10:00 – 12:00]

- Since the body is constantly changing, there is an **internal reincarnation** — gradual, subtle and nearly invisible, like computer parts replaced one at a time.
- Death and a new body is **external reincarnation** — sudden, drastic and visible.
- Kṛṣṇa says: don't get worked up by change; the same kind of change that happens within this life happens at death.
- What gives continuity despite change? Whether the computer's parts are changed or the whole computer is replaced, **the user remains the same**; so too the person occupying the body `12:00`.

#### Answering the question raised by 2.12 [12:00]

- 2.12 said none of the kings, nor Arjuna, nor Kṛṣṇa would ever cease to exist — but some of them will surely die on the battlefield. How?
- Kṛṣṇa answers: the bodies change, but the _dehī_ within stays alive; just as we are not unduly worked up by bodily changes in this life, we need not be worked up by the change to the next body.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The body undergoes internal reincarnation throughout life and external reincarnation at death, but the user of the body — the dehī — stays the same; seeing this, the sober person is not bewildered by either. [08:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.14 [14:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.14 -->

**Context.** Arjuna may accept the soul's eternality yet still fear that his relatives will die and he will be the cause; Kṛṣṇa's answer is that he must learn tolerance of the temporary contacts of the senses with their objects `14:00`, `18:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Mātrā-sparśa_ — the contact of the material senses with sense objects; _mātrā_ especially refers to such contact `18:00`.
- _Śīta-uṣṇa_, _sukha-duḥkha_ — dualities like heat and cold, happiness and distress, which have a beginning and an end and are therefore temporary `18:00`.
- _Bhārata_ — one of the forms of address Kṛṣṇa uses here, connected with Arjuna's bodily lineage `14:00`.

#### Why bodily names right after condemning the bodily conception? [14:00 – 18:00]

- In the preceding verses Kṛṣṇa repeatedly condemned the bodily conception, yet here He addresses Arjuna by names connected with the body. The teacher explains multiple levels:
  - **Spiritual level:** great devotees appeared in those dynasties; remembering the dynasty for the sake of remembering Kṛṣṇa increases remembrance of Kṛṣṇa.
  - **Inspirational level:** 2.15 again addresses Arjuna with an honorific calling him the best; any way a person's spiritual inspiration can be initiated and sustained is good. Respectful honour inspires people to act honourably and rise in spiritual life.
- "You belong to a great family — act greatly." As discussed in another course, we should neither crush nor inflate the false ego, but engage it `18:00`.

#### The leech analogy [18:00 – 20:00]

- Some leeches grip so tightly that ripping them off tears the skin; if allowed to fill their small tubules with blood, they let go by themselves.
- This does not mean we passively watch: some "leeches" can be removed or prevented.
- But where nothing can be changed, be patient — the thing will go away.

#### Two meanings of tolerance [20:00 – 22:00]

- Some see tolerance as impotence — "you're not doing anything."
- In today's media, "tolerance" usually means religious tolerance — respecting others' right to their beliefs. That idea is important, but the Gītā speaks of a different tolerance.
- Here tolerance protects **our right to put first things first** — a right that we ourselves usually take away by getting distracted.

#### Keep the main thing the main thing [22:00 – 24:00]

- "The biggest battle in life is to keep the biggest battle the biggest battle": we decide what is important, then one thing after another comes up and we accomplish nothing.
- **Road-rage example:** someone cuts me off on the road; I stay irritated into the office meeting and cannot present effectively.
- The ability to insulate ourselves from our surroundings keeps us protected and focused.

#### Tolerance is vigilance, not weakness [24:00]

- Kṛṣṇa gives examples of change: heat and cold, happiness and distress, childhood, youth and old age come and go.
- Tolerance is not impotence but **vigilance** — keeping our energies focused: "this is what I'm going to do, and these are the things I'm not going to do."
- It doesn't mean letting others use us like a wet rag; it serves life's purpose — spiritual advancement, which requires focus.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Tolerance in the Gītā is not passivity or mere acceptance of others' views; it is vigilance that refuses to let temporary dualities distract us from what matters most — spiritual advancement. [20:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.15 [26:00 – 30:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.15 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the fruit of tolerance: the person undisturbed by happiness and distress attains liberation, eternal life `26:00`, `28:00 – 30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Fruit of tolerance_ — liberation, eternal life `28:00 – 30:00`.

#### Choosing roads by destination, not appearance [26:00 – 28:00]

- Practising spiritual life is a matter of knowing what to put first.
- If I know my route, a smoother, more comfortable-looking detour may simply lead me in a U-turn back to where I started.
- We choose roads not by how they look but by where they lead; a good road is a bonus.
- Similarly, life's priorities cannot be set by first looks — not just people's appearance, but the way things feel to us at first, which may not show what they actually are.

#### From small things to big things [30:00]

- When we don't let the small things of life distract us, we can focus on the big things.
- Having described this, Kṛṣṇa moves on to explain where the eternity we attain is situated.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Just as we choose a road by its destination, not its surface, the tolerant person judges life's situations by where they lead — and that steady focus leads to liberation. [26:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.16 [30:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:2.16 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa explains that seers of the truth have concluded there are two natures: of the temporary there is no endurance, and of the eternal there is no cessation `30:00`, `36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Asat_ — the temporary; Prabhupāda translates it as "non-existent," clarified as the material body `36:00`, `44:00`.
- _Sat_ — the eternal; clarified as the soul `36:00 – 38:00`.
- _Dṛṣṭi_ — vision; when vision goes deep and reaches its conclusion, as the Vedas reach their conclusion in Vedānta, one sees the two natures `36:00`.
- _Māyā_ — not the body or the world itself, but our inability to see their connection with Kṛṣṇa `42:00 – 44:00`.

#### The Gītā as a philosophical poem [30:00 – 34:00]

- The verse uses figures of speech — literary ornaments that make speech beautiful and appealing.
- _Gītā_ means song: it was sung by Kṛṣṇa as the Mahābhārata war was about to begin.
- Scriptures contain both prose and poetry; poetry often has levels of meaning.
- The Gītā is a philosophical poem showing theistic devotional philosophy as the topmost; we are meant not only to understand it but to recite it — which is purifying and also aesthetically and musically enriching.

#### The inversion figure of speech [34:00]

- The second line inverts the structure of the first, creating a musical effect and emphasising the meaning.
- English examples the teacher gives:
  - "You can take a devotee out of Vṛndāvana, but you cannot take Vṛndāvana out of a devotee."
  - "Ask not what your nation has done for you; ask what you have done for your nation."
- The second half contrasts with and reinforces the first.

#### Two natures: body and soul [36:00 – 38:00]

- Generically the verse refers to material and spiritual nature; specifically to the body and the soul.
- Of the temporary (the body) there is no endurance; of the eternal (the soul) there is no cessation.

#### Square brackets versus parentheses [38:00 – 40:00]

- In Prabhupāda's translation, "the material body" and "the soul" appear in square brackets.
- A parenthesis is the author's own further clarification.
- A square bracket is added to qualify or clarify what has been said — e.g. a quote "he went to Rome but never came back," taken out of its play, gets the name of the character added in square brackets so the reader knows who "he" is.

#### Is the world an illusion? [40:00 – 42:00]

- After Prabhupāda spoke in a class about the world being illusion, a disciple tried to walk through a wall, thinking "this wall is illusion," and hit his head. Prabhupāda explained what "illusion" means.
- Like a dream: while dreaming, a tiger chasing us feels real; on waking we call it false because it has no lasting meaning.
- Illusion here means temporary: our relationships, possessions and hobbies now will not be there forever.
- To the extent we get caught up in them we suffer — perishing not only physically but emotionally, when what we are attached to is taken away.

#### Illusion is not seeing the connection with Kṛṣṇa [42:00 – 44:00]

- In _bhakti_ the illusion is not the body, relationships or the world at large; it is our inability to see them in connection with Kṛṣṇa.
- What we consider meaningful but do not connect to the Absolute Truth — know that to be _māyā_.
- Seeing the connection, we use those things in Kṛṣṇa's service.
- Things are therefore not illusion but illusory — temporary; attachment to the temporary without seeing its link to the eternal brings us under illusion.
- Looking deeply at the changing world, one infers a spiritual reality beyond it; Kṛṣṇa is building a flow of thought towards the eternal reality we must all move to `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The temporary is not "unreal" like a wall one can walk through; it is illusory because it does not last — and the real illusion is failing to see its connection with Kṛṣṇa. [40:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 2.17 [44:00 – 64:57]

<!-- vedabase:2.17 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says that what pervades the body is indestructible and no one can destroy the imperishable soul `44:00 – 46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Avināśī_ — indestructible; nothing can destroy it `46:00`.
- _Avyaya_ — imperishable; not degrading through old age — no deterioration `46:00`.
- _Sarvam_ — "all," given without a clear antecedent in the verse `44:00 – 46:00`.
- _Consciousness_ — the energy of the soul, radiated throughout the body `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### What does "all" refer to? [44:00 – 48:00]

- Impersonalists say _sarvam_ refers to all of existence and read the verse as supporting impersonalism.
- Prabhupāda explains it refers to the body.
- From context: Kṛṣṇa has been speaking about the body and its changes, and the next verse also speaks of the body — so reading _sarvam_ as all existence is an unwarranted interpretation.
- Application to Arjuna: Bhīṣma and Droṇa, whose death he fears, are particles of spirit pervading their bodies, and are indestructible `48:00`.

#### Pervasion and indestructibility [48:00 – 50:00]

- The teacher presents Rāmānuja's explanation: read _sarvam_ in light of what follows — whatever pervades everything else is indestructible.
- Generally one object destroys another by entering it:
  - a bullet enters the body;
  - water enters a drowning person;
  - a weapon enters the body;
  - termites enter a tree and destroy it from within.
- The _ātmā_ pervades everything (in the body) and so cannot be pervaded or penetrated by anything — hence it is indestructible. Arjuna's arrows cannot hit the soul `50:00`.

#### Consciousness is limited to one's own body [50:00 – 52:00]

- The soul is at one place and radiates its energy, consciousness, throughout the body.
- My consciousness pervades my body but not yours: if you have back pain, I cannot feel it — so _sarvam_ cannot mean all existence.
- Prabhupāda's analogy: a medicine spreads through the body via the blood; similarly consciousness spreads all over the body `52:00`.
- The point is to drive home to Arjuna: do not lament, because the soul is eternal.

#### The soul's size: literal and metaphorical [52:00 – 56:00]

- The purport describes the soul as one ten-thousandth of the tip of a hair.
- Literal? Then what exactly is "the tip of a hair"? It is hard to define.
- The teacher's answer: it is both:
  - **Metaphorical** — it indicates how extremely small the soul is.
  - **Literal** — the soul really has dimensions; it is not formless or an amorphous spray of consciousness, but a particle from which consciousness spreads outwards.
- Dimensions are not a monopoly of matter: in the spiritual world Kṛṣṇa and Rādhārāṇī have particular heights and forms; the soul has its original _svarūpa_ there and now exists like a tiny seed `54:00 – 56:00`.

#### Why we cannot see the soul [56:00 – 58:00]

- Prabhupāda also explains that the soul is accompanied by the Supersoul and quotes several verses, including one saying the soul is atomic and known by intelligence.
- If the soul were just tiny, a microscope with fine enough resolution would show it.
- We cannot see it not because it is microscopic but because it is spiritual, and the spiritual cannot be seen with gross material vision.

#### The seat of the soul among the five airs [58:00 – 62:00]

- The purport says the soul floats in the five kinds of air within the heart. Each air has a specific function, bringing about different movements within the body — a different way of looking at the body from modern reductionist medicine.
- **Vector analogy:** the airs are like vectors pushing in different directions; the point where their resultant is zero is where the _ātmā_ is situated.
- The airs are one mode of interaction between the gross and subtle bodies; the subtle body is the interface between the gross body and the soul `60:00`.
- One can perceive the soul's spiritual nature when freed from the contamination of the material airs.
- _Prāṇāyāma_ regulates the life airs to lift consciousness upward towards Kṛṣṇa; yogīs raise the soul through the _cakras_ to the forehead _cakra_ and try to escape bodily bondage — to be discussed in a later chapter `60:00`.
- Only an insane person would consider the atomic soul to be the all-pervading Viṣṇu `62:00`.

#### Heart, brain, stomach and the soul [62:00 – 64:00]

- Prabhupāda notes medical science accepts the heart as the seat of the body's energies but cannot ascertain that the source of energy is the soul.
- The body has several critical centres: the stomach generates energy (a stomach wound can be fatal), the heart circulates blood (a heart attack can be fatal), and the brain is a seat of consciousness (brain damage can be fatal).
- The interaction between soul, subtle body and gross body is intricate; the precise mechanism is hard to grasp, but a sophisticated system exists.
- The _ātmā_ is situated in the heart; its consciousness goes through the subtle body, through the mind to the brain, and outwards; Prabhupāda explains the energy also flows through the blood `64:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The soul cannot be destroyed because its consciousness pervades the body while nothing can pervade it; tiny yet truly dimensional, it is invisible to us because it is spiritual, not merely because it is small. [48:00 – 58:00]
</Callout>
