## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 8.23 [02:00 – 32:00]

<!-- vedabase:8.23 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa begins to describe the suitable times of departure, at which yogis leave this world and either return or do not return `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Deva-yāna_ and _pitṛ-yāna_ — the two Vedic paths of departure Kṛṣṇa discusses in the next verses `02:00`.
- _Kapha_, _vāta_, _pitta_ — Āyurveda's way of describing the body; when they are in balance the body functions properly `08:00`.
- _Cakras_ — energy centres; the yogic map of the body `04:00`, `12:00`.
- _Siddhis_ — yogic perfections, such as obtaining objects from afar `24:00`.

#### Entering foreign territory [02:00]

- The teacher cautions that this is "foreign territory": what Kṛṣṇa discusses here involves a lot of preliminary Vedic knowledge with which we are not familiar in our normal course of action `02:00`.
- Before discussing the two paths, he discusses the path of yoga a little more `02:00`.

#### Correct maps and appropriate maps [02:00 – 06:00]

- A map helps us navigate territory; if the map is wrong, we cannot navigate properly — travelling in Mumbai with a map of Kolkata, no matter how hard we try, we won't find our way `02:00 – 04:00`.
- So first we need a correct map. But even a correct map may not be appropriate: to find one's way in a city, a map primarily about geography won't help `04:00`.
- In Vedic culture there are multiple correct maps, but not all are appropriate for our spiritual advancement in Kali-yuga by _bhakti-yoga_ in Kṛṣṇa consciousness `04:00`.
- What we can know depends on what we already know: a sound that only irritates a car owner tells the mechanic exactly what is wrong `06:00`.

#### Three maps of the body [06:00 – 14:00]

- **Modern science** — the body's functioning depends on the proper balance, circulation and integration of its biochemicals (substances, studied in biology, that contribute to life); disease is a biochemical imbalance. The teacher calls this a reductionist view `06:00 – 08:00`.
- **Āyurveda** — sees the body in terms of _kapha_, _vāta_ and _pitta_; when balanced, the body functions effectively. People do get cured by both systems `08:00`.
- **The _cakra_ system** — sees the body in terms of its energy centres `12:00`.
- All are maps: the body is the same, but the framework decides how one sees it. Even an allopathic doctor recognises "heat" in the body as temperature; and the original Āyurvedic texts speak about God and the soul `10:00`.
- Like anatomical charts that show only the nervous system, or the internal organs, or the liver, which map is most appropriate depends on what we want to do `12:00`.

#### Energy fields and auras [14:00 – 20:00]

- According to modern science the universe is filled with vibrations: moving a hand sets the air vibrating, and the whole system of telecommunication — calling people by cell phone — is based on electromagnetic vibrations `14:00`.
- The three modes of nature (chapter 14) are another way of analysis, especially of psychological characteristics `16:00`.
- Popular depictions in comics and movies show light around saintly people, and the teacher mentions that technology has found every person has some kind of aura `16:00`.
- Why do people feel peaceful in a temple? Because of the presence of spiritual vibrations — ultimately the presence of Kṛṣṇa — even if materialistic people do not understand why `18:00`.
- In the path of yoga these energy fields can be maximised or minimised for one's benefit `20:00`.

#### Higher dimensions and yogic siddhis [20:00 – 30:00]

- When yogis advance, they gain access to subtler, higher dimensions and can do things not normally possible `20:00`.
- **Ant and mosquito** — an ant in a room going from the top-right corner to the bottom-left must crawl along walls and floor; a mosquito simply flies diagonally. Access to a higher dimension makes the inaccessible easily accessible `20:00 – 22:00`.
- **Starlight** — a star a thousand light-years away is seen as it was a thousand years ago; even at the speed of light we would arrive a thousand years later, and the star may have expired. Space travel in science fiction is limited by the speed of light `22:00`.
- **The worm and the hole** — normally one goes around the surface of a sphere, but a worm can make a hole and go straight through; whether such shortcuts exist in space-time is speculation. The point: the normal way of functioning can be changed `24:00`.
- Some magicians mislead people with tricks that imitate _siddhis_, but Vedic yogis, by developing subtle energy, could access subtler dimensions, travel faster and take shortcuts `24:00 – 26:00`.
- The teacher clarifies that his focus is not science: these dimensions are not exactly identical to dimensions as science uses the term, but they point in that direction `26:00 – 28:00`.
- The teacher also mentions a correlation between glands, hormones and the _cakras_; within their own worldviews, both systems see the possibility of accessing power `30:00`.

#### Why the yoga map is not ultimate [32:00]

- The knowledge of _cakras_ focuses a lot on the material: regulating the breath, sitting in a particular posture, activating the _cakras_, and raising the life-air to the highest `32:00`.
- The path of yoga does not rely primarily on the grace of the Lord; it focuses on material readjustment of the body `32:00`.
- _Bhakti_ focuses on remembering Kṛṣṇa — and by remembering Kṛṣṇa one returns to Kṛṣṇa. That is the best `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The yogic map of the body and its subtle energies is a correct map, but it rests on material readjustment rather than the Lord's grace; for spiritual progress in Kali-yuga the appropriate map is bhakti — remembering Kṛṣṇa and so returning to Him. [02:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 8.24 [32:00 – 38:00]

<!-- vedabase:8.24 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the path of light, on which those who know the Absolute depart and attain Brahman `32:00`, `36:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Agni_ — fire; _jyoti_ — light `32:00`.
- _Śukla_ — the bright (white) fortnight, as opposed to the dark fortnight `32:00`.
- _Uttarāyaṇa_ — the period connected with the earth's tilt `34:00`.
- _Brahma-vit_ — one who knows the Absolute `36:00`.

#### A route overseen by presiding deities [34:00 – 36:00]

- When the soul departs by the path of yoga described in the Vedas, the path it takes is determined like a train's route through different railway stations `34:00 – 36:00`.
- Different parts of the pathway are overseen by presiding deities: cosmic phenomena such as fire have deities in charge of them `36:00`.
- When a person knows the Absolute and leaves in that consciousness, those particular deities guide the person along the path, ultimately to Brahman `36:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The path of light is a cosmic route under the care of presiding deities, who guide the knower of the Absolute step by step to Brahman. [32:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 8.25 – 8.26 [38:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:8.25 -->

<!-- vedabase:8.26 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the other path, and then names these as the two ways of passing from this world — one of no return and one of return `38:00 – 42:00`.

#### Departing at the wrong time [40:00]

- Leaving at a time of death that is not exactly the right time does not mean zero results `40:00`.
- One still reaches Candraloka, the moon — the heavens `40:00`.
- But the stay there is not long-lasting; one comes back to the earth `40:00`.

#### Many preconditions [42:00]

- On these two paths of leaving the world, as described in this chapter, many preconditions have to be taken care of for success in one's spiritual journey; if they are not, one does not attain the goal `42:00`.
- By _bhakti-yoga_, by contrast, one makes the most tangible advancement and goes rapidly back to Kṛṣṇa `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The path of darkness gives only a temporary stay in the heavens and a return to earth, and even the path of light depends on many preconditions — whereas bhakti takes one quickly and tangibly back to Kṛṣṇa. [40:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 8.27 [42:00 – 52:00]

<!-- vedabase:8.27 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa says that the devotee who knows these paths is never bewildered, and therefore should always be fixed in devotion `42:00 – 46:00`.

#### Connection with BG 6.47 [42:00 – 44:00]

- The teacher recalls 6.47, the climax of the sixth chapter: the best yogi is the one who always remembers Kṛṣṇa — and that yogi returns to Kṛṣṇa `42:00 – 44:00`.

#### The devotee need not fear the time of death [44:00 – 48:00]

- One might worry: "At my time of departure, what if something happens around me and I don't remember Kṛṣṇa? What will happen?" Devotees should not be afraid of such things `44:00`.
- Some people, when ready to leave the world, go to Kāśī or Vṛndāvana to focus exclusively on cultivating remembrance in the best possible setting `46:00`.
- But "not bewildered" does not mean we just sit idle — we have to do something `46:00 – 48:00`.
- If we are diligent in service, we need not bother about exactly what will happen when we die; we do what we can in the process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, without worrying much about death `48:00`.

#### Vṛndāvana consciousness [48:00 – 50:00]

- Rather than relying on reaching a holy place at the end, we can bring Vṛndāvana consciousness into our lives through cultivation in this life `48:00 – 50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A devotee is not bewildered by the technicalities of departure; diligent service and cultivated remembrance in this life take care of the moment of death. [44:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 8.28 [52:00 – 58:00]

<!-- vedabase:8.28 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa concludes the chapter: whatever results come from Vedic activities, the person who takes up devotional service attains them all and reaches the supreme abode `54:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paraṁ sthānam_ — the supreme abode; the love of Kṛṣṇa `54:00 – 56:00`.
- _Rāja-vidyā_ — the title theme of chapter 9, the king of confidential knowledge `54:00`.

#### From acting in the world to acting for the goal beyond it [52:00]

- Our focus should be on Kṛṣṇa — not just on doing religious activities for the sake of gaining _puṇya_ `52:00`.
- In this chapter Kṛṣṇa shifts Arjuna's focus from acting in the world to acting for the goal beyond the world `52:00`.

#### Everything is included in devotion [54:00 – 56:00]

- The person who remembers Kṛṣṇa gets the results of Vedic activities, as prescribed through the stages of life in the Vedic system (beginning with the _brahmacārī_), and attains the supreme abode `54:00`.
- In the love of Kṛṣṇa everything else is automatically included `56:00`.
- One should learn this not from mental speculators; the highest perfection of life is to be completely in love with Kṛṣṇa `56:00`.
- The chapter ends with the call to stay fixed in yoga — and the best yoga is _bhakti-yoga_ `56:00`.

#### Bridge to chapter 9 [54:00, 58:00]

- The ninth chapter, _rāja-vidyā_, clearly continues this theme; the teacher links the sixth and ninth chapters as he closes the class `54:00`, `58:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Devotional service does not bypass the Vedic goals but contains them all; the love of Kṛṣṇa is the supreme abode and the highest perfection, and bhakti is the best yoga. [52:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
