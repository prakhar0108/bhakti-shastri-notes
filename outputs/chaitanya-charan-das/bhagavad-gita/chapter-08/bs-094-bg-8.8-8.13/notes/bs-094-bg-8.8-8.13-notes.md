## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 8.8 [06:00 – 26:00]

<!-- vedabase:8.8 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa states that by contemplating the Supreme Person with consciousness kept fixed on Him, one attains the Supreme Person `06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Paramaṁ puruṣam_ — the Supreme Person; Kṛṣṇa speaks of Him in the third person, not as "Me" `06:00`.
- _Kevala-bhakti_ — pure devotion: nothing but devotion `16:00`.
- _Pradhānī-bhūta bhakti_ — devotion that is the principal element, though mixed with other things such as _jñāna_; _bhakti_ is more than half `16:00`.
- _Miśra-bhakti_ — mixed devotion `18:00`.

#### Why Kṛṣṇa speaks in the third person [06:00 – 10:00]

- The most striking feature of the verse is that Kṛṣṇa refers to the Supreme Person in the third person `06:00`.
- He describes this path from the perspective of the seeker — the yogi `06:00`.
- By introspection and meditation the yogi can progressively move toward the ultimate reality and may even discover that it is personal `08:00`.
- But the specific identity of that Person — that the Absolute Truth has a blackish-blue complexion and wears a peacock feather — cannot be understood by introspection or meditation; it requires revelation `08:00`.
- Putting the chapter's verses together (remember Me and attain Me; fix the mind on Me) makes it clear from the flow of the Gītā that the Supreme Person is Kṛṣṇa `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### The axis: negative, zero and positive [10:00 – 12:00]

- The teacher describes an axis:
  - **Negative side** — material relationships and material activities.
  - **Zero** — freedom from material conceptions.
  - **Positive side** — spiritual reality.
- Moving from the negative to zero is universal: everyone has to become free from material conceptions and material identification `10:00 – 12:00`.
- On the positive side there can be slightly different trajectories — one may go to Ayodhyā, to Goloka Vṛndāvana, to Dvārakā `12:00`.

#### Cultural attraction versus one's spiritual form [12:00 – 16:00]

- We should not prematurely imagine what our _svarūpa_ is; first we must give up our strong identification with the present material body `12:00`.
- We may feel attracted to a particular form of the Lord because of the culture we grew up in — for example, songs heard from childhood. Such attraction may be more cultural than spiritual, because it is not necessarily at the level of the soul `12:00`.
- It is fortunate to have grown up hearing about the Lord, and that can be used to increase contemplation of Him `14:00`.
- Some feel, "I want to focus on Lord Rāma." The teacher says this is not wrong, but we should not mistake a cultural attraction for our spiritual one `14:00`.
- By chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa we become purified of material designations; then the soul's spiritual attraction to the particular form of the Lord with whom it is related will naturally manifest `14:00 – 16:00`.
- So we need not be bothered about this now — just chant Hare Kṛṣṇa `16:00`.

#### The chart: proportion of bhakti and destination [16:00 – 24:00]

- The next section of the chapter is explained with a chart: what is the destination of different kinds of practitioners `16:00`.
- Why _bhakti_ is different from the other paths `18:00`:
  - _Jñāna_ uses the intellect — material.
  - _Yoga_ concentrates the mind — material.
  - _Karma_ uses the body — material.
  - _Bhakti_ centres on the heart, the seat of emotions in the soul — spiritual. So _bhakti_ is essentially transcendental, while the others are influenced by the modes.
- The _bhakti_ we perform now may still be within the modes, mixed with something material — _miśra-bhakti_ — but there is an upward movement from "more than half" toward _kevala_, and that progression involves purification `18:00`.
- Destinations in the chart `20:00 – 22:00`:
  - _Kevala_ devotees attain the spiritual world — for a Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava, Goloka Vṛndāvana.
  - _Pradhānī-bhūta_ devotees who do not become _kevala_ by the end of the journey continue their attachment to the other process.
  - Those practising _jñāna-yoga_, _dhyāna-yoga_ or _karma-yoga_ can eventually attain a perfection such as the _brahma-jyoti_ — but only when they add _bhakti_.
- Without _bhakti_ no process gives its result; _bhakti_ is essential, but its proportion varies, and the destination varies accordingly `22:00`.
- In this section the teacher focuses on yoga, recalling the sixth chapter's description of the yogi fixing the mind on Kṛṣṇa, peaceful, fearless, in celibacy, holding head, body and neck straight `22:00`.

#### The object of meditation decides the result [24:00 – 26:00]

- When Kṛṣṇa is the object, one attains the ultimate perfection `24:00`.
- Yogis on other paths may also fix the mind, but on some other object `24:00`.
- If someone says, "This is my path and I want to follow it," that is fine. But if they consider their path supreme, minimise Kṛṣṇa's form, criticise devotional service and think "Kṛṣṇa's form is _māyā_; I want to go beyond it," that itself is illusion and can lead to an unfortunate result — a point Kṛṣṇa returns to in 9.11 – 9.12 `24:00 – 26:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Every path must first free us from material identification, but only bhakti carries us to the Supreme Person; the proportion of bhakti in our practice decides our destination, so we need not speculate now about our eternal form — chanting purifies, and our true attraction will manifest by itself. [10:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 8.9 [26:00 – 44:00]

<!-- vedabase:8.9 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes, again from the yogi's point of view, the attributes of the Supreme on which the yogi meditates `26:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kavi_ — one who knows everything `26:00`.
- _Purāṇa_ — the oldest; in this world experience is valued, and the oldest is the most experienced and wisest `26:00 – 28:00`.
- _Anuśāsitā_ — the controller `28:00`.
- _Aṇor aṇīyān_ — smaller than the smallest; He is also greater than the greatest `30:00`.
- _Dhātā_ — the maintainer of everyone `32:00`.
- _Acintya-rūpa_ — inconceivable form; _rūpa_ means form `32:00`.
- _Āditya-varṇa_ — luminous like the sun; _tamasaḥ parastāt_ — beyond darkness `36:00`.

#### The controller, and the paradox of evil [28:00]

- It is said that not a blade of grass moves without the will of the Lord `28:00`.
- This does not mean Kṛṣṇa wants bad things to happen — it is not that He wants terrorists to blow up buildings. They want to do it and Kṛṣṇa allows it, but ultimately He is in control `28:00`.

#### Smaller than the smallest, greater than the greatest [28:00 – 32:00]

- The heart is the centre of cognition and emotion, and Kṛṣṇa is present there as the constant companion, the unfailing friend of the soul; wherever the soul goes, Kṛṣṇa goes `30:00`.
- He is present as the Supersoul even in the bodies of microbes and within atoms — smaller than the smallest `30:00`.
- At the same time the whole universe comes out of Him — greater than the greatest `30:00`.
- The yogi's contemplation is interesting: it does not focus on Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, such as stealing butter, but on understanding His greatness `30:00`.

#### The maintainer [32:00]

- "My bank balance maintains me; I maintain my family" — true at one level `32:00`.
- At the ultimate level, if Kṛṣṇa's nature did not provide the arrangement, what would money do? The grains have to come by the arrangement of Kṛṣṇa's nature; our efforts alone do not maintain us `32:00`.

#### A form that is inconceivable [32:00 – 36:00]

- That the verse speaks of _rūpa_ — form — indicates that yogis also should meditate on the Absolute Truth as a person with form `32:00 – 34:00`.
- The teacher links this to the progression of yoga in the Third Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_, where Kapiladeva describes how the yogi meditates, with a beautiful chapter on the beautiful form of the Lord and how the yogi meditates on it `34:00`.
- Being inconceivable, His form cannot be understood by our own efforts; we understand by following scripture and its principles, becoming purified, and then coming to a higher spiritual perception `36:00`.

#### Beyond darkness [36:00 – 38:00]

- The phrase describing the Lord as sun-like and beyond darkness comes several times in Vedic literature `36:00`.
- The teacher connects it with a famous Upaniṣadic prayer to go from darkness to light `38:00`.
- This is not like thinking about the form of a movie star; it is meditation on a transcendental person `38:00`.

#### Meditation is a rewarding challenge, not a break [40:00 – 44:00]

- Many people think of meditation as a break from hard work — "I will forget my family and responsibilities and relax" `40:00`.
- _Bhakti_ does give relaxation, but that relaxation comes after purification; the mind is restless, and by chanting we are gradually making it peaceful `40:00`.
- We cannot expect peacefulness without becoming purified; we need patience `42:00`.
- If we expect meditation to be relaxing, the expectation only frustrates us — "I want to relax but I can't; I don't experience anything" `42:00`.
- Meditation rejuvenates us when we experience Kṛṣṇa; when we cannot concentrate and cannot experience Him, it starts to feel like a burden `42:00`.
- Hence the principle: the more we expect meditation to be a break from hard work, the more it becomes hard work; instead we should expect it to be a rewarding challenge `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The yogi is told to meditate on a Supreme Person who knows all, is oldest, controls and maintains all, is smaller than the smallest and greater than the greatest, and has an inconceivable, sun-like form — and such meditation should be approached as a rewarding challenge, not as a break from work. [26:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 8.10 [44:00 – 54:00]

<!-- vedabase:8.10 -->

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa describes the yogi who, at the time of death, by the strength of yoga fixes the life-air between the eyebrows and, remembering the Lord in devotion, attains the Supreme Person `44:00`, `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Yoga-bala_ — the strength of yoga practice; different yogis use different methods of controlling the mind `44:00`.
- _Bhruvor madhye_ — in the space between the eyebrows `44:00`.
- _Prāṇa_ — the life-air; it is situated in a particular centre in the body and can be raised `44:00 – 46:00`.
- _Cakras_ — centres of power or energy in the body `50:00`.

#### Connection with BG 8.7 [44:00]

- Kṛṣṇa is speaking here of the yogi trying to attain perfection by cultivating remembrance `44:00`.
- He had said in 8.7, "always remember Me and fight." How can one always remember Kṛṣṇa? By _bhakti_ — and the teacher stresses that if we want to remember Kṛṣṇa, _bhakti-yoga_ is the best `44:00`.

#### Prāṇa and the soul's exit [46:00 – 50:00]

- The life-airs perform different functions within the body, and the _prāṇa_ has a particular location that can be elevated `46:00`.
- In the yoga analysis, the location of the _prāṇa_ indicates the yogi's spiritual evolution; by aṣṭāṅga practice it moves upward, and the yogi is considered elevated `48:00`.
- At death the soul exits through whichever opening is closest to the _prāṇa_'s location `48:00`:
  - through a lower opening — the soul goes to a lower species or lower planets;
  - through the highest opening — the soul goes higher.
- The essential point `48:00`:
  - The soul is attached to the subtle body.
  - The subtle body is the link between the soul and the gross body.
  - The link between the subtle body and the gross body is formed by the _prāṇa_.
  - At death the gross body is left, and the subtle body carries the soul to the next body; the teacher notes that the _Vedānta-sūtra_ describes the _prāṇa_ also going along.

#### The cakras and kuṇḍalinī-yoga [50:00 – 52:00]

- The _prāṇa_ is located in one of the _cakras_; the teacher names _mūlādhāra_, _svādhiṣṭhāna_ and _maṇipūra_ from the chart `50:00`.
- The whole process of aṣṭāṅga-yoga is also called _kuṇḍalinī-yoga_; its purpose is to lift the _prāṇa_ so that the soul moves from the lower _cakras_ to the upper ones `50:00`.
- The most serious yogis, by the power of yoga, have the soul exit through one of the upper openings `52:00`.
- In this verse the _prāṇa_ is to be gradually brought upward and placed on the _ājñā-cakra_, between the eyebrows; remembering Kṛṣṇa in devotion, one then attains the divine Supreme Person `52:00`.

#### Inner consciousness and the cosmic hierarchy [52:00]

- The rest of the chart shows a correspondence between our inner level of consciousness and the outer levels of the cosmic hierarchy: reaching a particular inner level, a person attains the corresponding destination `52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
In the yoga map, the soul's destination at death depends on where the life-air has been raised; the yogi labours to lift it to the point between the eyebrows — yet even here, attaining the Supreme Person requires remembering Him in devotion. [44:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 8.11 [52:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** Kṛṣṇa states the qualifications of those who enter the Absolute by the yoga path, and says He will describe that process briefly `54:00`.

#### The demands of the yoga path [54:00 – 56:00]

- To follow this path one must:
  - practise _brahmacarya_ (celibacy);
  - become free from attachment;
  - then practise yoga to perfection `54:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa describes it only briefly, but as we look at it we will feel how demanding the process is `54:00 – 56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">

### Bhagavad-gita 8.12 – 8.13 [56:00 – 60:00]

<!-- vedabase:8.12 -->

<!-- vedabase:8.13 -->

**Context.** If one can remember the Supreme at the time of departure, one attains Him — but how can one do that at death? By practice, which Kṛṣṇa describes briefly in these two verses `56:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Dvāra_ — doors: the senses are like doors through which consciousness goes out and external objects come in `56:00`.
- _Hṛdi_ — in the heart; not the biological organ that pumps blood, but the seat of emotion `56:00`.
- _Pratyāhāra_ — withdrawal of the senses, followed by _dhāraṇā_ and _dhyāna_ `58:00`.

#### Closing the doors and fixing the mind [56:00 – 58:00]

- First, the doors of the senses are closed `56:00`.
- Then the mind is fixed on the heart — that is, on the deepest reality situated there `56:00`.
- In this way Kṛṣṇa outlines the yoga sequence: first sense withdrawal, then concentration and meditation `58:00`.
- The yogi then places the life-air at the top of the head `58:00`.

#### Bhakti along with yoga [58:00 – 60:00]

- The teacher points out that the process of _bhakti_ has to be there along with the process of yoga — the mind fixed on Bhagavān, in remembrance (_smaraṇa_) of Kṛṣṇa `58:00`.
- The whole process `60:00`:
  1. Practise _brahmacarya_.
  2. Close the senses.
  3. Raise the life-air.
  4. Fix the mind and remember Kṛṣṇa.
  5. Chant _oṁ_.
- One who leaves the body in this way attains the supreme destination `60:00`.

#### Preview of the next class [60:00]

- In 8.14 – 8.16 Kṛṣṇa speaks of the alternative: how one can think of Him through devotional service — to be discussed in the next class `60:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The yogi's deathbed method — closing the sense-doors, fixing the mind in the heart, raising the life-air and chanting oṁ while remembering Kṛṣṇa — shows that remembrance is the real goal, and that remembrance needs bhakti even within yoga. [56:00 – 60:00]
</Callout>
