### Text 8: the essence of all advice [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:noi/8 -->

**Context.** The teacher walks through the verse's key words and Śrīla Prabhupāda's translation, then places the verse within the structure of the book `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Tat_ — refers to the Absolute Truth, Lord Kṛṣṇa: His name, form and character `00:00`.
- Glorification — engaging oneself nicely in glorifying the Lord `00:00`.
- Systematic remembrance — systematically cultivating remembrance of the Lord `00:00`.
- Progressively — one engages first one's tongue and then one's mind `00:00`.
- _Anurāgī_ — those who have _anurāga_ (attachment) for Lord Kṛṣṇa; exalted devotees whom one should follow `00:00`.
- _Akhilam_ (all) — one should spend all of one's time absorbed in this engagement `00:00 – 02:00`.

#### Where the verse sits in the book [02:00]

- In a sense this verse completes the _upadeśas_ (instructions).
- After this, the text is less instruction and more glorification — how fortunate is one who can stay in Vraja, and then Govardhana and Rādhā-kuṇḍa, to be discussed in future classes.
- That is why this verse is called the essence of all advice.

#### Prabhupāda's translation in summary [02:00]

- Use one's full time — 24 hours a day — in nicely chanting and remembering the Lord's name, form, qualities and pastimes.
- Engage the tongue and mind gradually in this way.
- Reside in Vraja and serve Kṛṣṇa under the guidance of devotees, following in the footsteps of the Lord's beloved devotees who are deeply attached to Him.

### The movement exists to train the mind [04:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport starts with the basics and focuses on the mind; the teacher divides the purport into _why_ to train the mind and _how_ to train it, focusing mostly on why `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### A radical statement [04:00]

- Prabhupāda says the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement is especially meant to train the mind.
- This is not listed among the seven purposes of ISKCON, yet each of those purposes — teaching about God systematically, distributing literature, building a place for transcendental pastimes, bringing people together in a simple, God-centred life — involves changing the way people think, which is training of the mind.
- The movement's primary contribution is therefore not humanitarian (though devotees also do food relief); it is training the mind.

#### Yoga as reorientation of the mind [04:00 – 06:00]

- _Yoga_ means to connect the soul with Kṛṣṇa — not a physical connection made with rivets and screws, but a reorientation of the mind.
- The mind wants to stay disconnected because it is attracted to material things; training it means making it inclined toward and attracted to Kṛṣṇa.
- The teacher cites BG 7.1: the purpose of practising yoga is that the mind becomes attached to Kṛṣṇa `06:00`.

#### The mind as friend or enemy [06:00 – 08:00]

- Because the mind can act in either way, for our own good we must train it to act as our friend; otherwise it makes us do things that lead to our degradation.
- Example: a talented sports star who "chokes" at key moments may speak of "my demons" — not creatures with horns, but something within dragging him down. That something is the mind.

#### Why the mind becomes an enemy [08:00 – 10:00]

- It carries past impressions of material enjoyment, and also of trouble and anxiety, which overwhelm a person emotionally.
- Essentially the mind is affected by the lower modes — passion and ignorance — producing self-defeating emotions such as choking under pressure or, more relevant for devotees, succumbing to temptation.
- Temptation overpowers us in two ways:
  - **Externally** — a tempting object appears and is so attractive we fall for it.
  - **Internally** — desires suddenly surge up and grip us so strongly that, as Arjuna describes a seeker's inner conflict, one is engaged in sin as if against one's will.

#### Bubbles from below and the law of last thought [10:00 – 12:00]

- Just as fermentation deep in water suddenly sends bubbles to the surface, deep impressions of past indulgence suddenly rise in the mind, sometimes contrary to our moral and spiritual values.
- Prabhupāda calls this a dangerous situation because what we think of at death determines our destination — the law of last thought.
- At death, a time of great devastation and fear, the mind instinctively reaches for whatever gave it the greatest shelter during life. If we have cultivated Kṛṣṇa consciousness throughout life, the mind will go to Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Training the mind is the core work of the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement and of yoga itself, because an untrained mind — carrying deep impressions that can surface without warning — acts as our enemy, even at the decisive moment of death. [04:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Look at the mind before you look with the mind [12:00 – 16:00]

**Context.** To build the conviction needed for training the mind, the teacher introduces an acronym, M-I-N-D, describing four ways the mind acts inimically `12:00`.

#### Treat the mind as a separate person [12:00 – 14:00]

- "Look at the mind before you look with the mind" — the teacher mentions this is the title of a book he hopes to write.
- Driving analogy:
  - A trusted friend beside us says "look over there" — while holding the steering wheel, we still look ahead first and only then cautiously look aside.
  - A stranger in a bus we are driving says "look there" — we first evaluate whether the person is trustworthy before following the suggestion.
- Likewise, when the mind speaks, first evaluate its credibility: does what it says make sense?

#### Why learn the categories [14:00 – 16:00]

- The mind often lies in four major ways; knowing them lets us check whether a given thought falls into one of these categories and be cautious.
- The mind troubles us not only spiritually but materially too — a talented player chokes under pressure; addictions arise when the mind misleads us repeatedly in a wrong direction and traps us.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Before acting on a thought, step back and assess the mind like an unknown speaker — checking whether its suggestion fits one of its familiar lies. [12:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### The M-I-N-D acronym: four ways the mind tricks us [16:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** The four letters name four tricks; recognising each one lets us check it `16:00`.

#### M — The mind magnifies problems [16:00 – 20:00]

- Thinking about a problem does not solve it; overthinking makes the problem bigger in our mind and makes us feel smaller and overwhelmed ("this won't work, this won't work").
- **Overcooking a rotten vegetable** — cooking a rotten vegetable for 25 minutes instead of 5 will not make it good; overthinking will not fix a problem.
- We do need to think about how to deal with problems, but with a calm, collected, composed mind; in hyper-anxiety the mind just goes round and round and leaves us with a headache.
- What has happened has happened; the present and future are open for constructive action — focus there.
- The mind keeps our thoughts glued to the problem, making us worry, lament and feel depressed, sapping our inner vigour.
- "Don't tell God how big your problems are; tell your problems how big God is" — when we pray only about the problem, we are problem-conscious, not Kṛṣṇa-conscious `20:00`.

#### I — The mind imagines pleasures [20:00 – 26:00]

- The mind sees an object and spins into fantasy land about getting and enjoying it, wasting thought, time and energy.
- Example: young people walking down the street, swinging an imaginary bat, picturing themselves hitting a six off the last ball against Pakistan and becoming national heroes `20:00`.
- Daydreams may be merely wasteful, or sinful and immoral; sinful thoughts contaminate and can gather momentum into action, and even harmless ones distract from our essential interests `22:00`.
- **Vision versus daydreaming** `22:00 – 24:00`:
  - With a vision, we are in control — like Prabhupāda's vision of temples and devotees, "time is separating us from them."
  - Prabhupāda did not just envision; he worked vigorously with whatever limited resources he had. The more resourceful we are, the more we advance.
  - A vision is well thought out and accompanied by tangible steps; fantasy is the mind spinning around a sense object.
- **The new car** `24:00` — seeing the latest car, the mind races through loans, selling the old car, admiring onlookers, a raise, a new job, even a lottery. Imagining pleasures also magnifies problems.
- Our priority is to love and serve Kṛṣṇa; this does not mean living in poverty, but unfulfilled fantasies weaken, discourage and irritate us.
- **Not cheerleaders of the mind** `26:00` — cheerleaders applaud whatever another performs; we must be controllers who decide which direction the mind goes.

#### N — The mind neglects opportunities [26:00 – 32:00]

- The present is all we have and all we will ever have; the past is gone and the future is not in our control. Anything we do will be done when the future becomes the present.
- The mind pulls our thoughts to the past ("only if this were like that") or the future, neglecting what we can do now.
- We can learn from the past and prepare for the future — but only when in the mode of goodness and in control of the mind; otherwise we only lament or fantasise `28:00`.
- The mind treats life as a "practice run"; in spiritual life every moment is important, because Kṛṣṇa is always in our heart and every choice takes us closer to or away from Him `28:00`.
- **How the mind tricks devotees** `30:00`:
  - During service it says, "you're not studying or chanting properly — what's the use of service?"
  - During chanting it says, "you're not doing service."
  - It complains, "I don't get time to study," and when time comes it says, "you didn't sleep enough last night."
  - The mind always thinks of what it lacks and takes the path of least resistance.
- Do what is at hand: when chanting, just chant; use whatever time we get for scripture. Showing sincerity, Kṛṣṇa gives the intelligence to make more time, or even changes our circumstances `30:00`.
- Focus on what we can change; Kṛṣṇa helps with — or changes — what we cannot. E.g. we cannot guarantee all sixteen rounds will be attentive, but we can try to chant _this_ mantra attentively; hearing this class attentively, month after month, builds deep, systematic understanding — one step at a time `32:00`.

#### D — The mind denies realities [32:00 – 38:00]

- The mind refuses to acknowledge that the problem is the mind itself. Three falsities `32:00 – 34:00`:
  1. Blaming Kṛṣṇa — "Kṛṣṇa doesn't care; He's enjoying pastimes while I struggle."
  2. Blaming the process — "chanting doesn't work; _bhakti_ isn't powerful enough."
  3. Blaming oneself — "I'm too fallen; there is no hope for me."
- The reality: Kṛṣṇa loves us and waits in our heart for lifetimes; _bhakti_ is supremely potent; no one is so fallen that _bhakti_ cannot elevate them `34:00`.
- **False ego and perverse false ego** `34:00`:
  - "I am so great" — ordinary false ego.
  - "I am so fallen" — perverse false ego.
  - Both are I-centred; true ego is Kṛṣṇa-centred: "I am a servant of Kṛṣṇa; let me serve Kṛṣṇa."
- The teacher cites the _Bhāgavatam_'s two _brāhmaṇas_ — one in the Seventh Canto, and the _avantī_ _brāhmaṇa_ described by Lord Kṛṣṇa to Uddhava in the Eleventh Canto, who concludes that the cause of all his problems is the mind `34:00 – 36:00`.
- The ultimate denial `36:00`:
  - The mind denies that material existence itself is misery and must be left.
  - It accepts in theory that we will die, but says "not today or tomorrow"; yet death can come at any time, so what is most important should be made most urgent.
  - Remembering death is not meant to make us paranoid (about a bomb going off as we walk), but to raise consciousness from the material to the spiritual and impel serious practice `36:00 – 38:00`.

#### Catching the mind's tricks [38:00]

- The mind has misled us like this for many lifetimes; recognising it brings the conviction "enough is enough — I will not be fooled."
- The more attentively we chant, study philosophy and rise to goodness and transcendence, the more readily we catch the mind as it plays its tricks.
- Control begins with recognising that the mind is going out of control.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The mind magnifies problems, imagines pleasures, neglects present opportunities and denies realities — about Kṛṣṇa, the process, ourselves and death; naming each trick as it happens is the first step to mastering it. [16:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### Following the residents of Vraja; Prabhupāda as our model [38:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** Having established why the mind must be controlled, Prabhupāda explains the process: devotional service, which this verse recommends practising in Vṛndāvana under the guidance of its residents `38:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Rāgānugā_ and _rāgātmikā_ — terms the verse points toward at one level; to be discussed elaborately in _Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu_, also by Rūpa Gosvāmī `38:00 – 40:00`.
- _Svarūpa_ — one's spiritual form and identity, understood at very advanced stages `40:00`.

#### Models for advancement [40:00 – 42:00]

- To make tangible advancement we need models — devotees absorbed in love for Kṛṣṇa on whom we model ourselves.
- At very advanced stages, a devotee understands their _svarūpa_ and serves under a leader within a group (the teacher gives the example of a _sakhī_ as leader), meditating on and assimilating her mood of service.
- These stages are described by the _ācāryas_ in many books, but later _ācāryas_ have emphasised that we cannot rush prematurely into _rāga-mārga_; we focus on practising _vaidhī_ (regulated) devotion `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### Did Prabhupāda give the esoteric teachings? [42:00 – 44:00]

- Some feel Śrīla Prabhupāda did not speak much about the advanced pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.
- The teacher answers that Prabhupāda gave the process by which those teachings unfold through gradual advancement.
- The real Gauḍīya mood is not just remembering Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa, but remembering them in a way that pleases them; meditating prematurely on intimate pastimes can degrade us.
- What distinguishes the Vraja-vāsīs is selfless service: the _gopīs_ were ready to go to hell for all eternity to relieve Kṛṣṇa's headache for one moment `42:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's selfless sacrifice [44:00 – 46:00]

- Many pious people aspire to retire to Vṛndāvana to raise their chances of remembering Kṛṣṇa at death; few find the courage.
- Prabhupāda had already done so — left home and lived in Vṛndāvana as a _sannyāsī_ — and could have attained perfection there.
- Yet, like the _gopīs_ who left their homes when Kṛṣṇa called, he took his spiritual master's instruction to preach in English all over the world, went to America at 69 and preached tirelessly for about twelve years.
- His mood of selfless sacrifice is the essence of Vṛndāvana; internalising it brings rapid advancement.
- Prabhupāda said we enter the intimate mood of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa's service by being their selfless servants — hence assisting the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement in various services is itself advancement `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### Purification first [46:00 – 48:00]

- We cannot enter advanced pastimes merely by contemplating them; the right mood comes by purification.
- Prabhupāda gave preaching, _sādhana_, temples and association; following this systematically, advanced stages manifest progressively.
- Prabhupāda is the Vraja-vāsī who is our model. For his followers, his mercy comes through:
  1. the spiritual master, who represents him;
  2. his books;
  3. ISKCON's managerial structure, the GBC.
- Through Prabhupāda comes the mercy of the previous _ācāryas_, Caitanya Mahāprabhu and ultimately Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
We follow the Vraja-vāsīs not by prematurely meditating on intimate pastimes but by imbibing their selfless service — exemplified for us by Śrīla Prabhupāda, who left Vṛndāvana to serve his guru's order. [40:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Living in Vṛndāvana: a place and a mood [48:00 – 52:00]

**Context.** Does Rūpa Gosvāmī mean that everyone should drop everything and move to Vṛndāvana? The teacher says no `48:00`.

#### Jīva Gosvāmī's three students [48:00 – 50:00]

- Rūpa Gosvāmī taught Jīva Gosvāmī, his foremost intellectual successor.
- Jīva Gosvāmī trained three _śikṣā_ disciples from the Bengal–Orissa region — Śrīnivāsa Ācārya, Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura and Śyāmānanda Paṇḍita.
- Though deeply satisfied studying in Vṛndāvana and pained to leave their teacher, they were told to go back and preach; this led to a tremendous expansion of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism there, and a legacy of preachers.
- This was not defiance of the verse, because Vṛndāvana is not just a place; it is also a mood `50:00`.

#### Carrying Vṛndāvana [50:00 – 52:00]

- When Prabhupāda went to America in 1965, he did not leave Vṛndāvana — he carried it in his heart and replicated it in every centre.
- "We can take a devotee out of Vṛndāvana, but we cannot take Vṛndāvana out of a devotee."
- The Vraja-vāsīs fulfil Kṛṣṇa's specific desires within His pastimes; our mission, given through Caitanya Mahāprabhu, is to spread the holy name to every town and village and learn to love Kṛṣṇa.
- We live in Vraja by internalising selfless service, dedicating ourselves to Kṛṣṇa's mission, and living as exemplars in family, community and society — spreading the mood of Vṛndāvana wherever we are `50:00 – 52:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Residing in Vraja means carrying its mood of selfless service into whatever place Kṛṣṇa's mission puts us; a devotee can be taken out of Vṛndāvana, but Vṛndāvana cannot be taken out of a devotee. [48:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Four progressive stages of absorption [52:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** Returning to the verse's "progressively engage the tongue, then the mind," the teacher presents four stages elaborated by Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura `52:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

1. _Śravaṇa-daśā_ — the stage of hearing the philosophy; the most important for us now, in the neophyte stage `52:00`.
2. _Varaṇa-daśā_ — by constant hearing one internalises and accepts what was heard `52:00 – 54:00`.
3. _Smaraṇa-daśā_ — remembrance becomes uninterrupted and habitual, concentrating into meditation `54:00`.
4. _Sampatti-daśā_ — perfection: one clearly understands one's relationship with Kṛṣṇa, coming to know who one is in the spiritual world and returning to one's _svarūpa_ `54:00`.

#### Begin with hearing [52:00 – 56:00]

- Prabhupāda's movement offers _Bhāgavatam_ classes, outreach programmes and now, through the internet, abundant opportunity to hear wherever we are.
- We must give hearing an adequately important priority: what we hear goes inside and stays, gradually making the subtle body Kṛṣṇa conscious.
- If we practise the given process diligently, these exalted levels will be revealed progressively.
- The class closes with the verse's summary: progressively engage the tongue and mind under the guidance of exalted devotees, imbibe the mood of Vraja, and thereby attain perfection `54:00 – 56:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Absorption grows in stages — hearing, acceptance, constant remembrance, perfection — and all of it begins with giving regular hearing a real priority in our lives. [52:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
