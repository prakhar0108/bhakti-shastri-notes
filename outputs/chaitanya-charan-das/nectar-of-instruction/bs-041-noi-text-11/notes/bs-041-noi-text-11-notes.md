### Text 11: Rādhā-kuṇḍa, as dear as Rādhā [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:noi/11 -->

**Context.** The teacher goes through the verse's words and Prabhupāda's translation, then gives an overview of the text `00:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Preyasī_ — a lover; among all Kṛṣṇa's lovers, Rādhā is the topmost `00:00`.
- Just as Rādhā is the topmost of the _gopīs_, her _kuṇḍa_ is the topmost of places `00:00 – 02:00`.
- Even for the most advanced devotees it is difficult to obtain, what to speak of those just beginning devotional service `02:00`.
- _Snāna_ — bathing; bathing even once arouses _prema_, love of Godhead `02:00`.

#### The verse's points [02:00 – 04:00]

- The theme is _serving_ Rādhā-kuṇḍa — not merely bathing or living there.
- It belongs to Rādhārāṇī, the most beloved _gopī_, and is as dear to Kṛṣṇa as she is.
- Its glories are unlimited and can scarcely be comprehended by us.
- It is very rarely attained.

### Seeing the dhāma through service [04:00 – 10:00]

**Context.** How do we make sense of scripture's glorification of such places? Through service `04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Vimukha_ — turned away from Kṛṣṇa's face; though He is right next to us in the heart, we have turned away `06:00`.
- _Unmukha_ — turned toward Him `06:00`.
- _Sphūrti_ — spontaneous remembrance that Kṛṣṇa gives by His own free will `06:00`.

#### Not sightseeing but purification [04:00]

- Going to a holy place is not for sightseeing but for purification; when we are purified, spiritual vision awakens and we can see.
- Until that revelation comes, we go to the _dhāma_ primarily for purification, with an attitude of service.

#### Service unglues our vision [04:00 – 06:00]

- The teacher cites a Purāṇic verse quoted in _Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu_: Kṛṣṇa is not perceivable by the gross senses, but when we serve Him, beginning with the tongue, He reveals Himself.
- Our vision is glued and locked to matter; it is freed by serving Kṛṣṇa with our material senses, mind, intelligence and talents, investing as much of our heart as possible.
- When Kṛṣṇa sees us sincerely turning toward Him, He reveals Himself and gives spontaneous remembrance; consciousness becomes unlocked from matter `06:00`.

#### When Rādhā-kuṇḍa becomes naraka [06:00 – 10:00]

- Some think merely going to Rādhā-kuṇḍa makes them advanced, and others praise them for living in "the most sacred place."
- The teacher cites a Ṭhākura of the Gauḍīya line who did not entertain such pretension: for those who live there without controlling their senses, especially if they indulge in immoral activities, Rādhā-kuṇḍa can become _naraka_ (hell) `08:00`.
- A graded seriousness `08:00`:
  1. Sinful activities anywhere are bad.
  2. Sinful activities in a holy place are worse.
  3. Sinful activities in a holy place while pretending to be an advanced devotee are monstrously sinful, with grave results.
- As _sādhakas_ our greater fear is not karmic reaction but displeasing Kṛṣṇa, our spiritual master and the Vaiṣṇavas — yet we must remember that actions have consequences.
- This warning jolts us out of the "devotional fantasy" that just going to Rādhā-kuṇḍa will be wonderful `08:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Rādhā-kuṇḍa is approached not by sightseeing or by the prestige of living there, but by service that turns us toward Kṛṣṇa; without proper consciousness, the same place can become hell. [04:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Against imitating the pastimes [10:00 – 18:00]

**Context.** This warning is not theoretical: some have pervertedly claimed that Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa's pastimes are not only to be contemplated at the most advanced stage, but to be enacted by themselves `10:00`.

#### Two approaches to matter [10:00]

- The approach of analysis separates matter from spirit — rejecting matter and focusing on spirit through meditation and discrimination.
- _Bhakti_ uses matter in the service of spirit; by such service we are purified and elevated.
- But not all matter can be used in Kṛṣṇa's service — some entangles us. As one of the Gosvāmīs teaches, renunciation is needed so that we can rightly engage things in Kṛṣṇa's service; hence regulative principles remain `10:00`.

#### Left-hand tantra [10:00 – 14:00]

- The teacher distinguishes right-hand _tantra_ (such as deity worship, considered auspicious) from left-hand _tantra_ (widely considered inauspicious); BG chapter 16 speaks of unclean worship `12:00`.
- In deity worship Kṛṣṇa manifests through matter and lets us serve Him; left-hand _tantra_ distorts this into the idea that _any_ matter can be used, which the Vaiṣṇava _ācāryas_ have thoroughly rejected.
- Its idea is that creation is a dance of Śiva (spirit) and Śakti (matter) and that realisation comes through their "union" in sexual activity — a distorted idea that turns sinful acts into "religion" `12:00 – 14:00`.
- At times this crept into Vaiṣṇavism, with people claiming that Kṛṣṇa and Rādhārāṇī entered them and their companions to perform the _rāsa_; Prabhupāda said this is not _rāsa_ but a degraded conception `14:00`.

#### Pastime dramas and Prabhupāda's caution [14:00 – 18:00]

- In traditional dramas, devotionally trained small children (usually boys, sometimes young girls) play Rādhā, Kṛṣṇa and the _gopīs_; the dancing is purely aesthetic, nothing immoral is allowed, and viewers are uplifted by remembering Kṛṣṇa.
- In ISKCON's early days, devotees who saw this in Vṛndāvana staged such a drama in America (San Francisco or Los Angeles). Prabhupāda watched quietly; the next morning in class he said this should not be done in his society — it can take us in the wrong direction `16:00`.
- We do not want imitation of the exalted pastimes; our _ācāryas_ have cautioned against it time and again.
- Rādhā-kuṇḍa has tremendous potency but requires tremendous purity. Prabhupāda once even said "don't bathe" when devotees were frivolously swimming and splashing — so careful was he that even casual play there is inappropriate `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti engages matter in Kṛṣṇa's service under regulation; any attempt to enact or imitate Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa's intimate pastimes is a deviation the ācāryas and Prabhupāda firmly rejected, and Rādhā-kuṇḍa demands purity, not frivolity. [10:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Weighing Prabhupāda's words and scripture's promises [16:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** Does Prabhupāda's "don't bathe" mean a permanent ban? And does the verse mean a single bath automatically gives love of Kṛṣṇa? `16:00 – 18:00`

#### A hierarchy within Prabhupāda's words [16:00 – 18:00]

- As explained in the front matter of an ISKCON scholarly work, Prabhupāda's words come to us in four forms, from most universal to most context-dependent:
  1. **Purports** — written with time and thought; most universal; he considered them his most important legacy.
  2. **Lectures** — more contextual, spoken to those present.
  3. **Conversations** — with groups of people at particular times.
  4. **Letters** — most context-dependent, written to particular persons.
- He told devotees in one conversation not to bathe, but repeatedly glorified Rādhā-kuṇḍa in purports, as do scripture and _sādhus_. So we should not absolutise one statement into a ban — yet we take it seriously: we cannot be casual about something so precious `18:00`.

#### Many principles apply at once [18:00 – 22:00]

- Every scriptural statement has value and potency, but in real life many principles act together.
- **Law and killing** — killing is murder, yet killing an enemy soldier in war may win an award; killing a thief while protecting one's home, or killing someone attacking a woman's chastity, are very different situations. Applying a law to reality takes circumstances and other laws into account `20:00`.
- Likewise, the glories of holy places are real, but other factors also operate.

#### The climax of a process [22:00 – 24:00]

- If one bath alone gave love of Kṛṣṇa, Rūpa Gosvāmī need not have written the whole book — he could have repeated "go and bathe" eleven times.
- Instead he gives a series of profound texts that purify us progressively; when we bathe in Rādhā-kuṇḍa after that preparation, we are energised and "electrified" with love for Kṛṣṇa.
- The promise is no exaggeration, but it must not become a bargaining chip ("now I must get love"); a demanding attitude would bury devotion in the graveyard. A submissive, begging attitude coupled with systematic purification lets us relish Rādhā-kuṇḍa's glories `22:00 – 24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Scripture's promises are true but work alongside other principles: the promise of love from one bath sits at the climax of a whole process of purification, and is received with a begging, not demanding, heart. [16:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Mādhurya: one Lord as two, sharing completeness [24:00 – 42:00]

**Context.** Rādhā-kuṇḍa is exalted because Rādhārāṇī is; she is exalted because her love is the highest. How is that love entirely different from worldly sensuality and immorality? `24:00`

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Līlā_ — pastimes, play, or love-play; entirely voluntary and performed for the sake of joy `34:00`.
- _Ātmārāma_ — self-satisfied; Kṛṣṇa is already full of joy `36:00`.
- _Yogamāyā_ — arranges things in the spiritual world so that the sweetness of love is reciprocated `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### The original and the reflection [24:00 – 28:00]

- Seeing Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa as devotee and Lord, we focus on the devotional interactions, not on what we may mistake as sensual.
- What exists here exists in its original, pure form in the spiritual world — though much here is distorted and has no place there.
- The world's religious traditions warn that male–female attraction is dangerous and should be regulated through marriage, but do not explain where that attraction comes from; it is left as a fiat `26:00`.
- The Vedic tradition reveals the spiritual reality in its splendour: our relationships here exist there in original, pure form `28:00`.

#### One Lord as two [28:00 – 30:00]

- God manifests in male form as Kṛṣṇa; Rādhārāṇī is God Himself manifesting in female form. They are one person who become two to reciprocate love.
- Here, a man feels incomplete without a woman and vice versa; but two incompletes together become "incomplete squared" — only union with the supreme whole, Kṛṣṇa, makes us complete.
- Kṛṣṇa's relationships come not from incompleteness but from a desire to share His completeness, like a happy person who acts not to become happy but to share happiness `30:00`.

#### Frozen perfection? [30:00 – 34:00]

- Why would a perfect God do anything at all? Some Western, especially Greek, philosophers concluded God must be a "frozen perfection."
- **The painting** — as a painting approaches perfection, one dab less or one dab more spoils it; it must be frozen at that point. So, they reasoned, a perfect God cannot act, since we act only because we want something.
- The teacher calls this theoretically neat but practically unsound and emotionally unappealing — a God forever standing in a perfect pose `32:00`.
- Scripture sees God's perfection as the perfection of love, which involves reciprocation and dynamism. In love "everything else freezes" (as when two people in a film are lost in each other's eyes); even more, in Goloka everything _contributes_ to intensifying the love `32:00 – 34:00`.

#### Recreation or dancing? [34:00 – 38:00]

- Rāmānujācārya, in his _Śrī-bhāṣya_ commentary on the _Vedānta-sūtra_, explains that the Absolute acts as recreation — like someone who, after work, plays without obligation `34:00 – 36:00`.
- Looking deeper, this example is good but not perfect: a person plays _in order to_ enjoy, but the Absolute is already full of joy.
- A better example is **dancing**: a person overflowing with happiness jumps up and dances — dancing is the result of happiness, not its purpose. Kṛṣṇa's _līlā_ is likewise the overflow of His joy `36:00`.

#### Selfless love, arranged by yogamāyā [38:00 – 42:00]

- Kṛṣṇa relishes with His own expansion, Rādhārāṇī; the _gopīs_ are her expansions — of course with distinctions in _tattva_ — all meant for Kṛṣṇa's pastimes `38:00`.
- This has nothing to do with body-based worldly love; it is the Absolute relishing transcendentally, out of selfless love.
- By _yogamāyā_, Kṛṣṇa can forget His divinity and the _gopīs_ can forget He is God, for the sake of love; their awareness of His Godhood recedes to the background, and the foreground is intense, intolerable longing to be with Him `40:00`.
- The perfection of love is that everything detracting from love is forgotten — even Kṛṣṇa's Godhood — so that love climaxes again and again for all eternity `40:00 – 42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are one Lord become two, not to fill a lack but to share completeness; their līlā is the dance of overflowing joy, in which yogamāyā lets even Godhood be forgotten so that love can reign. [24:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### The real mango and the parakīya mood [42:00 – 54:00]

**Context.** Whatever attracts us in this world is a shadow and shallow spark of the vibrant original. Understanding this lets us give up the reflection willingly `42:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Svakīya_ — "one's own": love within marriage, carrying rights and possession `44:00 – 46:00`.
- _Parakīya_ — love as paramours, where everything else is subordinated so that love is intensified `46:00`.

#### The reflected mango [42:00 – 44:00]

- If someone simply says "don't eat that mango," we call them a killjoy.
- But if they explain it is only a reflection in a river, and point to the real mango on a tree on the bank, we gladly climb the tree; chasing the reflection only breaks our head.
- Male–female attraction within the sacred limits of _dharma_ is permitted, but it is not the real mango; the original is the devotee–Lord attraction, expressed highest when the Lord appears in male form and the devotee in female form. This reality is what Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu came to give `44:00`.

#### Why Rādhā's love is highest [44:00 – 48:00]

- Among conjugal loves — Lakṣmī for Nārāyaṇa, Sītā for Rāma, Rukmiṇī for the Lord of Dvārakā — all are supreme, but Rādhārāṇī's is highest because it is _parakīya_, not _svakīya_.
- **The married couple in a drama** — spouses who act as paramours on stage are, in reality, married. Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa eternally belong to each other (one Lord as two), so there is nothing immoral; in Goloka they act as paramours for the sake of intensifying the _līlā_ `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Giving up even the good [48:00 – 52:00]

- We readily understand giving up the bad for God; pure love also gives up the _good_ for God — which does not mean descending to bad, but rising above the good to the godly.
- Kṛṣṇa says in the Gītā to give up everything for His sake — even _dharma_ `48:00 – 50:00`.
- For a woman chastity is considered most prized; the _gopīs_ appear ready to give even that up — though in truth they are supremely chaste to Kṛṣṇa. They give up lower _dharma_ for higher _dharma_, demonstrating the purity of their love `50:00`.
- In the spiritual world love reigns supreme: Kṛṣṇa conceals His divinity and the _gopīs_ sacrifice their chastity so that love may reign — shown in many pastimes in Vṛndāvana and especially at Rādhā-kuṇḍa `50:00`.

#### Obstacles that reveal strength [52:00 – 54:00]

- There are no anti-devotional forces in the spiritual world; even obstacles exist to intensify love.
- **The shackled elephant** — an elephant chained to a big tree, pulling with all its might toward something attractive, shakes the whole tree: it realises its strength internally, and others see it externally.
- Likewise, obstacles let the _gopīs_ realise how great their love is, and let the world see how selfless it is.
- This love is the sacred gift Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu brought, Prabhupāda gave through the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement, and Rūpa Gosvāmī points us to `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Worldly attraction is the reflected mango; the real one is the Lord–devotee love of Goloka, where the parakīya setting and its obstacles reveal a love ready to give up everything — even the good — for Kṛṣṇa. [42:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>

### Who can serve at Rādhā-kuṇḍa [54:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** The teacher closes with the remaining purport, citing the previous _ācārya_ quoted there `54:00`.

#### The qualifications [54:00]

- Those interested in advanced devotional service, following in the wake of the _sakhīs_ and _mañjarīs_ — devotees at an exalted level of selfless love.
- This love is very difficult to understand, but becomes understandable under the protection of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who through Rūpa Gosvāmī and the _paramparā_ has given a pathway.
- Most importantly, they should be eager to go back to Godhead — one content with the reflected mango will not look for the real one.
- Even great devotees such as Nārada find Rādhā-kuṇḍa difficult to attain; only the greatly fortunate attain it, and that attainment is spiritual.

#### How to serve it [56:00]

- Bathe there as many times as possible — if we get an opportunity, we go and bathe — but more importantly live the rest of our lives so as to cultivate the right mood that lets us benefit.
- Give up material conceptions — not only "I am the body," but also thinking Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa's pastimes are material.
- In the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement we serve the spiritual master and the _guru-paramparā_, preparing our consciousness for purification and return to the spiritual world.
- The result: pure, selfless love of Kṛṣṇa in the wake of the _gopīs_ — attainable by bathing even once in proper consciousness — and ultimately returning to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī's shelter to serve under her, eternally happy in love with Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Rādhā-kuṇḍa is served by those who follow the gopīs' maidservants, long for the real mango, and give up material conceptions — even about the pastimes; such service, in the right consciousness, leads to Rādhārāṇī's shelter. [54:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
