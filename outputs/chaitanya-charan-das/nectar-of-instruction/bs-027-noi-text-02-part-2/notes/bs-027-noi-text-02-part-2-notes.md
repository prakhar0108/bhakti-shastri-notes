### Prajalpa: a hierarchy of speech [02:00 – 06:00]

**Context.** _Jalpa_ means talk; _prajalpa_ means speech that is useless, harmful or detrimental — similar to the urge of speech in Text 1 `00:00`, `02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prajalpa_ — not a call to become silent, but to know what talk is most productive for us as spiritualists `00:00`.

#### Bhīṣma's five levels of speech [02:00 – 04:00]

- Bhīṣma's long instructions to Yudhiṣṭhira on _rāja-dharma_ — one of the longest conversations in the Mahābhārata — include how a person should speak. In the teacher's adaptation, from highest to lowest:
  1. Speech about the Lord — glorification of the Lord.
  2. Sweet (pleasing) speech.
  3. Truthful speech.
  4. Useless speech.
  5. Blasphemous speech.
- Whatever is useless or blasphemous comes under _prajalpa_.
- Notably, sweet speech is placed above truthful speech.

#### Austerity of speech [04:00 – 06:00]

- In the seventeenth chapter of the Gītā, Kṛṣṇa describes austerity of speech, including speech that does not agitate others.
- By such austerities our speaking power is not dissipated and can be used more and more for Kṛṣṇa's sake.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prajalpa is not "speaking" but speaking wrongly; the ideal is a ladder — glorifying the Lord at the top, then sweet and truthful speech — while useless and blasphemous talk dissipates the power meant for Kṛṣṇa. [02:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### Blasphemy and rumour about devotees [06:00 – 20:00]

**Context.** Since the focus is _bhakti_, the teacher focuses on talk among devotees that harms devotion, in two broad categories: blasphemous and rumour-based `06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Blasphemous speech_ — speaking ill of devotees with the intention to malign them; whether it is true or false is not the point — the intent makes it offensive, a "mad elephant" offence that can destroy one's devotion `06:00`.
- _Rumour-based speech_ — spreading news about devotees who have slipped from moral standards `06:00`.

#### The wounded warrior [06:00 – 10:00]

- A warrior wounded in battle should be sent to the hospital; sending him to jail is not compassion but a kind of violence.
- All devotees, however advanced externally, have challenges; very few come intending to be hypocritical — Vaiṣṇava standards are so high that the material world offers better prospects for anyone who just wants to enjoy.
- If someone falls, help if we can; if we cannot, it is best not to talk about it. Talking about a fall brings no spiritual advancement; after an event, emotional impact far outweighs rational analysis, so little is learned.
- At the very least, don't be part of the rumour-mongering group; it is between that person, Kṛṣṇa and the authorities concerned `10:00`.

#### Technology spreads gossip [10:00 – 12:00]

- One person posts or mails something and it reaches thousands worldwide, most of whom cannot verify it; negative news sticks in the mind and contaminates our and others' consciousness.
- We should not assume senior devotees are perfect; if devotees are discussing others' falls, move away.
- Sometimes authorised persons must speak for educational or protective purposes — that is different; even then, speak so that it does not make it harder for the devotee to stay integrated in devotional service.

#### Prabhupāda's anger at the news-spreader [12:00 – 14:00]

- A dynamic _sannyāsī_ leader fell and wrote about it in a letter; a devotee who read it spread the news among everyone.
- When Prabhupāda came to know, he was more angry with the devotee who spread the news than with the one who had fallen.
- Basic human sensitivity: everyone needs a basic level of respect; we should not dismiss such needs as "false ego." If the dirty things in our house were displayed to the world, we would feel bad.
- This does not mean sweeping problems under the carpet: standards must be kept, and those who cannot follow them must make changes. But talking to others helps no one and hurts those already struggling.

#### Grievance websites and the purpose of coming [14:00 – 16:00]

- Some devotees with grievances against local authorities or the GBC vent through websites and mails.
- Yet each of us has seen that when we practise, we are purified — lust, greed and anger decrease, attraction to Kṛṣṇa increases. If the process works for me, let it work and help it work for others.
- Two concerns must be balanced: maintaining proper standards, and being compassionate and encouraging; leaders must deal with problems sensitively yet faithfully to the tradition. But behind-the-back talk hurts a community's spiritual atmosphere like few other things.

#### Kṛṣṇa gives what we want — and so does ISKCON [16:00 – 18:00]

- Kṛṣṇa gives what we desire, and to a large extent so does ISKCON:
  - **Pride** — stay a few years and one becomes a "senior devotee" whom people honour.
  - **Power** — devotees submit to authorities more than employees to bosses.
  - **Wealth** — fundraising puts money in one's hands, which can be misappropriated.
  - **Controversy** — there is more controversy in ISKCON than in the rest of the world, if that is what one seeks.
- Then I will be in the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement but "controversy conscious," defeating the purpose for which I came `18:00`.

#### What to talk about instead [18:00 – 20:00]

- Within the devotee community there is so much to discuss: what we have heard and read, and how to improve our services.
- If we must speak about someone's problem, do so prayerfully and cautiously, giving proper background and consciousness; otherwise we injure devotees' devotion, our own, and the community's atmosphere.
- "Spiritual energy" is not a fluid in the body; it is our enthusiasm to practise devotional service, which such talk saps.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Fallen devotees need the hospital, not the jail: help if you can, stay silent if you can't, and never join the rumour circuit — otherwise we come to ISKCON for Kṛṣṇa and leave controversy conscious. [06:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Falls do not disprove the process [20:00 – 32:00]

**Context.** The teacher addresses the shock of hearing that even prominent devotees fall, and why that does not undermine the process `20:00`.

#### Nothing new [20:00 – 22:00]

- As long as the material world exists, difficulties and falls are possible: "Don't be surprised at who falls; be surprised at who stays."
- From his series on the history of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism, the teacher notes that Advaita Ācārya — an intimate associate and incarnation of the Lord — had sons who became _māyāvādīs_. The soul has free will and can use it as it wants.
- Those who want to be purified by the process will be purified; those careless in following it will have difficulties.

#### The epidemic metaphor [22:00 – 26:00]

- The world is an epidemic-afflicted area; the germs are lust, anger, greed, envy, pride and illusion.
- Kṛṣṇa consciousness raises our immunity; remembrance of Kṛṣṇa is both prophylactic (prevents infection) and antiseptic (cures it).
- In an epidemic ward, a newly joined compounder and a decorated top surgeon who has saved lakhs of lives are equally vulnerable if they neglect precautions — even a top doctor can be fatally infected.
- **Limit of the metaphor:** germs come uninvited, but we often actively invite lust and greed by imagining things; so a fall involves misuse of free will and moral responsibility, unlike sickness `24:00`.
- **The point of the metaphor:** the potency of the process is not that anyone stays immune whatever he does, but that whoever adopts it stays immune and whoever neglects it loses immunity — top person or bottom person `24:00 – 26:00`.
- Where there is Kṛṣṇa there cannot be Māyā; conversely, without light there will be darkness `26:00`.

#### Talking about falls is itself Kṛṣṇa unconsciousness [28:00 – 32:00]

- When we talk about others' falls we are simply being Kṛṣṇa unconscious ourselves.
- Find the subjects and the association that nourish devotion; offer respects from a distance to devotees whose talk damages faith, and move away.
- If something heard online troubles us, ask a responsible senior devotee what actually happened — but choose carefully whom to ask, since not everyone gives responsible answers.
- A fall does not prove the process wrong; conversely it confirms it — even a big person who is not Kṛṣṇa conscious will have difficulties.
- Discussing such matters with uninformed people gives not tidbits of information but "little bits of poison."
- In every temple and community there are seriously Kṛṣṇa conscious devotees; associate with them and grow `30:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The process works like immunity: it protects whoever follows it and no one who neglects it, however senior — so a fall confirms rather than refutes Kṛṣṇa consciousness, and gossiping about it only makes us Kṛṣṇa unconscious. [20:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Niyamāgraha: rules as roads, principles as the compass [30:00 – 36:00]

**Context.** The fourth item: _niyama_ means rule; _āgraha_ means to insist on without understanding the purpose, and _agraha_ means to neglect `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Niyama-āgraha_ — fanatical insistence on the letter of rules without understanding their purpose `30:00`.
- _Niyama-agraha_ — whimsically neglecting or rejecting rules `52:00`.

#### The defining metaphor [32:00 – 34:00]

- Principles are like a compass; rules are like roads. Generally the road is the best way to travel in the compass's direction.
- **Principle:** a scriptural statement the teacher cites says all rules and regulations are subordinate to one principle — always remember Kṛṣṇa, never forget Him.
- **Rule:** chanting sixteen rounds on beads, audibly.
- **Landslide:** if the road north is blocked, trying single-handedly to clear the landslide leaves one stuck forever; one finds another road or even goes cross-country, while still moving toward the destination.

#### Exceptions in practice [34:00]

- A devotee too weak to chant loudly may be allowed to chant softly.
- One who becomes unable to speak can chant in the mind and is not breaking the vow.
- The rule and the principle are both important, but the principle is more important; rules vary with time, place and circumstance.

#### Temples and storefronts [34:00 – 36:00]

- The principle of a temple: a place where the Lord is glorified and His glories shared with others.
- Traditional rules — a _śikhara_ on top, deities facing a particular direction (e.g. east, so that the rising sun-god gets the Lord's _darśana_ and the day begins auspiciously) — help create a potent atmosphere, but are not paramount.
- Prabhupāda's early storefront temples had none of these; they were simply accessible places where the hippies, who would not come uptown, could glorify Kṛṣṇa.
- _Niyamāgraha_ would be to look at such vibrant temples and complain "this is not in that direction, that is not like that" `36:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Rules are roads and principles are the compass: normally the road is best, but when it is blocked we adjust the route — never the destination of remembering Kṛṣṇa. [32:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Niyama-āgraha: when preference becomes fanaticism [36:00 – 52:00]

**Context.** Insisting on rules without understanding facts and purpose makes us judgmental and can be very detrimental `36:00`.

#### Scriptural precedents for adjustment [36:00 – 38:00]

- A phrase recurs at significant places in the Bhāgavatam. When great sages came to Bhīṣma, he was too weak to offer the customary worship, so, knowing the principles of _dharma_, he offered it mentally — and it was accepted.
- In another instance, one who was meant to worship the Lord but was alone in the forest without deities improvised, and that too was accepted.
- Prabhupāda explains in the purports that things must be adjusted according to circumstance, and the _ācārya_ knows how — as he did in building temples.

#### Birthright brāhmaṇas [38:00 – 42:00]

- Respecting _brāhmaṇas_ is vital to Vedic culture because they are connected with Kṛṣṇa and share spiritual knowledge.
- But _brāhmaṇas_ who demanded respect by birth without brahminical qualities, knowledge or practice distorted the culture; birthright as the determiner of all privileges "messed up" Vedic culture.
- In the _bhakti_ tradition many topmost saints came from non-brahminical backgrounds, even outside the _varṇāśrama_ fold, and were recognised for their devotion — often facing persecution.
- As people of lower castes, feeling neglected, converted to other religions, such _brāhmaṇas_ did not care. A _brāhmaṇa_ is meant to take food but also give knowledge; one who only takes is irresponsible.

#### Principle versus preference [42:00 – 46:00]

- **Marking books:** some devotees say Prabhupāda's books are like deities and must not be underlined; others say marking helps them read, remember and recollect. There is no absolute standard here.
- Matters of principle differ from matters of preference; making preferences absolute makes us fanatical. Not everything has a rule — Kṛṣṇa has given us intelligence, which we receive through the _guru_, to make such decisions.
- **Temple worship:** standards exist, but a visitor who says "in my temple it was not like this" forgets time, place and circumstance; the Lord is there and is being worshipped. Standardisation, where authorised, should be done sensitively.
- **Three chapatis:** if Prabhupāda once said gurukula children get three chapatis, rigidly refusing a fourth ignores that children differ in activity and build `44:00 – 46:00`.

#### Core and periphery [46:00 – 50:00]

- Every tradition has an unchanging core and a periphery that must change, or the tradition cannot survive. We must know what to _adopt_ (take and present as it is) and what to _adapt_ (present according to time, place and circumstance).
- Prabhupāda did many things differently from his predecessor in externals, yet changed nothing essential.
- **Dress:** in India his appearance commanded respect in line with people's notion of a _sādhu_; in America, ordinary Western clothes would have gone unnoticed, whereas his distinctive dress attracted the hippies `48:00`.
- **Teaching the Gītā today:** the Gītā has no elaborate arguments for the existence of God or karma, because these were implied in Arjuna's culture; today a teacher must first establish that God, the soul and karma exist and that scripture is God's word — often most of a Gītā course prepares people to understand the Gītā `48:00 – 50:00`.

#### Imposing standards across cultures [50:00 – 52:00]

- Devotee etiquette is to bow down to senior devotees. But where slavery once existed and slaves had to bow before masters, public bowing may evoke that memory; to avoid a negative perception, things may need to change in public.
- Such adjustments are for Kṛṣṇa and advanced devotees to decide; imposing without understanding the situation creates unnecessary conflict.
- **Strictness versus fanaticism:** be strict with ourselves, but fanaticism is turning preferences into principles and leaving no room for individual choice within the broad principles; it can ruin Kṛṣṇa consciousness for ourselves and others.
- Prabhupāda's very ability to bring people from all over the world to Kṛṣṇa shows his potency to adjust to time, place and circumstance while gradually moving them toward purity.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Fanaticism is treating a preference as a principle: keep the core unchanged, adapt the periphery thoughtfully (as the ācāryas and Prabhupāda did), and leave room for individual discretion within the broad principles. [36:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Niyama-agraha: whimsically rejecting rules [52:00 – 62:00]

**Context.** The opposite error is to dismiss any inconvenient rule as "impractical, irrelevant, unimportant" — and gradually dismiss most of Kṛṣṇa consciousness `52:00`.

#### The core must continue [52:00 – 56:00]

- Rules are important and must be followed; devotees may have different levels of commitment, but the core must be clear.
- Prabhupāda was broad in many ways, yet particular about certain things: he taught Western disciples Indian cooking and devotional dress — not really "Indian" dress but the dress of the spiritual world, though it appears Bengali or Indian in this world.
- In professional settings this may not always be possible, but within the devotee community one can try to wear devotional dress; it helps one stay steady.

#### "Devotion is a matter of the heart" [56:00 – 58:00]

- Certainly devotion is of the heart — but the heart does not exist in isolation and is affected by what surrounds it.
- Traditional dress is simple, chaste and conducive to devotional consciousness; dress that exposes or highlights the body makes us and others more bodily conscious. Anyone may chant Hare Kṛṣṇa in any dress, but some dress is more conducive.
- **Posture example:** lying down with legs raised toward a devotee while claiming "I feel very humble" is a mockery — posture affects psychology, and dress likewise affects consciousness.

#### The sari question [58:00]

- A questioner on the teacher's website asked how the sari is chaste when it reveals the midriff. The teacher's answer: don't take Bollywood as the teacher of how to wear a sari — it can turn a dress of chastity into a dress of vulgarity.
- When a culture's purpose is known, everything is adapted to it; when the purpose is neglected, everything can be abused.
- Exceptions exist both ways, but the general pattern is that certain dress encourages certain consciousness.

#### Back to the roads [60:00 – 62:00]

- Abandoning roads to go cross-country means thorns, ditches, streams, rivers and snakes; the journey becomes slower and harder, and one may never arrive.
- So: follow the rules, understand that principles are more important, recognise that rules are normally the best way to follow principles, and let exceptions be made thoughtfully, in consultation with devotees, according to time, place and circumstance.
- Kṛṣṇa says that by following, one's desire to attain Him increases — attraction to Kṛṣṇa is the essence of _bhakti_; neglecting the rules lets that attraction decrease `60:00 – 62:00`.
- _Jana-saṅga_ and _laulya_ remain for the next class `62:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Rejecting rules because they are inconvenient is like abandoning the road for a thorny cross-country route: externals shape the heart, and following the rules is what steadily increases our attraction to Kṛṣṇa. [52:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
