### The metaphor of jaundice and sugar candy [00:00 – 04:00]

<!-- vedabase:noi/7 -->

**Context.** The teacher calls Text 7 fundamental — if we understand and internalise its essence, it alone can sustain us in the process `00:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Simile_ — an explicit comparison: "He was as powerful as a lion" `00:00`.
- _Metaphor_ — an implied comparison: "His roar was leonine" `00:00`.
- _Avidyā_ — ignorance, compared in the verse to bile (_pitta_), the jaundice affecting the tongue `00:00 – 02:00`.

#### How the metaphor works [02:00]

- When the tongue is afflicted by jaundice, sweet things cannot be tasted or relished.
- The Āyurvedic treatment for jaundice is to keep taking sugar candy daily and respectfully; the very sweet thing acts as the cure.
- As the patient is cured, the natural taste awakens and the disease is destroyed at the root.
- Likewise the holy name, form, character and pastimes of Kṛṣṇa are sweet like sugar candy; one afflicted by the jaundice of ignorance cannot taste them, but by carefully chanting every day the natural taste awakens and the disease is gradually destroyed at the root.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The very thing that does not taste sweet now — the holy name — is the medicine; by taking it carefully every day, the disease of ignorance is cured at the root and the natural taste for Kṛṣṇa awakens. [00:00 – 02:00]
</Callout>

### Symptom and cause: why we lack taste [04:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** The purport is summarised in a chart: from the **symptom** we go back to the **cause** `04:00`.

#### The symptom [04:00]

- No taste for Kṛṣṇa's name, form and pastimes — as the jaundiced tongue finds no taste in sugar candy.
- "I don't find Kṛṣṇa consciousness relishable" is a real problem, but the deeper problem is a misdirected heart — ignorance not just philosophically, but about the right object of love.

#### The individual cause: forgetfulness [04:00 – 08:00]

- The cause is _avidyā_ leading to forgetfulness — of our eternal position of loving and serving the Lord, in which the soul thrives eternally `04:00`.
- **How the soul falls** `06:00`:
  1. We all need happiness; if we do not find it in remembering and serving Kṛṣṇa, the hunger seeks fulfilment elsewhere.
  2. The desire to enjoy arises, and _māyā_ is right next to it — temptations within the heart (impressions of past wrongs pushing us) and outside it (the world's temptations).
  3. Indulgence strengthens those impressions, their future grip tightens, and release becomes very difficult.
  4. Caught by _māyā_, we take false shelters — wealth, sex, power and above all the body — which all let us down; ultimately the body becomes a source of misery.
- **"The history of universal defeat has not dampened the hope of imminent victory"** — everyone who sought happiness in material existence suffered and died, yet each still hopes to win `08:00`.
- We need hope, but true hope, not false hope; focusing all our energy on improving things materially under-uses our intelligence and misuses our energy `08:00`.
- **Comfort in the ocean** — the waves may sometimes be calm, but sooner or later they toss a person up and down; do our duty to keep things materially stable, but focus energy on spiritual advancement, which leads to eternal shelter through chanting `08:00 – 10:00`.

#### The collective cause: atheistic leadership [10:00 – 12:00]

- Today's leaders do not care for God; they give lip service if it wins votes.
- The teacher describes a well-known atheist politician who wrote that he could not understand people bowing to stone images, yet found a "working knowledge" of the ancient books useful for influencing people to do what he decided was good — a demoniac misappropriation of God's words, like Rāvaṇa wanting to enjoy Sītā, Lord Rāma's property `10:00`.
- People misled by such blind leaders fall into the categories Kṛṣṇa describes in Bhagavad-gītā 7.15 — those whose knowledge is stolen and the openly demoniac who oppose devotional endeavours and never surrender `12:00`.
- The overall culture of materialism and atheism bombards people and leaves them no time, energy or inclination to turn wholeheartedly to spirituality; doing something religious because it looks good is far from transformation `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Lack of taste for the holy name is only the symptom; its cause is forgetting our eternal service — individually through false shelters, and collectively through a culture shaped by atheistic leaders. [04:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### The cure and the stage beyond cure [12:00 – 20:00]

**Context.** Having found the cause, the chart turns to the cure — chanting the holy names attentively, "with care and attention" `12:00`.

#### An easy process [12:00]

- Chanting requires no yogic gymnastics or anything very difficult to do.

#### Mirror and fire [14:00 – 16:00]

- **Mirror** — dust on a mirror blocks its purpose; when the mind is cleaned, we perceive reality clearly.
- **Fire, three ways:**
  1. **Fire covered by smoke** — the soul's consciousness is covered by ignorance, as in Bhagavad-gītā 3.38; purification removes the smoke so the fire shines.
  2. **Fire as purifier** — devotional service is like a flame awakening in the heart that burns away impurities and reactions.
  3. **Fire to be extinguished** — material existence is a blazing forest fire in which we are afflicted, and chanting extinguishes it.

#### The spiritual hospital [16:00]

- The cure is available within the International Society for Krishna Consciousness, like a spiritual hospital.
- The sequence: a little faith → association of devotees → hearing → chanting and following the principles → impurities going away.
- Prabhupāda gives examples of people able to follow principles they could never follow earlier.

#### Cure versus recuperation [16:00 – 18:00]

- Any therapy has two parts: removal of the negative and restoration of the positive.
- After months of treatment for a disease like TB, the germs are controlled, but the patient is still weak and goes to a sanatorium to recuperate.
- Likewise, beyond cure lies _bhāva_: when the _anarthas_ are gone one begins to experience spiritual emotions — the awakening of dormant love of God `18:00`.
- Prabhupāda describes its symptoms: no bodily concept remains; one understands one's eternal form and relationship with Kṛṣṇa; one clearly sees Kṛṣṇa as light and _māyā_ as darkness and is undisturbed by _māyā_ `18:00`.
- Overall, the purport is a comprehensive overview of the process by which we gain access to eternal happiness `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Attentive chanting is an easy cure that cleans the mirror of the mind and extinguishes the fire of material existence; and beyond cure lies recuperation into bhāva, where dormant love of God awakens. [12:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Two kinds of happiness: graph and Gītā [18:00 – 28:00]

**Context.** The rest of the class focuses on happiness — how we must use our intelligence to persevere, giving up the lower taste for the higher `18:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Higher taste_ — happiness in connection with Kṛṣṇa `20:00`.
- _Lower taste_ — happiness from worldly things, worse still when immoral, sensual and anti-devotional `20:00`.

#### The graph of happiness versus time [20:00 – 22:00]

- **Material happiness** — some happiness in youth, but the curve keeps declining as the body's capacity to enjoy diminishes, then goes negative in sickness and old age; even in youth enjoyment is only some of the time.
- **Spiritual happiness** — starts low (happy in the temple, then the mind agitates at home), but as attraction to Kṛṣṇa grows through purification, happiness keeps rising — infinite when we connect with Kṛṣṇa in complete love.

#### "P" and "N": poison and nectar [22:00 – 24:00]

- Bhagavad-gītā 18.37–38: happiness in goodness — like spiritual happiness — tastes like **poison in the beginning and nectar in the end**.
- Happiness in passion — the nature of most material pleasures — is **nectar in the beginning and poison in the end**: addiction, complication, and suffering when temporary things are lost.
- Because we are diseased, chanting may feel like poison at first; if we persevere, the poison ends and the nectar begins.

#### Temporary senses, temporary objects [24:00 – 28:00]

- Senses are temporary and sense objects are temporary, so the pleasure of their contact is temporary — a wise person does not delight in it `24:00`.
- Most people think, "I can't enjoy because my objects aren't good enough" — a better-looking partner, a better car `26:00`.
- But even with the best objects, the capacity to enjoy is limited: even owning a five-star hotel, one cannot eat twenty-four hours a day `26:00`.
- **Painless vomiting pills** — some kings of the past, as in Egypt, feasted, had physicians give them pills to vomit, and feasted again; it terribly harmed the body and was given up — the body's capacity cannot be expanded `26:00`.

#### The soul's contact with Kṛṣṇa [28:00 – 32:00]

- Contact with the Absolute Truth gives supreme happiness; since soul and Kṛṣṇa are both eternal, that happiness is eternal `28:00`.
- The contact is not physical — touching the Deities' feet is purifying, but happiness comes through **service**; when the desire to serve unites soul and Kṛṣṇa, contact happens in consciousness `28:00`.
- In chanting there are a sound, a tongue and ears, but Kṛṣṇa is transcendental; it is the soul contacting Kṛṣṇa — if inattentive, chanting becomes mechanical, like a parrot `30:00`.
- **The sleeping soul** — the soul has no physical sleep; "sleeping" means disconnected from reality (its relationship with Kṛṣṇa) and absorbed in a dream-like pseudo-reality, active but illusorily; the sound of the holy name wakes us `30:00`.
- Chanting is not merely verbal; the heart, where the soul resides, must be involved; as the soul calls out to Kṛṣṇa the contact revives, and greater contact brings greater happiness `30:00 – 32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Material happiness is nectar turning to poison, limited by temporary senses and objects; spiritual happiness is poison turning to nectar, because it comes from the eternal soul's service-contact with the eternal Kṛṣṇa — so we must persevere through the initial bitterness. [20:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### FIT and SING: the nature of the two happinesses [32:00 – 46:00]

**Context.** Continuing the disease metaphor — jaundice not only removes the taste of sweetness but makes one feel sickly — the teacher shows how material existence is a place of misery, and gives two acronyms to make our intelligence "fit" `32:00`.

#### FIT: material enjoyment [32:00 – 34:00]

Using the example of a feast with _gulāb-jāmuns_:

- **F — Futility** — we hanker and do not get it: the feast turns out to be a rumour.
- **I — Insubstantiality** — we get it, but it does not live up to expectations: the cook spoiled the recipe.
- **T — Temporality** — we get it and enjoy it, but it ends: the sweets run out, or the stomach's capacity does.
- One of these three always happens, so material enjoyment is a doomed project; contemplating this frees us from fantasies of material happiness `34:00`.

#### SING: spiritual happiness [34:00 – 44:00]

"Sing your way to happiness":

- **S — Special** `36:00`:
  - All living beings get some material enjoyment, but only humans can experience spiritual happiness, and among them very few seek it.
  - People try to be special through fashions, hairstyles, cars, watches and phones, but material life is the same old eating and sleeping; spiritual life lets us be true trailblazers.
- **I — Increasing** `36:00 – 38:00`:
  - The more we love and serve Kṛṣṇa, the stronger our contact and the greater our happiness.
  - Material happiness is like a drop; Kṛṣṇa consciousness is an ocean of nectar — we are only at its edge, and the deeper we go, the more we get.
- **N — Non-dependent** `38:00 – 40:00`:
  - Material happiness depends on externals — from alcohol or drugs to a sports fan's team, the stadium, the broadcast, electricity and the TV set — none of which we control.
  - "Non-dependent," not "independent": dependence on Kṛṣṇa is the supreme independence, because it frees us from depending on anything else; Kṛṣṇa is always in our heart.
  - Prabhupāda went to America, glorified Kṛṣṇa wherever he went, and was happy everywhere.
- **G — Generous** `40:00 – 42:00`:
  - Spiritual happiness grows by sharing; when a devotee relishes Kṛṣṇa consciousness, others are inspired and benefit.
  - **Fifty _rasagullās_** — among twenty-five devotees each gets two; if one takes ten, others get less: material enjoyment is at someone else's expense.
  - A verse the teacher cites describes those who speak about Kṛṣṇa as bringing nectar that relieves burning hearts and auspiciousness to all who hear — the greatest welfare workers `42:00`.

#### Chanting is the most practical thing [44:00]

- "What is my chanting going to do for the world's problems? I should do something practical."
- Chanting is the most practical thing: it purifies us so we are not dragged by lust, anger and greed into harming ourselves and others; we minimise the problem and contribute to the solution.
- Attentive chanting is for our purification, for the pleasure of the spiritual master and Kṛṣṇa, and for the benefit of the world.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Material enjoyment is FIT — futile, insubstantial, temporary — while the happiness of the holy name is SING — special, increasing, non-dependent and generous — which makes chanting the most practical contribution we can make to the world. [32:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>

### Conviction, commitment and the mother-child metaphor [46:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** The intelligence can understand all this, but the mind just says "I want to enjoy"; the teacher shows how to make the intelligence stronger than the mind `46:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Conviction_ — philosophically understanding clearly that this pleasure leads to misery and that the path of poison-then-nectar is worth persevering on `46:00`.
- _Commitment_ — a vow, as at initiation: come what may, I will chant sixteen rounds every day `46:00`.

#### Quantity as impetus for quality [46:00 – 50:00]

- One may see devotees chanting inattentively and think it better to chant fewer rounds attentively.
- But the commitment to quantity is a big impetus for developing quality: if I must spend one and a half to two hours chanting every day for the rest of my life, why spend it bored? Better to struggle to be attentive and get the taste `46:00 – 48:00`.
- Quality comes two ways `48:00`:
  - by our effort — forcing ourselves to be attentive;
  - by Kṛṣṇa's mercy — sometimes we simply feel inspired.
- Inspired quality is fleeting; depending on it makes our journey erratic and sporadic — sometimes even a U-turn toward sense gratification. Steadiness comes from commitment `48:00`.

#### Both are needed [50:00]

- With a doctor, first one must be convinced one is sick and that this is the treatment; then one must commit to taking the medicine and avoiding what is forbidden.
- It is the commitment — taking the medicine — that transforms, but conviction is what makes one take it.
- So we must regularly hear classes and study philosophy; if we stop, our impetus to chant and connect with Kṛṣṇa goes down.

#### Mother, child, doctor and medicine [52:00 – 56:00]

- **Intelligence = mother**; **mind = child** — restless, changing its likes, immature `52:00`.
- **Spiritual master = doctor** — he may smile at the child and give a chocolate, but the serious discussion is with the mother; she is responsible for making the child take the medicine `52:00`.
- The spiritual master's kind words, embrace or touch are like the doctor's smile — good, but not what cures; that mercy should lead us to take shelter of the process he gives `52:00 – 54:00`.
- His philosophical and practical instructions speak to our intelligence; the **holy name is the medicine** `54:00`.
- When the mind says "I don't want to chant," the intelligence, like the mother, must be gentle but firm; slowly the disease goes and the natural taste awakens, and _bhakti_ becomes joyful `54:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Conviction from hearing philosophy makes us willing to take the medicine, and commitment to a fixed quantity makes us actually take it; the intelligence, like a mother, must gently but firmly make the mind chant until the natural taste returns. [46:00 – 56:00]
</Callout>
