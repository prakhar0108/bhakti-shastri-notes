### Saṅga: association and attachment [02:00 – 06:00]

**Context.** The teacher explains the word _saṅga_ and its two-way relationship with attachment `02:00 – 04:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Saṅga_ — has a double meaning: association, and attachment; the two meanings are related `04:00`.
- _Jana-saṅga_ — here _saṅga_ primarily means association; _jana-saṅga_ refers to the association of worldly-minded people `06:00`.

#### A symbiotic relationship [04:00 – 06:00]

- By association, desires and attachment develop; and by attachment we seek association.
- **Hostel example:** someone who arrives at a new hostel or place, feeling distressed, finds out who is like him and mixes with those people — attachment draws association.
- We want both to become spiritual: when we associate with devotees, who have spiritual desires and emotional attachment to Kṛṣṇa, and see how seriously they study scripture, worship and serve Kṛṣṇa, we too begin to feel like loving and serving Him.
- Similarly, if we have attachment to devotion, then wherever we go we will seek out devotees and try to associate with them.
- This reciprocity is the symbiotic nature of association and attachment.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Association breeds attachment and attachment seeks association — so the same cycle that binds us to worldly people can, with devotees, bind us to Kṛṣṇa. [04:00 – 06:00]
</Callout>

### What association really is: transfer of emotions [06:00 – 12:00]

**Context.** The teacher asks what exactly happens when we associate with someone `06:00`.

#### Physical proximity is not association [06:00 – 08:00]

- The teacher gives an example attributed to his spiritual master involving flies, illustrating that mere physical presence, without any capacity to understand, is not association.
- Physical proximity does generally shape and affect most people — but in itself it is not _saṅga_.
- _Saṅga_ essentially means emotional exchange — sharing and transfer of emotions.

#### Close but untouched [08:00]

- People employed at a temple who are not devotees and not inclined to become devotees may work there for 25 years; some live right next to a temple.
- They are physically near devotees, yet their hearts do not change, because their emotional world is entirely different from the devotees' — there is no intersection, so no transfer of emotions, and effectively no association.

#### The cricket final [08:00 – 10:00]

- In a hostel of cricket-mad students, when the Indian team wins the final with a six, the whole hostel erupts and everyone shares the excitement.
- This infectious effect can be positive or negative, depending on the nature of what is going on in the association.

#### Emotions carry desires [10:00 – 12:00]

- Everybody wants to be happy, and people celebrate what makes them happy; by engaging in those emotions we take on their sources.
- Emotions and desires are very similar; emotions are more like feelings that shape our aspirations. When we enjoy the emotions materialistic people enjoy, we get the desires they have.
- Such desires are:
  - _non-devotional_ — e.g. just talking about cricket and similar topics;
  - _anti-devotional_ — talk that involves breaking the regulative principles.
- **Clubs example:** golf players and others form clubs to come together over what they like. Similarly the purpose of the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement is to provide a forum where those interested in Kṛṣṇa come together and nourish their attraction — learning who Kṛṣṇa is, our relationship with Him, why and how we should love Him `12:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Association is not sharing a space but sharing emotions; whatever emotions we share, we absorb the desires behind them — which is why a forum of people attracted to Kṛṣṇa is so valuable. [06:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### First level: bad association stirs our desires [12:00 – 16:00]

**Context.** The teacher describes three levels at which bad association affects us, beginning with the least dangerous: our desires `12:00`.

#### Desires come and go [12:00]

- Sometimes desires are low; sometimes they suddenly become strong; then the surge is temporary, and the intelligence comes back online.

#### Two examples [14:00 – 16:00]

- The teacher narrates an incident in which a man passing through an area where some women were became attracted; the desire overpowered him because of that association. The incident is connected with Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, and the person was later engaged in some other service.
- **A modern example:** while chatting in a friend's room, the friend's computer is open and suddenly some nasty sites or terrible videos come up; at that moment we are carried along, and only afterwards do we come out of it.
- This is bad association: people who have such desires transmit them, and we are overcome; but after some time, when we come out of the association, our intelligence returns `16:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
At the first level, bad association produces a temporary surge of desire that overpowers us in the moment; once we step away, intelligence returns — so stepping away quickly matters. [12:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Second level: bad association damages intelligence [16:00 – 22:00]

**Context.** More serious is when the intelligence itself is damaged, as in the case of Ajāmila `16:00`.

#### Ajāmila [16:00]

- Ajāmila had good qualities; he lived in a very spiritual culture, was a _brāhmaṇa_ and was already married.
- When he saw a _śūdra_ and a woman together, his intelligence was affected.
- In such a society, for a person to take another woman and throw his own wife out of the house — despite the good advice people would surely give — shows that his intelligence had come to see the wrong as right.

#### The downward sequence [18:00]

- The teacher describes the sequence of degradation:
  1. From attachment comes lust.
  2. From lust comes anger.
  3. From anger comes _sammoha_ — illusion.
  4. From illusion comes forgetfulness — loss of memory.
  5. Then comes the destruction of intelligence (_buddhi-nāśa_).

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
When bad association damages the intelligence, we no longer merely slip — we justify the wrong as right, as Ajāmila did; the chain runs from attachment through lust, anger and illusion to the loss of intelligence. [16:00 – 18:00]
</Callout>

### Third level: bad association attacks faith [22:00 – 30:00]

**Context.** Deeper still is an attack on our faith; the teacher contrasts intelligence and faith `22:00`.

#### Questions about the world's arrangement [22:00]

- The teacher refers to the arrangement in the world for our provision — food, clothes, water, heat and light — operating under the principle of karma, and to questions such as why rain falls on the ocean.
- Many reasons can be given; the focus here is not the many classes and articles on such topics, but the single point that bad association can attack our faith.

#### Kaikeyī's bubble [22:00 – 26:00]

- Initially, when Mantharā brought the news that Rāma was going to be crowned, Kaikeyī was so happy that she gave her a necklace for the good news.
- Through association, she came to think that Rāma was the cause of her trouble, and built a bubble of imaginary fantasies to justify all the wrong things she did and all the hurt she caused.
- That bubble burst when she was rebuked: "You are not my mother; had you not been my mother, I would have killed you; you are the goddess of destruction incarnated in our family" `26:00`.
- This is the most dangerous level.

#### Care with what we expose ourselves to [26:00 – 30:00]

- Today such attacks can come through talk and groups the teacher mentions; it becomes very, very difficult to recover, so we must be careful about what we expose ourselves to `28:00`.
- Nobody denies there are problems in the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement; not everyone in it is Kṛṣṇa conscious, and when people act wrongly there will be consequences.
- But we must distinguish principles from institutions and individuals: some individuals may be in unfortunate situations, yet they do not falsify the principles or impede the process. The principles are still valid; one who understands their value and learns the process will benefit.
- The teacher then cites Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura on this theme `28:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The deepest damage of bad association is to faith: like Kaikeyī, we can build a bubble of fantasies that turns the Lord into the cause of our troubles — so guard what we expose ourselves to, and never let individuals' failings eclipse the validity of the principles. [22:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### Keeping Kṛṣṇa the first priority [30:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** The teacher turns to how Kṛṣṇa can be displaced from our lives, not dramatically but gradually `32:00`.

#### Subtle displacement [32:00]

- Serious devotees rarely replace Kṛṣṇa with Māyā directly; rather, Kṛṣṇa subtly slips in priority — to second priority, tenth priority, and finally to infinity.
- Kṛṣṇa reciprocates according to how we surrender to Him; if we are not intense in devotional service, we are not offering our consciousness to Him, and He cannot give to us because our consciousness is not there.
- **Mother and gulab jamun:** a mother wants to give her child a nice gulab jamun, but if the child is not there, how can she give it? Kṛṣṇa wants to give, but we are not available to Him `32:00 – 34:00`.

#### Keep the first priority first [34:00]

- Thinking about other things may seem unimportant, but the first priority is to keep the first priority first.
- We can make a resolution — even a written list — to keep it in mind in the proportions of our life.

#### The flight to Māyāpur [34:00 – 36:00]

- On a flight from Mumbai to Kolkata, on the way to Māyāpur, the plane does not fly in a straight line; according to pilots it is off its exact course most of the time, and yet it reaches its destination.

#### Regulated sādhana against forgetfulness [38:00]

- Forgetfulness of Kṛṣṇa is the danger; a regulated _sādhana_, practised every day, is very helpful.
- We may not eliminate forgetfulness at once, but we can at least resolve to counter it, as far as practically possible.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Māyā rarely replaces Kṛṣṇa outright; she lets Him slide down our priorities. Kṛṣṇa wants to give, but we must be present to receive — so keep the first priority first, and let daily regulated sādhana keep correcting our course. [32:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### The purport's conclusion: a better world and ISKCON [38:00 – 50:00]

**Context.** Having described the six elements that damage _bhakti_ and how to counter them, Śrīla Prabhupāda describes the result of avoiding them — first for society, then for individual seekers through ISKCON `38:00 – 40:00`.

#### An ideal society [38:00 – 40:00]

- If people avoided these six activities, society would approach an ideal; there is enough in this world for everyone's need, but not for one person's greed.
- The root problems are greed, a selfish ego and power-hunger; as these decrease, competition over limited resources also decreases.

#### For individual seekers [40:00]

- After this global view, the purport turns to those who want to practise; this is provided by the Kṛṣṇa consciousness society for individual seekers who want to practise seriously.

#### "Consciousness" [40:00 – 42:00]

- When the teacher first heard the name International Society for Krishna Consciousness, he was confused by "consciousness," which sounded like a scientific word.
- In the hippie era in America, "consciousness" was a popular word — consciousness expansion; some decades ago it became associated with global harmony.
- ISKCON seeks to awaken divine consciousness in the individual at the grassroots level, through association and philosophy; as one person becomes purer, society benefits. The purpose is to help as many people as possible become Kṛṣṇa conscious `42:00`.

#### What is an institution? [44:00 – 48:00]

- Critics say institutions become power-hungry, people become corrupted, and the spiritual element is lost. It happens, no doubt — but we should not think of an institution as something external imposed on religion.
- **River analogy:** waters from the mountain top come together and create a riverbed through the plains to the ocean; the path forms so that they can reach the ocean. Similarly, the continuous flow of people toward God forms an institution — something that grows naturally out of religion because of people's need to move toward God `46:00`.
- Once the river flows nicely, someone may dam it to take its water for themselves. Similarly, when there are facilities, money and assets, materialistic people may come and use them for their own enjoyment.
- The remedy is to keep standards properly and seriously; then other things become sidelined, those materially minded cannot stay, and the more serious people move forward `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Education and training [48:00 – 50:00]

- Contamination is always possible, but the purpose of the institution is to provide facilities and a support system for people attracted to these principles who want to follow them.
- _Education_ means knowing what to do; _training_ means knowing how to do it — how to chant, worship the deity, study scripture, make spiritual advancement, practise, love Kṛṣṇa and return to Him.
- This facility is provided to all of us through the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement; the goal is to engage more and more in the association of devotees `48:00 – 50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
An institution like ISKCON is not imposed on religion but grows from it, like a riverbed formed by waters flowing to the ocean; despite possible contamination, it exists to educate and train sincere seekers and give them the association that carries them to Kṛṣṇa. [38:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>
