## Text-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Three levels of devotees and three kinds of respect [00:00 – 10:00]

<!-- vedabase:noi/5 -->

**Context.** Rūpa Gosvāmī describes three kinds of devotees and the respect each deserves `00:00 – 02:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- The word for tongue or mouth — when Kṛṣṇa's name is on someone's tongue, that person deserves respect `00:00`.
- Respect offered with the mind — this person is not ordinary; among millions of beings, he chants Kṛṣṇa's names `00:00`.
- Initiation — here refers not only to first initiation but also to second initiation; such a person engaged in worship deserves obeisances `00:00`.
- To serve humbly — "to hear and to serve"; hearing itself is a service `00:00`.
- No inclination to criticise — not merely refraining from criticism, but having no such inclination in the heart `02:00`.

#### "No inclination" to criticise [02:00]

- Often we refrain from criticism only out of fear of backlash — like an employee who wants to burst out at a tyrannical boss but holds back. The inclination is still there.
- The advanced devotee has no inclination to criticise. This does not mean he never criticises, but he takes no delight in criticising.
- One should desire such association, cherish and treasure it, and serve faithfully.

#### The three levels [02:00 – 04:00]

- **First level** — one who chants Hare Kṛṣṇa, whatever his understanding; e.g. someone who comes to the temple, prays before the deity and chants — the _kaniṣṭha_.
- **Second level** — one who takes up serious worship — the _madhyama_.
- **Third level** — one completely devoted to undeviated worship of the Lord and not at all inclined to criticise others — the _uttama-adhikārī_.
- All three are important for our advancement; our spiritual master and guides need to be more advanced than us (an _uttama_ or a very advanced _madhyama_) `04:00`.

#### Kaniṣṭha versus madhyama [04:00 – 08:00]

- Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted all of us to rise at least from _kaniṣṭha_ to _madhyama_.
- The _kaniṣṭha_ notion: "I have a direct relationship with Kṛṣṇa; I worship the deity, then go home — how I deal with others doesn't matter; I can shout at people in passion and ignorance, as long as I fold my hands before the deity."
- The _madhyama_ understands that the relationship with Kṛṣṇa develops through how we relate with others too, and that _bhakti_ is a full-time activity.
- In a generic sense, the teacher contrasts those who practise _bhakti_ as traditional culture (for whom _bhakti_ is a part of life) with serious practitioners (for whom _bhakti_ is their whole life — indeed, life is a part of _bhakti_) `04:00 – 06:00`.
- "Life is a part of _bhakti_": _bhakti_ extends over many lives; perfection in one life is rare. Gītā 6.44–45 describe how spiritual attraction carries over and one is purified over many lives `06:00`.
- By Mahāprabhu's and Prabhupāda's mercy this life can be the **final lap of a marathon** — or there may be many more laps, depending on our seriousness `08:00`.

#### A community, not isolation [08:00 – 10:00]

- The spiritual world is not a place of silent, exclusive meditation; everyone is focused on Kṛṣṇa, but they serve Him together as a community.
- So: the new devotee is honoured in the mind; the serious devotee receives obeisances; the advanced devotee receives obeisances, submissive hearing, and our hankering for his association.
- Through that association his undeviated desire to serve Kṛṣṇa comes from his heart through his lips into our heart, other desires get subordinated or silenced, and we are progressively purified `10:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Text 5 asks us to honour every chanter, offer obeisances to the serious worshipper, and hanker to serve the non-envious advanced devotee — because through his association his desire to serve Kṛṣṇa enters our heart. [00:00 – 10:00]
</Callout>

### Terms, typologies and the meaning of adhikāra [10:00 – 20:00]

**Context.** The teacher explains the purport's typology and cautions that the same terms are used differently in different works `10:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Kaniṣṭha_ — the neophyte; worships only the deity and, not realising the value of devotees, behaves badly with them; the teacher jokes that "neophyte" means one who keeps finding "new fights" `10:00`.
- _Prākṛta-bhakta_ — _prākṛta_ means material (from _prakṛti_, material nature); one who sees material things alone is a _kaniṣṭha_ `14:00`.
- _Adhikāra_ — authority; an _adhikārī_ is one who has authority. _Kaniṣṭha-adhikārī_ — inferior authority; _uttama-adhikārī_ — excellent, superior authority `14:00 – 16:00`.

#### Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's different usage [10:00 – 14:00]

- In his _Kṛṣṇa-saṁhitā_, Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura uses the terms differently: _kaniṣṭha_ — those who see only externals; _madhyama_ — more intellectual people who look beyond appearance to substance (not necessarily devoted); _uttama_ — transcendental devotees.
- He wrote to attract intellectuals repelled by simplistic ritualism, who felt rituals (worship of different deities) were external and divisive.
- He sort of flattered them as _madhyamas_ who see deeper — then pointed to a higher _uttama_ level, where beyond externals one sees a transcendental reality full of variety, pursued through _samādhi_ and spiritual vision.
- His message: don't dismiss the religious culture because of some superficiality; rituals have original spiritual prototypes.

#### Where the terms come from [14:00]

- Rūpa Gosvāmī himself does not use the words _kaniṣṭha_, _madhyama_, _uttama_ here; he describes three levels, and Prabhupāda, drawing on other Gauḍīya writings including Rūpa Gosvāmī's own, gives the nomenclature.
- The _Bhāgavatam_ also describes advanced, intermediate and new devotees (discussed in the Īśopaniṣad course).

#### Adhikāra is not birth [16:00]

- Some claim only those born _brāhmaṇas_ may study and teach scripture; Prabhupāda agreed that _brāhmaṇas_ should — but a _brāhmaṇa_ is one with brahminical qualifications, which anyone can develop by sincere study under a spiritual master.
- _Adhikāra_ is determined by one's level of consciousness — one's frame of reference.
- The _kaniṣṭha_ cannot see beyond matter; it is not that things are forbidden to him, but his own consciousness makes them impossible to see — so he is told not to go in certain directions yet.

#### Progression, not discrimination [16:00 – 20:00]

- **Third-standard student and triple integral calculus** — telling him to study it later is not discrimination; he simply cannot understand it yet.
- **Schoolchild and post-doctoral researcher** — both study books, but the child pays fees and buys books, while the researcher is given books, facilities and a salary, because his study is more productive for society.
- Similarly in Vedic culture the more advanced are given more facilities, because they please Kṛṣṇa more and are more productive even from the world's view.
- In the _bhakti_ tradition this progression is open to everyone; a _kaniṣṭha_ need not remain one permanently `18:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Adhikāra means the authority that comes from one's level of consciousness, not birth; the levels describe an open progression — like a student moving from school to research — not discrimination. [14:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### The madhyama: behaviour reflects and affects Kṛṣṇa consciousness [18:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** The _madhyama_ has the four characteristics discussed earlier and deals differently with different people according to their disposition towards Kṛṣṇa `18:00 – 20:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Prema_ for Kṛṣṇa, the object of love; _kṛpā_ (compassion) for the innocent; _maitrī_ (friendship) for devotees; _upekṣā_ (neglect) for the atheists `20:00`.

#### Reflects [20:00 – 22:00]

- If I love someone and that person is criticised, I feel pain and want to defend them; so if I feel nothing when Kṛṣṇa or His devotees are blasphemed, my Kṛṣṇa consciousness is superficial.
- Hanumān did not stay "cool and serene" when he heard Rāvaṇa minimising Lord Rāma — he became angry, and that anger expressed his devotion.
- The _madhyama_, therefore, either counters blasphemy or moves away from it.
- Likewise, the desire to share Kṛṣṇa consciousness with an innocent person — to connect Kṛṣṇa's lost children with Him — reflects our Kṛṣṇa consciousness `22:00`.

#### Affects [22:00]

- Speaking about Kṛṣṇa enlivens us; when people ask questions, we go deeper into the subject, and our understanding, realisation and purification increase.
- So the _madhyama_ understands that Kṛṣṇa consciousness is not just done in isolation before the deity; it is reflected in and affected by his behaviour with different kinds of people, and he behaves so that it is protected and nourished.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
For the madhyama, how we deal with Kṛṣṇa, devotees, the innocent and the inimical both reveals the depth of our Kṛṣṇa consciousness and shapes it. [20:00 – 22:00]
</Callout>

### Respecting chanters without pigeonholing [22:00 – 30:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda's purport makes the verse relevant to contemporary readers with the example of a famous musician — George Harrison, though not named in the purport `22:00 – 24:00`.

#### The famous musician [24:00]

- He chanted Hare Kṛṣṇa, invited devotees, liked them and spoke about Kṛṣṇa whenever he could; Prabhupāda says we don't hesitate to offer respect to such a person.
- He was connected with other spiritual teachers too, several of them impersonalists; Prabhupāda knew this, never criticised the others, and simply presented his message of chanting and serving Kṛṣṇa — which attracted him.
- Prabhupāda focused on the chanting, not the mixed conceptions; as _sādhakas_ we should be able to see the good in others.

#### Mixed chanters [24:00 – 26:00]

- Until people properly come to Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, their conceptions are likely mixed — e.g. chanting Kṛṣṇa's name along with demigod mantras or names of self-proclaimed gods.
- We can't label them as Māyāvādīs; at least in the mind we offer respect, and outwardly too if practical.

#### Labels are for our advancement [26:00 – 28:00]

- Prabhupāda also refers to some initiated but not very serious practitioners as _kaniṣṭha_ — so the categories are not mechanical.
- Any typology is for classification; translating it into reality is always complex. The goal is not to pigeonhole people but to make our own spiritual advancement.
- In general, if someone has spoken deeply on scripture, practised for decades and is devoted to Kṛṣṇa, we understand he is not a _kaniṣṭha_ and respect him accordingly.
- Criteria are objective: we cannot assign someone a lower category because we hold a grudge.
- Even serving and befriending initiated worshippers who are not yet at the _madhyama_ level brings advancement `28:00`.

#### Respect for the demigods [28:00 – 30:00]

- Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura writes that a _kaniṣṭha_, on becoming a worshipper of Kṛṣṇa, can become fanatical and condemn demigod worshippers as stupid.
- There is a clear explanation of why demigod worship is for the less intelligent, but beyond that it is each individual's decision; there should be no condemnation.
- Devotees do not worship the demigods as independent lords, but respect them as Kṛṣṇa's representatives and exalted devotees.

#### The madhyama's firm faith [30:00]

- The _madhyama_ may have inconclusive knowledge of _śāstra_ — unable to establish conclusively what a text means — and may lack answers to some arguments, yet his conviction is undeterred and he keeps practising faithfully and firmly in chanting.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Honour anyone who chants Kṛṣṇa's name and see the good in them; the three categories guide our own advancement, never a licence to pigeonhole or demean others. [24:00 – 30:00]
</Callout>

### The uttama-adhikārī and the art of understanding scripture [30:00 – 42:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda describes the _uttama_ in a down-to-earth way, not in terms too esoteric for disciples `30:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Uttama_ — _tama_ means darkness; _ut_ means beyond: beyond darkness `30:00`.
- Hermeneutics — the art of understanding scripture; not mere interpretation `32:00`.

#### Prabhupāda's description [30:00]

- Pure, free from _anarthas_; seriously engaged in the Lord's service; spreads Kṛṣṇa consciousness; serves the guru; offenceless.

#### Am I an uttama? Quote wars [32:00 – 34:00]

- One might reason: "I chant, preach, am initiated and avoid offences — so am I an _uttama_?"
- A basic principle of hermeneutics is to consider the complete picture, not a fragment.
- Otherwise we get "quote wars" — like the Cold War between the US and USSR, constant scheming without shooting: "Prabhupāda said this" versus "Prabhupāda said that." Quote wars are not characteristic of an _uttama_.
- Quotes are important but have contexts; the _uttama_ has many characteristics, of which these are only some.

#### Uncritical, even when criticised [34:00]

- Even in devotee association serious criticism may occur; the _uttama_ tolerates it and may clarify, but does not love criticising.

#### Why Prabhupāda spoke strongly [34:00 – 38:00]

- Prabhupāda sometimes used strong epithets like "fools and rascals," even of a prime minister — seen from spiritual vision, a person with no attraction to God, however powerful, is living like an animal.
- His intent was to enlighten: speaking strongly to disciples dispelled the glamour of the material world in conditioned souls' minds.
- Yet when he met such a person, he was polite, respectful and cordial — because that person could not yet understand spiritual vision, and needed elevating qualities and the basics of Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- The _ācārya_ speaks according to time, place and circumstance, for what is best for each person.

#### Hate the disease, not the diseased [38:00 – 40:00]

- Pure devotion does not mean whitewashing everything.
- A doctor, by his oath, cares for everyone irrespective of caste, creed or nationality — but does not pretend everyone is healthy; different patients need different treatment.
- Likewise people are at different levels of consciousness and need spirituality made accessible to them; understanding this, the _ācārya_ sets up systems by which Kṛṣṇa reaches people.

#### Chanting is the most advanced activity [40:00]

- In an age of Google and Wikipedia, chanting, dancing and singing may look primitive, but Prabhupāda said it is the life and soul of the living entity.
- We want to leave the "hospital" of material existence not for selfish enjoyment, but to serve Kṛṣṇa more purely and fully.

#### Three visions [40:00 – 42:00]

- _Kaniṣṭha_ — sees only the body, not the soul.
- _Madhyama_ — sees body and soul, and tries to shift focus from body to soul.
- _Uttama_ — sees beyond material things to the spiritual; every soul is a potential devotee of Kṛṣṇa.
- The _uttama_ is completely free from desire for worldly name, fame and glory — remarkable in one so advanced `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The uttama is understood from the complete picture, not selected quotes; he has no taste for criticism, speaks strongly only to enlighten, and like a good doctor hates the disease, not the diseased. [30:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Receiving service, protecting faith and different definitions of success [42:00 – 52:00]

**Context.** The advanced devotee receives service from others, and also protects the faith of newcomers `42:00 – 44:00`.

#### Accepting service [42:00 – 44:00]

- Receiving service can be detrimental if we think, "I'm so advanced that people should serve me" — pleasing to the ego, unhealthy for devotion.
- The pure devotee understands Kṛṣṇa is manifesting through him; if serving him is how others become attracted to Kṛṣṇa, he accepts it.
- From our side we should not think, "Why does this person take service from everyone?" — by accepting service he gives us an opportunity to serve.

#### Why the uttama is uncritical [44:00]

- Prabhupāda's purport adds that a Vaiṣṇava is known by how many others he has made Vaiṣṇavas.
- The _uttama_ feels he has committed so many wrongs in the past that he deserves criticism in general, even if not for this specific charge; he feels regret and prays for forgiveness.

#### Not in front of the children [44:00 – 46:00]

- The _uttama_ protects new devotees' faith by not unnecessarily airing controversies.
- Parents who disagree generally don't fight in front of their children, since seeing parents scream at each other can be traumatic; they talk privately.
- Likewise senior devotees should resolve disagreements privately; public criticism among devotees can badly damage young devotees' faith and inspiration.

#### Different definitions of success [46:00 – 50:00]

- Devotees have different inclinations and priorities, so they may have different definitions of success — all within the scope of what Prabhupāda gave:
  - distributing as many books as possible;
  - building temples or opulent deity worship;
  - brahminical culture — deep study of scripture and systematic education;
  - building _varṇāśrama_ projects;
  - educating the next generation through schools and retreats;
  - college preaching to attract young people with leadership potential.
- These are all valid; they need not be competitive or exclusive. Each devotee selects one aspect of Prabhupāda's mission to advance in and reach out through.
- **Refuting atheism** — Prabhupāda said we advance by refuting atheistic theories like evolution, but not every devotee must study science. For those so inclined it is not "material": besides direct glorification of Kṛṣṇa, removing misconceptions that obstruct people from seeing His glories — like the claim that He doesn't exist — is also glorification `48:00 – 50:00`.
- Compromising on the regulative principles is a different issue — but even that is best handled not by public criticism but by meeting the devotee carefully, asking questions, presenting our perspective, and seeking harmonious reconciliation `50:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Advanced devotees accept service only to give others a chance to serve, settle disagreements privately to protect new devotees' faith, and honour the many valid ways of serving Prabhupāda's mission. [42:00 – 50:00]
</Callout>

### Initiation and Prabhupāda's example with his godbrothers [52:00 – 62:14]

**Context.** The purport discusses who can initiate, and the teacher closes with Prabhupāda's dealings with his godbrothers as an illustration of the uncritical heart `52:00`.

#### Who can initiate [52:00 – 54:00]

- The ideal is an _uttama-adhikārī_, but Prabhupāda presented that one who strictly follows Kṛṣṇa consciousness, with steady commitment, and is approved by the leaders of the devotee community, can initiate — he need not have reached an assurance of never falling.
- If one is not initiated by an _uttama_, one's advancement is limited to the extent one's spiritual master is advanced — but the spiritual master is also advancing, and having covered more terrain, can always guide us.
- The test of a Vaiṣṇava: how many Vaiṣṇavas he has made `52:00 – 54:00`.

#### The title "Prabhupāda" [54:00 – 56:00]

- When Prabhupāda came to India with Western disciples, some godbrothers — leaders in Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's time, while Prabhupāda had been a _gṛhastha_ on the periphery — could not digest his success.
- One objected to his using the title "Prabhupāda." Prabhupāda asked his disciples to leave the room, had his letterhead brought, and showed that it bore his name as a Swami, with no "Prabhupāda" — he had never taken the title himself.
- The devotees had first called him "Swami," but Prabhupāda said that was a common address; asked what to call the spiritual master, he gave several options, and the devotees chose "Prabhupāda," which became formalised. The godbrother was pacified.

#### "Why did he appear?" [56:00 – 58:00]

- Others criticised him for not visiting Mahāprabhu's birthplace in Māyāpur; Prabhupāda said where Mahāprabhu appeared is not as important as why — to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness, which he was doing.
- Hearing repeated criticism, he said: ask them who has opened more temples, written more books, made more devotees than me.
- Then, gravely: "I have inherited the legacy of my spiritual master" — not property (there were property disputes after his guru's departure, and Prabhupāda received none) but the desire to serve and glorify Kṛṣṇa. This was humility and a statement of fact, not pride.

#### Respect, firmness and forgiveness [58:00 – 62:00]

- At functions he ensured his godbrothers sat at the same level as he; he never violated Vaiṣṇava etiquette with them.
- When some began to poison his new disciples against him over small technicalities, he told the disciples not to associate with them, calling them envious — to protect the disciples' faith, not from disrespect.
- When one godbrother who had strongly criticised him passed away, Prabhupāda did not say he went to hell; he said his spiritual master is very powerful, and since this godbrother had done much service, the spiritual master took him back `58:00 – 60:00`.
- In his final days Prabhupāda asked visiting godbrothers to forgive any harsh words spoken in preaching; one replied that he had committed no offence but glorified Kṛṣṇa as no one else could — not a great offender but a great purifier `60:00`.
- So advanced devotees may sometimes speak strongly, but there is no desire to criticise in their hearts — only to educate and prevent misunderstandings. We should not hype up conflicts among advanced devotees; Kṛṣṇa has a plan we may not know.

#### Nārada's example [60:00 – 62:14]

- By serving such devotees our heart is purified and we advance rapidly — as Nārada, a maidservant's son, served great devotees during the four months of _cāturmāsya_, developed immense attraction for Kṛṣṇa, and eventually attained Him.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prabhupāda showed the uttama's heart: firm when protecting disciples' faith, yet respectful, forgiving and free from any desire to criticise — and serving such devotees, like Nārada, brings rapid advancement. [52:00 – 62:14]
</Callout>
