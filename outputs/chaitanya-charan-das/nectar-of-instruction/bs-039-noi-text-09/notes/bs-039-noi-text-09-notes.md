### Is the spiritual world a fancy? [04:00 – 20:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda's purport begins with the spiritual world — three-fourths of the total creation and superior to the material world. Since some call it a comforting fantasy, the teacher offers several lines of reasoning `04:00`.

#### 1. The heart's longing for the extraordinary [04:00 – 10:00]

- Those sceptical of the spiritual world happily watch Disneyland shows, get lost in Harry Potter's magic school, and enjoy horror films and paranormal fiction.
- The material world is ultimately the same everywhere — birth, eating, sleeping, mating, defending, struggle for existence — only the forms differ; so people want something new `06:00`.
- Two questions: why do people need such make-believe for entertainment, and why do they take it so seriously? Because the heart longs for something beyond the ordinary `06:00`.
- When we dismiss God's kingdom as mythology, we create our own mythology and absorb our consciousness in it — an obsession with illusion that brings no transformation, purification or elevation `06:00 – 08:00`.
- Attraction to the supernatural is natural; it should not be suppressed but rightly directed.
- This longing is not conclusive proof, but it indicates the possibility of something that fulfils it `08:00`.

#### 2. The wisdom traditions [08:00 – 10:00]

- All great wisdom traditions speak of an eternal place — by different names (the teacher mentions _jannat_ as one).
- This world is described as a station on a journey, not a destination; we can be mere wanderers or pilgrims.
- Spirituality converts a disastrous journey — ending in old age, disease and death — into a glorious pilgrimage back to a place from which one does not return `10:00`.

#### 3. Scripture and its students [10:00 – 12:00]

- Even if someone does not accept scripture as God's word, those who studied scriptures throughout history have been among the wisest people, with remarkable scholarship, logic and analysis — and they spoke of the spiritual world.
- **Machine without a manual** — God provides food, water, air, heat and light; a maker who supplies a machine but no manual would be incomplete. God provides the needed knowledge through scripture, the manual for life, which names the spiritual world as life's destination `12:00`.

#### 4. The eternal soul [12:00 – 14:00]

- The body is dead matter and matter cannot experience matter; the experiencer is consciousness, which stays the same though the body changes — the soul.
- If the soul is eternal and nothing here is eternal, there must be an eternal place where it belongs.
- **Gold tablet on a tiled floor** — something incongruous with its surroundings makes us ask where it came from; as gold comes from a gold mine, the spiritual soul comes from a place where everything is spiritual `14:00`.

#### 5. The limits of science [14:00 – 20:00]

- Science gives tremendous power, but over a narrow slice of reality: the visible spectrum is tiny within the electromagnetic spectrum; scientists speak of parallel universes, inaccessible by definition `14:00`.
- **Scale of perception** — a beauty queen's face under a microscope shows troughs and valleys, not beauty; under an electron microscope, only electrons running around `16:00`.
- The more we know, the more we realise how little we know — the paradox of science `16:00`.
- **Earth as a marble** — space travel, hailed as proof of human greatness, showed Earth as a tiny, vulnerable blue marble amid asteroids and meteoroids in a vast universe `16:00 – 18:00`.
- Scientists propose imperceivable concepts such as black holes, dark matter and dark energy (within the material world, the teacher notes); particles once dismissed as fantasies are now taken seriously `18:00`.
- To dismiss anything as impossible reflects an arrogance not in harmony with the spirit of science; an honest scientist would be open-minded `18:00`.

#### Converging lines [18:00 – 20:00]

- These independent lines of reasoning converge and are confirmed by scriptural revelation: the spiritual world is not just a reality but the supreme reality, which remains when everything here is destroyed.
- It is Kṛṣṇa's abode, from which He descends and sends His representatives to invite us back `20:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The heart's longing for the extraordinary, the testimony of wisdom traditions and scripture, the eternal soul and science's own humility all point to the spiritual world as the supreme reality — and our longing should be directed there rather than into make-believe. [04:00 – 20:00]
</Callout>

### Infinity and Vaikuṇṭha [20:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** If the spiritual world is infinite and three times the material world, is the material world also infinite? `20:00`

#### Levels of infinity [20:00 – 22:00]

- The set of natural numbers is infinite; the set of odd numbers is also infinite but contained within it; the set of prime numbers is infinite and smaller still.
- Our usual logic cannot be straightforwardly applied to infinity.
- The comparison's point: the spiritual world is far greater, grander and more wonderful than the material world with its innumerable universes.

#### Vaikuṇṭha: without anxiety [22:00 – 24:00]

- _Vai_ means without, _kuṇṭha_ anxiety — a realm free from anxiety, with eternal existence and eternal happiness.
- It is the cherished aspiration of wise souls, who go there and never come back.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Quantities like "three-fourths" emphasise that the spiritual world infinitely surpasses the material, and its very name — Vaikuṇṭha, "without anxiety" — tells us why it is our real goal. [20:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Hierarchy in terms of rasa [24:00 – 32:00]

**Context.** Are there hierarchies in the spiritual world? Yes — but they must be understood carefully, in terms of _rasa_ `24:00`.

#### Everyone there is perfect [24:00]

- Everyone in the spiritual world is liberated, perfect and happy.
- "Mathurā is higher than Vaikuṇṭha" does not mean Vaikuṇṭha's residents are miserable and wishing to go elsewhere.
- A devotee of Lord Rāma is not thinking "Kṛṣṇa is higher, so I want to serve Him"; in terms of _rasa_, he is completely satisfied serving Lord Rāma.

#### The story of a seeker [24:00 – 28:00]

- The teacher recounts a story told in a scripture: a seeker meets various great people, receives a mantra from a spiritual master, becomes purified and attains Vaikuṇṭha.
- He is happy and grateful to see the Lord worshipped in opulence, but after some time grows restless, wishing to see a blue cowherd boy; by the Lord's arrangement he comes to Gokula in Vṛndāvana and then attains the spiritual world again.
- The residents of Vaikuṇṭha, by contrast, are satisfied worshipping Lord Viṣṇu, whom they consider supreme; the story accepts that fully — one devotee's _rasa_ cannot be imposed on another.

#### Objective and subjective [28:00 – 30:00]

- In terms of intimacy and sweetness, Kṛṣṇa's pastimes are more intimate and sweeter. Objectively, Viṣṇu does not have a father and mother; Kṛṣṇa does, and _vātsalya-rasa_ is very intimate.
- Yet there is room for transcendental subjectivity: for Viṣṇu's devotees, Viṣṇu is the best; for those seeking Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa is the best.
- Two errors to avoid:
  - Imposing our _rasa_ on others, or becoming insecure when others do not accept our hierarchy.
  - Saying "everything is the same" — there are levels of perfection, and objective reasons for calling Rādhā-kuṇḍa the highest.

#### The devotee of Lord Rāma in South India [30:00]

- In South India, the Lord met a devotee of Rāma so distressed that Sītā had been touched and abducted by Rāvaṇa that he could not even cook for his guest.
- The Lord did not tell him "forget Rāma, worship Kṛṣṇa"; appreciating his sentiment, He later found a scripture stating that Sītā was never touched — only an illusory Sītā was taken — and brought it back to the devotee, who was enlightened.
- The Lord elevated some who were not fixed to a higher _rasa_, but allowed people to practise according to their own _rasa_. So we should not use the hierarchy to thrust things on others, but cherish what we have been given.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The hierarchy of holy places is a hierarchy of rasa and intimacy, not of satisfaction: everyone in the spiritual world is fulfilled, so we cherish our own tradition's highest ideal without imposing it on others or flattening all levels into one. [24:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Mathurā and the nature of a dhāma [30:00 – 44:00]

**Context.** Mathurā is higher than Vaikuṇṭha because Kṛṣṇa appeared there — and because, being in the material world, it is accessible to us `30:00 – 32:00`.

#### Accessible mercy [32:00]

- We cannot attain Vaikuṇṭha right now; but because the Lord descended here, His abode has become accessible.
- The teacher cites a Purāṇic verse in which the Lord says He is not present in Vaikuṇṭha nor in the hearts of _yogīs_, but where His devotees chant His glories.
- Meaning: He is present in both places, but they are not accessible to us; we can, however, join devotees glorifying Kṛṣṇa and relish His glories there. Similarly, Mathurā is the spiritual world itself made accessible in the material world.

#### Why it looks ordinary: adhikāra [34:00 – 38:00]

- Visitors see sewage, dirt, hogs and commercialisation — so in what sense is it the spiritual world?
- The _dhāma_ is perceived according to one's _adhikāra_ (qualification).
- **Mainframe computer** — the universe is like a huge mainframe with many users; all the data is there, but ordinary users can only enter or modify some data, while settings can hide parts of the system from them entirely; a company's owner or CEO may have complete access, others access according to role `34:00 – 36:00`.
- In material consciousness, with impure senses and heart, we see the _dhāma_ only at a material level; its spiritual reality is present but unmanifest to us. Advanced, purified devotees have access, and when we are purified our spiritual perception will awaken `36:00`.

#### How to perceive the dhāma [36:00 – 42:00]

1. **Scriptural description** — "see with the ears": hear from scripture, and from devotees who explain it as they have realised it `38:00`.
2. **Sensory perception** — go to the places of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, behold Govardhana and the Yamunā; even if we do not perceive them as spiritual, their potency is there, and engaging the senses in Kṛṣṇa's service is _bhakti_ `38:00`.
   - In a temple we go not so much to see the Lord as to be seen by Him; beholding the deities is serving the Lord with our eyes `38:00`.
   - Simply by going to the _dhāma_ we become purified; saints throughout history have glorified it and yearned to go again and again `38:00 – 40:00`.
3. **Prayer for spiritual revelation** — as we become purified, our spiritual senses awaken and, by Kṛṣṇa's mercy, He reveals the _dhāma_ `40:00 – 42:00`.

#### Examples from the tradition [40:00 – 42:00]

- After taking _sannyāsa_, one great devotee said that, having renounced the world in terms of relationships, he would now renounce it in terms of location and go to Vṛndāvana, the ultimate destination manifest here `40:00`.
- Prabhupāda struggled hard to obtain land in the _dhāmas_, made Māyāpur ISKCON's world headquarters because the Lord's potency is there, considered Vṛndāvana his home and chose to leave his body there `40:00`.
- Great Vaiṣṇavas sometimes heard Kṛṣṇa's sweet flute or beheld His form — revelations that came even in Purī, what to speak of Vṛndāvana; such revelations conquer the heart `40:00 – 42:00`.
- The _dhāma_ is covered by _yogamāyā_; the covering is removed by the _sādhaka_'s endeavour for purification combined with Kṛṣṇa's mercy descending through devotees `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
A dhāma is the spiritual world made accessible, but it reveals itself according to our qualification; we hear scriptural descriptions, serve with our senses in the holy place, and pray for the revelation that purification and Kṛṣṇa's mercy bring. [32:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Vṛndāvana and Govardhana [42:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** Returning to the hierarchy: Mathurā is the larger district; within it is Vṛndāvana — the twelve forests where Kṛṣṇa performed pastimes `42:00`.

#### Why Vṛndāvana is higher than Mathurā [42:00 – 44:00]

- Kṛṣṇa's appearance in Mathurā is glorious, but He did not stay long: He was taken away immediately after birth and, after returning, stayed only a few years because of attacks, then left (later going to Dvārakā).
- Vṛndāvana is where He performed His most intimate pastimes in their full splendour.

#### Why Govardhana is higher still [44:00 – 48:00]

- Generally one _rasa_ is reciprocated in a pastime; in lifting Govardhana, Kṛṣṇa was with all His devotees together for seven days — accepting food from Yaśodā, being beheld by the _gopīs_ in _mādhurya_ — so many _rasas_ were relished at once `44:00`.
- Govardhana served Kṛṣṇa as an umbrella of protection, and the _gopīs_ call Govardhana the greatest among the servants of Hari — normally it is the _gopīs_ who are exalted, yet they exalt Govardhana `46:00`.
- Govardhana provides many facilities for Kṛṣṇa's pastimes: caves for rest from sun and rain, pasture for the cows, streams of fresh water, fruit trees and flowering shrubs `46:00`.
- Around its periphery are places likened to the eyes of Govardhana, if Govardhana is considered a person; these are especially special `46:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Each step up the ladder is a step in intimacy: Vṛndāvana for Kṛṣṇa's intimate pastimes, Govardhana for gathering every rasa at once and for offering itself as His umbrella and playground. [42:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### Rādhā-kuṇḍa: love in liquid form [46:00 – 56:00]

**Context.** Rādhā-kuṇḍa lies in the Govardhana area itself and was personally discovered by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu; advanced souls have resided there since `46:00 – 48:00`.

#### Rediscovery [48:00]

- When Kṛṣṇa departs, the places of His pastimes remain, but by the passage of time things decline; five hundred years ago, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu revealed the place again.
- Rādhā-kuṇḍa is the love of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa manifested in liquid form.

#### The pastime of its appearance [48:00 – 52:00]

- Kṛṣṇa killed a demon who had come in the form of a cow; the _gopīs_ said that, demon or not, killing a cow is sinful, and He must bathe in all holy rivers to be purified.
- Kṛṣṇa summoned all the sacred rivers in personified form, made a _kuṇḍa_ and bathed there.
- He then told the _gopīs_ that they had sided with the demon and also needed purification. The _gopīs_ dug a trench with their bangles, intending to fill it laboriously with water from the Yamunā.
- The personified rivers humbly asked to serve them; seeing their humility and sincere devotion, the water from Kṛṣṇa's _kuṇḍa_ was allowed to flow into Rādhā-kuṇḍa `50:00`.

#### Why Rādhā-kuṇḍa is specially exalted [52:00]

- Both _kuṇḍas_ are extremely sacred, but Rādhārāṇī's love is so great that even Kṛṣṇa cannot understand it — which is why He comes as Caitanya Mahāprabhu to understand it.
- Rādhārāṇī is the Lord Himself manifest as the devotee, to give us an opportunity for love and service; her love is accessible to us in liquid form at Rādhā-kuṇḍa.
- Prabhupāda speaks of it repeatedly in his purports as the most sacred place; he brought his Western disciples on pilgrimage, told them the pastimes of each place and asked them to pray there `52:00`.

#### Reverence, not frivolity [52:00 – 54:00]

- When some devotees were tossing water and fooling around at Rādhā-kuṇḍa, Prabhupāda became very grave: "Don't bathe here — you come here for purification, not for sense gratification."
- A frivolous attitude is terribly inappropriate in such a sacred place.

#### Taking shelter of Rādhā-kuṇḍa [54:00]

- Bathing is important, but real shelter of Rādhā-kuṇḍa — of Rādhārāṇī — is in the mood of selfless service, to which Prabhupāda's merciful descent gives us access.
- We also go there, take _darśana_, hear the glories and bathe, and are immensely purified.
- For most of us it is not practical to reside at Rādhā-kuṇḍa — living in a holy place we may take it cheaply and commit offences — so Prabhupāda and our teachers say that residence there is more a matter of mood; cultivating selfless service and devotion will eventually grant residence in the spiritual world.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Rādhā-kuṇḍa crowns the hierarchy because it is Rādhārāṇī's love made accessible in liquid form; we approach it with reverence, and truly take its shelter by cultivating her mood of selfless service wherever we live. [48:00 – 54:00]
</Callout>
