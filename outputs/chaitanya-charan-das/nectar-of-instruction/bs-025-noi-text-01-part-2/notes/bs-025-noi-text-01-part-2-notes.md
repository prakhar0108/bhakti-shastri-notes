## Text-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Jihvā-vega: the urge of the tongue [00:00 – 12:00]

<!-- vedabase:noi/1 -->

**Context.** _Jihvā-vega_ is the tongue's urge to eat (as distinct from _vāk-vega_, the urge to speak). People are controlled by their tongues and run here and there — even devotees eating in non-devotional shops. The solution is _prasādam_, in two parts: be grateful when Kṛṣṇa supplies, accepting it as mercy, and take it at regulated times `00:00`.

#### Suicide by the tongue [02:00]

- Many people are inviting death by how they eat — not only the kinds of food but the quantity.
- An old Egyptian proverb: we live on one-third of what we eat; the remaining two-thirds keeps the physician alive.
- Obesity is fast becoming one of the most widespread causes of avoidable disease and even death.

#### Tongue and belly overlap [02:00]

- _Jihvā_ concerns taste — wanting a variety of delicious items; _udara_ concerns quantity — eating more than the body needs.
- They are connected, and variety leads to quantity; the point is not hair-splitting but the common principle of controlling the tongue.

#### A culture that pulls both ways [02:00 – 06:00]

- In the past food was mainly given in charity; even when sold, it was never marketed as aggressively as today — the sheer amount of marketing is historically unprecedented.
- At the same time society glamorises the thin figure and treats obese people as almost "visually untouchable"; people undergo bariatric surgery or fall into anorexia.
- So people want both a thin figure and the tongue's delights — "damned if you do, damned if you don't."

#### A game we can't win and can't quit [06:00 – 08:00]

- A skinny, unskilled person in the ring with a huge boxer, under a rule that he may never leave, simply gets beaten again and again.
- Material desires are:
  - **insatiable** — satisfying them makes them blaze further, so we cannot win;
  - **irresistible** — at the material level we cannot say no, so we cannot quit.
- This is everyone's predicament, not only devotees': even materialists, with limited bodies, resources and lifespans, must live with unfulfilled desires `08:00`.
- So we must rise to a higher level of consciousness; controlling the senses at the same material level is nearly impossible `04:00 – 06:00`.

#### Diets and self-control are not the answer [08:00 – 10:00]

- Control merely to look good leads to fads such as "gorge one day, fast the next," which the teacher says are physically and psychologically hazardous because the body has its own clock and system.
- Some people seem very controlled and selective in eating; self-control is appreciable but not in itself a sign of spiritual advancement. The teacher cites a figure who had great self-control yet was spiritually most regressed, whose whole motive was to disrupt the order of Lord Viṣṇu and destroy Him `10:00`.
- Self-control is valuable — even essential for steady advancement — but should not be glorified as a separate virtue.

#### Prasādam regulates the senses automatically [10:00 – 12:00]

- On the "expressway" of devotional service, the senses are regulated automatically.
- _Prasādam_ circumscribes what we eat — a major level of sense control amid countless allurements.
- Within those limits there are still nearly endless allurements; we need enjoyment, and _bhakti_ provides it within the precincts of _dharma_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
At the material level the tongue's urges are insatiable and irresistible — a fight we can neither win nor quit — so the answer is not mere dieting or willpower but rising to devotional consciousness, where prasādam automatically regulates what we eat. [02:00 – 12:00]
</Callout>

### Prasādam and fasting in devotional consciousness [12:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** Taking _prasādam_ is not against devotion, but habitually depending on opulent _prasādam_ can be a serious problem `12:00`.

#### Does it remind us of Kṛṣṇa? [12:00 – 14:00]

- _Kṛṣṇa-prasādam_ is Kṛṣṇa — but the question is whether it reminds us of Kṛṣṇa.
- If we remember it only as delicious food, it still purifies at a subconscious level because it was offered, but consciously we are not remembering Kṛṣṇa, and overeating has physical consequences.
- Kṛṣṇa's manifestations in this world act transcendentally to purify us, yet at the material level behave like material substances: the Deity must be cleaned and the flies moved away. Their transcendence lies not in miraculous physical effects but in purifying the heart.
- How much purification we receive depends on our remembrance of Kṛṣṇa.
- Prabhupāda would often invite devotees to eat more and more — a wonderful culture of _prasādam_ — yet in the purport says not to eat or dress opulently: feasts on special occasions are fine, but habitually opulent eating is undesirable `14:00`.

#### Fasting: sacrificing the good for the best [14:00 – 18:00]

- Prabhupāda says that fasting without remembering Kṛṣṇa is simply a fat-reducing exercise with little spiritual benefit.
- Three levels:
  - the bad — _pāpa_ (sin);
  - the good — _puṇya_ (piety);
  - the best — _bhakti_, Kṛṣṇa.
- _Prasādam_ is not mere _puṇya_, but it has a material aspect that can distract; sometimes we sacrifice even the good for the best, showing single-pointed focus on Kṛṣṇa `16:00`.
- Kṛṣṇa says the irresolute have many branches of desire; our desire is spread over many objects, so our desire for Kṛṣṇa is weak. Fasting gathers those scattered desires onto Kṛṣṇa: "Do I desire You more than food, water and sleep?" — if not all life, then at least on special days `16:00`.

#### The ego's feast versus devotion's fruit [18:00 – 20:00]

- Fasting while thinking of food tortures us; fasting and becoming proud — watching what others eat and judging their lack of control — means the body fasts while the ego feasts.
- Fasting should be devotion's fruit: during the fast our time, thoughts and desires go to Kṛṣṇa, not elsewhere.
- When hunger torments, turning the mind to Kṛṣṇa gives non-material nourishment — as Parīkṣit Mahārāja told Śukadeva Gosvāmī that hunger did not trouble him because he was drinking the nectar of Kṛṣṇa-_kathā_ coming from Śukadeva `18:00`.
- Fasting forces us to become conscious of Kṛṣṇa — otherwise we become conscious of the hungry stomach — so it can be a devotional austerity, not just a physical one `20:00`.

#### Levels of fasting and their health benefits [20:00 – 22:00]

- Waterless fasting is difficult for many and, the teacher says, not so good for health even according to Āyurvedic doctors; fasting with water is recommended also for health — the body, like a machine, needs rest to cleanse and heal itself.
- Gradations, from strictest to lightest:
  1. Waterless fasting.
  2. Water fasting.
  3. Juice or fluid fasting (e.g. lemon water).
  4. Fruit only.
  5. Raw foods — fruits and vegetables.
  6. No grains, once a day; then twice; then thrice.
- Many devotees think fasting only means not taking grains — restricting the choice of diet but not the quantity or frequency — and so miss _Ekādaśī_'s potential to take them deeper `22:00`.

#### Fasting is a means, not another god [22:00 – 24:00]

- More important than fasting is extra chanting and remembrance of Kṛṣṇa — but physical restriction reduces distraction and helps focus the mind.
- If one is physically unsuited, fasting is not necessary; better to stay awake remembering Kṛṣṇa and eat what is required than to fast and sleep all day.
- Fasting should not become another object of thought; it should redirect thoughts from food to Kṛṣṇa.
- Going to someone's house because the food will be nice is circumventing the principle; yet we need not deliberately eat tasteless food. Our process is to accept whatever Kṛṣṇa offers — a feast when it comes, but not a feast every day `24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prasādam and fasting both work through remembrance: prasādam purifies most when it reminds us of Kṛṣṇa, and fasting becomes devotion's fruit — not the ego's feast — when it gathers our scattered desires onto Him. [12:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Udara-vega: variety, quantity and frequency [24:00 – 32:00]

**Context.** Controlling the belly involves regulating the variety, the quantity and the frequency of eating `24:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Hunger_ — the stomach is empty and the body needs fuel for its metabolic functions; it is generic `24:00`.
- _Pseudo-hunger_ — "I am hungry for _gulāb jāmun_"; it is always specific `24:00`.

#### Genuine hunger versus pseudo-hunger [24:00 – 26:00]

- The body does not mind whether it gets chapatis, parathas or bread cooked by devotees, as long as it is nourishing.
- Wanting a particular item is not wrong if it can be offered to Kṛṣṇa, but we should recognise it as the mind's need and then decide whether to fulfil it; the body's need must be fulfilled.
- Whenever hunger becomes specific, it is pseudo-hunger — and pseudo-hunger is what causes fat `26:00`.
- Excess variety and quantity drag consciousness down to passion or ignorance — oversleeping or wanting to enjoy — whereas satisfying genuine hunger does not.

#### Frequency: Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya and the principle of mercy [26:00 – 30:00]

- After his momentous conversation with Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu in the Jagannātha temple, Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya was converted; the next morning Mahāprabhu gave him _prasādam_.
- Sārvabhauma quoted a verse from the _Purāṇas_ saying _prasādam_ should be eaten as soon as it is obtained; Mahāprabhu was so ecstatic it was as if He had conquered the universe, the purpose of His descent fulfilled.
- The main lesson is not "eat _prasādam_ whenever it comes" but the transformation of Sārvabhauma's heart: a dry logician and impersonalist who relied on rigid adherence to rules came to have far greater faith in the principle of mercy `28:00`.
- In our case we often see _prasādam_ not as mercy but as an opportunity to enjoy `30:00`.
- We should not judge others who take _prasādam_ whenever it comes; for ourselves, when _prasādam_ comes at odd times we can take a little, offer obeisances to it (as was done in someone's last days), or keep it for later.
- Best is to keep fixed _prasādam_ times, so we stay Kṛṣṇa-conscious rather than food-conscious, always thinking where to get good food `30:00`.

#### Quantity: learning by experience, adjusting to service [30:00 – 32:00]

- Prabhupāda often quoted Āyurveda: half the stomach for food, a quarter for air and a quarter for water.
- There is no formula for "half"; we learn by experience — too little and we feel very hungry afterwards, too much and we feel sleepy.
- Quantity changes as we grow, with our physical engagement, energy and digestion.
- Regulation can be dynamic: during a _yātrā_ with heavy physical service, fasting may hinder service, so better not fast then; with more service we may eat more.
- _Udara-vega_ is not about sticking to a fixed quantity, but eating what the body needs for the engagement we have `32:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Regulate the belly by variety, quantity and frequency: satisfy generic hunger and recognise specific cravings as pseudo-hunger, keep fixed prasādam times, and adjust quantity to one's service — always seeing prasādam as mercy rather than enjoyment. [24:00 – 32:00]
</Callout>

### Upastha-vega: retrieving the sacred purpose of sex [32:00 – 36:00]

**Context.** The Vedic scriptures do not reject sex; it is an important part of life, the way new life comes into the world, and not contrary to religious principles `32:00`.

#### Balance, not intrusion [32:00 – 34:00]

- Restricting sex even within marriage may seem an intrusion into private life; the point is not restriction but a balanced perspective.
- Paradoxically, the more culture glamorises sex, the more it devalues it: constant display of attractive forms keeps people artificially stimulated and agitated, and sex becomes an activity for physical release without real intimacy — as in prostitution.

#### Sacred, therefore reserved [34:00 – 36:00]

- Sex is reserved for procreation not because it is sinful but because it is so sacred and valued.
- It offers human beings the opportunity to become co-creators with God, who uses a male and female to create new life.
- Trivialising it to casual relief degrades what is special and can become even more unregulated — even into violating others and sexual crimes `36:00`.
- The point is not to **deprive** but to **retrieve**: recognise the activity's purpose and act according to it, maintaining spiritual vision `36:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti does not condemn sex but honours it as sacred co-creation with God; regulating it means retrieving its purpose, not depriving oneself — while a culture that glamorises it actually trivialises it. [32:00 – 36:00]
</Callout>

### Reviewing the commentary: speech and information [36:00 – 42:00]

**Context.** The teacher briefly reviews the commentary on the six urges, most points having been covered already `36:00`.

#### Prajalpa of karmīs, jñānīs and karma-kāṇḍīs [36:00 – 38:00]

- The commentary identifies the urge to speak with the _prajalpa_ of _karmīs_, _jñānīs_ and _karma-kāṇḍīs_; all the newspapers, magazines and channels talking of so many things lead to _prajalpa_.
- A social commentator said all the news is "old things happening to new people" — a disaster there last year, a disaster here now; one team wins a tournament, then another.
- Devotees need not be oblivious of the world — basic functional awareness is fine — but endless talk on social, political and cultural issues that leads to no Kṛṣṇa-conscious conclusion wastes time; our focus is awareness of ourselves as souls, through scripture, association and spiritual attunement `38:00`.

#### Information divorced from function [38:00 – 42:00]

- Never before has information been so divorced from function; in the past, information served one's function — a potter learned pottery.
- Today much information has no function, so we create one: quizzes on trivia (how many daughters a film star has, how many times someone married), sometimes winning lakhs of rupees. Studying for a competitive exam that requires such knowledge is fine.
- The point is not to suppress curiosity — consciousness is the soul's nature — but to turn the "curiosity radar" towards Kṛṣṇa, since material information is endless and ultimately useless `40:00`.
- This habit carries into scripture: we listen to classes looking only for new information, yet scriptural information is meant for transformation, like a doctor's prescription meant to cure. At the end of the Gītā, Arjuna does not say "very good class, Kṛṣṇa" — he says he will do what Kṛṣṇa says, and does it `42:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Idle news and trivia are information divorced from function; direct curiosity towards Kṛṣṇa, and hear scripture not for novelty but as a prescription meant to transform us. [36:00 – 42:00]
</Callout>

### Reviewing the commentary: mind, anger and the physical connection [42:00 – 48:00]

**Context.** The commentary classifies the mind's urges, and counts anger among them `42:00`.

#### Unregulated attachment and anger [42:00 – 44:00]

- The mind's urge is unrestricted, unregulated attachment — accepting no rules: "I want to enjoy the way, time and quantity I like."
- Even without scriptural rules, practical restrictions come; when desires are unfulfilled, we become angry — so anger is counted as an urge of the mind.

#### Why typologies exist [44:00]

- Scriptural classifications are never meant for hair-splitting; reality is complex, and no classification maps onto it point by point.
- A typology gives a framework — a guideline — for thinking and living. Whether anger is a separate urge or a subcategory of the mind does not matter.

#### Tracing anger to its root [44:00]

- Anger comes from frustration; frustration comes from expectation.
- Tracing back, we can check whether the expectation was warranted — often it was not.
- So anger is curbed not only by not getting angry, but by lowering expectations: this world is a place of misery where things go wrong, and high expectations prepare us for frustration and suffering.

#### Tongue, belly and genitals in a straight line [46:00 – 48:00]

- The commentary notes that the tongue, belly and genitals lie in a straight line.
- The point: desires are not purely mental; they are connected with the body, which can stimulate them and through which they express themselves. The physical affects the mental, and the mental affects the physical.
- Lying on an easy chair with a comfortable headrest to study strengthens the desire to sleep, or even creates it.
- Overeating bloats the stomach, passion increases in the body and seeks expression — illicit sex being one way.
- The tongue's urge for delicious food is stimulated by the six _rasas_ (tastes); controlling that agitation keeps us from succumbing `46:00 – 48:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Anger grows from frustrated, unwarranted expectations rooted in unregulated attachment; and since body and mind affect each other, controlling the tongue — the top of the tongue-belly-genitals line — helps check the whole chain. [42:00 – 48:00]
</Callout>

### The prasādam prayer: a meditation on our condition and Kṛṣṇa's mercy [48:00 – 54:00]

**Context.** The commentary brings in the song by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura that Prabhupāda gave as the _prasādam_ prayers `48:00`.

#### Lamentation for lack of faith [48:00]

- The song has two parts; it begins with a verse from the _Mahābhārata_.
- It is first a prayer of lamentation: alas, we lack faith in three manifestations of mercy — the holy name, _prasādam_ and the devotees — through which faith could increase.
- Then it meditates on how glorious _prasādam_ is.

#### Beyond saying grace [48:00 – 50:00]

- Many world religions have a tradition of saying grace — thanking God for food, a good tradition since food is essential for survival.
- But nature provides food to everyone; thanking God only for bodily maintenance is thanking Him for life at the animal level — necessary, but not a very high consciousness.
- The _ācārya_'s prayer focuses instead on how Kṛṣṇa manifests as _prasādam_ so that we can control our senses and be liberated from material existence through devotional service `50:00`.

#### Our trapped condition [50:00 – 52:00]

- The body puts us in a trap of ignorance, covering higher knowledge and higher taste.
- The senses drag us towards death and towards activity that perpetuates karmic bondage and repeated birth and death.
- We are surrounded by an ocean of almost infinite sense objects.
- Among the senses the tongue is the most voracious; those who control it find the others come under control without much difficulty, while the tongue's enjoying mentality spreads to the rest of the body `50:00 – 52:00`.

#### The rescuing grace [52:00]

- Seeing us struggling and sinking in _saṁsāra_, Kṛṣṇa comes as _prasādam_ to enable us to conquer the tongue.
- We take it to our heart's content, not our tongue's content — the tongue is never contented and keeps us discontented.
- Having taken this nectar, we seek energy for devotional service, calling out for mercy so that we can serve Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The prasādam prayer is not mere thanks for survival but a profound meditation on our vulnerable condition in material existence and on Kṛṣṇa's rescuing grace through prasādam — taken to the heart's content, not the tongue's. [48:00 – 52:00]
</Callout>

### Prasādam as a spiritual principle across cultures [54:00 – 62:00]

**Context.** The commentary then speaks of stomach disease from overeating and lists foods of different kinds; foods in the modes will be discussed with the seventeenth chapter of the Gītā `54:00`.

#### Foods and consciousness [54:00]

- Meat-eating (eating corpses) and intoxicants are _tāmasika_; overly spicy food is _rājasika_; milk and grains are _sāttvika_.
- Beyond all these is food offered to Kṛṣṇa — delicious — but visiting a rich man's mansion just for the food is not devotional consciousness: the _prasādam_ is transcendental, but our consciousness is passionate.
- _Prasādam_ is transcendental because it is offered to Kṛṣṇa, but we are not transcendental, so it has certain effects on us; we must check what effect we want and what effect is actually coming.

#### Pizza prasādam [54:00 – 58:00]

- Many Indians visiting a temple abroad get a culture shock hearing of "pizza _prasādam_," because for them _prasādam_ is a sweet placed reverently in an outstretched palm at the temple.
- _Prasādam_ is not a cultural commodity but a spiritual principle: whatever we eat, we first offer to Kṛṣṇa and then take the remnants as His mercy — so all our food should be _prasādam_, not just a morsel at the end of a function.
- Kṛṣṇa does not want the flesh of His other children, so non-vegetarian food cannot be offered; within that, cuisines differ by culture, and where pizza is normal food, offering and taking it is perfectly all right `56:00 – 58:00`.
- The cultural translation of the principle varies with time, place and circumstance — for some a sweet, for others a full meal `58:00`.

#### The honest test: what helps me remember Kṛṣṇa? [58:00 – 60:00]

- This does not make every item equally desirable. Externally, for function, we may call an item Italian or Chinese, but internally the focus should be Kṛṣṇa; if we are thinking more of "Chinese" than of _prasādam_, our consciousness is elsewhere.
- Mantra rock: for those familiar with rock, it spiritualises their music and brings Kṛṣṇa into it; for those habituated to traditional culture, they may think more of the rock than the mantra `58:00 – 60:00`.
- Each of us must take a devotional inventory: which activities help me maintain remembrance of Kṛṣṇa? We need not give up all other non-anti-devotional activities, but must be honest and not pretend they equal activities that help us remember Kṛṣṇa better.
- The teacher clarifies he is not saying parathas are higher _prasādam_ than pizza; the point is to check individually what brings greater remembrance — being excited only by an item's material features is not devotional consciousness `60:00`.

#### The two-way bridge [60:00 – 62:00]

- Prabhupāda taught his Western disciples not only universal spiritual principles but specific cultural practices — traditional dress, cuisine and songs — wanting traditional culture to continue.
- The Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement acts as a bridge taking traditional Indian culture to the world.
- But a bridge cannot easily allow one-way traffic: culture from all over the world also comes through ISKCON to traditional places in India, so devotees who had never heard of certain foods such as pizza now encounter them `62:00`.
- This is not bad, but we must be aware whether what is happening takes us towards or away from Kṛṣṇa, and act according to what is favourable or unfavourable for our _bhakti_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Prasādam is a universal spiritual principle — offer everything to Kṛṣṇa and take His remnants as mercy — whose cultural expression may vary; the real test, for food or music, is honestly asking what helps me remember Kṛṣṇa. [54:00 – 62:00]
</Callout>
