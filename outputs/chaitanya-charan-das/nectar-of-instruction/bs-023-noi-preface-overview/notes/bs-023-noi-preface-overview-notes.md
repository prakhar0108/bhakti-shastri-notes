### Overview: the nine stages of bhakti as a medical treatment [06:00 – 14:00]

**Context.** The teacher presents the book's overview through the nine stages of _bhakti_ that Rūpa Gosvāmī describes in his other book, _The Nectar of Devotion_, using the metaphor of a sick person seeking treatment `06:00`.

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- _Śraddhā_ — preliminary, positive curiosity: "What is this Hare Kṛṣṇa? Is there something beyond this life?" Like a patient who believes he has a disease and goes to the doctor `06:00 – 08:00`.
- _Sādhu-saṅga_ and _bhajana-kriyā_ — coming into the association of devotees and learning to practise devotional service, the spiritual treatment `08:00`.
- _Anartha-nivṛtti_ — _anartha_ is that which has no meaning (_artha_ also means value or money); it is the misconception of ascribing meaning and value to what is meaningless and valueless. In this stage the misconception "retires" from consciousness, like symptoms subsiding under treatment `08:00 – 10:00`.
- _Niṣṭhā_ — steady faith, like a banyan tree that even an elephant cannot uproot, unlike a banana tree that can be shaken down `10:00`.
- _Ruci_ — taste for Kṛṣṇa, service to Kṛṣṇa and His paraphernalia; glimpses may come now, but the real taste is far deeper `10:00`.
- _Āsakti_ — attachment, not just to devotion but to Kṛṣṇa Himself `12:00`.
- _Bhāva_ — spiritual emotion, the preliminary "next door" to _prema_ `12:00`.
- _Prema_ — full, comprehensive, all-consuming love for Kṛṣṇa that floods the heart; the stage of the devotees of Vṛndāvana and our ultimate aspiration `12:00`.

#### The patient and the doctor [08:00 – 10:00]

- Faith — "I have a disease; I must go to the doctor" — leads to inquiry.
- The doctor explains the disease and prescribes treatment — the practice of devotional service.
- The treatment works — prominent symptoms subside (_anartha-nivṛtti_).
- Seeing ourselves get better, faith grows strong: "this treatment works; I want to become fully healthy" `10:00`.

#### Taste and attachment [10:00 – 12:00]

- Right now we have a taste for worldly, sensual things; as we become spiritually healthy, a taste for Kṛṣṇa develops.
- Negative parallel: a person given a free taste of drugs first likes them, then gets hooked; positively, the taste of devotion leads to attachment to Kṛṣṇa `12:00`.

#### The creeper of devotion [12:00 – 14:00]

- Rūpa Gosvāmī explains that devotional service is like a creeper given by the spiritual master.
- It must be protected in two ways:
  - a fence, so that no predators damage or destroy it;
  - watering, so that it grows.
- Protected and nourished, it eventually blossoms and gives the fruit of _prema_ — and how to do this is what _The Nectar of Instruction_ teaches `14:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti grows like a cure: faith brings us to the doctor, practice removes the symptoms, faith deepens into steadiness, taste, attachment, emotion and finally love — and _The Nectar of Instruction_ teaches how to protect and nourish that growing creeper. [06:00 – 14:00]
</Callout>

### The Preface: under the supervision of Rūpa Gosvāmī [14:00 – 18:00]

<!-- vedabase:noi/preface -->

**Context.** Prabhupāda's Preface opens strikingly: the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement is conducted under the supervision of Rūpa Gosvāmī `14:00`.

#### Humility and connection to the paramparā [14:00]

- Prabhupāda does not say the movement is under his own supervision, though without him there would have been no Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement in the West.
- He was constantly preaching, guiding and overseeing — at first a "one-man army," later the commander of a fallible army whose soldiers he would raise and inspire again to fight.
- The movement spread all over the world within eleven years — something with few, if any, parallels in world history.
- His statement shows a profound humility and connectedness with the _ācāryas_ and the _paramparā_.

#### A book for the whole world [14:00 – 16:00]

- Devotees thought the book was meant only for devotees and published a very limited run.
- When Prabhupāda came to know, he wanted it distributed widely: though meant for devotees, the whole world should eventually become devotees, and new people too can benefit.
- He saw the whole world as filled with potential devotees.

#### Progression in Vedic revelation [16:00]

- The _Bhāgavatam_ gives the highest truth of Kṛṣṇa's supremacy, but after the start of Kali-yuga that truth was not widely propagated.
- Other processes gained prominence in sequence: _karma-kāṇḍa_; Buddhism and Jainism as a refutation of it; impersonalism as propagated by Śaṅkarācārya as a refutation of those; then the Vaiṣṇava _ācāryas_, by whom _bhakti_ was re-established.
- Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu established the conclusion of Vedic literature — pure devotional service to Śrī Kṛṣṇa — which became central to all subsequent understanding in the tradition.
- Mahāprabhu is Kṛṣṇa Himself, come again to reveal the essence of His descent 5,000 years ago and the way to access His pastimes.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
By placing the whole movement under Rūpa Gosvāmī's supervision, Prabhupāda models humility and shows that the movement's power comes through the disciplic line that carries Mahāprabhu's conclusion — pure devotional service to Kṛṣṇa. [14:00 – 16:00]
</Callout>

### Mahāprabhu's missions for the Gosvāmīs [16:00 – 24:00]

**Context.** The Lord can do everything Himself, but He always has His associates play a part; Mahāprabhu recruited prominent, intelligent and charismatic individuals while travelling and sent them to Vṛndāvana `16:00 – 18:00`.

#### The four missions [18:00 – 20:00]

1. Build temples to glorify the pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.
2. Rediscover the lost places of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa's pastimes.
3. Write literature establishing devotional service as the ultimate teaching of Vedic literature — both books on Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality and conclusion of the Vedas, and books on how to practise.
4. Exemplify the practice of pure devotional service.

- The first two concern places; the second two concern books.

#### Tangible signs of success [18:00 – 20:00]

- Prabhupāda would say that when a temple is built, a part of the material world is offered back to Kṛṣṇa and spiritualised.
- He felt that every time a book was published, an empire was won: a book is an "empire of thought" whose wisdom conquers hearts as it is read.
- Both temples and books are ultimately meant to attract people, so the third measure is how many people become devotees `20:00`.

#### Mahāprabhu in Purī and the Gosvāmīs' role [20:00]

- In His last eighteen years Mahāprabhu stayed in Purī, relishing pure love of Kṛṣṇa with intimate devotees — demonstrating the love of the spiritual world.
- Spreading Kṛṣṇa consciousness to everyone else was done primarily by the Gosvāmīs: they preached to scholars in Vṛndāvana and, especially through their books, laid foundations for future generations and systematised Mahāprabhu's teachings.

#### Two literary heritages [20:00 – 24:00]

- The Bengali associates wrote mostly pastime books — important, but the teachings do not come systematically in them.
- The Vṛndāvana Gosvāmīs wrote the philosophical teachings systematically.
- The two heritages were integrated in one magnum opus, the _Caitanya-caritāmṛta_, by an author born in Bengal, steeped in the pastimes since childhood, then instructed to go to Vṛndāvana and trained comprehensively under the Gosvāmīs.
- Even when the Gosvāmīs describe pastimes, they do so to illustrate _siddhānta_ — e.g. a book by Rūpa Gosvāmī that describes the Lord's incarnations not chronologically but to show Kṛṣṇa as highest among the Lord's forms and the _gopīs_ as highest among devotees `24:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Mahāprabhu's mission was carried forward through temples, holy places, books and personal example — and the Gosvāmīs' books gave His teachings the systematic philosophical foundation that later generations, including ISKCON, stand on. [18:00 – 24:00]
</Callout>

### Understanding Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa through the Gosvāmīs [24:00 – 30:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda explains that only by developing attraction to the lotus feet of the Gosvāmīs can we understand devotion to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa `24:00 – 26:00`.

#### The lotus feet as instructions [24:00]

- Touching the feet of Vaiṣṇavas is very good if they allow it, but we cannot force ourselves on them.
- Their lotus feet manifest in a more accessible and transformational way through their instructions — both their books and the lives they live.

#### Richer revelation at a historical moment [26:00]

- The whole movement comes down through the _paramparā_ — from Mahāprabhu through the Gosvāmīs and the spiritual masters to us.
- At a particular historical moment the revelation became richer: Mahāprabhu appeared to bestow on human society the "science of Kṛṣṇa."
- That science is best seen in those who exemplify it most vividly — the _gopīs_; the most exalted of Kṛṣṇa's activities are those with the _gopīs_.

#### Rūpa Gosvāmī as Rūpa Mañjarī [26:00 – 28:00]

- At a deeper level the Gosvāmīs are associates of Rādhārāṇī manifested as associates of Mahāprabhu.
- Along with the _gopīs_ are the _mañjarīs_ — younger assistants in Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; Rūpa Gosvāmī is Rūpa Mañjarī in the spiritual world.
- So he knows the intimate pastimes not by intellectual analysis or scripture study alone, but by personal experience.
- In this age he appears as a _sannyāsī_, exemplifying renunciation, organisation and extraordinary devotion — showing how renounced devotion is practised.

#### Why renunciation matters [28:00]

- It is very easy to misunderstand the pastimes and imitate on a material level what is done on a spiritual level — leading to self-destruction.
- Therefore the pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa can be understood only through the teachings of the Gosvāmīs and great souls like them.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The intimate pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are reached through the instructions of the Gosvāmīs — who know them by experience yet lived as renunciants — not by imitation, which only leads to self-destruction. [24:00 – 28:00]
</Callout>

### The necessity of self-mastery [28:00 – 38:00]

**Context.** The first point Prabhupāda makes in the purport is that we must have self-mastery on the spiritual path `28:00 – 30:00`.

#### Body and soul are opposite [30:00]

- The body is temporary, dead and unconscious, prone to diseases and miseries; the soul is eternal, conscious and blissful (_ānanda_).
- Because of this incompatibility, the soul cannot really enjoy at the body level, despite a few pleasures here and there.
- To detach from the body and experience spiritual reality requires purification, centred on self-mastery.

#### The drunkard's recovery [30:00]

- A drinker does not become sober just by stopping, but by stopping and then finding constructive activities that free him from the obsession.
- Yet stopping is the first requirement: while in a stupor thinking "I am the king of the world," recovery cannot even begin.
- Likewise, regaining spiritual consciousness means giving up bodily consciousness, which is caused largely by the uncontrolled mind and senses `32:00`.

#### Riveted to the body [32:00]

- As soon as sense objects come before us, they cause forgetfulness of spiritual identity — e.g. when a man and a woman see each other, each starts thinking how to attract the other, and both become riveted to bodily identity.
- A rivet seals something shut; similarly, uncontrolled mind and senses, material desires and the resulting attachments rivet the soul's consciousness to the body.

#### Occasional peace versus steady peace [32:00 – 34:00]

- Some think spiritual life is about experiences — dancing in the temple and feeling peaceful. That is good, but we want steady peace, not occasional peace.
- Steady peace comes only through purification — becoming free from the desires that rob our peace: lust, anger, greed and illusion.
- Even our anxieties, looked at deeply, generally originate in illusion; the world's sufferings come from everyone's uncontrolled mind and senses `34:00`.
- Without self-control one can practise, but not steadily — practising, being dragged down, practising again.

#### "One's first duty is to control his mind and senses" [34:00 – 36:00]

- Prabhupāda writes that in all spiritual affairs one's first duty is to control the mind and senses; without that, one cannot advance.
- This may frighten us: "I chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, but I can't control my senses — will I make no advancement?" It means the matter is serious.
- Fire and water: lighting a fire while pouring water on it — the two efforts almost cancel each other.
- Rowing with the anchor down: one may row all night, but the boat cannot move while the anchor of attachment remains. The efforts are not wasted — they accumulate as spiritual credit — but they manifest when we resolve to stop pandering to the senses `36:00`.

#### Why against illicit sex? [36:00 – 38:00]

- In America, people asked Prabhupāda why he was against sex. He replied that he was not — his students marry — but against illicit sex.
- His simple explanation: for spiritual advancement the mind must focus on spiritual truth, ultimately Kṛṣṇa; if it is constantly thinking how to get an attractive partner, how can it think of God?
- Love for Kṛṣṇa purifies the mind and awakens natural attraction to Him, but attachment to many worldly things leaves no room to think of Him.

#### Rising to goodness [38:00]

- Prabhupāda says one must raise oneself to the platform of goodness by following the instructions; then everything about further progress will be revealed.
- The teacher notes that in the mode of goodness one can perceive Kṛṣṇa's inner voice more clearly.
- The sequence is explained in a series of verses in the second chapter of the First Canto of the _Bhāgavatam_: coming to goodness, one becomes joyful, practises _bhakti_, and by that becomes liberated.
- We do not give up instructions after reaching goodness; but where instructions are unclear in a situation, discretion and the inner voice become much clearer in goodness.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Bhakti and sense indulgence are like fire and water — or rowing with the anchor down — so self-mastery is the first duty: stop pandering to the senses, rise to goodness by following instructions, and steady peace and further guidance follow. [30:00 – 38:00]
</Callout>

### The attitude of the follower [40:00 – 44:00]

**Context.** Prabhupāda's memorable statement: advancement in Kṛṣṇa consciousness depends on the attitude of the follower `40:00`.

#### What "attitude" means here [40:00]

- Today "attitude" is popular — "think positive, you will achieve whatever you dream."
- Read in context, Prabhupāda is speaking of sense and mind control: it may seem very difficult, but a determined devotee — "I will become sense-controlled, I will become mind-controlled" — will eventually achieve it by practice.

#### Becoming a gosvāmī [40:00 – 42:00]

- The _gosvāmīs_ are those who control the mind and senses; the highest success in life is to become a _gosvāmī_ and a pure devotee of the Lord, following the instructions of this book.
- A _gosvāmī_ learns to control the senses and, with controlled senses, serves Kṛṣṇa and develops love for Him.
- The _Bhāgavatam_ says the Lord is conquered by the love of those who have conquered their senses — because their love is pure and undistracted.
- So we must focus on both: developing devotion to Kṛṣṇa and controlling mind and senses.

#### "Easier" — the traffic-rules analogy [42:00 – 44:00]

- Prabhupāda says that following these instructions strictly makes life successful more easily; "easier" is comparative — spiritual advancement is not easy, and without the instructions it is much tougher.
- Driving without traffic rules may feel easier — turn anywhere, go anywhere — but brings constant hazards: oncoming vehicles, police with tickets. With the rules, hazards still exist but are fewer.
- Scriptural rules are like the road: sometimes blocked, congested or difficult, requiring adjustments (discussed later), but moving along a road is far easier than moving where there is none `44:00`.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Advancement depends on the follower's determined attitude — "I can and I will become sense-controlled" — and following the ācāryas' rules makes the difficult journey far easier, like driving on a road with traffic rules. [40:00 – 44:00]
</Callout>
