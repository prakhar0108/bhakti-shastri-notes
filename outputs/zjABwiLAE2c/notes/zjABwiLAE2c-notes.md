## Shloka-Wise Notes & Analytical Commentary

### Bhagavad-gita 3.20 [07:22 – 17:24]

<!-- verse:3.20 -->

**Context.** Arjuna may wonder whether anyone has actually attained perfection while remaining active. Krishna answers with Janaka and other _rāja-ṛṣis_: they attained complete perfection through prescribed work and continued acting for _loka-saṅgraha_. [07:35 – 09:33]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Karmaṇā:** Through work. The teacher emphasizes that perfection is not restricted to external renunciation. [07:56 – 08:09]
- **Saṁsiddhim:** Complete or highest perfection. [08:00 – 08:09]
- **Janakādayaḥ:** Janaka and others; the plural broadens the evidence beyond one exceptional king. [08:13 – 08:38]
- **Loka-saṅgraham:** Acting to hold society together and present a proper example for people in general. [08:41 – 09:33]

#### Janaka broadens Arjuna's conception [09:09 – 09:33]

Janaka, the father of Sita, did not abandon royal duties. He and many other saintly kings attained perfection while acting without selfish attachment. Krishna therefore directs Arjuna to remain in his own prescribed work.

#### Negative frustration is not spiritual qualification [09:37 – 12:28]

Temporary disgust with worldly difficulty is unstable. The teacher distinguishes such frustration from genuine spiritual attraction:

- Conflict at home, pressure at work, dismissal from a job, or a failed relationship can produce an impulse to leave everything.
- Mere aversion to difficulty is insufficient for renunciation.
- Stability requires positive attraction to Krishna, not only hatred of material inconvenience.
- Until the heart is purified, one should not prematurely abandon prescribed work.

#### Spiritual priority makes detachment possible [12:32 – 16:58]

- The conditioned soul readily becomes attached even to small habits.
- Deliberate time for chanting, hearing, and spiritual practice creates a higher connection.
- Detachment does not arise automatically; it grows from this higher connection.
- Material and spiritual duties both have a place, but balance begins by making spiritual life the first priority. Only then can material life be ordered around it.
- Janaka represents this integration: spiritually connected, socially responsible, and unattached to the fruits of royal duty.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Perfection does not require abandoning responsible action. Like Janaka and other saintly kings, one should cultivate a higher spiritual connection, perform prescribed duty without selfish attachment, and act for the welfare and guidance of others. [08:00 – 17:24]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.21 [17:32 – 39:53]

<!-- verse:3.21 -->

**Context.** Krishna states the general principle behind Janaka's conduct: people imitate the actions of those they regard as great, and the standards leaders establish through behavior become society's standards. [17:49 – 18:51]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Yad yad ācarati:** Whatever conduct or behavior a person performs. [17:54 – 18:08]
- **Śreṣṭhaḥ:** A great or leading person. [18:08 – 18:11]
- **Pramāṇam:** The standard established through one's own conduct. [18:28 – 18:44]
- **Anuvartate:** Others follow or reproduce that standard. [18:44 – 18:51]

#### No action is isolated [18:54 – 21:25]

Our conduct affects other people. The teacher says a society can be judged by its heroes because people reproduce what their heroes normalize. When public admiration shifts from character to money, glamour, or popularity, society's practical definition of success also shifts.

#### Leadership as position and disposition [21:46 – 24:58]

The teacher presents four combinations:

1. **No position and no disposition:** The person lacks both formal influence and exemplary character; the damage is comparatively limited.
2. **Position without disposition:** The person holds administrative, political, or spiritual authority but lacks the corresponding character; this is the most dangerous category.
3. **Disposition without position:** The person has no formal office but lives with exemplary character; this is an ideal foundation.
4. **Position with disposition:** Formal influence and exemplary character are united; this produces the greatest positive effect.

Every person leads at some scale because everyone influences family members, peers, dependents, or people they guide.

#### Practice gives force to teaching [25:01 – 26:24]

In the story of the boy who ate too much jaggery, a saint asked the mother to return after ten days. Only after giving up jaggery himself did he instruct the boy. The principle is that teaching lacks full force when the teacher does not practice it.

#### Replace criticism with responsible contribution [26:35 – 33:38]

- People are quick to criticize managers, teachers, temple leaders, and other authorities while overlooking their own influence.
- Each person moves among three roles: leader, equal, and subordinate.
- Growth comes from asking:
  - As a leader, how well am I serving those entrusted to me?
  - As an equal, how good a friend and collaborator am I?
  - As a subordinate, how well am I serving and cooperating?
- Radhanath Maharaja's foundational question, as quoted by the teacher, is: “How can I best serve in every situation?” [29:39 – 30:08]
- No material management system, mutual understanding, or leader is perfect. Krishna alone perfectly understands every living being. Therefore, one should improve one's own contribution instead of becoming only a critic. [30:18 – 33:38]

#### The power of example [33:45 – 39:26]

- The teacher's retelling of Lakshmi choosing her husband illustrates how a fault-finding outlook can identify limitations everywhere; even Krishna is criticized by the envious. [33:45 – 34:50]
- The greatest gift parents give children is not wealth, toys, or education, but their example. [35:09 – 35:39]
- An _ācārya_ teaches by conduct. A leader who smokes cannot effectively teach the public to stop smoking. [36:07 – 37:19]
- Role models should be chosen by generosity over wealth, character over charisma, and integrity over popularity. The acharyas, Six Goswamis, and Srila Prabhupada are offered as spiritual role models. [37:32 – 38:49]
- Even when change is not immediately visible, every choice leaves an impression on the person making it. [38:52 – 39:26]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Leadership begins with conduct, not title. Because every person influences others, the practical question in every role is not merely “What is wrong with the leader?” but “How can I best serve and set a standard worth following?” [21:46 – 39:26]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.22 [39:37 – 45:52]

<!-- verse:3.22 -->

**Context.** Krishna now gives His own example. Arjuna may claim that a purified person has no need to work, but Krishna has no prescribed obligation, unfulfilled desire, or need in any world, and He still remains engaged in duty. [39:57 – 42:15]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Kartavyam:** Prescribed duty. Duties apply according to social and spiritual position, but Krishna is under no such obligation. [40:24 – 40:55]
- **Triṣu lokeṣu:** In the three worlds; the teacher expands this as all planetary realms. [40:55 – 41:03]
- **Anavāptam:** Nothing Krishna desires remains unattained. [41:03 – 41:18]
- **Avāptavyam:** Nothing remains that Krishna needs to obtain. [41:18 – 42:05]
- **Varta eva ca karmaṇi:** Nevertheless, Krishna remains engaged in work. [42:05 – 42:13]

#### Krishna's answer to the qualified renunciant [42:13 – 43:20]

Arjuna remains a king and kshatriya with obligations to protect dharma. If he abandons the battlefield, less-qualified people may copy the external renunciation without possessing his qualification. Krishna's own conduct shows that freedom from personal need does not cancel responsibility to set a proper example.

#### The Supreme has nothing to achieve [43:23 – 45:24]

Drawing from the purport, the teacher describes Krishna as:

- The controller of all controllers.
- The supreme object of worship among all worshipable beings.
- The protector and maintainer of all.
- One with no equal or superior.
- One whose manifold energies act naturally, without any deficiency that He must remedy through work.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
The highest freedom from personal need does not imply careless inactivity. Krishna has no prescribed duty and nothing to obtain, yet He works to establish dharma and guide those who naturally follow authority. [40:24 – 45:52]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.23 [45:55 – 51:20]

<!-- verse:3.23 -->

**Context.** Krishna explains the consequence if He were ever to stop carefully performing duty: all people would follow His path. The greater the authority, the more consequential even a moment of negligence becomes. [46:05 – 47:11]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Yadi:** If. [46:05 – 46:16]
- **Na varteyam:** If Krishna did not remain engaged. [46:16 – 46:22]
- **Jātu:** At any time, even momentarily in the teacher's explanation. [49:59 – 50:11]
- **Atandritaḥ:** Attentive, alert, and free from laziness or drowsy negligence. [46:22 – 46:40]
- **Mama vartma anuvartante:** People would follow Krishna's path. [46:40 – 46:55]

#### Krishna's attentive household example [47:11 – 47:55]

The teacher recalls Narada Muni's visit to Krishna's palaces in Dvaraka. Krishna was seen carrying out appropriate household and royal responsibilities in every setting, demonstrating attentive engagement rather than indifference.

#### Drowsiness as a picture of inattention [47:59 – 49:55]

- A fully sleeping driver will stop; a fully alert driver can proceed.
- The dangerous state is _tandrā_: neither fully asleep nor fully awake.
- The teacher applies this to devotional practice: inattentive chanting, hearing, service, dealings with devotees, and reception of the spiritual master's instruction all become vulnerable to offense.
- Inattention is described in the lecture as the “mother of all offenses.” [49:19 – 49:55]

#### Maya asks for one moment at a time [49:59 – 51:20]

Spiritual life is shaped by moment-to-moment choices. The teacher cites Ajamila, Indra's neglect of Brihaspati, Romaharshana Suta's failure to honor Balarama, and Indradyumna Maharaja's neglect of Agastya Muni as examples where brief inattention carried grave consequences. Exact proper names and episode details remain subject to transcript verification.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Responsible action must be attentive action. A leader's momentary negligence can become another person's lifelong precedent, and devotional steadiness is likewise protected one attentive choice at a time. [46:22 – 51:20]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.24 [51:23 – 01:02:10]

<!-- verse:3.24 -->

**Context.** Krishna states the social result of abandoning His exemplary duties: the worlds would fall into ruin, disorder would spread through the population, and the peace of living beings would be destroyed. [51:23 – 52:28]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Utsīdeyuḥ ime lokāḥ:** These worlds would be ruined or destroyed. [51:49 – 52:09]
- **Na kuryāṁ karma:** If Krishna did not perform prescribed work. [52:09 – 52:17]
- **Saṅkarasya kartā:** He would become the cause of social and generational disorder. [52:17 – 52:32]
- **Upahanyām imāḥ prajāḥ:** The population would be harmed rather than protected. [51:33 – 52:32]

#### Krishna reverses Arjuna's social argument [52:32 – 53:49]

In Chapter 1, Arjuna argued that fighting would lead to _varṇa-saṅkara_. Krishna now explains that abandoning righteous duty and setting an example of unqualified renunciation would itself make others neglect their responsibilities, producing the disorder Arjuna fears.

#### Influence scales both service and damage [53:51 – 56:26]

- “The higher one is situated, the greater the damage or service one can do.” [53:51 – 54:04]
- Conduct continually preaches, even without words:
  - Arriving late teaches that the class is unimportant.
  - Sleeping in class teaches that its message is irrelevant.
  - Listening attentively teaches that the message matters.
- Maharaja Parikshit's absorption while hearing from Sukadeva Goswami teaches through example: “Bhagavatam is my life.” [55:00 – 55:18]
- Because the Lord is the father of all living beings, the misdirection of the population carries responsibility at the level of leadership. [56:10 – 56:26]

#### Follow the Lord; do not imitate the powerful [56:29 – 01:02:10]

The teacher distinguishes _anusaraṇa_ (following instruction and authorized example) from _anukaraṇa_ (imitating extraordinary acts):

- Follow the Lord's instructions and conduct that is consistent with those instructions.
- Do not imitate extraordinary acts merely because a powerful _īśvara_ performed them.
- One cannot imitate Krishna lifting Govardhana Hill or Lord Shiva drinking poison.
- The sun remains uncontaminated while purifying dirty places; an ordinary person does not possess that capacity.
- Krishna's Dvaraka pastimes display His exemplary conduct as a king, while extraordinary intimate pastimes are not a license for imitation.
- The lecture cites Srimad-Bhagavatam 10.33.30–31 in this connection; exact Sanskrit recitation is omitted because the captions are uncertain. [57:33 – 01:02:10]

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
When leaders abandon duty, imitation magnifies the damage into social disorder. Follow Krishna's teachings and authorized example, but never imitate extraordinary divine acts without the corresponding qualification and guidance. [51:23 – 01:02:10]
</Callout>

### Bhagavad-gita 3.25 [01:02:12 – 01:05:57]

<!-- verse:3.25 -->

**Context.** Krishna returns from His own example to Arjuna's practical method. The ignorant work with attachment; the learned also remain active, but without attachment and with the intention of guiding people on the right path. [01:02:38 – 01:03:57]

**Key Terms Explained by the Teacher**

- **Saktāḥ:** Attached to work and its results. [01:02:56 – 01:03:21]
- **Avidvāṁsaḥ:** Ignorant or not spiritually learned. [01:02:42 – 01:03:26]
- **Vidvān:** A learned person. [01:03:29 – 01:03:55]
- **Asaktaḥ:** Without attachment. [01:03:33 – 01:03:57]
- **Cikīrṣuḥ loka-saṅgraham:** Desiring to act for the guidance and welfare of society. [01:03:57 – 01:04:01]

#### Same external action, different consciousness [01:03:41 – 01:04:39]

- An ordinary king may govern from attachment to power, wealth, or position.
- Arjuna is instructed to perform the same royal and martial work without selfish attachment.
- A fruitive ritualist may perform sacrifice to attain heaven or material benefit.
- Bharata Maharaja may outwardly perform sacrifice while possessing entirely different consciousness.

#### Consciousness gives action its potency [01:04:39 – 01:05:57]

Activity becomes more potent and manifests its effect more fully as consciousness develops. This applies to chanting, hearing, and service as well as ordinary duty. External work is visible; the consciousness behind it is not, but Krishna values that unseen intention. Therefore, the learned person does not merely copy the attached worker's motive; the learned person performs necessary work without attachment and for _loka-saṅgraha_.

<Callout type="idea" title="Takeaway">
Karma-yoga does not necessarily change the visible task; it changes the consciousness and purpose behind it. The learned remain active like others, but work without selfish attachment to guide society and satisfy Krishna. [01:03:41 – 01:05:57]
</Callout>

## Audience Q&A & Clarifications

### 1. Why does Krishna perform bewildering or apparently transgressive pastimes? [01:06:16 – 01:09:47]

- **Question:** If conditioned souls may misunderstand Krishna's extraordinary acts, why does He perform them?
- **Teacher's clarification:** Extraordinary acts establish the distinction between the Supreme Lord and an ordinary living being. Krishna also descends to exchange loving pastimes with His devotees, not merely to deliver a lecture. Envious persons will find fault even in apparently conventional conduct, as criticism of Lord Rama also demonstrates. Loving disposition tends not to dwell on faults, whereas envy invents faults even in the faultless.

### 2. Should a young woman work outside the home to retain social influence? [01:10:27 – 01:15:11]

- **Question:** Since modern society often gives more weight to earners, should young women pursue employment while balancing bhakti?
- **Teacher's clarification:** Society contains many mistaken perceptions, and no single choice can correct them all. Employment is not spiritually faulty; an educated woman may benefit from gaining work experience, especially given uncertain family circumstances. Later, according to her family situation and meaningful devotional engagement, she may continue or leave employment. Not being able to work is also not a fault. The governing principle is to perform one's present roles well: devotional practice should not become an excuse to neglect reasonable family responsibilities, because imbalance weakens the example one hopes to set.

### 3. When should we follow conduct, and when should we only follow instruction? [01:15:24 – 01:19:36]

- **Question:** How can one distinguish proper following from blind imitation of Krishna or empowered controllers?
- **Teacher's clarification:** The Lord's instructions are authoritative. His conduct may be followed when it is consistent with those teachings, such as honesty, friendship, compassion, and responsible kingship. Extraordinary acts beyond scriptural instruction are not to be imitated. Guru and sadhu guidance are necessary when the distinction is unclear; independent interpretation easily produces mistakes.

### 4. Do extra rounds counted informally while working constitute an offense? [01:19:40 – 01:20:17]

- **Question:** After completing regular rounds attentively, may one chant extra mantras on a counter while working and occasionally speaking?
- **Teacher's clarification:** The teacher says this is not an offense once the regular sixteen rounds are complete; extra chanting may continue in this way.

### 5. What does “disposition” mean in the leadership framework? [01:20:31 – 01:20:45]

- **Clarification:** Position means the external post; disposition means a person's conduct and character.

### 6. Must a self-realized person model material duty rather than spiritual duty? [01:20:50 – 01:23:36]

- **Question:** Why should a self-realized person model material duties instead of setting an example only through spiritual duties?
- **Teacher's clarification:** A realized devotee may set an example through spiritual duty and may leave social or _varṇāśrama_ obligations for bhakti without fault. The present passage argues from the level of _jñāna-yoga_ and _niṣkāma karma-yoga_: Krishna wants Arjuna to fight, so He explains that even one qualified for _jñāna-yoga_ should continue unattached duty rather than inspire unqualified imitation. This does not negate the later teaching about full devotional surrender.

- **Jaggery and the Saint [25:01 – 26:24]:** Instruction gains force when the teacher first practices it.
